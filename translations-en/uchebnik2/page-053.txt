From there no news came; and he sent to the Olegovichi, to the Davidovichi, and to the Polovtsian princes.

And so with the Olegovichi, and with Prince Vladimir Davidovich, and with Prince Vladimir Andreevich, his nephew, and with his sons, and with the Polovtsians, he gathered a great host and went to Kiev against the great princes of Kiev, against his brother Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash and against his nephew Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich; and having come, they stood opposite the city of Kiev on the meadow, and pitched many tents, and surrounded the city with great forces.

But Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich and Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash stood firmly against them and did not let them cross the Dnieper River, and fought with them on the water, sailing in boats, for Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich wondrously devised battle in boats, so that the rowers in the boats were invisible; only the oars were slightly visible, but the men were not visible, and arrows flew from them at the opponents, but none flew to them, for they were covered with boards very cunningly; and they were swift as birds, and stood in armor, and shot as they wished: in each boat there were two helmsmen—one at the stern and the other at the bow—and they went as they wished, stern-first or bow-first, without turning the boat, and their battle was terrible to the enemies.

But Grand Prince of Pereyaslavl Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky came close with the Olegovichi and the Davidovichi, and with the Polovtsians, and with all his host, to the Vitichev ford, and the Kiev boatmen hindered them; they went to Lake Dolobskoe and from there went along the bank to Zolotitsa. Along the Zolotitsa they entered the Dnieper from below; the Polovtsians went along the meadow; and Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich went on this side of the Dnieper River along the hill, and his boats went along the Dnieper. Coming to the Vitichev ford, they stood opposite, and there was a fierce battle, and the Kiev boatmen overcame the opposing side. And Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich himself fought manfully with them, not allowing them to cross the Dnieper.

The Polovtsians were very wicked and cunning, and began to deliberate and speak thus to Grand Prince of Pereyaslavl Yuri Dolgoruky and all his princes: “You stand here with all your princes and all your host, and manfully attack them at this ford, without sloth; but we will go secretly to seek a place to swim across the Dnieper River to them.”

And so, going to and fro, they came to Zarub; a guard stood there and did not let them cross the Dnieper, and fought fiercely with them. But they, as if enraged, sitting on horses in armor with shields and spears, and shooting like cunning demons—some shooting while fording, others while swimming—thus covered the Dnieper River with a multitude of warriors.
