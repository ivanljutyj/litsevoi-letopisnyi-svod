Some time later, Grand Prince Feodor Olegovich, grandson of Ivan, marched against him with an army, and a fierce battle took place on the Smyadva River on the first day of June. God helped Prince Ivan Vladimirovich Pronsky; many warriors on both sides fell dead, but victory was won. And then Ignaty Semenovich Zherebtsov, voivode of Kolomna, was killed, along with Mikhail Lyalin, Ivan Brynok, and many Kolomnians; the Murom voivode Semyon Zhiroslavich was taken captive, and many Muromians were killed. Christian blood was spilled, because each hated his brother, violated his borders, and desired to seize the lands of others.

In the same year the Ryazan princes Feodor Olegovich, grandson of Ivan, and Ivan Vladimirovich Pronsky concluded peace and love between themselves and confirmed it by kissing the cross, saying: “Why do we indulge the devil, quarrel in vain, and spill Christian blood? We are all of one family, brothers and kinsmen. Let us all abide together in peace and love; let each rule in his own patrimony in unity and brotherly love; let no one cross the borders of another’s principality or begin war and enmity, but let all together fight against demons and our enemies, the peoples of other faiths.” And so they began to rule in peace and love in their principalities, and great joy came to Ryazan over the peace, love, and unity of the great Ryazan princes.

In the same year the Novgorodians of Veliky Novgorod asked the Lithuanian prince Vitovt Keistutovich for Prince Semyon Lugven Olgerdovich, and he sent him to them. The Novgorodians gave him the dependent towns that had formerly belonged to him.

In the same year in Novgorod Vladyka Ioann, Archbishop of Novgorod, covered the Church of Holy Sophia with lead and gilded the great dome.

In the same year, by command of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, the town of Rzhev was built of timber; its voivodes were Prince Yuri Kozelsky and Yuri Vasilyevich.

In the same year, in the month of June, there was a great fire in Rostov. Many churches burned; the cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God burned so that the stones crumbled. In the church were beautiful holy icons, many ancient books, gold and silver vessels adorned with pearls and precious stones, vestments, and precious and valuable things; all of this burned in the fire. And the princely and boyar courts and the courts of common people with their property burned. Then there was a severe drought, and besides that a storm and strong wind; many people also burned, more than a thousand. Such a great fire had not occurred in Rostov for two hundred years.
