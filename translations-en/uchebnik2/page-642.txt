And they, before his eyes, so that he saw it, wrote his name — Matthew. And so, by God’s grace, he remained in the holy angelic monastic habit for eight days.

And at the end of the eighth day, on Tuesday evening, when his noble sons were sitting around him, he blessed them all and dismissed them.

He left only Archimandrite Cornelius and the hieromonk Parthenius, and he commanded them to sing the canon for the departure of the soul. And when the canon ended, the hour came; he stretched out his feet, which carried him away from the glory of this world, and the blessed Matthew reposed quietly and meekly, and in good confession departed to the Lord God, whom he had loved. According to his faith it so happened that there were present at that time the venerable monks of the Holy Mountain, Savva and Spiridon, spiritual men. They prepared the body of the blessed one according to the custom of the Lavra, which they had seen on the Holy Mountain. He reposed in the monastery of St Athanasius on Tuesday late in the evening. And in that same hour his repose became known to the whole city, and people wept for him in all the houses, and there was commotion in the city that night because of this sorrow. On the morning of Wednesday at dawn people began to flock from the whole city, men and women, servants and the poor, and infirm children; the old and sick dragged themselves, saying: “Let us give the last honor to our good defender.”

But the blessed Bishop Arsenius gathered the whole assembly. And from all the city archimandrites and abbots, priests and deacons came together, lit candles and censers, and in bright vestments came to the bier of the blessed and noble Matthew.

The bishop ordered that his honorable body be placed in the great cathedral church of the Holy Savior together with his fathers, where he himself had ordered himself to be laid while still alive. His noble sons wept much for him. Who can describe the tender lamentations and tears of his beloved eldest son, the noble Ivan, who said, weeping: “Father, my father, my sweet and most beloved lord, dear light of my eyes, good instructor and guide of my youth! I always rejoiced when I looked upon the beauty of your face, which is like an angel of God. Now who will comfort the mournful grief of my heart, when I have been separated from your sweet and inexhaustible love? And I shall no longer see your good and beloved face, nor your honorable noble gray hairs, nor shall I have my fill of fair conversations with you, nor hear your quiet teaching and good voice.” And he said much else, weeping, with sorrow in his heart.
