On Savva of Serbia

Saint Savva remained in the archbishopric for a long time after his return from the holy places, and, going around the land of his fatherland, he instructed all in piety, asking them to avoid heresy and always to be renewed by the Orthodoxy of the holy faith. “I believe in One God” — every day, evening, and night the holy fathers commanded to be read, and before sleep it is useful to say this aloud and to terrify the incorporeal enemies. If the evil spirit fled from Saul at the melodies of the pipe and the twanging of David’s harp, then still more fearsome are the trumpets by which the Holy Trinity in the one Godhead is confessed, and then they will be terrified and flee. Moreover, by such a confession of the Orthodox faith every heresy is conquered. He taught all diligently to give alms and do good works, to turn away from evil deeds, fornication, and adultery, and those who had concubines he ordered to be married lawfully; and to those living in monasteries he handed on the laws that he had learned from the holy fathers.

Saint Savva avoided human glory, desiring to end his earthly life in foreign lands; and, having summoned the bishops and all who held authority, he taught them much, and choosing one of his disciples named Arsenius, a hieromonk, more renowned than all for his virtues, he appointed him archbishop and bequeathed to him to act just as he had seen Savva himself act.

He himself, having blessed all and received blessing and forgiveness from all, took much wealth from the archbishop and King Vladislav, and boarded a ship on the western sea in Diocletia. Robbers saw that he had much gold with him and hid in the sea bays; the Lord darkened their minds with darkness and gloom, while the sea was very rough, and the ship was on the verge of shipwreck, but the saint prayed and by the sign of the cross calmed the storm. And he sailed to Jerusalem, and was received by Patriarch Athanasius, and venerated the Most Pure Tomb, and visited the house of Saint John the Theologian, which he had bought from the Saracens to leave as his inheritance, and the church of the Holy Resurrection and the monasteries, and distributed much gold to the poor.
