And they began to fight with Lithuania, having received help from the Tatar army. And Lithuania began to fight with the Muscovites; much blood was shed, and the Tatars seized much plunder.

And the Moscow boyars, commanders, and nobles rejoiced, but the gray-haired old men did not approve of it, saying: “Our boyars are acting wrongly by calling the Tatars to their aid and giving them silver and gold. Was it not in this way that Kiev and Chernigov suffered many calamities and misfortunes, when they began to fight among themselves, called the Tatars to their aid, brother fought against brother, killed one another, seized neighboring lands, while the Polovtsians watched the Russian people, the army, and the bravery of our princes, then attacked us, conquered us, and defeated all—so it will happen now.” Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich warred for some time with his father-in-law, Grand Prince Vitovt Keistutovich of Lithuania, then concluded a truce, but discord between them increased; both suffered losses and endured a certain suffering. At that time there were no experienced boyars in Moscow; the young ones held councils about everything, rejoiced in strife, wars, and bloodshed, and did much contrary to custom.

Yedigei, however, continually maintained quarrels between them, knowing that they were relatives—one a father-in-law and the other a son-in-law—and did not especially desire war and bloodshed, but peace and love; yet he never ceased to set them at odds. He watched the Russian people and the Russian army, waiting for a suitable time to attack Rus. And at that time Prince Svidrigailo Olgerdovich sent word to Moscow to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, wishing to unite with him against Vitovt. Svidrigailo was by faith a Pole, but inclined to battle, a brave and strong warrior. The grand prince with his boyars was glad of his proposal and gave him many cities, almost half of the Grand Principality of Moscow, including the glorious city of Vladimir, in which is the cathedral church of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God, called Zlatoverkhaya, because it had five golden domes; in it is the wonder-working icon of the Most Pure Mother of God, from which many miracles and signs come and which strikes fear into the infidels. And all this was given to a foreign Pole, and therefore many misfortunes befell us.

And the brave Prince Svidrigailo Olgerdovich himself, and his brave army, fell into confusion and were terrified like little children during Yedigei’s invasion, and fled. The strife of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich with Vitovt lasted a third year; both came to the Ugra River, stood a little on the bank, and concluded peace according to the old custom, and parted, each to his own.
