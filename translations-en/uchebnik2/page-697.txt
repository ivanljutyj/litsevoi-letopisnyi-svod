And you now, in my land, shall appoint a metropolitan to the Kievan see.” The bishops said to him: “Although you now possess the Kievan province, you possess earthly authority, and not the church authority of hierarchs: for one thing is the authority of church hierarchs, and another is the authority of a king over the land. You rule worldly affairs as a king, while a hierarch administers church affairs as a hierarch. And in all the Russian land there is only one metropolitan; and even if he now resides in Moscow because of the Tatar invasion, he nevertheless governs in the Kievan land and is named and renowned as the Metropolitan of Kiev! And you have from this neither reproach nor expense, but only glory and profit, in that you observe the ancient customs and laws.” The prince answered the bishops: “If you do not appoint a Kievan metropolitan in my land, you will die an evil death.”

All the aforementioned bishops gathered and by compulsion appointed the Bulgarian Gregory Tsamblak as Metropolitan of Kiev on the fifteenth day of the month of November. And Vytautas ordered them to write a justifying document. They, compelled by Vytautas, sat together with Metropolitan Gregory, chosen by them themselves, and began to write a justifying document for themselves: “Every good gift and every perfect gift, coming down from above from the Father of lights, may it descend also upon us, the humble, and we hold to it; and now by the help of this gift we receive consolation in our great sorrow, that our mother, the church of the Russian metropolis, is comforted in like manner. The humble bishops of the Russian lands, beloved Orthodox friends and brothers in the Holy Spirit: the humble Archbishop Theodosius of Polotsk of the Kievan metropolis, the humble Bishop Isaac of Chernigov, the humble Bishop Dionysius of Lutsk, the humble Bishop Gerasimus of Vladimir, the humble Bishop Sebastian of Smolensk, the humble Bishop Chariton of Kholm, the humble Bishop Euthymius of Turov, seeing the Kievan church in desolation and neglect by Metropolitan Photius, who is the head of all Rus, and all the flock of Christ, coming to perdition without protection, grieve over this, remain in deep sorrow and confusion, like sheep without a shepherd, having a shepherd and teacher only in name and not in his deeds.
