We decide all things and reason just as they do, but only depart from them, not wishing any longer to endure the violence of the emperor of Constantinople against God’s church: for the great patriarch himself, and the divine and sacred council of Constantinople, cannot appoint a metropolitan according to the rules of the apostles, but appoint only those whom the emperor commands. Alas! Everywhere the gift of the Holy Spirit is sold and bought at appointment, as his father the emperor acted in our days toward the Kyiv church and the Russian metropolitans, Cyprian, Pimen, Dionysius, and many others, paying no heed to church honor and order, but only to promises of money, silver and gold, and other property. Hence come great debts, expenses, rumors, confusion, riots, and murders, and worst of all, dishonor to the church of the Russian metropolitanate. Therefore we have judged and decided that it is unworthy of us to accept such metropolitans, who for money have been appointed by the emperor from simple laymen, and not by the will of the patriarch, not according to the traditions of the apostolic and patristic councils. For this reason we, all the bishops, gathered by the grace given to us by the Holy Spirit, and appointed the worthy chief hierarch of the Russian Church, Metropolitan Gregory, in the 6924th year from Adam (the 1424th year from Christ), indiction.”

The Most Reverend Photius, Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus, having learned of this decision, was deeply offended and took great pains to overcome these quarrels and schisms within the church and to gather all into one, as originally there was one ecclesiastical authority, one metropolitan over all the bishops in the whole Russian land. And he wrote letters in which he opposed the division of the church: “At first there were not two Kyiv metropolitanates, but one sacred authority of the Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus over all the bishops of the Russian land.” And having written a letter, he sent it to Kyiv to the princes and boyars and to all Orthodox Christians; in that letter it said: “Vytautas has committed great violence and has brought confusion and schism into the one Church of Kyiv and all Rus, guided not by a conciliar search for the truth, not by the study of the sacred canons, not by the ordination of the holy Patriarch of Constantinople, which he had at first accepted, not by church tradition, but by self-will; acting according to his own desire, he did everything by threatening with secular power. And according to his worldly desire he forced the bishops to appoint another metropolitan to the Kyiv metropolitanate, whom the sacred canons do not accept but excommunicate and reject. So we too do not accept but reject: the one who has split the authority of the Kyiv metropolitanate in two we do not accept but cast aside. So we also say to you: do not accept such self-will and division of the church, so that two metropolitans should rule in one sacred province; but we call upon you to turn away from such innovation and self-will, and then the mercy of God and our blessing will remain with you all. Amen.”
