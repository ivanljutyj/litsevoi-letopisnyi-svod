And David of Murom, having gathered his host, went up the Oka to the aid of Vsevolod Yuryevich.

Grand Prince Vsevolod, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, went from Vladimir to Moscow with all his sons on August 19, a Sunday.

His son Prince Constantine met him with the Novgorodians and Pskovians, and they greeted him with great honor, and he stayed there in Moscow a week.

And from there he went to the Oka River with all his sons: Constantine, Yuri, Yaroslav, and Vladimir; and the Ryazan and Murom princes gathered with their host and stood on one side of the Oka River to aid Vsevolod Yuryevich: Prince Roman Glebovich and his brother Svyatoslav Glebovich with two sons, Mstislav and Rostislav Svyatoslavich, and the two Igorevich brothers, Ingvar and Yuri, and Vladimir’s sons, Gleb’s grandchildren, two brothers Gleb and Oleg—nephews of Roman and Svyatoslav. Prince Vsevolod Glebovich died in Vironsk. His son Kir Mikhailo was son-in-law to Prince Vsevolod Chermny Svyatoslavich.

A report came to Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich from Gleb and Oleg Vladimirovich, saying: “Our uncles, Roman Glebovich and Svyatoslav Glebovich, and Svyatoslav’s sons Mstislav and Rostislav, Gleb’s grandchildren, have counsel and friendship with the Olgovichi and are coming against you with evil intent; beware of them as of serpents.” Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, hearing this, was afraid and began to consult with his like-minded men about what to do. And they advised him thus: “As they are treacherous toward you, so you also act treacherously toward them. Pretend love: arrange a dinner and invite them with great honor to a feast, and thus expose their treachery. Then deal with them according to their deeds. Let no one know of this until the time comes.” In the same year Grand Prince Vsevolod, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, with all his forces went to the Oka River, and having come, pitched tents by the Oka River with the Rostovites, Suzdalians, Vladimirians, Pereyaslavlians, Novgorodians, and Pskovians on the bank and in the meadows, and a vast military force assembled. Prince Roman Glebovich with his brothers was on the other side of the Oka River. Grand Prince Vsevolod, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, sent to them with honor.

When they came and sat in the tent, the grand prince himself was still in his tent, and from there he sent to them Prince David of Murom and his tysyatsky Mikhail Borisovich, denouncing their treachery. They swore that they had not only not thought of it but had not even heard of it. “Whence has this slander against us come to you? What Satan has announced such a thing to you against us—then judge us together with him.”
