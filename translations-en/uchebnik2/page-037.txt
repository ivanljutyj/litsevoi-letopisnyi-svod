They said: “We are glad to fight for our lord the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich and will go against them, some on horses, some by water in boats; but we fear that as once before it was under the great prince of Kiev Izyaslav, son of Yaroslav, grandson of great Vladimir: the Kievans freed the great prince of Polotsk Vseslav, son of Bryacheslav, grandson of Izyaslav, great-grandson of great Vladimir, who sat captured in Kiev in prison with his children, and placed him on the throne of the great principality of Kiev; and here now the enemy of our lord prince and of us is neither bound nor in prison nor under guards, but in this city, in Kiev the populous, in the monastery of Saint Theodore he lives; and he will do what he wishes, for we are his enemies and he ours; though he is a monk, we fear him and wish to kill him, so that we may all be without fear and go to battle for our prince.”

Kliment, metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus', forbade and prohibited them, saying: “Do not do such a thing as to kill the monk Igor, former great prince of this same city of Kiev, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of great Vladimir; if you do such a thing, you will draw God’s wrath upon yourselves, and enmity with his brothers and his kin will never be appeased.” Also Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, and Lazar the tysyatsky, and the second tysyatsky Roguilo Vladimirovich forbade them to kill Prince Igor.

And two boyars came, sent to them by the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, saying thus: “Our lord, the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, sent us to you not to kill Prince Igor Olegovich, who has already become a monk, but commanded you to come to him for war against the adversaries.”

The Kievans cried out with a great voice and ran swiftly to kill the monk Prince Igor Olegovich; and Prince Vladimir Mstislavich mounted a horse and galloped after them; but countless crowds of people went through the city of Kiev and thronged in the streets, and Prince Vladimir Mstislavich could not overtake them, to get ahead of them to the monk Prince Igor Olegovich in the monastery and hide him from them; and he turned his horse to the right past Gleb’s court, wishing to hurry, and did not succeed.
