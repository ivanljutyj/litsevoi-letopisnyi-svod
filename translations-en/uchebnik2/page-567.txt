The Grand Prince showed great honors to Metropolitan Cyprian, bestowed gifts on him, and held a great feast over three days, arranging a grand celebration, and gave many gifts.

And on the fourth day, archimandrites and abbots, presbyters and deacons, and all priests and monks gathered at the Grand Prince’s. He, having summoned there also his boyars and the whole sacred council, sent for Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus’. And all of them began to complain against their Vladyka Vislen of Tver, saying that under him church discord and sedition had begun.

Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus’, with the bishops, with the whole sacred council and with the guests, with the Greek metropolitans, began to judge according to the divine and sacred canons of the holy apostles and holy fathers. And there was much slander against Euthymius, Vladyka of Tver; all rose against him, all slandered him: archimandrites, abbots, priests, and monks, and boyars, and nobles, and simple people. Metropolitan Cyprian with the whole sacred council began to judge according to the canons of the holy apostles and holy fathers and commanded Euthymius to remain in his monastery until he investigated everything and reached some decision.

The Grand Prince began to ask that another bishop be appointed for them. Metropolitan Cyprian with the whole sacred council removed Euthymius, Vladyka Vislen, from the archbishopric and gave to Grand Prince Michael Alexandrovich his protodeacon Arsenius, a wondrous man, noble and very virtuous. He tried to reconcile them and restore love between the Grand Prince and Vladyka Euthymius. And there was no peace and love between them, but only enmity and quarrels increased. Then much other slander was also spoken against Euthymius, Vladyka Vislen, heavy and unbearable, and there was fierce enmity and strife. Metropolitan Cyprian was troubled by all this, that he could not extinguish the enmity and establish peace and love.

In the same year Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus’, returned to Moscow, taking with him from Tver Euthymius, Vladyka Vislen of Tver, and commanded him to live in his Chudov Metropolitan Monastery, which was located in the center of Moscow. With him also came to Moscow his protodeacon Arsenius, fearing to accept the Tver see, seeing there enmity and strife, which troubled and terrified him.

In the same year Tsar Tokhtamysh sent his tsarevich Bektut with an army against Vyatka. He went, took Vyatka, and some people he cut down, while others he took captive and led to Tsar Tokhtamysh. In the same year another mighty tsar from the land of Shamakhi came against Tsar Tokhtamysh, and there was a great battle. And Prince Vasily Dmitrievich fled from the tsar beyond the Yaik and came to his patrimony in Moscow.
