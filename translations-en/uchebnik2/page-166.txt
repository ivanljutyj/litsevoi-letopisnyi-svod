He kept Miroshka, the Novgorod posadnik, and Ivanko and Foma with him; and the Novgorodians grieved for Miroshka.

In that same year Grand Prince Vsevolod of Vladimir, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, set out against Chernigov together with the Ryazan, Murom, Smolensk, and Kiev princes and with a multitude of Polovtsians, and took with him the Novgorod posadnik Miroshka, Ivanko, and Foma; and he sent to the Novgorodians and ordered them to go to Luki. They went with their prince Yaroslav Vladimirovich, his kinsman by marriage.

Vsevolod Yuryevich, having entered the land of Chernigov, did much evil, burning volosts and villages.

The Chernigov princes brought to their aid the Polovtsian princes and a large Polovtsian army and set out against them. Soon a battle took place between them, but the Lord God did not permit great bloodshed, and they made peace among themselves. All the princes left Novgorod free: let Novgorod be free, and wherever the Novgorodians wish, there let them take a prince for themselves. Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich released Foma to Novgorod, but did not release Miroshka and Ivanko.

The Novgorodians were angry with Vsevolod Yuryevich; they did not like how the prince had treated their posadnik Miroshka and Ivanko.

All the princes dispersed, each to his own place, and Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich returned to Vladimir.

On the Death of David of Smolensk

In the year 6706 from Adam (1206 from Christ), Prince David of Smolensk died, having been tonsured into the schema; and after him his nephew Mstislav Romanovich sat to rule in Smolensk.

In the same year a son, John, was born to Vsevolod on the twenty-eighth day of August.

In the same year Paul was appointed to the episcopal see in Pereyaslavl Rusky.

In the same year Vladyka Martyrius, Archbishop of Novgorod, founded a monastery for himself and built in it a church of the Holy Transfiguration of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ.

In the year 6707 from Adam (1207 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod of Vladimir, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, went to war against the Polovtsians together with his son Constantine; when they learned of this, they fled to the sea.

Vsevolod Yuryevich rode through their winter camps and near the Don River, but did not find them and returned to Vladimir.
