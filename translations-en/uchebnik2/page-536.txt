That same spring Ivan, archbishop, traveled to Pskov from Novgorod, and performed prayer services, and sprinkled all with water; and the plague ceased; and he returned healthy with those close to him to Novgorod.

In the same year the posadnik Grigory Yakovlevich built a stone church of the Protection of the Most Pure Mother of God at the Gates. In the same year there was a severe plague in Novgorod.

In the same year ambassadors from the Pope of Rome came to Moscow.

Prince Lugven of Lithuania, named Simeon in holy baptism, sent his ambassadors to Novgorod the Great, Ovogimont and Bratosha, wishing to be with them in Novgorod and to sit in the towns that Narimant had owned.

In the same year Prince Semyon Lugven Olgerdovich, grandson of Gedimin, came to Novgorod on the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God, and the Novgorodians received him with honor, and he sat in the same towns that had been under Narimant Gediminovich, in the month of August. In the same month of August Tatars came by sudden raid to the Ryazan outskirts.

In the year 6897 from Adam (the year 1397 from Christ)

In Novgorod the Great there was a severe plague, and in Pskov, and in the volosts, and in the villages. That same autumn, in the month of September, from the fifth day to the ninth, rain fell without ceasing, and then snow fell, and there were frosts and great cold. That same winter, from St Nicholas’s Day to the Epiphany, there were great frosts without ceasing, and there was great hardship for people and livestock.

Pimen’s Journey to Constantinople. That same spring Metropolitan Pimen went for the third time to Constantinople to the patriarch, and with him Michael, bishop of Smolensk, and Sergius, archimandrite of the Savior, and his elders and servants, and the protopope, and the protodeacon, and other priests and deacons. The beginning of that journey was from the city of Moscow in the month of April on the 13th day, on Great Tuesday of Holy Week. Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was angry with the metropolitan because he had gone without his counsel; there was also some discord between them; and so the journey began. And Metropolitan Pimen commanded Michael, bishop of Smolensk, and Sergius, archimandrite of the Savior, and all: if anyone wishes, let him write down everything about this journey—how they went and where what happened, or who returned or did not return back; and we wrote all this down. And so they went from Moscow, as was already written above, and came to Kolomna on Great Saturday. And in the holy week of Pascha they went to Ryazan along the Oka River and reached Perevitok; and there Eremey, bishop of Ryazan, a Greek, met us.
