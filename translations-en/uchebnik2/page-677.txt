The glory of a city is its old people; counsel with elders and with men white with gray hairs is honorable. But there is an old age that is not according to years, yet is honorable, according to the words of Solomon: “For gray hairs are wisdom to men,” he says, “and the maturity of old age is a blameless life.” But everyone knows that the beauty of a city is eldership, since eldership is honored by God Himself; it is said in Scripture: “Ask your father, and he will declare to you; and your elders will tell you.”

In the same year the Novgorod posadnik Anfal went with an army against the Bulgars along the Kama and the Volga. Along the Kama River went one hundred nasads, and along the Volga River one hundred and fifty nasads; the Kama nasads arrived first, but the Volga ones did not manage to come up. And the Tatars attacked them, killed them all, and captured Anfal himself and led him away to the Horde.

In the year 6918 from Adam (1418 from Christ)

On the first day of the month of September, Metropolitan Photius came from Constantinople to the Russian land, to Kiev, installed by Matthew, Patriarch of Constantinople. All the Russian princes and boyars and simple people received him with great joy and love, with great honors, and there was joy over this throughout the whole country.

In that same autumn Prince Svidrigailo Olgerdovich, grandson of Gediminas, whose heroic strength, daring, and courage in battle were extolled, grew weary fleeing with his brave Lithuanians from the Tatars of Edigey. And with the glorious Russian cities of Vladimir, Pereyaslavl, Kolomna, and many other cities and volosts, villages and lands—what is the point of saying much about it?—he possessed almost half of the entire Muscovite principality, yet he won no victory over the Hagarites; and returning to Lithuania, he plundered and ravaged the city of Serpukhov, and from there went away to his own land with great wealth.

The Tale of the Repose of the Blessed Arsenius, Bishop of Tver

His repose occurred quietly and peacefully, but no one was surprised at this, since God’s judgments are unknown. Therefore we must attend to our own soul, weep over our sins, and not seek out God’s hidden judgments. “Do not investigate things higher than yourself, and do not seek things deeper than yourself,” said Athanasius the Great.
