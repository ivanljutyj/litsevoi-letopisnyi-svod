Metropolitan Photius did not refuse, but joyfully set out soon to him in Galich with an offer of peace in that same year. And he came to Yaroslavl on the Nativity of John the Forerunner, and did not linger there, but only supped with Prince Ivan Vasilievich; the princes of Yaroslavl begged him to serve the Liturgy for them in the morning, but he did not heed them and went on to Galich.

And Prince Yuri Dmitrievich, hearing of this, gathered all his patrimony and met him with his children and boyars and his best men; and he gathered all the common folk from his towns and volosts, and from villages and hamlets, and there was a great multitude of them; and he stationed them on the hill by the city, on the metropolitan's arrival recounting and showing him his many people.

But the metropolitan, entering the posad of that city, went to the cathedral church of the Transfiguration of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ; that church was in the posad, in the field by the lake. And coming into the church, he prayed and went out, and looked at that people standing on the hill, and said to Prince Yuri Dmitrievich: "Son, Prince Yuri, I have not seen so many people in sheep's wool," for all were in coarse homespun coats. Prince Yuri had wanted to show how many people he had; but the hierarch took this as mockery of himself; and then he went to his cell, where he had stopped. And he began to speak to Prince Yuri Dmitrievich about peace, so that there should be no bloodshed between him and the grand prince.
