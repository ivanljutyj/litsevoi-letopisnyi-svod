In the year 6724 from Adam (1224 from Christ)

Pakhomy, Bishop of Rostov, died. He was a tonsured monk of the Caves Monastery, lived in Kiev for 5 years, and pastured the spiritual flock in the monastery of Saint Peter for 13 years. In Rostov he sat on the episcopal throne for two years and departed to the Lord.

And Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich, with Simon, Bishop of Suzdal and Vladimir, and with the whole sacred council, laid his relics with honors in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God in Rostov.

In the same year Matthew, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed as bishop in Rostov the monk Cyril from the metropolitan monastery of the Holy Great Martyr Demetrius.

In the same year Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich founded a stone church in the monastery of the Holy Transfiguration in Yaroslavl.

On the Battle at Lipitsa of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with the Prince of Novgorod Mstislav Mstislavich

In the same year Prince Mstislav Mstislavich went with the Novgorodians from Novgorod against his son-in-law, Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich.

And traitors ran over from his regiment to Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, his son-in-law: Vladislav Zavidovich, Gavrilo Igorevich, Yuri Alekseevich, Gavrilo Milyatinich, and they took their wives and children with them.

And the Novgorodians went by Sereger (Seliger), and came out to the upper reaches of the Volga.

And Prince Mstislav Mstislavich went with the Prince of Pskov Vladimir, and having come, they took Zubtsov.

And when they were on the Volga, Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk came to them with the Smolensk people.

And from there he sent to Torzhok to Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich about peace, while he himself stood on the river Kholkhna.

And Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich sent to them, saying: “What peace do you seek? If you have set out, go; for the hare goes to blood; but we have a hundred men against one of your men.”

And the princes Mstislav and Vladimir with the Novgorodians pondered: “What does this mean? We want peace, but he wants war. What will be?”

And Yaroslav Vsevolodovich gathered the Tverites and blocked the ways from Novgorod and the river Tvertsa.
