The prince cared for alms so greatly that along the streets and at the crossroads, in prisons and in many other places, he sent food and drink on wagons; he fed the hungry, gave drink to the thirsty, clothed the naked, and delivered the poor from misfortune, remembering the word of Christ: “If you do good to one of these least of My brethren, you do it to Me”; and the words of David, who said: “Blessed is the man who cares for the poor and needy”; and again: “Blessed is the man who is merciful and gives all day long”; and the words of his son Solomon, who said: “Sins are cleansed by alms and faith, and he who gives to the poor lends to God”; and the words of the holy prophet Daniel, who said: “Cleanse your sins by alms, and your transgressions by generosity shown to the poor.” Such were the life and deeds of the blessed Prince Andrew, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir, called Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of Grand Prince Vladimir. We beseech you, blessed Prince Andrew Bogolyubsky, having boldness before the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos, pray continually for your own brother Vsevolod, for our lord prince, and for all your kin, that the Lord may grant them victory over their enemies, and that they may rule for many years on earth in piety in Christ with their princesses and their children, and that their seed may endure from generation to generation until the end of the world, and that their reign may be quiet and undisturbed, and that they may enjoy every earthly good thing in the Lord, and receive heavenly grace through the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be glory and dominion with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.

In the same year, after the murder of Prince Andrew Yuryevich, the Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Pereyaslavlians assembled together in the city of Vladimir and decided: “Our lord Prince Andrew has been killed, and he has no children; his son rules in Novgorod, and he is young in years, while his brother is in Rus, that is, in the Kiev lands. To whom then shall we send, that he may come to reign among us? The neighboring princes of Ryazan and Murom live near us; we tremble and fear them, lest they suddenly attack and seize us with their troops. We have no prince—let us send to Prince Gleb of Ryazan and say to him: ‘God has taken our lord prince, and now we wish your brothers-in-law to reign among us, Prince Mstislav Rostislavich—grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh—and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich.’”

And having so decided, with prayer to the Most Pure Theotokos they sent their envoys to Ryazan to Prince Gleb, saying: “We wish to summon your brothers-in-law to the principate; send your envoys with ours to Rus, let them go to fetch our princes to Rus.” And they forgot the kissing of the honorable cross to Prince Yuri Dolgoruky and his younger children. Prince Gleb of Ryazan, hearing of this, rejoiced that such honor had been shown him, that his brothers-in-law were being summoned to reign among them.
