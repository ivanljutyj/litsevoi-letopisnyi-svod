At the ship stood the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb in red garments, holding their hands on their shoulders; and the rowers sat in the ship as if covered in mist. And Boris said to Gleb: “Brother Gleb, command them to row swiftly, so that we may help our kinsman, Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich.” Seeing such a vision of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb and hearing such speech, Pelgusy stood trembling and in great horror until the ship passed out of his sight toward Valaam.

Then Pelgusy set off quickly, and Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich met him; and Pelgusy, seeing the prince, rejoiced and told him alone all that he had seen and heard. And the prince said to him: “Do not tell this to anyone, brother, until the Lord does as He pleases.”

And then, rushing still more swiftly, Alexander attacked the enemy at the sixth hour of the day; and there was a great battle with the Romans; and the Novgorodians killed a countless multitude of Romans; and Prince Alexander made a scar on the face of the king himself with his sharp sword. Then six brave men distinguished themselves in the army of Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich, who fought courageously beside him.

The first was named Gavrila Aleksich; he saw the king’s son being led under guard to the ship; and Gavrila rode after them on horseback right up to the ship along a plank laid to the ship; and the enemies entered the ship ahead of him, and then, turning, threw him and his horse from the plank into the sea. But Gavrila, by God’s will, escaped from there unharmed and attacked again, and fought stubbornly with the commander himself in the midst of the enemy army; and there their commander Spiridon was killed; and their bishop was killed there too.

The second Novgorodian was named Sbyslav Yakunovich; attacking many times, he fought with a single axe; and there was no fear in his heart; and countless enemies fell by his hand; and they marveled at his strength and courage.

The third was Yakov Polochyanin, a huntsman of the prince; he attacked the enemy regiment with a sword and fought courageously; and the prince praised him.

The fourth Novgorodian was named Misha; he attacked on foot with his company and destroyed three Roman ships.

The fifth was from Alexander’s junior retinue, named Savva; he broke through to the king’s great golden-topped tent and cut down its pillar; and the troops of Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich, seeing the tent fall, rejoiced.

The sixth was from the servants, named Ratmir; he fought on foot; and a multitude of Romans surrounded him; and then he perished. All this became known from our lord Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich and from others who took part in that battle at that time.
