And Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich, grandson of Svyatoslav Yaroslavich, came to them in Novgorod from Chernigov, from his brother Vsevolod Olegovich, grandson of Svyatoslav Yaroslavich; and then they sent Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, away into exile with his wife, children, and mother-in-law, saying: “Go wherever you wish.”

Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich then went to his uncle, Grand Prince Yaropolk Vladimirovich, in Kiev; and Yaropolk gave him Vyshgorod, and he sat there one year.

In the same year the posadnik Yakun Mikhalkovich, a renowned man, was killed in Novgorod, and the posadnik office was given to Ivanko Pavlovich. In the same year the posadnik Miroslav Mikulich died in Novgorod, and the posadnik office was given to Konstantin Mikulich.

In the same year the tysyatsky Ivan Andreevich, called Dolgy, was killed in Ryazan.

In the year 6644 from Adam (1144 from Christ)

Grand Prince Yaropolk of Kiev brought his brother, Prince Andrei Vladimirovich Monomash, from Vladimir to Pereyaslavl to reign.

In the same year the Chernigov princes Vsevolod, Svyatoslav, and Igor, sons of Oleg, grandsons of Svyatoslav, great-grandsons of Yaroslav, great-great-grandsons of the great Vladimir, began to unite their forces, and they waged war on villages and towns along the Sula, and came to Pereyaslavl Russky, and wrought much evil, and burned Ustye, and withdrew, and encamped on the Suboy.

And Grand Prince Yaropolk of Kiev, son of Vladimir Monomakh, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, came out against them with his brothers, and they met in battle and fought hard, but soon the Polovtsians fled from the Olegoviches. And the druzhina of Grand Prince Yaropolk and his brothers, the best brave men, pursued them, and, pursuing, killed many Polovtsians, and turned back; and they did not find their princes, and fell into the hands of the Olegoviches, while the Polovtsians, returning, fell upon their rear.
