And let no one be surprised that Providence gave me help in composing this tale, for God is my helper in this, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, in portraying the love and virtue of the tsar. I shall set forth praise of him not as in the tales of the ancient Greek philosophers, but truthfully, as in the mirror of Divine Scripture. Are there many people in the world whose life has shone with such brightness, glory, and honor-worthy renown, and whose name has been so glorified? He was beautiful in appearance, pure in soul, and perfect in mind! At times it is said that the bonds of friendship sometimes compel one to speak in praise of a man. But the great prince, guardian of piety, light and adornment of life, received a sense of dignity from his forebears. As the great Dionysius wrote: “The stirring of water comes from the wind; the moisture of the earth perishes by the sun; the mind is the ruler of human senses, and by the union of the senses the mind nurtures a garden in the heart. And the heart yields the mind’s fruit to the world.” The great Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was of a famous lineage. Other parents were not granted to bear such a child, nor was it fitting that such a wondrous child should be born of other parents, but in all things is the will of God the Almighty. Can anything be added to his glory, for it is immeasurable, like the sea, which is full even without the rivers flowing into it. Some people are surrounded by glory at the beginning of their path, others in the middle of life, and others in old age. But he was virtuous in all the years of his life. It happens that a man is born pious and brings glory to all his ancestors; so it was with this one. And I shall not exaggerate if I say that his glory resounded throughout the world, and his greatness in all the ends of the universe. To whom can this great prince, Dmitry Ivanovich, tsar of the Russian land and heir of the great principality, gatherer of the Christian Russian land, be compared? Come, beloved wise churchmen, and render worthy words of praise to the ruler of the Russian land. Shall I call you an angel? But though you were in the flesh, you lived an angelic life. Shall I call you a man? But you performed deeds beyond human understanding. Shall I call you the first-created? But he, having received the commandment from the Creator, transgressed it. But you kept your vows after holy baptism in purity. Shall I call you Seth? But people called him a god because of his wisdom; yet you, preserving purity, were a servant of God, and holding God’s throne, you proved to be lord of the Russian land. Shall I liken you to Enoch? But he was translated into unknown lands; your soul, however, the angels carried up with glory to heaven.

Shall I call you Noah? But he was saved from the flood in the ark; you, however, kept your heart from sinful thoughts in a pure body, as in a palace. Shall I call you Eber, who did not join the foolish peoples at the building of the tower? You destroyed the pillar of impiety in the Russian land and did not join the irrational peoples to the destruction of Christianity. Shall I call you Abraham? But you are like him in faith, and by your life you surpassed him—
