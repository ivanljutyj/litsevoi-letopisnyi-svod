Having come close to the boats, the Bulgars rushed into battle, while the Belozersk men, strengthened by God's help, began to fight them.

When they saw them, the Bulgars, terrified by the help of God and of the Most Pure Mother of God, fled, pursued by no one, but only by the wrath of God. The Belozersk men with their other warriors, seeing the help of God and of the Most Pure Mother of God, pursued after them; and the Polovtsians fled as well, and about two thousand of them drowned then.

Vsevolod stood before the city for ten days, seeing his brother growing weak, while the Bulgars sent envoys to him with a petition.

And Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich of Vladimir made peace with the Bulgars and returned to Vladimir by water in boats, and sent the horses to the Mordva; and Prince Izyslav Glebovich was brought and laid in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God in Vladimir.

In the year 6693 from Adam (the year 1193 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich sent to Kiev to Nikifor, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, asking him for a bishop for Rostov; he asked that Luke, abbot of the Church of the Saviour at Berestovo, be appointed bishop.

Metropolitan Nikifor sent him the Greek Nicholas as bishop in Rostov; but Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich did not want him and again asked for Luke, abbot of the Church of the Saviour at Berestovo; the prince asked this repeatedly.

Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich also sent to the Grand Prince of Kiev Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, asking that the same Luke, abbot of the Church of the Saviour at Berestovo, be appointed bishop.

Nikifor, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed Abbot Luke bishop of Rostov and Suzdal, and Vladimir, and all the land of Rostov, on the eleventh day of March, on the commemoration of our venerable father Sophronius, Archbishop of Jerusalem. This abbot was silent, long-suffering, humble, meek, merciful, and consoling to the sorrowful and grieving; he overcame worldly passions within himself, was strengthened by the weapon of the precious cross, and by the labors and ascetic struggles of a holy life, fencing himself about on every side with watchfulness; for better is help from watchfulness than help from works, and by true compunction and perfect humility he sanctified himself.

In the same year there was a fire in the city of Vladimir on the seventeenth day of April, and almost the whole city burned down, and holy churches to the number of thirty-two.
