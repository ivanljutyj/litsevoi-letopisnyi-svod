All the while Prince Andrei spoke before the tsar of his own rightness in this dispute with his elder brother, and Prince Andrei’s accomplices said the same, and in everything Prince Andrei accused his brother: “He does not want to submit to you, O Tsar, and does not want to pay you your tribute.” And so Prince Andrei for the second time brought the tsar’s wrath upon his elder brother.

In the same year there was a sign in the heavens. In the same year Grand Prince Svyatoslav Yaroslavovich of Tver, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, with the Tver retinue, and Grand Prince Daniil Alexandrovich of Moscow, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, with the Moscow retinue, and the posadniks of Novgorod, with the Novgorod retinue, went to war against Pereyaslavl against Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich.

He, learning of this, turned to Heaven, sighed, and wept, and said: “You see, O Lord, this injustice; for I did not wrong them, and I did them no injustice. Why do they rise up against me and persecute me continually, wishing to draw my soul out of me, and to kill these soldiers of mine, and to destroy the memory of me? O Lord and my God, help me and save me.” And then, after weeping for a long time, he mounted his horse and went to meet them with his troops at the city of Dmitrov.

They, not having reached Dmitrov, stopped and stood there five days, conducting negotiations, and then concluded peace and dispersed.

In the same year Prince Andrei Alexandrovich received from the tsar a great host—Turantemir, Alynya, and many Tatars—and set out with them from the Horde, from the tsar, and with him were his counsellor and helper Semyon Tonilievich, and many other counsellors and helpers of his; and Prince Andrei went against his elder brother, Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, and they did great evil to Christians.

But Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, with his retinue, his princess, his children, and all his court, fled to the Horde to Tsar Nogai and told him everything in order, recounting it to him with tears; and he gave many gifts to him and to all his princes. Tsar Nogai listened to him and held him in honor.

In the same year the Tatars ravaged the Lithuanians and returned to the Horde with great booty.

In the year 6791 from Adam (1291 from Christ)

Maxim, a Greek, was appointed metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus by the Patriarch of Constantinople.
