And for another reason: if anything happens, let these men act according to their custom. Their names are: Vasily Kapitsa, Sidor Elferiev, Konstantin, Kozma Koverya, Semion Antonov, Mikhailo Salarev, Timofey Vesyakov, Dmitry Chernoy, Dementy Salarev, Ivan Shikh. And the Grand Prince came to Kolomna on Saturday, the 28th day of the month of August, on the commemoration of our venerable father Moses the Moor. Before the Grand Prince, many commanders had gathered there and met the Grand Prince on the small river Severka. And Gerasim, Bishop of Kolomna, met him at the city gates with crosses.

On Sunday early in the morning the Grand Prince ordered all the princes, boyars, and commanders to ride out to the Field and appointed commanders for each regiment.

And the Grand Prince took into his own regiment the Belozersk princes with their host, for they were very bold and courageous, and to the right wing he appointed his brother Prince Vladimir Andreevich, giving him into his regiment the Yaroslavl princes with their host. And to the left wing he appointed Prince Gleb of Bryansk. In the vanguard regiment he appointed Dmitry Vsevolozhsky and Vladimir Vsevolozhsky.

The commander of the Kolomna regiment was Mikula Vasilyevich; the commander of Vladimir and Yuryev was Timofey Valuevich; the commander of Kostroma was Ivan Rodionovich Kvashnya; the commander of Pereyaslavl was Andrey Serkizovich; and Prince Vladimir Andreevich’s commanders were Danilo Belous, Konstantin Kononovich, Prince Feodor Yeletsky, Prince Yuri Meshchersky, and Prince Andrey Muromsky.

The Grand Prince, having arrayed the regiments, entered the church and prayed to the Lord God, and to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to all the saints, and received blessing from Gerasim, Bishop of Kolomna, saying: “Bless me, father, to go against the Tatars.” And Gerasim, Bishop of Kolomna, blessed him and all his host to fight against the impious Tatars.

And the Grand Prince set out from Kolomna with many forces, and having come, halted by the Oka at the mouth of the Lopasnya River. And there came to him his chief commander Timofey Vasilyevich, the tysyatsky, grandson of Vasily, great-grandson of Veniamin, with a great host that had remained in Moscow.

And here, having heard all the tidings, he ordered them to cross the Oka River and commanded each regiment, saying: “If anyone goes through the Ryazan land, let no one touch anything or take anything from anyone, nor touch a single hair.”

And in Moscow he left his commander Feodor Andreevich with his father Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus, and with his wife, Grand Princess Evdokia, and with his sons Vasily and Yury.
