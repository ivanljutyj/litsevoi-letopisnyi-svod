In the same year Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich went against his uncle, Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and drove him out of Vladimir, and took his mother and wife, and seized his boyars and commanders, bound them in iron fetters, and shut them in dungeons; and his uncle Prince Vladimir Andreevich fled to Peremyshl, and from there fled to Hungary to the king, for his sister was married to the Hungarian king.

In the same year the Cumans came against Ryazan, against Bystraya Sosna, and, having taken many captive, withdrew to their own lands; and not reaching their own people, they grew careless: the pursuit came upon them while they slept, recovered the captives, and killed them.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Kiev Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh went against Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich to Vladimir with his sons, taking with him as aid his son-in-law Prince Yaroslav Volodimirich of Galich, wishing to take Vladimir for his nephew Prince Vladimir Andreevich, grandson of Monomakh.

But Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, seeing their multitude and having no strength to resist them, shut himself in the city; and they came and besieged the city; and he fought against them from the city, and many fell on both sides.

And Prince Yuri Dolgoruky said to his sons and to his son-in-law Prince Yaroslav Volodimirich of Galich: “I am already an old man, and he is still young, but he remains wholly in the passions of the many things of this present life. I am in great fear of the end of life and of the judgment of trial, and already from the sorrows of this vain and fleeting, deceitful life I wished to make peace with him by faith and truth of the kissing of the cross; but he is still stirred by the passions of this present life, and it seems to me that he understands neither himself nor knows what is inevitably to come. But for me, already at the end of life, it is not by bloodshed and quarrel, but by long-suffering and humility, that I should yield.”

And having said this, he went back to his own lands with his sons; and his son-in-law Grand Prince Yaroslav Volodimirich went to his great principality in Galich, and Prince Vladimir Andreevich, grandson of Monomakh, went to Peresopnitsa and Chealnitsa, and the Pogorinsky towns, and Dorogobuzh.

In the same year Nester, Bishop of Rostov, went from Rostov to Kiev to Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, to bow and receive blessing; but he was slandered by his own household before Metropolitan Constantine, and was placed under suspension.

In the year 6665 from Adam (the year 1165 from Christ)

Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich of Chernigov began to gather an army against the Grand Prince of Kiev Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh, and with him was Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich of Smolensk.
