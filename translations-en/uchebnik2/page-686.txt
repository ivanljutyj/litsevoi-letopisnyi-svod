At that time there was a quarrel between the brothers, Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich and Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin, and Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver ordered his brother Prince Vasily Mikhailovich to be taken prisoner together with his boyars and servants on the twenty-eighth day of June. And he sent his governor to Kashin, and ordered his princess to be brought to Tver.

On the next day, Thursday after Vespers, he sent his brother Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin under guard to Novy Gorodok.

And they were at Perevoloka, dismounted there from their horses, and the prince galloped ahead of all on horseback, wearing only a terlik and without a kiver, through the ford across the Tmaka; they galloped on farther off the road. And it happened that in a certain village he met a man who was concerned for him; that man hid the prince in the forest and rode with him to Moscow. And they searched for him for a long time, but did not find him. In the same year the grand princes concluded unity between themselves—Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich of Lithuania and Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver—and decided always to act together.

In the same year, two days before Elijah’s Day, there was a sign in Kashin: in the evening they saw a sickle in a cloud.

In the same year Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver went from Moscow to the Horde to Tsar Zeleni-Saltan Tokhtamyshevich.

In the same year the princes of Nizhny Novgorod left the Horde, and the tsar of the Great Horde, Zeleni-Saltan Tokhtamyshevich, granted them their lands.

In the same year, on the first day of the month of August, on the commemoration of Saints Eleazar and Salome and her seven sons, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow set out for the Horde to Tsar Zeleni-Saltan, son of Tokhtamysh, with his nobles and with great gifts; Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of Yaroslavl set out together with them.

In the same year there was a flood during the Dormition Fast: the water rose very high in all the cities. In the same year, on the fifteenth day of the month of August, on the Dormition of the Most Pure Theotokos, Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver set out for the Horde to Tsar Zeleni-Saltan, son of Tokhtamysh, by boats along the Volga, having received the blessing of his father Bishop Antony and of the whole sacred council. His sons and all the people saw him off with tears; many boyars with servants set out with him, and some boyars with servants escorted the prince as far as Nizhny Novgorod. There the prince sent them all back, and he himself went on farther to the Horde. And at that time in the Horde, before his arrival, by the mercy of God and through the intercession of His Most Holy Lady our Theotokos, our evil enemy Tsar Zeleni-Saltan, son of Tokhtamysh, died—he was shot during a battle by his brother Kerim-Berdei.
