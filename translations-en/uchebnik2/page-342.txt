Prince Alexandrovich of Moscow prevailed, and slew many boyars and people, and took their prince Konstantin Romanovich of Ryazan by some stratagem, because of the sedition of the Ryazan boyars themselves. And he brought him with him to Moscow and kept him in captivity, but under guard and in all honor, for he wished to secure an agreement with him by a cross-kissing oath and to send him back to his patrimony, to the Grand Principality of Ryazan.

In the same year Prince Alexander Glebovich of Smolensk, grandson of Rostislav, great-grandson of Mstislav, great-great-grandson of Davyd, great-great-great-grandson of Rostislav, forefather of Mstislav, great-great-forefather of Vladimir Monomakh, went with an army to Dorogobuzh and besieged the city, and cut off the water, and wrought much evil upon the people. But Prince Andrei Vyazemsky came against him beneath Dorogobuzh with an army and helped the people of Dorogobuzh, and then beneath Dorogobuzh they killed Prince Alexander Glebovich of Smolensk's son, and wounded Prince Alexander Glebovich himself and his brother, Prince Roman Glebovich, and of his army they killed two hundred men of Smolensk. In the same year Grand Prince Andrei Alexandrovich of Vladimir went with an army, with the people of Vladimir and the people of Novgorod, against the Germans.

And they took the German city of Venets on the Neva River, and burned it, and slew the people. And their wisdom and their city fortifications proved to be of no avail without God's help, as it is written: "Unless the Lord guards the city, the watchman keeps vigil in vain."

In the same year the city of Torzhok burned. In the same year there was a congress of all the princes in Dmitrov concerning the principalities, and there was great talk. And Grand Prince Andrei Alexandrovich of Vladimir, and Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich of the Yaroslavichs of Tver, Grand Prince Danilo Alexandrovich of Moscow, Prince Ivan Dmitrievich, grandson of Alexander, of Pereslavl, divided their patrimonies among themselves and made peace in the love of Christ.

But Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich of the Yaroslavichs of Tver and Prince Ivan Dmitrievich of the Alexandrovichs of Pereslavl did not come to agreement, and, not having concluded peace and love in Christ between themselves, they parted.

In the same year a son, Alexander, was born to Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich, grandson of Yaroslav of Tver.

In the year 6810 from Adam (the year 1310 from Christ)

There was a very great storm, and much harm was done to people: it tore apart buildings, broke forests, and killed people and cattle.

In the same year there was a sign in the heavens: a star appeared in the west with rays upward, sending forth as it were a tail toward the south downward. In the same year the tonsure ceremony was held for Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich of Tver's son Dmitry.
