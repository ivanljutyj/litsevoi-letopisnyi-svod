“For have all the Christian regiments already fallen dead?” And the great and wise commander and daring warrior Dmitry Bobrok Volynets said: “Woe, prince, great woe! For our sins the wrath of God has come upon us. But the time has not yet come for us to go out against these adversaries. Let us endure a little longer, sending up prayers to God in contrition of heart, and He will cast down our enemies.” Prince Vladimir Andreevich wept, and raising his hands to heaven, prayed with tears to the Lord God, and to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to all the saints. And when they wanted to go out against their enemies, a strong wind blew against them in their faces, and beat hard, and held them back. And Dmitry Bobrok Volynets said: “Let no one go out to battle; the Lord restrains us.” And he moved them all to weeping and tears, and all wept, praying with tears at length to the Lord God, and to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to all the saints. And Dmitry Bobrok Volynets himself wept bitterly and shed many tears. And the ninth hour was already ending; the Lord looked with merciful eyes upon all the princes of Rus, and upon the strong commanders, and upon all Christians. And then suddenly a wind blew from behind them, urging them to go out against the Tatars.

Then Dmitry Bobrok said to Prince Vladimir Andreevich: “My lord prince, the hour has come, the time has drawn near.” He also said to the whole army: “Lords, fathers, brothers, children, and friends! Rise up, the good time has come for us. For the power of the Holy Spirit helps us.” And so all went with fury and divine zeal against the unbelieving and hostile enemies, and the Christians were not afraid; they dared like great warriors.

The faithful saw that angels were helping the Christians, and regiments of holy martyrs, and the great warrior of Christ, George, and the glorious Demetrius, and the great princes of Rus, the holy Boris and Gleb; and with them was the great Archangel Michael as commander of the full regiment of the heavenly powers.

The regiments of the pagans saw the two commanders, the three-sun regiments and their flaming arrows, coming at them, and the godless Tatars fell from the fear of God and from the Christian weapons. God raised the right hand of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich for victory over the foreigners.
