The Betrayal of Theodore of Simonov, nephew of the Venerable Abbot Sergius of Radonezh, concerning the administration of the Russian metropolitanate.

That same summer, on the 10th day of the month of July, Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod, grandson of Vasily, great-grandson of Michael, great-great-grandson of Andrei, reposed in the monastic order; in holy baptism his name was Thomas, and his monastic name was Theodore. And he was laid in his patrimony and inheritance, in Nizhny Novgorod, in the stone church of the Holy Savior, beside his father Konstantin Vasilyevich and beside his brother Prince Andrei; he was on the grand principality two years, and in his patrimony, on the grand principality in Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod, 19 years; and all the years of his life were 61.

Then Tsar Tokhtamysh heard of his death and grieved for him, since he was his favorite; and after him he gave the grand principality of Suzdal and of Nizhny Novgorod to his brother Prince Boris Konstantinovich, for he was then with the tsar in the Horde, together with his son Prince Ivan; and Tsar Tokhtamysh bestowed great honor on him, and that same summer, toward autumn, he released him to his patrimony, to the grand principality in Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod.

And he came on the 8th day of the month of November with his son Ivan and with his nephew Prince Semyon Dmitrievich, together from the Horde, with a grant from the tsar; and he sat on the grand principality, on his patrimony and inheritance in Suzdal and in Nizhny Novgorod, and in Gorodets with his sons and with his nephews in peace and love.

In the year 6892 from Adam (the year 1392 from Christ)

Tokhtamysh, Tsar of the Volga and supreme Tsar of all the hordes, granted Prince Michael Alexandrovich of Tver his patrimony and inheritance, the grand principality of Tver, and said to him thus: “I myself know my uluses, and every Russian prince in my ulus, in his own fatherland, lives according to the old custom and serves me faithfully, and I favor him; and as for the wrong done before me by my ulus-man Prince Dmitry of Moscow, I have frightened him, and he serves me faithfully, and I favor him according to the old custom in his patrimony; but you go to your patrimony, to Tver, and serve me faithfully, and I favor you.”

And Prince Michael Alexandrovich of Tver came from the Horde from Tsar Tokhtamysh with a grant and with honor in the month of September, and he left his son Prince Alexander in the Horde. At that same time Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow was also in the Horde with Tsar Tokhtamysh. And a certain Horde prince troubled them, promising to give each of them the grand principality, “and I will persuade the tsar to this,” he said.

That same autumn there was in Vladimir a fierce envoy named Adam from Tsar Tokhtamysh.
