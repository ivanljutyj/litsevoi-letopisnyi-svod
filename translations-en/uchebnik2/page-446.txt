Then Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver subjected to his authority his brother Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich of Kashin.
From there Keistut passed by Torzhok, and with him Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver; they took Torzhok, and Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver installed his governor in it, while the Lithuanian army went home with a great multitude of captives.

In Novgorod, at the Holy Saviour’s, the great bell rang by itself three times. In the same year Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod began to rebuild Nizhny Novgorod in stone.

In the same year in Russa they built a stone church of St Nicholas. In the same year Prince Boris Konstantinovich built himself a town on the Sura River and named it Kurmysh.

In the same year in Veliky Novgorod they built a stone church of the Holy Saviour on Ilyina Street.

In the same year, during St Peter’s Fast, the Novgorodians, posadniks and boyars came to Torzhok, concluded an agreement with the Novotorzhians by kissing the cross, removed the governors of Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, killed his Tver merchants, plundered their property, and killed and consigned to fire the other Tver people. They fortified the town, set up a palisade, gathered a multitude of warriors, and prepared for battle with Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver. Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver went with his army to Torzhok on the thirty-first day of May, took position before Torzhok at mid-morning, and began humbly to send envoys to them and to ask: “The Tverites have seized and plundered the town, but I want nothing from you; only install my governors in Torzhok.”

The Novgorodians disregarded this proposal, came out against him from the town and from all sides onto the field of battle. There was a fierce battle; Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver overcame them, and then the Novgorod commanders were defeated: posadnik Alexander Avvakumovich, Ivan Timofeevich, Ivan Shakhovich, Grigory Shchebelkovich, Timofei Daniilovich, Mikhail Grozny, Denisy Vislov, and many others were killed.

The Novgorodians were seized with fear and fled toward Novgorod, and some toward Torzhok; many of them were killed and some captured, and the posad caught fire from the field side. A strong wind blew toward the town, and fire spread throughout the whole town; the Novotorzhians fled away with their wives and children straight into the hands of the Tverites, some burned, and some suffocated from the smoke in the Church of the Holy Saviour.
