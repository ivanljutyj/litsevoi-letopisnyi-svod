On Isaac the Silent

That same winter the monk Isaac the Silent passed away under the Venerable Abbot Sergius. He had lived a good life and possessed every virtue: obedience, humility, chastity, silence, abstinence, beggarly poverty, non-acquisitiveness, handiwork, fasting, vigil, quietude, guilelessness, the constant keeping of the word of God in his mouth, the remembrance of death, and plainness of clothing; and above all he was devoted to quietude and silence, to the reading of the divine Scriptures, to prayer, to compunction, and to tears. Truly he was wonderful and worthy of praise, an earthly angel and a heavenly man.

On the Smolensk Battle

That same spring the Grand Prince Svyatoslav Ivanovich of Smolensk, with his brother Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and with his sons the Svyatoslavichi, Gleb and Yuri, gathered many forces and set out with an army against the city of Mstislavl. For before that the city of Mstislavl had belonged to Smolensk, but the Lithuanians had taken it for themselves; and they wished to take it back from Lithuania.

And as they went they did much evil to the Lithuanian land, warring against the Lithuanian land. Some Lithuanian people the Smolenskians took captive, tortured with various tortures, and killed; and others, men and women and infants, they locked in huts and burned. For others they forced apart the walls of a house from top to bottom, thrust hands between the logs, and squeezed people from corner to corner; and hanging others lower down, inserting their hands between the beams, they crushed them; and in this way they did up to the top along all four walls; and so they did in many houses and set them on fire in great fury. They impaled infants on spears, and others, tying their calves together, they hung on poles like carcasses, head down; inhumanely, without mercy, they tortured them.

And the Grand Prince Svyatoslav Ivanovich came with his sons, Prince Gleb and Prince Yuri, and with his nephew Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, to the city of Mstislavl on Wednesday of Holy Week, on the 18th day of April; and Prince Svitrigailo Olgerdovich was in the city. And they shut themselves in the city, and the Smolenskians stood before the city of Mstislavl for 11 days, but achieved nothing. And on the next day, in Thomas Week, in the middle of the morning, regiments of the Lithuanian army appeared near the city from one side, and the Smolenskians saw in the field the banners of the Lithuanian army. In the first regiment was Grand Prince Skirgailo Olgerdovich, and in the second regiment his brother Prince Koribut Olgerdovich, and in the third regiment their brother Prince Semyon Lugven Olgerdovich, and the fourth regiment was Vitovt Kestutievich with a multitude; and with countless Lithuanian forces they advanced quickly across the field toward the city.
