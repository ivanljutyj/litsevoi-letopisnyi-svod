And the Smolensk prince Vladimir Rurikovich, the Pskov prince Vladimir, and prince Vsevolod Mstislavich Romanovich with his retinue went through their own foot soldiers with their regiments; Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov also came up with his regiments, and they began to fight, and blood flowed like water.

And Prince Mstislav Mstislavich with his regiments rode three times through the regiments of Yuri and Yaroslav, and he himself was firm and courageous, possessing great strength and zeal; he held an axe in his hand and cut mercilessly, and while pursuing he became entangled.

And Alexander Popovich came at him with drawn sword, wishing to cleave him, for this warrior was mighty and famous. But he cried out, “I am the prince of Novgorod, Mstislav Mstislavich!” He stopped at once, and thus God saved him from death. And Alexander Popovich said to him, “Prince! Do not dare, but stand and watch! If you, the head, are killed, what shall all the others do, where shall they betake themselves?” And the battle was savage, and the strife great, and the dead lay everywhere. And the princes broke through the regiments and reached the baggage trains of Prince Yuri. When Prince Yuri and Yaroslav saw this, they fled with their younger brothers.

Mstislav then said to the Novgorodians, “Do not give yourselves over to plunder, brothers, but devote yourselves to battle; if you give in to plunder, they will sweep us away.” And the Suzdal regiments were defeated on the twenty-first day of April, on Thursday of the second week after Easter. A great multitude was slain, and others drowned in the rivers. They said that in that battle nine thousand two hundred and thirty-three were killed in the Suzdal regiments, while of the Novgorodians only five men were killed and one Smolensk man.

And only sixty Suzdal men were taken captive; the rest fled, some to Vladimir, some to Suzdal, some to Pereyaslavl and to Yuryev.

In Vladimir, the remaining priests, monks, wives, and children, seeing those fleeing toward the city, rejoiced, thinking that the prince’s messengers were fleeing to say that ours had prevailed.

But Prince Yuri alone came running to the city at midday in a single shirt—the battle had been at the noon meal—and he began to ride around the city saying, “Strengthen the city!” The people of Vladimir were in confusion, and instead of rejoicing there was weeping.

Toward evening people came running, some wounded and some naked, and during that night as well they kept running in.

In the morning Prince Yuri summoned the people and said, “Brothers of Vladimir! Let us shut ourselves in the city; perhaps we shall fight them off.” The people answered him, “Prince Yuri!
