And now the see of the metropolitan of the Kievan church has come to complete desolation, therefore we grieve for the great cathedral Kievan church and for God’s people; let us ask the Lord to grant us help.”

Vytautas, hearing such words, rejoiced that his desire was being fulfilled, and ordered the Kievan church to be inspected and everything in it inventoried. He also ordered the cities, lands, and villages of the metropolitan to be inventoried.

And news of this reached from Kiev to Moscow, to Metropolitan Photius. He learned that wicked people had slandered him—people who had fled from him from Moscow to the Chernigov bishop, and from there to Lithuania to Vytautas, and had set him at odds with Vytautas there. The metropolitan was greatly angered, and confusion and sedition began in God’s holy church.

Photius then wished to go to Constantinople, having learned that Vytautas intended to place another metropolitan in the see of the metropolitan in Kiev. And he wished first to go to Kiev to learn what was happening and to make peace with Vytautas if that were possible; if it were not, he decided to go from Kiev to Constantinople to the patriarch, so that he would forbid Vytautas to install another metropolitan in Kiev. And so he set out from Moscow for Kiev.

And when he reached Lithuania, Vytautas seized him and did not permit him to go to Constantinople to the patriarch, but, having robbed him, sent him back to Moscow.

And the cities belonging to the metropolitan of the great cathedral Kievan church, and the lands and villages, he distributed to his nobles, and the vicars of Metropolitan Photius, after robbing them, he sent to Moscow.

In that same year there was a fire in Moscow, and upon Savva Avraamiev, one of those slanderers who had slandered Metropolitan Photius, fire, like a cloud, flared from Photius’s chamber and burned him alive.

In the same year, on the 7th day of the month of June, on Friday before the liturgy, darkness descended upon the earth and lasted a very short time—one hour or a little more. Then all the stars were visible, as though it were night.

Then a merchant with goods came from Lithuania and turned to Metropolitan Photius, saying: “Forgive me, hierarch of Christ! When recently there was a fire in Moscow and all my wealth burned, I became very angry and grieved; and now darkness has come again, fear and trembling seized me, all my inward parts shook, all the members of my body grew weak, so you see me now in great trouble.
