He also asked for freshly caught fish, but they answered that it could not be procured.

And then suddenly the waves swelled and cast a very large fish at his feet, without even splashing him; this fish was enough for the saint and those with him to eat for many days. Those sailing with the saint said then that they had never eaten such sweet fish. And when the saint had satisfied himself with the God-given fish, his illness immediately passed.

And soon he sailed to Constantinople, and in the monastery of the Most Holy Theotokos called Verghetis he found rest, redeeming precious vessels and relics of the saints, and was greatly enriched by this, wishing to bring them to his own country. From Constantinople he set out by ship to the Bulgarian and Zagora lands and informed his kinsman by marriage, Tsar Asan, of his return, and he sent to meet him, and afterward came out himself.

And the saint came to the city of Tarnovo, and was received with great joy by the tsar and Patriarch Joachim, and the tsar gave him his warm chambers. The tsar and the patriarch honored the saint.

The feast of the Holy Theophany came, and on the eve of the feast they ordered Saint Sava to serve the service and bless the water, sprinkling the tsar and all the people, and there was great festivity at the tsar’s on this occasion, and he gave the saint much gold to distribute to the poor. After the feast of the Holy Theophany the tsar went hunting, but the saint fell ill and understood that the time of his departure to the Lord had come; he wrote a testament, and sent with his disciples to his own country all the holy relics and vessels, and golden lamps with pearls and stones, and the sacred vestments that he had redeemed, and gave his blessing to King Vladislav, Archbishop Arsenius, and all the people.

And when Sunday came, the saint partook of the Divine Gifts, with great joy saw the angels coming to him, and, thanking God, gave his soul into His hands.

And the patriarch sent to the tsar, asking where to bury the saint’s body; the tsar, hearing of this, was greatly grieved that he had not been informed of the saint’s illness; although Saint Sava himself had not ordered the tsar to be informed of his illness, so that he might die in peace.

And the tsar ordered his relics to be placed in the monastery that he himself had founded, in the church of the Holy Forty Martyrs, and much gold was distributed during his burial, and many healings occurred then.

Later King Vladislav himself came to his father-in-law Tsar Asan, requested the body of the saint, and brought it to his own country with great honors; the saint’s body remained whole, in no way harmed, and emitted fragrance.
