In that same year in Novgorod Seversky died the tysyatsky Ivan Bogdanov. In that same year in Novgorod Seversky died Prince Ivan Olegovich.

In that same year Prince Gleb, son of Yuri, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, went from his father Prince Yuri of Suzdal to the Grand Prince of Chernigov Vladimir Davydovich and to his brother Prince Izyaslav Davydovich and to Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich for help.

The Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich summoned him to himself, but he did not go to him; instead he went against his son, Grand Prince Mstislav, to Pereyaslavl. And having come there, he accomplished nothing, since many troops of Mstislav Izyaslavich had gathered in Pereyaslavl, and the bogatyr Demyan Kudenovich was there as well.

Prince Gleb Yuryevich, possessed by fear, withdrew from Pereyaslavl with loss, having lost many people; and he went to Ryazan and joined with Grand Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich; and having gone, they took the city of Kursk, and there they installed their governors throughout the whole land of Kursk. Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich and Prince Izyaslav Davydovich also came to them; and having gone, they took the city of Popash, and came to Vyrevets, but the people of Vyrevets did not surrender; they also came to Evkhanets, but the people of Evkhanets did not surrender.

Hearing of this, the Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich, that the Olegoviches had united and were attacking his sons' cities, gathered many troops and went with his brother Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, and with voivode Stanislav, and with his uncle Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, Grand Prince of Vladimir, with all his Volhynians. When they reached Chernaya Mogila, his brother Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich came there to help him with the Smolenskians; and from there they went against them to the Sula. They, hearing of this, went to Chernigov. The Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich, having gone, took the cities: Vsevolozh, called Uniy, and Belaya Vezha, and Bakhmach; and many people fled to Chernigov.

He, going on, took three more cities, and returned to Kiev, and from Kiev he released from himself his brother, Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich, to Smolensk.

Prince Gleb Yuryevich went to Ryazan, and was in the cities of Chervleny Yar and on the Great Vorona, and again returned to the Chernigov princes for help.

Hearing of him, the Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich sent to him, summoning him to himself in Kiev; he promised to go to him, but did not go and stayed not long in Novgorod Seversky. His boyars began to say to him that he should go to Pereyaslavl for the great principality, “because...”
