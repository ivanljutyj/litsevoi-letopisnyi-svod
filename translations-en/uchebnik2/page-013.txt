He kept him with him, and gave him Pereyaslavl by the kissing of the cross, as he had agreed with his brother Mstislav Vladimirovich according to their father’s command, to give Pereyaslavl to them, him and Mstislav.

Vsevolod Mstislavich, after Sunday matins, entered Pereyaslavl and sat in it; and on the same day before dinner his other uncle, Yuri, with Svyatopolk, came against him; and they drove him out of Pereyaslavl and committed many murders.

And Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh himself sat in Pereyaslavl for eight days. And his brother Yaropolk sent a great force against him, and brought him out of Pereyaslavl by the kissing of the cross, as was said before.

And for the second time he sent to Pereyaslavl another son of his brother Mstislav Vladimirovich, Izyaslav, as was said earlier, by the kissing of the cross, and his nephew Vsevolod Mstislavich he sent to Novgorod, and his brother Yuri Vladimirovich he transferred with an oath to Polotsk.

He left his brother Svyatopolk in Polotsk and came to Pereyaslavl on the Feast of the Mother of God against Izyaslav Mstislavich.

The people of Polotsk drove his brother Svyatopolk out of Polotsk, and installed Vasilko Svyatoslavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, to rule in Polotsk.

The Grand Prince of Kiev Yaropolk, seeing the injustice of his brothers, was dismayed and gave Pereyaslavl to his brother Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, and removed his nephew Izyaslav Mstislavich from Pereyaslavl with great compulsion, and comforted him, telling him not to grieve over anything, saying to him: “I will make it all up to you some day.”

That same winter Yaropolk summoned his brothers to him and told them not to wrong their nephews, and with tears he said: “Brothers! This life is vain, and all things pass away. Where are our fathers, grandfathers, and great-grandfathers? Did they not die? So too must we die, and each of us will receive according to his deeds.”

And he gave to his nephew Izyaslav Mstislavich Turov, Drochev, and Pinsk in addition to Minsk, his former domain, for Minsk alone remained of his former domain.

Yaropolk also gave him many gifts: pearls, gold, silver, garments, horses, armor; and he honored him greatly and sent him away to his aforenamed cities.

In the same year the Polovtsian prince Amurat was baptized in Ryazan.
