In the year 6754 from Adam (the year 1254 from Christ)

Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich and Prince Ivan Vsevolodovich, grandsons of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-grandsons of Vladimir Monomakh, with their sons, came from the Tatars to the land that had come to them from their father.

And Tsar Batu sent Grand Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich to the khan's sons.

In the same year Prince Mikhail Vsevolodovich of Chernigov, grandson of Svyatoslav Olegovich, was killed by the accursed Tsar Batu in the Horde, and with him his boyar Feodor. God permitted this to happen in the Russian land because of the increase of our sins. As in the time of Noah and in the time of Lot punishment from God came upon the earth, so in these times God sent punishment by His hand upon the Christian land. The ancient prophets prophesied, as it is said: "If you are willing and obey Me, you shall eat the good things of the earth; if you are not willing and do not obey Me, the sword shall devour you." And when all this was fulfilled, the wrath of God appeared upon all who live on the earth. It is said: "If you wish, you shall live in righteousness; if you do not wish and do not obey, one of you shall chase a thousand, and two shall put ten thousand to flight." And behold, the wrath of God came, and unbelieving peoples were sent against the land, destroying cities and conquering in wars. Some shut themselves up in cities, but the impious Tsar Batu took many cities and shed much blood; and he went into the steppe, into the Polovtsian land.

In the preceding year he sent his Tatars ahead of him; they took the remaining Russian cities: Pereyaslavl, and Chernigov, and Mordva, and Murom, and Gorokhovets, the city with the cathedral church of the Most Holy Mother of God of Vladimir.

Batu sent his voivode Mengukak to inspect the city of Kiev; and Mengukak saw the city of Kiev and marveled at its beauty and greatness.

In the city of Kiev there then reigned the confessor of Christ, the blessed and great Prince Mikhail of Chernigov, son of Vsevolod, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Oleg, great-great-grandson of Svyatoslav, Yaroslav, and Vladimir, who baptized the Russian land. This blessed and great Prince Mikhail from his youth loved God and His Most Pure Mother, and raised his mind to God; he was meek and merciful to the poor; and he renounced all the empty things of this world, its riches and vain glory, which is worse than a spider's web. And Mikhail was ready for struggles even unto suffering for the faith of Christ, so as to preserve the faith blamelessly and keep his confession pure; and so it came to pass.
