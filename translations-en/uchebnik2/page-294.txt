Tsar Batu came to the land of Hungary and destroyed all the cities and took the people captive; but the ruler Vlaslov, to whom Saint Sava gave the name Vladislav, did not manage to gather with his people because of the great distance in his lands.

For Batu it was a most favorable time, and he did as he wished; but King Vladislav for many days remained on the pillar mentioned earlier, in great sorrow, taking neither bread nor water, and he prayed to Christ God with tears that He would change His wrath to mercy and deliver him from his enemies.

Vladislav saw from the pillar on which he was standing how his sister fled to him into the city; and the Tatars, overtaking her, seized her and led her away to Batu; King Vladislav saw this and turned to even greater weeping and tears; and he prayed to Christ God that by His help and His mercy He would deliver him from his enemies. So he prayed, and tears flowed from his eyes like river torrents; and where the tears fell on the marble, they passed through it, as even in our days one can see on that pillar in the marble. And by this sign the nobles who were with him and all his close attendants knew that help and mercy would come from God.

And soon after this a certain man, very radiant and awe-inspiring, so that fear and trembling seized all who were with the king at the sight of him, stood before the king and said: “Because of your tears the Lord God gives you victory over Tsar Batu; go quickly against him, in this hour.” They began to look at the face of the one speaking, and they saw him no more.

And they came down from that pillar and saw a saddled horse, which no one was holding but which stood by itself, and a battle axe was on the horse. And from this sign they became even more certain that help would come from God.

King Vladislav at that hour mounted that horse, took that axe in his hand, and went out of the city with his troops that were there with him; and suddenly, without warning, he attacked the camp of Tsar Batu. Batu at that time had a very small army, since all his Tatars had dispersed in detachments; and the Tatars who were with him at that hour were struck by the wrath of God, as if dead, and fled in great shame.

Tsar Batu saw his misfortune and did not know what to do, and he fled, driven by the wrath of God; and the king’s sister was with him. King Vladislav with his army pursued them and killed many Tatars; he also overtook Tsar Batu himself and fought with him; and the king’s sister began to help Batu.
