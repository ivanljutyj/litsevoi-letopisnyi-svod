In the same year Izyaslav went against Yaroslavich to Turov, while Yaroslav Izyaslavich himself went from Lutsk, and Yaropolk Andreevich, and the Galician aid, and Ryurik Rostislavich with the Smolensk men, and Vladimir Mstislavich; for they too strove to take possession of Turov. And the Polochane, coming to Turov, burned its villages, while the Berendichi ravaged around Pinsk and beyond the Pripyat.

Those in the city fought hard, sallying out against the enemy, and many were wounded. And Yuri Yaroslavich prayed much, sending out from the city to Izyaslav, saying: “Receive me, brother, into your love.” But Izyaslav did not wish this, for he wanted to take from him Turov and Pinsk. And he stood about the city for ten weeks, and a plague fell upon his horses; and having accomplished nothing, he returned to his own land, without making peace with him, and many came back from that war on foot.

The Tale of the Origin of the Precious Cross
on the first day of the month of August

It should be known concerning this “Pantokratoros hemera Kyriou Theou Sotiros hemon,” which means: “On this day of the Almighty Lord God and Savior our Jesus Christ,” on which we celebrate the mercy of God and of His Most Pure Mother, and the origin of the Precious Cross. In Constantinople reigned the right-believing and Christ-loving Emperor Manuel, while in Rostov and Suzdal the great prince Andrei Bogolyubsky sat on the throne of his father, in the great principality of Rostov and Suzdal; in Kiev and throughout all Rus then Constantine was metropolitan, and in Rostov Nestor was bishop.

These two autocrats lived in great love with one another, and it happened that both set out on campaign on the same day: the one from Tsargrad against the Persians, and the one from Rostov against the Bulgars, who are called Kazanians. Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, had the custom, when going to war, that two presbyters in sacred vestments carry the icon of the Most Pure Mother of God and the Life-giving Cross of Christ; and he, with tears, with a pure soul and a contrite heart, fell down before the image of the Mother of God and the Precious Cross, saying thus: “Most holy Lady, Sovereign Mother of God, who gave birth to Christ our God, wall and help and hope of all, I, a sinful and unworthy servant of Thine, have Thee as wall and help and protection, and the Cross of Thy Son as an invincible weapon against the enemy”; and he also said thus: “Lord God Jesus Christ our God, through the prayers of Thy most pure Mother and by the power of Thy precious Cross, help us against these godless barbarians, that Thy most holy name and that of Thy most pure Mother may be glorified.”

And so all prayed with tears and faith and crossed themselves, and kissed the image of the Most Pure Mother of God and the Precious and Life-giving Cross. And first he himself partook of the holy and life-giving mysteries, and also those who were with him.
