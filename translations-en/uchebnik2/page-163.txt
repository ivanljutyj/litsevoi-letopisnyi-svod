They, frightened, sent their envoys to Vsevolod, bowing and agreeing to fulfill his will; he, believing them, dismounted from his horse.

And he sent other envoys to Rurik Olegovich, saying: “Brother! We have never had any grief with you, and although we did not manage this winter to conclude an agreement with Vsevolod and with you and your brother David, yet you are close to us; conclude an agreement with us, kissing the cross, that you will not go to war with us until we make peace with Vsevolod and David.”

Rurik, after consulting with his close advisers, sent an envoy to Yaroslav, wishing to unite him with Vsevolod and David in peace.

And he brought them to swear on the cross that they would not fight until the agreement was concluded, and Rurik himself also kissed the cross before them on the same, and dismissed his retinue, and sent his sons home, and released the Polovtsians to their lands, giving them many gifts, and himself went to Ovruch.

On the Betrayal of the Chernigov Princes

In that same winter, during Great Lent, Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with his brothers, the Chernigov princes, broke the agreement and the kissing of the cross, about which they had agreed with Rurik and had kissed the cross with him, that they would not fight until Vsevolod’s and David’s envoys arrived.

And, without waiting for this, Yaroslav sent his nephew to Vitebsk against his son-in-law David Rostislavich; Rurik at that time was not in Kiev, he had gone to Ovruch, having dismissed his brothers, trusting in the kissing of the cross.

And the Olegoviches, not reaching Vitebsk, began to conquer the Smolensk territory.

David Olegovich heard of this and sent his nephew Mstislav Romanovich and Rostislav Vladimirovich with his regiment, and the Ryazan prince Gleb, his son-in-law, and the Smolenskians together with them. They had already come close when at that time, on Tuesday of the second week of Lent, at the very Mass, the earth shook throughout the Kiev land; in Kiev stone and wooden churches shook, and all the people from fear could not stand on their feet, but fell prostrate, trembling with fear, and on that same day the Chernigov and Smolensk regiments began to gather together.

The Olegoviches, however, had taken care beforehand to draw up their regiments and stood tramping in the snow: then heavy snow was falling. Mstislav and his retinue came out of the forest, saw the regiments, but had not managed to draw up their own regiments, and quickly went against them, and fought with Prince Oleg’s regiment, trampled his banners, and cut down his son David.
