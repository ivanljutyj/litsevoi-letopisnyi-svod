And they went against Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, and the Black Klobuks rushed at him before all the others. He, seeing their great force, fled at once; they pursued him and caught up with him, and killed his small retinue, and seized others with their hands, and also overtook Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich himself as he fled into the pine grove; and they began to slash him on the head with sabers. Ivor Gedenevich struck him with a spear in the shoulder, another pierced him with a spear above the knee, another struck him with a spear in the lower back; as he fled, they also shot him with a crossbow in the armpit. He fell from his horse, and while he still lay alive, Prince Rostislav Mstislavich and Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich came to him and began to cry and weep, saying: “My lord Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich! Was the grand principality of Chernigov not enough for you, that you drove me out of Kiev, and when you had already taken Kiev, why did you plan to drive me out of Belgorod?” He lay and sighed heavily, and much blood flowed from him; then he asked for water to drink. Prince Rostislav and Prince Mstislav ordered wine to be given to him, but he said: “I want water.” They gave him wine; and having drunk the wine, he fell asleep with eternal sleep.

And they ordered him to be carried to Chernigov; and Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich of Chernigov laid his body in the church of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb. Such was the course in this age full of temptations and in the swiftly flowing life of Grand Prince Izyaslav, son of David, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir.

Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich again placed his uncle Prince Rostislav Mstislavich on the grand princely throne in Kiev, and himself went to Vladimir Volynsky for the great reign.

In the same year the city of Vladimir was built very large by Prince Andrew Bogolyubsky of Rostov and Suzdal, son of Yuri Dolgoruky; and he wished the throne of the grand principality to be in this city. But the Rostovites and Suzdalians did not want this, saying: “Rostov is the older and greater city, and so is Suzdal; the city of Vladimir is our suburb.”

The Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal, Andrew Bogolyubsky, wished not only to turn the city into a grand-princely city, but also to establish a metropolitan see in it, and according to ancient laws and statutes to satisfy and honor all in Christ.

This Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal, Andrew Yuryevich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, was God-loving and man-loving, and well-disposed toward church laws and affairs, and he rendered great honor to hierarchs, priests, and monks. Therefore God glorified him; for everywhere his name was glorified: “For those who glorify Me,” as He said, “I will glorify.”
