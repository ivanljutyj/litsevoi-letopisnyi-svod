The Tale of the Taking of Moscow by Tsar Tokhtamysh and of the Captivity of the Land of Ryazan

Tsar Tokhtamysh reigned in the Volga kingdom and held the coastal cities in the Horde and in Sarai.

And it was in the third year of his reign: he sent his Tatars to the city called Bolgary, which is Kazan on the Volga, and ordered the Christian merchants to be slain, the Russian merchants to be plundered, and their boats and goods to be taken and carried over to his ferry crossing.

And he himself, in great fury, gathered many forces and went to the Volga with his princes and with all the Tatar force.

And he crossed to this side of the Volga River and went against Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich toward Moscow in a surprise raid, and against all the Russian land; he led his host without news, suddenly, with skill and such cunning—giving no news of himself, so that he might not be heard of.

Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod heard of this and sent two of his sons, Prince Vasily and Prince Semyon, to Tsar Tokhtamysh. They set out but did not find him, for his march to Rus was swift, and they pursued after him for several days and only just crossed his path at a place called Sernache.

And they went after him along his road with great diligence and overtook him on the borders of the Ryazan land.

And Prince Oleg of Ryazan met Tsar Tokhtamysh on the borders of his Ryazan land and bowed to him in homage, asking that he not ravage his land.

And he led him around his land and became his helper for victory over Rus and his abettor in doing harm to Christians. And he uttered certain other words about how to enslave the Russian land, how to take the stone city of Moscow without difficulty, and how to defeat and take captive Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

And at that time news had just come to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich announcing the arrival of the Tatar host. Although Tokhtamysh did not want anyone to bring news to the Russian land of his coming—and for that reason all Russian merchants had been seized, plundered, and detained, so that there might be no news in Rus—nevertheless there were certain well-wishers within the Horde’s borders who resolved to do this, being supporters of the Russian land.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich heard such news, that Tsar Tokhtamysh himself was coming against him with the multitude of his forces, and he began to gather regiments of warriors and gathered many soldiers.

And he rode out of the city of Moscow and wished to go to meet the enemy host. He also began to hold counsel about this and to deliberate with his brother and with others.
