O how the most honored creation of God, created in His likeness and image, is seen without breath and dead; alas, it is vile, full of unclean worms emitted by foul breath. How wisdom vanishes, how speech is hidden, how the twofold dissolves, how the threefold is concealed, the fourth perishes, the fifth is corrupted, breathless and dead? Alas, passions, alas! The double decade perishes, while the seventh and eighth foreshadow the dissolution of what is to come. Earth is our mixture, earth covers all, and earth is our resurrection. Alas, passions, alas for me! Naked I come forth to weep; I am an infant; naked I come and naked I shall depart; I labor and trouble myself in vain, knowing the end of life. O wonder, how we all alike come from darkness into light, and from light into darkness; from our mother’s womb we come weeping into the world, and from the sorrowful world weeping into the grave. The beginning and end of all is weeping. What then is needful: sleep, shadow, dream, worldly beauty? Alas, alas, life is woven from many passions, but all passes like the beauty of a flower, like dust, like a shadow.” After the death of this metropolitan, other Russian metropolitans to this day order a similar document to be placed in their coffin after their repose, reading it aloud beforehand. In the same autumn the Pskovians quarreled with the Germans and began to fight one another. The Pskovians gathered a large army.

And they went with the army to conquer German lands; a German army met them on the Siritsa River.

And a battle took place on the 9th day of October; God helped the Pskovians; they defeated the wicked, impious Germans and pursued them through the German land for 20 versts to Novgorodok, beating and killing many with bare hands.

And from there the Pskovians went to Kirempe, wishing to approach German Yuryev. And Akort, Master of Riga, met them with all the German seaboard beyond the Selilovo battlefield, and there was a fierce battle; God helped the Pskovians in the battle with the Germans; the master fled with a small retinue, and the Pskovians pursued him to Kirempe, killing them. And the Pskovians returned to their homes with a great victory, with much booty and wealth, bringing many Germans with them; in Pskov there was great joy.

In the same autumn, on the 14th day of November, Prince Rodoslav of Ryazan reposed, son of Oleg of Ryazan, grandson of Ivan.

In the same autumn in Tver Prince Dmitry Eremeevich reposed.

In the same autumn in Tver Princess Anastasia reposed, wife of Prince Eremey, mother of Prince Dmitry Eremeevich.

In the same winter, on the 16th day of January, Prince Pyotr Dmitrievich Ivanovich married in Moscow the daughter of Prince Polievkt Vasilievich.
