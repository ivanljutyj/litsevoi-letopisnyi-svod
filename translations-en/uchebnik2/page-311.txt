And learning of this, the Germans were afraid, and in fear sent their envoys with a great petition and many gifts, and asked for peace, fully agreeing to the terms of Prince Yaroslav.

And the Germans gave gifts to all — to the grand basqaq and to all the Tatar princes; for they greatly feared the Tatars and the very name of the Tatars. And then they fulfilled all the commands of the grand prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, and withdrew entirely from Narva, and returned everything that had been seized.

In the same year Mitrofan, bishop of Sarai, departed from his place in Sarai and wrote his resignation with his own hand, and sent it to the metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus.

In the same year the most reverend Cyril, metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, made Theognostus bishop of Russian Pereyaslavl and Sarai.

In the year 6778 from Adam (1278 from Christ)

The Novgorodians rose in revolt, and gathered, and held an assembly, and began to drive the grand prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich out of Novgorod; and they killed his friends and supporters, and plundered their houses and property; and they sent envoys to Gorodishche to the grand prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, and wrote these accusations against him: “Why, prince, do you do wrong? You keep many hawks and falcons; you have taken from us the river Volkhov and other waters for duck hunting; and you keep many dogs; you have taken from us the field for hare hunting. You have taken the courtyard of Aleksa Mortkin, and taken much silver from Nikifor Matveyevich, and from Roman Boldyrev, and from Varfolomey Alekseyevich. And why do you drive out of Novgorod the foreigners who live among us? And in many other things you are guilty, prince. And we now, prince, cannot endure your oppressions; so leave us, prince, peaceably, and we shall find a prince for ourselves.”

On the very next day the grand prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich sent to their assembly his son Svyatoslav and his tysyatsky Andrey Vorotislavovich with a greeting and these words: “Forgive me these offenses, and I shall do so no more, and I kiss the cross, and promise to observe your will in all things.” The Novgorodians answered: “Prince, we do not want you; leave us peaceably; if not, we shall drive you out even without your consent.”

And the grand prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, grandson of Vsevolod, departed from them from Novgorod and came to Vladimir, and began to gather an army, wishing to go to war against Novgorod.
