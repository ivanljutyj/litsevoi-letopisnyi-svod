In the same year there was a great revolt in Lithuania: they rose up and killed Grand Prince Kestutis Gediminovich, and killed his boyars; for Kestutis had taken the great reign of Lithuania, and therefore there was revolt and bloodshed among them.

Then Kestutis’s son, Prince Vytautas, fled to the Germans, and from there he did much evil to the Lithuanian land. In the same year Prince Jogaila of Lithuania married. This account was written in the fifth year of this decade.

In the year 6890 from Adam (1390 from Christ)

Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, came from Constantinople to Novgorod the Great from the most reverend ecumenical Patriarch Nilus with a blessing and with letters, in which was written about the expenses for ordinations, and teachings of the law of God according to the sacred canons and the Divine Scriptures, strengthening against temptations and against the heresy of the Strigolniki; other teachings he commanded Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, to speak orally in his place, in the presence of Archbishop Alexius of Novgorod. Thus he did in Novgorod and in Pskov, and he stopped the disturbances and temptations concerning the expenses for ordinations: for one thing is a fee, and another are the expenses and outlays for ordinations. And so concerning this he delivered to them letters from the ecumenical Patriarch Nilus, and, having taught sufficiently according to the patriarch’s command, Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, went from Novgorod and Pskov to Suzdal.

And he came to Suzdal to his see on the sixth day of January. And he received the archbishopric from the most reverend Patriarch Nilus, and the ecumenical patriarch with all the sacred council blessed him, and commanded him to be called and to be archbishop in Suzdal, and in Nizhny Novgorod, and in Gorodets, and that after him archbishops should remain in those affairs and ranks. Furthermore Nilus, the ecumenical patriarch, with the sacred council, gave him sacred vestments with four crosses, and gave him a sticharion with fringes; for this is a sign of the great metropolitan dignity—a phelonion with crosses and a sticharion with fringes; and “metropolitan” is interpreted as mother of cities and head of the eparchy that is under him. Furthermore Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, brought with him from Constantinople the Passion of the Savior, and relics of many saints, and crosses, and wonder-working icons with relics.

In the same year Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin died and was laid in the cathedral church of the Resurrection, in his patrimony, in the city of Kashin, on the sixth day of May.

That same summer a son, Prince Andrei, was born to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, on the fourteenth day of August, and Feodor, abbot of Simonov, baptized him.
