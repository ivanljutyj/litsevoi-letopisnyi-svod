Although Alexander received from God the honor of reigning on earth, and had a wife, and begot children, yet he had more humble-mindedness than any other man. He was very tall, the beauty of his face was like that of Joseph the Beautiful, his strength was like a part of Samson’s strength, his voice was like a trumpet among the people, his courage was like that of the Roman king Vespasian, son of Nero, who captured the whole land of Judea. That Vespasian raised his regiments and ordered them to approach the city of Jotapata; the townspeople came out and defeated his regiments; he alone came out and drove the enemy army back to the city gates, and, smiling to his retinue, said: “Why did you leave me alone?” So too this Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich conquered everywhere, and was never conquered.

Alexander showed much mercy, like his God-protected father Yaroslav, following his example in all things. He gave much gold and silver for captives, sending to Tsar Batu in the Horde for Russian captives who had been seized by the godless Tatars; Alexander ransomed them and delivered them from harsh slavery and from many misfortunes and calamities.

God always preserved Alexander himself, and the prince remained unharmed everywhere from all enemies. Most wonderful of all was the mercy of the Lord that was accomplished in him. Alexander was fearsome and terrible to all enemies, and everywhere his name was feared. Wisdom and a quick mind were given to him from God, like Solomon. Most of all he loved just judgment, about which he often instructed his boyars with words from Divine Scripture: that they should first of all ask God for wisdom, and abstain from drunkenness, and humble themselves before God; that they should not forget to judge justly, nor associate with the powerful of this world; nor take unjust reward; nor offend anyone, but deliver the offended from the hands of offenders; and take nothing beyond what was allotted to them, but be content with the pay appointed to them. And so Alexander spoke many times, sometimes terrifying them with his authority, sometimes reminding them of eternal recompense, which at Christ’s Dread Judgment will be given to each according to his deeds. The boyars and all the people, seeing the wisdom granted him from God, could answer nothing, but with all their soul promised to do as he commanded them. And thus Alexander courageously and righteously, according to God’s law, governed the dominions granted him by God.

And so he was heard of in many distant lands; and many strove to see him.
