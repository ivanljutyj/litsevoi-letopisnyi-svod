In the year 6893 from Adam (1393 from Christ)

There was a sign on the twenty-third day of September, at the first hour of the day: the sun rose, and a cloud came from the west very swiftly, like darkness, and darkened the daylight until the third hour. And it was so dark as on a dark autumn night, and people did not understand what it was. And there was great sorrow and grief among the people; then gradually clouds became visible, some yellowish, and some crimson-like and thin. From some directions clouds passed at mid-morning, at dinner time, and after dinner; in places fiery clouds moved, and sparks fell to the earth and set fires. And it was so terrifying and dreadful that all thought the Second Coming of Christ was at hand. That same autumn Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver married his son Prince Boris to a daughter of Grand Prince Svyatoslav Ivanovich of Smolensk; and he was wed in the Church of the Holy Saviour in Tver on the eighth day of November, the Synaxis of the Archangel Michael.

That same autumn two Greek metropolitans came from Constantinople: one was named Matthew and the other Nicander, and with them archdeacons and other dignitaries, and they invited Metropolitan Pimen to Constantinople to Patriarch Nilus. That same winter the Novgorod posadniks held a veche according to old Novgorod custom, posadnik Fedor Timofeevich and tysyatsky Bogdan Avvakumovich, and they confirmed it by kissing the cross—that they should not be summoned to Moscow to the metropolitan for judgment, but that the Archbishop of Novgorod, Alexius, or whoever after him might be archbishop in Novgorod, should judge them; and they should be judged according to Greek law, and in matters of right and guilt they should abide by the faith, by the Gospel of Greek law. And the posadnik and tysyatsky should judge by their own courts according to Russian custom, by cross-kissing; and for the trial each of the two litigants should have two boyars and two householders from each side.

That same winter Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver married his son Prince Vasily to a daughter of Prince Vladimir Olgerdovich of Kiev, grandson of Gediminas; and he was wed in the Church of the Holy Saviour in Tver. In the same year the work of the holy and wise George Pisides was translated, “Praise to God on the Creation of All Creation: ‘How magnified are Thy works, O Lord! In wisdom hast Thou made them all,’” whose beginning is: “From every deed and God-spoken word, both tongue and mind and reason and heart...”

In the same year the most severe Prince Oleg of Ryazan took Kolomna, coming in a raid, and took captive the Kolomna namestnik Alexander Andreevich, called Ostey, and many other boyars and best people he took captive and led away with him. And having plundered gold and silver and all goods, he withdrew to his own land with much captive spoil and wealth.
