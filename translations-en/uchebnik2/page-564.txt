In the same year in Kiev died Metropolitan Dionysius, and he was laid in the Pechersk Theodosius Monastery. In the same year died Alexis, Archbishop of Novgorod, and he was laid in the Church of the Holy Resurrection, in the monastery on Derevyanitsa.

That same winter, before the beginning of Great Lent, Cyprian, Metropolitan of all Rus, went to Moscow, and the grand prince’s boyars met him while he was still far away.

In the year 6898 from Adam (1398 from Christ)

When he approached Moscow during Great Lent in Mid-Lent week, the grand prince himself, Vasily Dmitrievich, met him with his mother Evdokia, with his brothers, with the boyars and with all Christians at Kotel. And he received the blessing with his mother and his wife, with his brothers, with the boyars and with all Christians. And there was great joy, and all together went into Moscow.

Metropolitan Cyprian vested in hierarchal robes at St Nicholas the Old, and entered the city with crosses and the holy council, and, coming to the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, took his metropolitan see, possessing his dues according to ancient custom. And there was great joy in the city of Moscow. Then also came with him to Moscow the two Greek metropolitans named before—Matthew of Adrianople and Nicander of Gaianon—and Theodore, Bishop of Rostov, to whom Patriarch Anthony of Constantinople granted the archbishopric and named him Archbishop of Rostov. With him also were Euphrosynus, Bishop of Suzdal; Jeremiah the Greek, Bishop of Ryazan; Isaac, Bishop of Chernigov; Michael, Bishop of Smolensk; Daniel, Bishop of Zvenigorod.

Then all the Russian bishops, before each departed to his own diocese, came to Metropolitan Cyprian in Moscow, saying: “Cyprian has now become Metropolitan of all Rus and has come from Kiev and sat in Moscow on his metropolitan see.”

In the Rostov diocese Bishop Theodore took his seat, whom the patriarch appointed Archbishop of Rostov; in Suzdal, the Greek bishop Euphrosynus; in Turov, Bishop Theodosius. All of them received their dioceses in one year. That same spring, during Great Lent, died the servant of God Ivan Rodionovich Kvashnya, who was named Ignatius in monasticism, and he was buried in the Monastery of the Savior on Vskhodnya.
