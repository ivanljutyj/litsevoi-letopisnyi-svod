In the same year the Polovtsian princes came against Rus with a great host, and having taken many Christians captive, returned to their own lands.

In the same year the Novgorodians went against the Chud, and defeated them, and returned to their own lands with captives and much wealth.

In the same year Danslav Lazutich went from Novgorod to the Dvina to collect tribute according to Novgorod custom, and with him five hundred men. And they met with the Suzdalians and Ryazanians on Beloozero, and there were seven thousand three hundred of them, and they joined battle, and there fell then one thousand three hundred Suzdalians, but of the Novgorodians only 15 men; and they took tribute from the Zavolochians, and another from the Suzdalians and Ryazanians.

And from that time Grand Prince Andrei was angry with Novgorod. And he gathered a great host, and sent his son Mstislav to Novgorod with all the Suzdalian force, and with him the Smolensk princes Roman and Mstislav, and the Murom and Ryazan princes, and many other princes, and the Toropets people and Polotsk people, and almost the whole Russian land was united; of princes alone there were then 72; and in Novgorod there was then Prince Roman, son of Mstislav, grandson of Izyaslav, great-grandson of Mstislav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh.

They came into the Novgorod land and wrought much evil: they took and burned villages, and cut down people, and took wives and children, and property, and cattle.

And they came to the city. The Novgorodians set up an outer fortification around all Novgorod; and the Suzdalians with all the princes attacked, and there were many battles, while the townspeople fought stoutly from the city.

And for three days envoys from both sides met many times to pacify the strife, but they achieved nothing peaceful or loving; the hierarchal throne in Great Novgorod was then held by the wonderworker Ivan, called Ilya in the world, whom for his exceeding great virtue the most reverend Metropolitan John of Kiev and all Rus honored with the rank of archbishop, for before him there had been bishops in Novgorod; and the posadnik then was Andrei Yakunovich.

And then this Archbishop Ivan, together with their prince Roman Mstislavich, and the posadnik Yakun Andreevich, with a multitude of all the people, in great sorrow prayed to God and the Most Pure Mother of God for the salvation of their city.

On the third night, when the holy Bishop Ivan stood praying before the image of our Lord Jesus Christ, he was seized with awe and heard a voice saying to him: “Go to the church of the Holy Saviour on Ilyina Street, and take the icon of the Holy Mother of God, and set it upon the outer fortification facing the enemy.” The archbishop, hearing the unusual voice, remained all night without sleep, praying to the holy Mother of God.
