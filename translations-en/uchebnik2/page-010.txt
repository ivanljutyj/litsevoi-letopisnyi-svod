And there was a great tumult in Novgorod, and the people were in turmoil here and there, and they did not want Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich in Novgorod among them, and many began to flee to Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich in Pskov, and as they fled they plundered many houses and villages. Then Konstantin Nezhatin, Ivanko Dobrynin, Timoshka Yakshich, Nester Petrilov, and many others fled.

Svyatoslav Olegovich then began to interrogate the Novgorod boyars, and, having questioned them thoroughly, he executed some, shut others in dungeons, and bound others by the kissing of the cross.

After that Svyatoslav Olegovich also gathered the whole land of Novgorod, and his brother Gleb, and the host of the city of Kursk, and the Polovtsians; and they went against Pskov to drive out Vsevolod.

But the Pskovians did not submit to Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich, and did not drive Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich from them, and they stood firm and were not afraid, because news had come to them beforehand that Svyatoslav Olegovich was coming against them with great force, and with his brother Gleb, and with the Polovtsians; and they made abatis and blocked the roads.

Prince Svyatoslav stood for a while in perplexity and said: “It is better to trust in God than to shed blood with my brothers; for all things are possible from God, and He will direct it as He wills”; and he returned to Novgorod, and each went his own way.

In the same year Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, reposed in Pskov, on the eleventh day of February, in Cheese-fare week, on Thursday; and he was laid in the Church of the Holy Trinity, which he himself had built. The Pskovians then stood up for his brother Svyatopolk; and there was no peace with the Suzdalians, nor with the Smolenskians, nor with the Polotskians, nor with the Kievans; and in those quarrels a great famine came upon the land, and captivity, and unceasing bloodshed from the pagan Polovtsians, and from the Poles, and from the Chud; and the land grew desolate in emptiness, according to the word of Christ, who said in His Gospel: “Every kingdom divided against itself shall be laid waste, and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand.”

In the same year the Polovtsians warred against Kursk, and took captive all the land, and burned it. In the same year a great host of Poles gathered, and warred against Rus, and wrought much evil.

In the same year the blessed Princess Euphemia, wife of Prince Vladimir Monomakh, reposed, and was laid in Berestovo in the Church of the Holy Savior. In the same year the Novgorodians drove their prince Svyatoslav from them out of Novgorod—son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir the Great—who had sat on the throne in Novgorod for two years and five months.
