And they told everything to Edygei, Prince of the Horde. Edygei, sometimes called the father of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, but himself concealing his treachery, calling the prince his son while wishing to make war on him and take him captive, sent to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, saying to him deceitfully: “Know, Vasily, that Tsar Bulat-Saltan has set out with all the Great Horde against Vitovt to avenge himself on him for what he has done to your land. Do you render him royal honors: if you do not go yourself, then send your son to the tsar, or your brother; if not them, then send to him one of your nobles, fearing nothing, for I will help you in everything.” Thus Edygei schemed deceitfully so that the troops would not gather together and offer him resistance.

Edygei’s envoy came to Moscow and delivered Edygei’s words. Grand Prince Vasily and all the people were perplexed, knowing nothing of this news, and therefore did not gather troops, but sent to Edygei one of the nobles named Yuri with a retinue, saying that if an army should soon be gathered, he would send it after him.

Edygei took Yuri and soon set out. In Moscow they waited for news from Yuri, but did not receive it.

And suddenly a certain man came who had learned that the Tatar army had already come near. Hearing this, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich did not manage to gather even a small army.

And so the city was besieged. In it the grand prince left his uncle Prince Vladimir Andreevich—grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Daniel of Moscow—and his brothers Prince Andrei Dmitrievich and Prince Peter Dmitrievich, the voivodes, and a multitude of people; while he himself, with the princess and their children, went to Kostroma.

And in the city confusion began; the people were terrified and began to flee, caring neither for their property nor for anything else, while robbers and thieves and plunderers began to enrich themselves by pillage.

And they ordered the outer town to be set on fire, and there was a great tumult; the people wailed and shouted, and the fire roared, and tongues of flame rose into the air, and the city was surrounded on all sides by the Tatars.

It was already the fifth day, and evening was coming on, while the Tatar regiments, growing in number, settled for the night in the field, fearing to approach close to the city walls because of the shots from the city.

And Edygei himself, having come up, took his stand in Kolomenskoye and saw how all the people were terrified and no one dared go out against him, and he himself dismissed his army. Thus the Lord God humbled us before our enemies.
