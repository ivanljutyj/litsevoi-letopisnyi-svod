To take captive and burn; but there was no news of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich as to where he was: in Torzhok or in Tver?

Yaroslav, however, hearing that his districts and villages were being plundered and their people taken captive, went from Torzhok to Tver, taking with him the senior and best young men of Novgorod and all the Novotorzhians, and he sent his chosen men to reconnoiter. They rode out fifteen versts from the city of Tver and came to where the princes were standing with the Novgorodians.

And Prince Mstislav sent Yarun with chosen men against them, expecting a great host.

And Yaroslav’s advanced detachments rode against him, and God helped Yarun: he captured thirty-three of Yaroslav’s scouts, killed seven, and the rest fled to Tver. This was the first victory, in the fifth week of Holy Lent, on the day of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Mother of God.

And when they learned that Prince Yaroslav was in Tver, they began to disperse in raids without fear.

From there they sent Prince Vladimir’s boyar Yavolod to Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov in Rostov, and they sent Prince Vladimir of Pskov with the Pskovians and Smolenskians to escort him to the frontier; and they themselves went along the Volga, fighting, and burned Shosha and Dubna.

And Prince Vladimir with the Pskovians and Smolenskians took the city of Konstantin, burned it and all the Volga region. And the voivode Eremey met them, sent from Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich from Rostov, saying to them: “Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich bows to you and says: ‘I am glad to hear of your coming. I am sending you a host to help, five hundred men; and send Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich Romanovich, my brother-in-law, to me with all your messages.’”

And at once they sent Prince Vsevolod, his brother-in-law, to him, while they themselves went down the Volga, abandoning the wagons and mounting horses.

They went toward Pereyaslavl, fighting, and came to Gorodishche, to St Mary’s, on the Sara River, on the ninth day of April, at Easter.

And there Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich came to them from Rostov with the Rostovians. When they met and, embracing, kissed, they rejoiced with great joy and by kissing the cross agreed to be in unity and concord.

And they dispatched Prince Vladimir of Pskov to Rostov, and they themselves went to Pereyaslavl in St Thomas’s Week.

And Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich went to his brother, Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich, having gathered with all his force, and with the Novgorodians and the Novotorzhians.
