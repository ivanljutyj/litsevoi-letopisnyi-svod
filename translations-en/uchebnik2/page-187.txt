He also asked for freshly caught fish, but was told that it could not be obtained.

And then suddenly the waves swelled and threw at his feet a very large fish, without even splashing him; this fish was enough for the saint and those with him to eat for many days. Those sailing with the saint said then that they had never eaten such delicious fish. And when the saint had satisfied himself with the God-given fish, his illness immediately passed.

And soon he sailed to Constantinople, and in the monastery of the Most Holy Mother of God called Vergitis he found rest, redeeming precious vessels and relics of the saints, and by this he acquired much, wishing to bring it to his own country. From Constantinople he went by ship to the Bulgarian and Zagora lands and informed his kinsman by marriage, Tsar Asan, of his return; and Asan sent men to meet him, and afterward came out himself.

And the saint came to the city of Tarnovo, and was received with great joy by the tsar and Patriarch Joachim, and the tsar gave him his warm chambers. The tsar and the patriarch honored the saint.

The feast of Holy Theophany came, and on the eve of the feast they ordered Saint Sava to serve the service and bless the water, sprinkling the tsar and all the people; and on this occasion there was a great celebration at the tsar’s, who gave the saint much gold to distribute to the poor. After the feast of Holy Theophany the tsar went hunting, but the saint fell ill and understood that the time of his departure to the Lord had come; he wrote his testament, and sent with his disciples to his own country all the holy relics and vessels, and the golden lamps with pearls and stones, and the sacred vestments which he had redeemed, and he gave his blessing to King Vladislav, Archbishop Arsenius, and all the people.

And when Sunday came, the saint partook of the Divine Gifts, with great joy saw the angels coming to him, and, thanking God, gave his soul into His hands.

And the patriarch sent to the tsar, asking where the saint’s body should be buried; when the tsar heard this, he was greatly grieved that he had not been informed of the saint’s illness; although Saint Sava himself had forbidden them to inform the tsar of his illness, so that he might die in peace.

And the tsar ordered his relics to be placed in the monastery which he himself had founded, in the Church of the Holy Forty Martyrs, and much gold was distributed during his burial, and many healings took place then.

Later King Vladislav himself came to his father-in-law Tsar Asan, requested the saint’s body, and brought it to his own country with great honors; the saint’s body remained whole, in no way damaged, and gave off fragrance.
