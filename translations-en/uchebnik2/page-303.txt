Having learned of this, the Germans were frightened, and in fear they sent their envoys with a great entreaty and many gifts, and asked for peace, fully agreeing to Prince Yaroslav’s terms.

The Germans gave gifts to all, both the great basqaq and all the Tatar princes; but they greatly feared the Tatars and even the Tatar name. Then they fulfilled all the commands of Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, withdrew entirely from Narva, and returned everything that had been seized.

In the same year Mitrofan, bishop of Sarai, departed from his see in Sarai and wrote a renunciation with his own hand, and sent it to the Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus.

In the same year His Grace Cyril, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, made Theognostus bishop of Pereyaslavl-Russky and Sarai.

In the year 6778 from Adam (the year 1278 from Christ)

The Novgorodians rose in revolt, gathered, and held an assembly, and began to drive Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich out of Novgorod; and they killed his friends and supporters, and plundered their houses and property; and they sent envoys to Gorodishche to Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, and wrote accusations against him as follows: “Why, prince, do you act unjustly? You keep many hawks and falcons; you have taken from us the river Volkhov and other waters for duck hunting; and you keep many dogs; you have taken from us the field for hare hunting. You took the courtyard from Aleksa Mortkin, and took much silver from Nikifor Matveyevich, and from Roman Boldyrev, and from Varfolomey Alekseyevich. And why do you drive out of Novgorod the foreigners who live among us? And in many other things you have offended, prince. And now, prince, we cannot endure your oppressions; depart from us peaceably, and we will find ourselves a prince.”

On the very next day Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich sent to their assembly his son Svyatoslav and his tysyatsky Andrey Vorotislavovich with obeisance and these words: “Forgive me these offenses, and I will do so no more, and I kiss the cross, and promise to observe your will in all things.” The Novgorodians answered: “Prince, we do not want you; depart from us peaceably; if not, we will drive you out even without your consent.”

Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, grandson of Vsevolod, departed from them from Novgorod and came to Vladimir, and began to gather an army, wishing to go to war against Novgorod.
