"If my uncle, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash, is dear to me, let him come to us in Kiev." They all said with one voice: "It is worthy and righteous; what is pleasing to you is pleasing to us."

And he sent to Vyshgorod to Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash, saying: "Be a father to me; I have sinned before you, I did not render you honor; but from now on Kiev is yours, enter it into the great reign."

And Vyacheslav Vladimirovich entered Kiev; and they kissed and established great love, and both sat together in the great reign in Kiev. And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich gave seniority to his uncle, Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash, while he himself was as a son to him and as the younger in all things; for he saw in him his lord and father, and in place of a father he honored him in the great reign in Kiev.

And thus they sent away from Kiev a great host—Czechs, Poles, and Hungarians—to their kings with great honor and gifts; for the King of Hungary was son-in-law to Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev, and the King of Bohemia was his kinsman by marriage, and so they had great love among themselves.

Then Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev sent to them his son Prince Mstislav with love and honor; and after that he sent to his brother, Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich of Smolensk, in Smolensk, that he had driven Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky from the great reign of Kiev, and that they had joined in love with his uncle, Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash, and how he had sent for him, and how he had seated him in the great reign over all Kiev, and had established him as his lord and father, and had joyfully informed him of everything; and he commanded him to come to him speedily, and to exercise every caution and care both on the road and everywhere, fearing Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky.

In that same year, in spring, Grand Prince Rostislav Yuryevich of Pereyaslavl, grandson of Vladimir Monomash, died in his great principality in Pereyaslavl, on Good Friday; and his brothers, Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky, Prince Gleb Yuryevich, and Prince Mstislav Yuryevich, laid him in the Church of St Michael, near his uncles, Prince Svyatoslav Vladimirovich Monomash and Prince Andrei Vladimirovich Monomash. His son: Mstislav.
