With sorrow and grief in his soul, he went from Moscow to Veliky Novgorod together with his son Feodor and Prince Semyon Mstislavich Vyazemsky, and with his brother Prince Vladimir Svyatoslavich. The Novgorodians received them with honors. And they gave him thirteen cities: Rusa, Ladoga, Oreshek, Tiversky, Korelsky, Koporye, Torzhok, Volok Lamsky, Porkhov, Vyshgorod, Yama, Vysokoye, Kokshin, and Gorodets; and Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich kissed the cross to all Novgorod for life and death, promising that if enemies came against Novgorod, Prince Yuri would defend them together with the Novgorodians. And the Novgorod posadnik Aleksandr Fomich and the tysyatsky Kirill Dmitrievich kissed the cross to Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich for all Veliky Novgorod.

In the same year, the Most Reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', appointed the former abbot of the Borisoglebsky Monastery at Golyanishchevo as bishop in Sarai.

In the same year, a clock was installed in Moscow in the courtyard of the grand prince behind the Church of the Annunciation, and Lazar, a Serbian monk who had come from the Serbian land, made it.

In the same year, Prince Alexander Ivanovich of Tver came to Tver from Lithuania, from Vytautas Kęstutaitis.

In the same year, Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', released from Moscow to Novgorod Lord Ioann, Archbishop of Novgorod, who had been in confinement in Moscow under Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', for 3 years and 6 months; he had been confined in the settlement at the Monastery of St Nicholas the Old. He became quiet, meek, and humble; God delivered him from such sorrow, saved him, and had mercy on him.

And he came to Novgorod, and the whole city met him with crosses, with the whole sacred assembly, at the Church of St Nicholas at Yaroslav's Court, with joy and gladness; on that day there was a great celebration. They said to him: “Master, holy hierarch of God, you were taken captive by Metropolitan Cyprian in Moscow and lived in patience, thanking God. But we remained in grief and sorrow, praying for you day and night, and behold, the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos, have returned you, and we have forgotten all sorrows and illnesses. Do not forget us either in your prayers to the Lord; ask for us grace and mercy in this present and future life, that through your prayers we may receive forgiveness of sins.” And so they rejoiced with great joy over their master, the lord hierarch.

In the same year, on the twentieth day of the month of July, the Most Reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', went to Lithuania to the Grand Prince of Lithuania Vytautas Kęstutaitis, and to Kiev.
