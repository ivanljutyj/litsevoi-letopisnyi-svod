Those who are at peace and in love with them do not fear them, so that they, having chosen a suitable time, receive evil instead of what they desired.

Thus this Horde prince Edygei, the most noble of all Horde princes, ruling the entire Horde tsardom, appointing as tsar whom he pleased, was treacherous toward Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich: he surrounded him with high honors, bestowed rich gifts, and besides called him his beloved son, promising to strengthen his power and raise him above all Russian princes; he received with honor the envoys coming to the Horde from the prince and sent them back to Rus with gifts and peace, promising to have eternal peace with him.

Soon Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich became angry with his father-in-law Vitovt Keistutovich, Grand Prince of Lithuania, and wished to make war on him because of earthly wealth, since at that time Vitovt Keistutovich held all the Lithuanian and Kiev lands. And Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich told his grievance against his father-in-law Vitovt Keistutovich to the great Horde prince Edygei, wishing to receive help from him, since Edygei loved him and considered him his son. And the crafty and treacherous Edygei promised to help his son Grand Prince Vasily in every way, saying: “Let the other Russian princes see our love for each other; let them be meek and peaceful toward you, let them fear you, since I help you; therefore you will exalt yourself above them and frighten them all.”

Prince Edygei also sent to the Grand Prince of Lithuania Vitovt Keistutovich, saying: “Become my friend, and I will be your friend. Know that your son-in-law Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow wishes to invade foreign lands and not to defend his own but to make war on you; he wants to seize your lands. Beware of him, for he has said much to me about you, and sent me and the tsar silver and gold so that I myself would go or persuade the tsar to go with all the Horde to war against you, to take captives and burn your land, so that he might rule in your cities. Thus he kindles enmity toward you in us. But my love for you will never fade; hear my words and tell no one about this.”

Edygei rejoiced, thinking: “When they begin to fight among themselves, then their troops will diminish; if not all of them meet death, they will be exhausted, and I will have time to fight whomever I wish, and there will be no opponents for me.” And he sent to Moscow to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, approving his campaign against Vitovt and offering him help.

Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, having gathered his princes, boyars, and counselors, told them Edygei’s words; the princes, boyars, and counselors rejoiced, and all Moscow rejoiced at such love of Edygei for the grand prince.
