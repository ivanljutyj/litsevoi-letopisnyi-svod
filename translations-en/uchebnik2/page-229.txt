And then they set the city aflame from all sides; and fire engulfed the city from every quarter; and there was a mighty storm, and it was terrible to behold; and a very great cry arose in the city.

The Bulgar prince fled out through another gate and rode away on horses with a few warriors; and those who fled on foot—the men they killed, and the women and children they took captive; and some burned in the city; and some themselves cut down their own women and children, and then killed themselves.

Some of Svyatoslav’s warriors dared to enter the city for plunder, and barely escaped the flames, while some burned there. Prince Svyatoslav stood there until the whole city had burned; and he took the city of Oshél on the fifteenth day of June.

Then Svyatoslav went from there to his ships; and when he was at the ships, a storm arose with rain, so that even the ships were tossed about; and afterward the storm began to abate.

Prince Svyatoslav withdrew with his regiments to a place sheltered from the wind, into the forest, and the Murom princes with him; there they also made camp for the night.

On the next day, after dining there, they set off up the Volga.

The Bulgars in the Great City and in other cities learned that their city of Oshél had been taken, and they were greatly grieved.

All the Bulgars gathered with their princes, some on horses and others on foot, and came to the bank. Svyatoslav learned that the Bulgars, having gathered, were waiting for him at the landing places, and he ordered all his warriors to put on armor, raise the banners, and station the regiments in the ships.

Regiment after regiment went forth on the ships, striking drums, blowing trumpets, and whistling on pipes, and the prince himself went after them. The Bulgars walked along the bank and saw their kinsfolk being led away: some their fathers, others their sons and daughters, some their brothers and sisters and fellow tribesmen; and the Bulgars halted, bowing their heads, grieving in their hearts, and closing their eyes.

Svyatoslav passed the landing places and halted at the mouth of the Kama; and there Voislav Dobrynevich came to him with warriors from Rostov and Ustyug, with many captives and great plunder; they had been sent on ahead earlier and had gone down to wage war along the Kama; and along the Kama they had taken many towns and several villages, and burned them all, and cut down the people, and led others away captive.

From there Svyatoslav sent word ahead to his brother, Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich.

Svyatoslav reached Gorodets with his army, and they came out of the ships and galloped on horses toward the city of Vladimir.
