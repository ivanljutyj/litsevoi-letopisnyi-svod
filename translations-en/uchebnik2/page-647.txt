And when they approached Smolensk, Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich sent a messenger into the city, saying: “If you do not open the city gates and do not receive your lord Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk into his patrimony, the Grand Principality of Smolensk, then we shall stand long beneath the city walls and deliver all of you to the sword and fire. Decide for yourselves what you choose: death or life.”

Opinions among the people were divided: some rejoiced in their lord Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich, while others hated him. He, entering Smolensk in the month of August, killed the Poles, Vitovt’s voivodes. At that time the namestnik and voivode of Vitovt, Prince Roman Mikhailovich of Bryansk, ruled in the city; the prince killed him too, and killed the Bryansk and Smolensk boyars who opposed him, but he released the princess, Prince Roman’s wife, and his children.

And so Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk took his seat in the Grand Principality of Smolensk, in his patrimony and hereditary domain.

And Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan with the other princes and with his army went into Lithuania, fought there, and returned with a great number of captives.

In that same year peace and agreement were concluded between the Novgorodians and Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, while Anfal Nikitich and Gerasim Rasstriga with the army of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich set out beyond the Volok to the Dvina; on St Peter’s Day they took part in a battle, seized captives, plundered churches and monasteries, killed many Christians, and hanged others on trees.

And they captured the Dvina posadniks Andrei Ivanovich, Osip Filippovich, Naum Ivanovich, and Dmitry Rostislavich and led them away with many other captives.

Then the Novgorod voivodes Stepan Ivanovich, his brother Mikhail, and Nikita Golovnya, having gathered the Vazhane, overtook Anfal and Gerasim Rasstriga, fought them at Kholmogory, and recovered from them their Novgorod boyars: Andrei, Osip, Naum, and Dmitry.

And at the same time Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent his boyars with an army of three hundred men to Torzhok Alexandrov.

And they captured Semyon Vasilyevich, the son of the posadnik, and Mikhail Feofanovich; they took their lives, plundered churches, cut down people, committed much evil, and returned.

In that same year, in the month of August, on the night before the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God, from midnight until dawn blood-red pillars were visible at the ends; it was terrifying to see.
