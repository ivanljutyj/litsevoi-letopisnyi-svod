In the same year Vyacheslav, son of Vladimir Monomakh, began to rejoice when he heard that the Olegovichi had been scattered. And relying on his seniority, he heeded his boyars and did not show honor to his nephew Izyaslav Mstislavich, and took back his cities that Grand Prince Vsevolod Olegovich of Kiev had taken from him, and not only those cities but also occupied Vladimir with all Volhynia, and installed in it his nephew Prince Vladimir Andreevich.

But Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev sent his brother Rostislav and took the cities from him, and bound his governors in iron fetters; he also took the city of Turov, bound its tysyatsky in iron fetters, and installed his son Yaroslav in it.

In the same year Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev went to the Davydovichi for a council against Svyatoslav Olegovich; and he sent Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, his brother Prince Vladimir Davydovich, and his son Mstislav with a great host against Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich.

And having come to Novgorod Seversky, they wrought much evil around Novgorod Seversky. Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich sent to them asking for peace; but they said: “You have lost your brother Prince Igor Olegovich, and you will be at peace and in unity with us.” He replied: “It is better for me to accept death than to renounce my brother Prince Igor Olegovich; while I have a soul in my body, I cannot lose him.”

They went and plundered Igor’s village, in which there was much wealth of every kind, and set fire to his courtyard and threshing floor, on which were a thousand great stacks, and they plundered, burned, and laid all waste.

And from there they sent to meet Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev, while they themselves went to Putivl, the city of Svyatoslav Olegovich; and the people of Putivl did not surrender to them.

Only when Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev came to them did the people of Putivl surrender. And he installed his Kiev governors over them, and all the property of Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich that was in Putivl they plundered. And word came to Grand Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich that his city of Putivl had been taken, and all his property had been plundered, and that they all wanted to go after him to Novgorod Seversky, and besiege him in Novgorod Seversky, and having taken him, blind him and put him to an evil death.

He quickly fled to the city of Karachev, and soon Prince Izyaslav, son of Davyd, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of great Vladimir, set out in pursuit after him with fourteen thousand warriors, wishing to seize him and all who were with him.
