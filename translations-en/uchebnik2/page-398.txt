In the same year Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich of Tver had a disagreement with Princess Anastasia and with Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich.

His boyars and noble servants began to seize their volosts, and a great threat hung over them. Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich could not endure this and went from Tver to Grand Prince Simeon Ivanovich in Moscow. In the same year Grand Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich of Tver went to the Horde to Tsar Jani Beg, son of Uzbeg.

In the same year Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich also went from Moscow to the Horde.

In the same year Grand Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich of Tver reposed in the Horde, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, descendant of Vladimir Monomakh, descendant of Vsevolod, descendant of Yaroslav Vladimirovich.

In the same year Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, sent his tribute collectors from Kashin to the appanage of Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich at Kholm. And they took tribute from the people in Kholm, and went to the Horde to Tsar Jani Beg, son of Uzbeg. But in the Horde there was already his nephew, Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich of Kholm. When Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich learned that his uncle, Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver, had taken tribute in his patrimony, he was offended and went from the Tsar out of the Horde with an envoy to meet him; at Bezdezh he met his uncle, Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin, and plundered him. When Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin learned that Tsar Jani Beg had given Vsevolod Alexandrovich of Kholm and Tver the whole principality of Tver, he was greatly grieved by this.

In the year 6855 from Adam (the year 1355 from Christ)

Magnus, King of Sweden, sent his envoys, monks and presbyters, to Novgorod the Great to the prince with these words: “Command that a council be assembled in the place you yourselves designate, and summon your philosophers to this council, and I will gather my philosophers and send them to this council in the same place. Let them dispute about the faith according to Scripture. And let them determine which faith is better. If your faith is truly right, then I will convert to your faith. If our faith is right and true according to Scripture, then convert to our faith without doubt. And we shall all be of one mind, in peace, in spiritual and bodily love and in every good counsel, and all together we shall be fearsome to our enemies. If you do not wish to do so, then I will go against you with all my strength and army.”

Archbishop Vasily of Novgorod answered Magnus, King of Sweden, thus: “If you wish to know which faith is better, which is right and true...”
