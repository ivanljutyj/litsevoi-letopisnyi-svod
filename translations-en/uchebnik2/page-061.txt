In the morning Izyaslav came up to Yaroslavl and, passing it by, halted for the midday meal; there the king sent to him his voivode with a regiment, more than a thousand men.

Izyaslav dined there and then went with his regiments to the king. When he drew near his camps, the king mounted his horse with his men and rode out joyfully to meet Izyaslav; and then they embraced with great love, and having arrived, sat with the king in the tent and began to take counsel how early the next day to go to battle at the San River.

Early in the morning the king, beating the drums and drawing up his regiments, set out; and sending to Izyaslav, he said: "Go with your regiments alongside me, and where I stand, there stand you also; then in all things our common cause will prosper." Izyaslav said to the king: "Son, so be it."

And they went to the San River below the city of Peremyshl, and there stood with all their regiments; Vladimir stood on this side with his regiments. The king had seventy-three regiments, besides Izyaslav's regiments.

And having entered Vladimir's Galician land, they halted. It was then Sunday, and the king, according to his custom, did not wish to go anywhere on Sunday, while Vladimir would not allow the army to enter his land; he met and seized the king's foragers.

In the morning the king rose and set out, while Vladimir retreated back beyond the mountain and halted; and so the king began to station his regiments at the fords, while the Galicians stationed their regiments opposite them and could not stand against the king.

Vladimir, having come, stood with his regiment, and the king stood opposite him, while Izyaslav with his regiments went up the river to the fords; and having come, he stood above the king at another ford, and many other Hungarian regiments went past Izyaslav higher up to the fords. Izyaslav saw this and said to his druzhina: "Brothers and druzhina! God has never left the Russian land and Russian sons in dishonor; in all places they have preserved their honor; now, brothers, let us stand for this in these lands and before these foreign peoples; may God grant us to preserve our honor." And Izyaslav said this to his druzhina, and rushed with all his regiments into the ford; the king saw Izyaslav's regiments and likewise ordered his own regiments; but they did not stir, because Vladimir had sent out to them much of his wealth. The king said: "I cannot kill him, for he entreats me and repents of his guilt; if he now kisses the cross and submits, then as God grants between me and him: either I shall be in the Hungarian land, or he in the Galician."

Vladimir sent to Izyaslav, saying: "Brother! I bow to you and repent of my guilt, because I am guilty; now, brother, receive me to yourself and forgive me, and compel the king to receive me; and may God grant me to be with you."
