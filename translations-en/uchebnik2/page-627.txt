In that same year Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow sent his boyars, headed by Andrei Alberdov, with a retinue beyond Volok, to the Novgorodian towns on the Dvina and to the whole Dvina land, proposing that they come over to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow and withdraw from Novgorod, promising that “the Grand Prince will be able to stand up for you and defend you from Novgorod.” The Dvinians, Ivan Nikitin and all the Dvina boyars, went over to the side of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich and on that kissed the cross to the Grand Prince.

And the Grand Prince took from Novgorod Volok Lamsky with its districts, and Torzhok with its districts, and Bezhetsky Verkh. Then he renounced his cross-kissing to Novgorod and returned the cross charter, and the Novgorodians renounced their cross-kissing toward the Grand Prince and returned his cross charter to him. After this Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, sent his steward Klementy to Novgorod the Great to his son Vladyka Ioann. When he arrived, he began to speak thus: “Lord Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, blesses you, his son Ioann, Archbishop of Novgorod, and commands you to come to him in Moscow for a council concerning hierarchical matters.” And Vladyka Ioann sent the Metropolitan’s steward Klementy back to Moscow, and himself began to prepare to go to Moscow. And the Novgorodian posadniks, tysyatskys, and boyars, and all Novgorod the Great with tears petitioned their lord Vladyka Ioann, saying: “Convey, lord, to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich our blessing and put in a good word for us, your children, for Novgorod the Great.” And Vladyka Ioann promised them this, and the Novgorodians sent their ambassadors with him: posadnik Bogdan Avvakumovich, Kirill Dmitrievich, and men of substance.

And Vladyka Ioann of Novgorod came to Moscow to his spiritual father Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, and received from him blessing and honors. And Vladyka Ioann went to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, blessed him and conveyed to him a good word from Novgorod, and the Novgorodian ambassadors conveyed the petition from Novgorod.
