Then Olgerd understood that Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich had not managed to gather a large army, and was himself sitting besieged in the city of Moscow. Then Olgerd began to advance, pressed on toward Moscow, and, arriving there, took his stand before Moscow.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich then ordered the posad outside Moscow to be burned, and he himself shut himself in the city with his spiritual father, the most reverend and blessed Alexius, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, and with his cousin Prince Vladimir Andreevich, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Daniel, great-great-grandson of the blessed Alexander, and with all the boyars and people.

Olgerd stood before Moscow for three days and did not take the city, but he wrought much harm, burned much, took many people captive and led them away with him, and seized all the livestock as well. This happened because of our sins, and never before had so much harm been done to Moscow by Lithuania, although they had seen much harm from the Tatars; but from Lithuania they saw only this wretched and ruinous evil.

Forty-one years passed from Fedorchuk’s campaign to Olgerd’s attack.

In that same winter Prince Vladimir Andreevich of Moscow, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Daniel, went to the aid of the Pskovians and remained there from the Sbor to St Peter’s Day.

In that same winter Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich renounced Gorodets and all the appanages of Prince Semyon in favor of Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, and Prince Eremey was released to Tver.

In that same winter Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich of Kashin, grandson of Mikhail, great-grandson of Yaroslav, in Kashin ordered the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God to be moved from the monastery into the town, and that holy place to be dug up; then the bones of dead monks, long buried there, were disturbed in their coffins.

And in that same spring Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich of Kashin, grandson of Mikhail, great-grandson of Yaroslav, fell ill with an unknown illness, and his princess also fell gravely ill. God had mercy on him, but his princess Vasilisa departed this life on the twentieth day of April.

And Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich was filled with fear and dread, and began to beg forgiveness from Vladyka Vasily and the whole sacred council. Vladyka Vasily commanded him not to lay waste that place utterly, and, having heeded the Vladyka’s counsel, the prince built on that place a small church in the name of the Most Pure Mother of God; later it was dismantled.

In that same year there was a great famine in Tver.

In that same year the Germans came with an army to Izborsk with a bishop, masters, and commendators. The Pskovians gathered an army against them, and the Germans, not having taken the city, went away home.
