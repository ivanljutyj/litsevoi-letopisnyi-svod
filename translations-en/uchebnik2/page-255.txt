And there the Tatars surrounded them, and there was a very fierce battle, and the Tatars drove them back to the city fortifications.

And there they killed Prince Roman Ingvarevich of Ryazan; and they killed Eremey Glebovich, the commander of Vsevolod Yuryevich; and they killed many other people. But Prince Vsevolod fled to Vladimir with a small retinue.

The Tatars went to Moscow; and they came and took Moscow; and they killed its commander, Filipp Nyanko; and they captured alive Vladimir, the son of Grand Prince Yuri.

And the Tatars killed all the people, from the old to infants, and some they led away captive, and having gathered much property, they departed.

Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich, learning of this, wept much with Bishop Mitrofan, and with his princess, and with his children, and with his boyars, shedding many tears. And the grand prince entered the church, and crossed himself before the holy icons, and kissed Bishop Mitrofan, and his princess, and his children, and his boyars; and there was great weeping in the city; and no one could hear what they said to one another amid tears and sobbing.

And Yuri left in his place in Vladimir, together with the bishop, his children Vsevolod and Mstislav, and his commander Pyotr Oslyadyukovich; while Yuri himself went beyond the Volga with his nephews: with Vasilko Konstantinovich, and with Vladimir Konstantinovich, and with Vsevolod Konstantinovich.

And they encamped on the Siti River. And Yuri awaited his brothers: Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, with their troops; and he himself began to gather troops; and he appointed Zhiroslav Mikhailovich as commander.

At that time the Tatars came to Vladimir on the third day of February, on Tuesday, before Meatfare Week, on the feast day of Saint Simeon the God-Receiver. And the inhabitants of Vladimir, with their princes and with the commander Pyotr Oslyadyukovich, shut themselves up in the city.

The Tatars approached the Golden Gate, bringing with them the young prince Vladimir, son of Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich; and they began to ask, saying: “Is Grand Prince Yuri in the city?” The people of Vladimir began shooting at them. They said: “Do not shoot,” and came closer to the gate, and showed them their young prince, Prince Vladimir, son of Yuri, with the words: “Do you recognize this son of your prince?”

The prince’s son had a sorrowful face and was utterly weakened from hunger. And Vsevolod and Mstislav stood on the Golden Gate and recognized their brother Vladimir. Oh, how pitiful it was to see a brother! How could one not shed tears! Both Vsevolod and Mstislav with their boyars, and all the townspeople wept as they looked at Vladimir.
