RUS (1122–1159 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 1

In the year 6633 from Adam (1133 from Christ)

And after him his eldest son, Mstislav, sat in Kiev on the great princely throne. Still young, he fought at Koloksha with his uncle, Oleg Svyatoslavich, grandson of Yaroslav.

Because Svyatoslav, the elder brother of Vsevolod, Mstislav’s grandfather, himself wished to sit on the great reign; and Mstislav overcame him and drove him to Tmutorokan, and from there to Ryazan. And Yaropolk, son of Vladimir Monomakh, brother of Mstislav, went to Pereyaslavl.

After this the Polovtsians heard that Prince Vladimir Monomakh had died, and quickly came with the accursed Torks, wishing with them to make war on the Russian land; but God turned their plot into folly, and they succeeded in nothing.

For Yaropolk had word of it, and that night he drove the Torks into the city. And when the Polovtsians turned back, Prince Yaropolk, strengthened by God’s help, did not wait for other help either from his brother or from anyone; but with his Pereyaslavlians alone he overtook them at Steny; and the Polovtsians turned back and went, armed, to meet him. Then the right-believing Prince Yaropolk, calling on God’s name and remembering his father, dared with his retinue, and defeated the heathen by the power of the Precious Cross. And so many were slain, and others drowned in the rivers.

And so Prince Yaropolk returned, praising and glorifying the Lord God for such divine help. In the same year there was a great storm with hail in Novgorod, and it tore apart the buildings, and drove herds of cattle into the Volkhov; some drowned, and others were caught barely alive.

In the same year they finished painting the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God in Novgorod, in the Antoniev Monastery. In the same year the Novgorodians seated Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich on the throne in Novgorod, on the 19th day of May.
