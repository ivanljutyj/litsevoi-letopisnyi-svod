In the same year, Tatars from the Volga Horde of Mamai came raiding to Nizhny Novgorod. The prince was not in the city at that time, all the people fled, and the townspeople abandoned the city and ran beyond the Volga.

And at that time Prince Dmitry came from Gorodets, and seeing that the city had been taken by the Tatars, he sent them a ransom for the city. But they did not take the ransom; instead they burned the city. There burned, for the sins of men, the church of the Holy Saviour and all its treasures, and there burned its doors, skillfully made of gilded copper.

And from there the Tatars went forth fighting, captured many prisoners, and conquered the Berezovoe Field and the whole district.

In the same year, Mamai, prince of the Volga Horde, sent Prince Bigich with an army against Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow.

The grand prince gathered his army and went against the Tatars into the land of Ryazan, beyond the Oka. The Russian army met the Tatars at the Vozha River in the land of Ryazan, and they stood facing one another, separated by the Vozha River.

Before long the Tatars crossed the Vozha, galloped at a trot, and with a battle cry charged Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

He with his warriors stood firmly against them, and Prince Andrei Polotsky struck them from one side, and Prince Danilo Pronsky from the other, while Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich himself struck them from the front.

And in that same hour the Tatars fled beyond the Vozha River, throwing away their spears.

And our warriors pursued them, and they beat the Tatars, cut them down, stabbed them, cleft them in two; a great multitude of Tatars was killed, and others drowned in the river.

This battle took place in the evening; the Tatars fled all night, and in the morning a thick mist descended.

And before noon, or a little after, our men set out after them and went far.

And they found in the field their overturned dwellings, tents, nomadic wagons, yurts and alachugs, and carts, and in them much property.

And taking all the Tatars’ spoil, they returned home with plunder and joy.

Then Dmitry Monastyrev and Nazar Daniilov, son of Kusakov, were killed by the Tatars.

And of Mamai’s princes there were killed then Khazibey, Kovergui, Karabaluk, Kostrov, and Bigich. This battle took place on the eleventh day of the month of August, on the commemoration of the holy martyr Euplus the deacon, on Wednesday late in the evening.

Then too, during that battle with Bigich at the Vozha, they captured a certain priest, Ivan Vasilyevich, a tysyatsky, who had come from the Horde.
