Forgive me, holy hierarch of Christ!” Photius was amazed at what had happened to him, forgave him, blessed him, and commanded him to pray to Christ God for his sins with pure thoughts and a humble heart, to fast and to give alms. This man fulfilled everything and recovered by the grace of Christ. His name was Thomas Lazarev.

In the same year Vytautas Keistutovich gathered the bishops of his land and said to them: “Choose whom you wish as metropolitan in Kiev, and let him go to Tsargrad to the patriarch, so that he may appoint him metropolitan of Kiev.” And they chose Gregory Tsamblak, who was a Bulgarian by birth. Some bishops did not want to choose him, saying: “It is better for us to submit to Photius.” But Vytautas threatened them with his authority. And they submitted.

And Vytautas sent to Tsargrad to the patriarch complaints against Metropolitan Photius, and sent Gregory Tsamblak there also, so that he might be appointed metropolitan in Kiev. “There is no other way,” he said, “to avoid oppression from Photius, except to appoint another metropolitan in Kiev.” The prince also sent his charters to the emperor.

The emperor and the patriarch learned of this, but did not wish to agree. Then Vytautas ordered his bishops to list the accusations against Metropolitan Photius and send them to Constantinople to the emperor and patriarch, so that they might agree to appoint Gregory Tsamblak as metropolitan in Kiev. The emperor and the patriarch did not agree to this.

In the same year the Novgorodians sent Yuri Antsifirovich to Vytautas to ask for peace. Vytautas agreed with them, ended the dispute, and concluded peace with them according to the old customs, but they did not break the peace treaty with the Germans. Yet Vytautas did not conclude a peace treaty with the Germans. In the same year Vytautas warred with the Germans and caused much harm to their land.

In the same year in Great Novgorod the monastery on Derevyanitsa burned, and the stone church of the Most Pure Mother of God burned, in which three people burned.

In the same year all Christians throughout the Russian land suffered a terrible disease, the bone-breaker. In the same year in Novgorod the Nerevsky End burned, from the church of Saint Vladimir to Gzen. Eight stone churches burned, and five wooden ones. In the same year there was a severe drought and terrible thunder.
