Seven weeks passed, and the forty days after the death of Metropolitan Theognostus had passed, when Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich died, grandson of Daniel, great-grandson of blessed Alexander, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-great-great-great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-great-great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, son of Yaroslav, son of Vladimir, on the 26th day of April. And he was laid in his patrimony in Moscow, in the Church of the Holy Archangel Michael. He reigned thirteen years, and he was thirty-eight years old in all.

In that same year Prince Andrey Ivanovich died, grandson of Daniel, great-grandson of Alexander, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, on the third day after the fortieth-day commemoration of his brother Semyon Ivanovich. And he was laid in Moscow in that same Church of the Holy Archangel Michael, on the 6th day of July.

In that same year, to the deceased Prince Andrey Ivanovich was born a son, Vladimir, on the fortieth-day commemoration of his father, Prince Andrey Ivanovich.

In that same year the princes went to the Horde to Tsar Janibek, son of Uzbeg, to contend for the grand principality of Vladimir: Prince Ivan Ivanovich, grandson of Daniel, and Prince Konstantin Vasilyevich of Suzdal.

Then the Novgorodians also sent their envoy Semyon Sudakov to the Horde to Tsar Janibek, asking the grand principality of Vladimir for Prince Konstantin Vasilyevich of Suzdal. The tsar gave the grand principality of Vladimir to Prince Ivan Ivanovich, brother of Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich. The Novgorodians then remained in discord for a year and a half, but nothing evil happened.

In that same year envoys came from Constantinople, sent by Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich and the most reverend Theognostus, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus: Demetrius Davydovich and Yuri Vorobyov, and the metropolitan’s envoys Artemy Korobyin and Mikhail Shchebraty, sent to the blessed and sweet-speaking Philotheus, Ecumenical Patriarch, and to his spiritual son, Tsar John Kantakouzenos, and they brought from Constantinople from the patriarch and the tsar an ordinance that Alexei should be Vladyka of Vladimir and go to Constantinople to the patriarch to be consecrated by him as metropolitan. In that same year, in Peter’s Fast, on the 22nd day of July, the Ryazanians took Lopasnya.
