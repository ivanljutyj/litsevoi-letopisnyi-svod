And they seized their three princes, the Igoreviches, Prince Roman Igorevich and his brothers, and beat them much, and leading them through the streets, beat them, and hanged them all with their wives and children.

In the same year Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich fell ill and sent to Rostov for his eldest son, Prince Konstantin, having during his lifetime given him the great principality of Vladimir, and Rostov to his other son, Prince Yuri.

But Prince Konstantin, son of Vsevolod Yuryevich, did not go to Vladimir to his father, but sent to him, saying: “O wondrous and beloved father! Do not be angry with me, unworthy, for that which I wish to ask of you. Since you have loved me more than others and as the eldest son wish to set me as chief, then give me the old and ancient city of Rostov and Vladimir with it. If your honor does not wish to do so, then give me Vladimir and Rostov with it. Do not reject my entreaty, father, if this is pleasing to you.”

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich sent for him a second time, that he should come to him; but he did not go.

And a third time he sent to him, that he should come to him in Vladimir; but he did not obey his father and did not go to him, saying: “Let him give me Vladimir also, with Rostov.” And Grand Prince Vsevolod did not bless him.

Grand Prince Vsevolod summoned all his boyars from the cities and volosts, and Bishop John, and the hegumens, and the priests, and the merchants, and the nobles, and all the people, and gave after himself Vladimir to his son Yuri, and brought them all to the cross, and all the people kissed the cross to Yuri.

He entrusted to him also his brothers. But Konstantin, learning of this, knit his brows, being angry with his brothers, especially with Georgy and with all the counsellors who had devised such a thing, and there was much unrest and confusion from this, and many people departed to various places in turmoil.

In the same year Prince Mstislavich went with an army against the Chud called Torna, with the Novgorodians, and having taken many captives, returned to his own land.

In the same year Prince Mstislav Mstislavich went to the Chud city of Medvezhya Golova, laid waste the volosts and villages, and stood before the city, and took tribute from them.

In the same year in Novgorod there was slander against Bishop Mitrofan, because from the beginning of the world Satan has not wished good to the human race; he put envy into Cain against Abel, and Cain killed his brother Abel; likewise after them, from generation to generation, he brought much evil upon each through seditions and murders. So now too Satan rose up between Mitrofan, Bishop of Novgorod, and Prince Mstislav Mstislavich with the posadniks, and wrought much slander and evil counsel against him, and did not let him justify himself.
