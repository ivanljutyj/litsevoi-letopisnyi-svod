He issued a charter according to which, throughout his whole patriarchate, wherever he might go, he could serve and ordain bishops and presbyters, and teach, and baptize, and all must obey him in everything; after this the tsar and the patriarch sent the saint off with great honors and joy.

And he came to the land of his fatherland, and his relatives and all the people met him. His brother Stefan was then gravely ill.

Savva, having come to him, healed him by his prayer; the saint performed many other miracles, built churches, established a bishopric, appointed bishops in all the cities of his region, and began to teach all to care for the flock, and he crowned his brother with the royal crown and made him king, and brought with him from Tsargrad icon-painters and marble craftsmen, and founded a great cathedral church, painted it, and adorned it with marble, and many other churches.

And the unbaptized who lived in heresy he ordered to be baptized, and those who had been baptized but afterwards fell into the Latin heresy, after reading the prayer and anointing with myrrh, he joined to the faithful; but those who did not submit, even if they were in orders, he ordered his brother King Stefan to expel from the fatherland with disgrace, so that they might not disturb the rest by the beauty of their speech; by this he did not allow the thorns to grow together with the vine.

The Hungarian king heard of this and was enraged at the election of Despot Stefan to the kingdom, because he had made himself like a king by birth, and he set out against him, wishing to force him to submit to himself. Archbishop Savva, at the request of his brother Stefan, went out to meet him and for a long time urged him to incline to peace, but did not persuade him; and that day there was intense heat.

The saint sent to ask him for ice, and it was not found in all the land; the saint prayed, and heavy hail fell around the place where he had encamped, and, having thanked God, he himself tasted it and, putting hailstones into wine, quenched his thirst, and he put hail stones in a large silver platter and sent it to the king, and ordered everyone to take a sufficient quantity. The king, seeing this, was seized with fear and, by the saint’s teaching, abandoned the Latin heresy, accepted the Orthodox faith, and concluded an eternal peace with his brother the king; and he himself, having lived long enough, died, and after his death miracles occurred from his grave.

Then his brother Stefan became very ill and begged the saint to allow him to be clothed in the monastic habit; but the saint did not permit it for the time and left for his bishopric, and Stefan sent to him with tears, saying that he no longer hoped to see him.

Saint Savva returned and, finding his brother already dead, grieved greatly over this, prayed much with tears, and resurrected his brother, and clothed him in the monastic habit.
