On the third day, unrest and rebellion began in the city of Vladimir among both noble and common Vladimirians; all gathered with weapons in the prince’s courtyard, saying: “Lord prince! We lay down our heads and shed our blood for you, protecting you and wishing you well; but you associate freely with your and our enemies, with Prince Gleb of Ryazan and his son Roman, and with his brother-in-law Prince Mstislav Rostislavich. And when these favorites of yours and their friends—your and our enemies, the Suzdalians and Rostovians, of whom there is a great multitude both in the city of Vladimir and everywhere outside the city—rise up and begin a revolt, then the last deception will be worse than the first. Blind them or put them in prison.”

The great prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, being merciful, did not wish to do this, but because of the people’s agitation and rebellion he ordered them imprisoned, and sent to Ryazan, saying: “Hand over to me my enemy, Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich, the brother-in-law of Prince Gleb; if you do not do so, I will march against you with a great host.”

The Ryazanians said among themselves: “Because of these Rostislavich princes, disaster and ruin will come upon our princes too; let us go to Voronezh and seize it.” Then Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich fled to Voronezh, and so he moved from city to city, not knowing where to go from his many sorrows and grief.

And they went to Voronezh, captured him, and brought him to Vladimir to Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich; he ordered him to be taken from them, and he was imprisoned with the others.

Then all the people rose up again, shouting and crying: “We want to blind them.” The great prince Vsevolod Yuryevich was greatly grieved by this, since he was merciful, but he could not restrain the people.

They came, seized the prison, and taking Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich, blinded them and released them. They went away with festering eyes, and came to Smolensk, entered the church of the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb at Smyadyn, and there regained their sight on the fifth day of the month of September, on the commemoration of the murder of Prince Gleb.

Prince Gleb of Ryazan died in prison, and his son Prince Roman, after being forced to swear by kissing the cross and being humbled, was released to Ryazan.

In that same winter, Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich went to Novgorod; the Novgorodians received them with honors and great joy and enthroned Prince Mstislav Rostislavich in Novgorod, and gave his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich Torzhok, and gave Prince Yaroslav, Vsevolod’s nephew, Volok Lamsky.

In the same year, Prince Roman Glebovich of Ryazan defeated many Polovtsians.
