There was a great sedition in Novgorod, and the people were in turmoil here and there, and they did not want Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich among them in Novgorod, and many began to flee to Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich in Pskov, and as they fled they plundered homes and many villages. Then fled Konstantin Nezhatin, and Ivanko Dobrynin, and Timoshka Yakshich, and Nester Petrilov, and many others.

Svyatoslav Olegovich began to interrogate the Novgorodian boyars, and having extracted information, he executed some, shut others in dungeons, and bound others by the kissing of the cross.

Also afterward Svyatoslav Olegovich gathered all the Novgorod land, and his brother Gleb, and the host of the city of Kursk, and the Polovtsians; and they went against Pskov to drive out Vsevolod.

The Pskovians did not submit to Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich, and did not drive Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich from among them, and they stood firm and were not afraid, because news had come to them beforehand that Svyatoslav Olegovich was coming against them with great force and with his brother Gleb and with the Polovtsians; and they made abatis and blocked the roads.

Prince Svyatoslav stood awhile in perplexity and said: “It is better to trust in God than to shed blood with his brothers; for all things are possible with God, and He will direct as He wills”; and he returned to Novgorod, and each went his own way.

In the same year Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, died in Pskov, on the eleventh day of February, in Cheese-fare week, on Thursday; and they laid him in the Church of the Holy Trinity, which he himself had built. The Pskovians stood up for his brother Svyatopolk; and there was no peace with the Suzdalians, nor with the Smolenskians, nor with the Polotskians, nor with the Kievans; and in those quarrels there came a great famine throughout the land, and captivity, and incessant bloodshed from the pagan Polovtsians, and from the Poles, and from the Chud; and the land grew desolate in emptiness, according to the word of Christ, who said in His Gospel: “Every kingdom divided against itself shall be made desolate, and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand.”

In the same year the Polovtsians warred against Kursk, and took the whole land captive and burned it. In the same year a great host of Poles gathered and warred against Rus, and wrought much evil.

In the same year the blessed Princess Euphemia, wife of Prince Vladimir Monomakh, died, and was laid in Berestovo in the Church of the Holy Savior. In the same year the Novgorodians drove from Novgorod their prince Svyatoslav, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, who had sat on the throne in Novgorod for two years and five months.
