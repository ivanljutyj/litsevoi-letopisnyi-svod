And so the monk Peresvet, a novice of the venerable abbot Sergius, went against the Tatar champion Temir-Murza, and they struck hard, so loudly and powerfully that the earth trembled, and both fell to the ground dead, and there both met their end; their horses also fell dead at that same hour.

And already the seventh hour had come. The grand prince said to all his princes and boyars and to all the host: “Brothers, the time has come for us to drink the cup. Let this place be our grave for the name of Christ, and for the Christian faith, and for all Orthodox Christendom.”

And so both great forces came together in battle. And the fight was fierce and the slaughter very cruel, and blood flowed like water, and a countless multitude of dead fell from both forces, Tatar and Russian, and Tatar bodies fell upon Christian, and Christian bodies upon Tatar, and Tatar blood mingled with Christian. Everywhere lay a multitude of dead.

And horses could not step over the dead. Not only did they kill with weapons, but they struck one another, and died beneath horses’ hooves, and suffocated from the great crowding, for it was impossible to fit on the Kulikovo field between the Don and the Mecha because of the great multitude of assembled forces.

And then the great Russian foot host broke like trees and lay like mown hay. And it was very terrible to see. But still the Christian host fought without weakening. Yet for our sins God permitted it, and the Tatars began to prevail, and already many of the noble great princes and boyars and commanders bowed to the earth like trees.

And already the great prince Dmitry Ivanovich himself was knocked from his horse. He mounted another horse. The Tatars knocked him from that horse too and wounded him. With great difficulty he barely left the battlefield into the oak grove and came under a newly cut tree, many-branched and many-leaved, and there, hiding himself, he lay on the ground. The Tatars began to prevail. And they cut down the great banner of the grand prince, and killed his beloved confidant Mikhail Andreevich Brenk, and killed many princes, boyars, commanders, and countless servants.

And already the eighth hour had passed, and the ninth hour had come, and everywhere the Tatars were prevailing. Then Prince Vladimir Andreevich, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Daniel, great-great-grandson of Alexander, cousin of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, standing in the oak grove with the hidden western regiment and with the wise and valiant commander Dmitry Bobrok Volynets, weeping greatly, could see the Christian host beaten and a few others still wandering over the battlefield.
