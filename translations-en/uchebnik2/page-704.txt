That same winter, Prince Daniel Borisovich of Nizhny Novgorod, together with his brother Prince Ivan, fled from Moscow from Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich.

On the Taking of the City of Kremenets

That same winter, Prince Dashko, son of Feodor Ostrozhsky, captured the city of Kremenets, which belonged to Prince Vytautas, by the following stratagem: on Maundy Thursday he sent two of his men, Dmitry and Ilya, ahead of him, saying to them: “You will go to serve Kondrat Prus, the voivode of Kremenets. And when I approach the city, you shall unlock the drawbridge and lower the bridge.” They came to the voivode of Kremenets and bowed low to him. He received them warmly; they began craftily to win his trust, but he did not understand their cunning—such a thought never even entered his mind. And Prince Dashko came to the city at the appointed time, on the night of Holy Week, with five hundred warriors. And his advisers, whom he had sent ahead to serve, lowered the drawbridge and laid down the bridge.

And in this way Prince Dashko Feodorovich entered the city with his army, killed Voivode Kondrat, cut down the king’s and Vytautas’s officers, and freed Prince Svidrigailo from the iron fetters in which he had spent eight and a half years.

And from there Svidrigailo set out for the Hungarian land, captured Lutsk on the way, and took one hundred and fifty horses from the Volhynian boyars.

Omen

That same year in Novgorod there was a sign in the church from the icon of the Honorable and Glorious Protection of the Most Pure Mother of God: it was as if blood flowed on both sides of the icon’s cover. This sign was fulfilled in the month of April: civil war began in Novgorod; there was much bloodshed and killing, and with difficulty did Archbishop Simeon calm everyone.

That same year Mikhailo Rossokhin killed Anfal and his son Nestor in Vyatka.

That same year, on the 20th day of the month of June, Bishop Dionysius was installed in Rostov.
