After the princely death the princess gave 100 grivnas of silver and 50 of gold; and after her own death the princess gave villages with servants and all.

In the same year died Prince Boris, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, on the second day of May, on the memory of Saint Epiphanius, Metropolitan of Cyprus, and he was laid in the church of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb, which his father, Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky, had built on the river Nerl, where the encampment of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb had been.

In the same year Rogvolod Borisovich went from Svyatoslav Olegovich to seek a volost for himself, taking Svyatoslav’s regiment, because his brothers had not shown him mercy and had taken his volost and all his property; coming to Lutsk, he began to communicate with the Druchians. The Druchians were glad of him, and coming to him, they invited him to themselves, saying: “Come, prince, do not delay; we are glad for you. If there is war, we and our children will fight for you.”

And more than three hundred men of the Druchians and Polotskians came out to meet him; and he entered the city with great honor, and the people were glad for him; and they drove out Gleb Rostislavich, and the townspeople plundered his court, and took all the property of his druzhina.

And Gleb came to his father in Polotsk, and in that city there was a great tumult, for many wanted Rogvolod; with difficulty Rostislav calmed the people, gave them many gifts, and brought them to the cross; and he himself went with Vsevolod and with Volodar and with all his brothers against Rogvolod to Drutsk. Rogvolod shut himself up in the city, and they fought hard, and many fell on both sides. The Druchians reviled them with much mockery. Rostislav made peace with Rogvolod and granted Rogvolod additional volosts; and Rostislav returned home with his brothers.

The Polotskians then formed an evil counsel against their prince, against Rostislav Glebovich, and broke their cross-kissing oath to him, for they had kissed the cross to him: “Since you are our prince, may God grant us to live with you, and to have no harm toward you according to the cross-kissing oath.” And they broke what they had said.

And they sent secretly to Rogvolod Borisovich of Drutsk, saying to him: “Our prince! We have sinned before you, in that we rose against you without cause and plundered your property and the property of your druzhina, and handed you over to the Gleboviches into great distress; but if you do not hold against us what we did in our folly, and if you kiss the cross to us, then we are your people and you are our prince; and Rostislav, seizing him, we will give into your hands, and do with him as you wish.”

Rostislav learned of their conspiracy and went from Polotsk to Minsk; and Rogvolod entered Polotsk, and they received him with honor.

In that year Ivan Berladnik went from Izyaslav into the field to the Polovtsians, and with the Polovtsians encamped before the Danube cities, and there on the Danube he captured two vessels and took much goods in them.
