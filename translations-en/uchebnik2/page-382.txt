The Vladyka of Novgorod, Vasily, went to Moscow to Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich with honor.

In that year Prince Feodor of Galich died. In the same year Prince Feodor Alexandrovich of Tver, grandson of Mikhail, great-grandson of Yaroslav, came from the Horde from Tsar Azbyak, and with him the ambassador Avdul.

In the year 6844 from Adam (1344 from Christ)

Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich Kalita went to the Horde to Tsar Azbyak. In that same winter he came from the Horde from Tsar Azbyak with grants and honor to his patrimony.

In that year Anthony, Bishop of Rostov, died. In that year the Most Reverend Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, appointed Gabriel as bishop in Rostov.

In that year Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver went from Pskov to Tver, taking his son Prince Feodor, and again returned to Pskov.

In that year Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver, living in Pskov, began to grieve and sorrow, reasoning thus: “If I meet death here, what will become of my children? It is known to all that I fled from my principality, and all will know that I met death in a foreign place. And then my children will be deprived of their principality. It is better for me, for God’s sake, to die than to live in evil.” And he sent his boyars to Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, asking for blessing and prayers from him and from all the hierarchs, because he had decided to go to the tsar in the Horde. And having received blessing and prayers from Metropolitan Theognost and from all the Russian bishops, he began to prepare to go to the Horde with his boyars and servants.

In the year 6845 from Adam (1345 from Christ)

Prince Semyon Ivanovich was born, grandson of Daniil, son of Vasily. That same autumn there was a great flood.

That same autumn Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver went from Pskov to the Horde. Having come to Tsar Azbyak, he said to him: “My lord, free tsar, I have done you much evil, but I have come to you to receive death or life. As God instructs you, I am ready for anything. If, for God’s sake and by your royal majesty, you grant me mercy, I will thank God and your mercy. But if you put me to death, then I am worthy of death. And here is my head before you.” Tsar Azbyak marveled at the reasonableness of his words and his humility and said to his princes: “Do you see, princes, Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver, how by humility and wisdom he saved himself from death?” And all marveled. And Tsar Azbyak showed him great honor...
