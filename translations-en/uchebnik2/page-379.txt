In the year 6849 from Adam (the year 1349 from Christ)

The Most Reverend Theognostus, Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus, went to Novgorod the Great with a great multitude of church clergy and with many of his servants. And it was a heavy burden for the lord archbishop of Novgorod to support them, and there were many gifts for the monasteries.

That year Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich of Vladimir and Moscow sent his governors to Novgorod.

That same autumn Tsar Azbyak of the Horde died. He reigned in the Great Horde for 19 years. Under this Tsar Azbyak, by his command, five great Russian princes were killed in the Horde: Grand Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich of Tver, and his two sons, Grand Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich and his brother Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich, Grand Prince Vasily of Ryazan, Grand Prince Ivan Yaroslavich of Ryazan; and three appanage princes: Prince Alexander Novoselsky, Prince Fyodor Alexandrovich, grandson of the holy blessed Grand Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich of Tver, and Prince Fyodor Ivanovich of Starodub. Under this same Tsar Azbyak, the pious Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich of Moscow was killed in the Horde by Grand Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich of Tver, without the command of Tsar Azbyak. And in all, nine great and appanage princes were killed in the Horde by Tsar Azbyak.

And after him his eldest son, Tinbek, sat on the throne in the Horde.

And his other son Janibek killed his younger brother Khadyrbek. And for this his elder brother Tsar Tinbek became angry with him, and there was enmity between them.

That year Grand Prince Gedimin of Lithuania died. His sons: Narimant, Olgerd, Yevnutey, Keystutey, Koriad, Lyubort, Montivit. After him his second son Olgerd ascended the grand princely throne of Lithuania.

This Olgerd was very wise and spoke many languages, and surpassed all others in power and rank; he was temperate in all things and avoided all that is vain: amusements, games, and the like. But he cared for his realm day and night; he abhorred drunkenness and drank neither wine, nor mead, nor beer, nor other drinks, so greatly did he hate drunkenness. He had great temperance in all things, and therefore was a man of great intelligence and understanding, and courageous. And by his wisdom he conquered and acquired for himself many lands and countries, cities and principalities, and possessed great power. His principality rose above all others. Neither under his father nor under his grandfather had it been so.

That year Prince Ivan Ivanovich, grandson of David, married the daughter of Dmitry of Bryansk.
