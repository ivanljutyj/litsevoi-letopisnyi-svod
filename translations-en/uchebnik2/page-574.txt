…and extreme poverty. You see that we are poor and old, and in all things we have scarcity and extreme poverty. Your brothers have married and think how to please their wives. But you are unmarried and think how to please God. You have chosen the good path; this will not be taken from you. But we have now no one to serve us, no well-wishers, no relatives, no one at all. Therefore, look after us for a while, and for this you will receive a reward from the Lord.” He did so, serving and helping his parents, obeying them with humility and long-suffering.

Later, after some time, his parents were tonsured as monks and departed this life. The blessed one buried them with funeral hymns: for forty days he read memorial services, liturgies, and prayers over them. He kindly gave alms to the poor, honoring the memory of his parents, and returned to his house with his parents’ blessing, as though he had acquired a spiritual treasure.

Having come home, he began to free himself from the worldly cares of this age. He regarded the house and all the things in it as something unnecessary to him, recalling what is said: “Be still, and know that I am God.” And again it is written: “They counted all things as refuse, that they may gain Christ God.”

He also recalled what is said: “The life of this world is full of sighing and despondency; do not touch it.” The prophet said: “Come out from among them and be separate, and do not touch the uncleanness of this world”; and another prophet said: “Depart from the earth and ascend to heaven”; and David said: “My soul clings to Thee; Thy right hand has upheld me”; and again: “Lo, I have fled afar off and dwelt in the wilderness, waiting for God who saves me.” The Lord said in the Gospel: “If anyone wishes to follow Me, unless he renounces all that is in this world, he cannot be My disciple.” So, having strengthened soul and body with these words, he summons to himself Peter, his younger brother according to the flesh, and leaves to him his father’s inheritance and all that was in the house necessary for life. He himself took nothing, following the words of the divine apostle: “I count all things as refuse, that I may gain Christ.”

Stephen, his elder brother by birth, did not live long with his wife, and she died after bearing two sons, Clement and Ivan. This Ivan, Stephen’s son, was tonsured by his uncle, the venerable abbot Sergius, and was deemed worthy of the priestly order, and founded a monastery at the place called Simonovo, on the bank of the Moskva River, not far from the city. This will be told further on.

Stephen, Sergius’s elder brother, after the death of his wife, was tonsured as a monk in the Monastery of the Protection of the Most Pure Mother of God at Khotkovo.
