To Michael in Kyiv came false ambassadors from the godless Tsar Batu; but he guessed their treachery and ordered them killed.

The great prince Michael himself fled to the Hungarians with his household, and was there an exile in a foreign land, like Abraham or Jacob. Other Kyivans fled to distant lands, some hid in earthen caves, and some shut themselves in the city and there prayed to God with tears and repentance.

The godless Batu himself came to the city of Kyiv and made a great battle, and captured the city of Kyiv on the sixth day of December; and those remaining in the city he did not spare but killed; in Kyiv he took Dmitr the tysyatsky wounded and alive; and of those who hid in caves, in the mountains, and in the forests, few remained.

And Batu went into the Volhynian land and there captured cities: Kamenets, Vladimir, and Galich. The tysyatsky Dmitr, seeing that the Russian land was perishing from the impious, was tormented by pity because of this, and said to Batu: “You cannot, tsar, remain in the Russian land, for the land is strong: they may gather against you, and you will not be able to leave here; it is time for you to go to the Hungarians.” He said this not wishing Batu well, but giving help to the Russian land.

Batu listened to his advice and went to the Hungarians. Kings Bela and Koloman came out to meet him.

There was a great battle, and the Hungarians fled, and the Tatars pursued them to the Danube River. Then Batu remained there three years and reached Volodava with war.

The pagans returned to their land, having captured many countries, and many Christians were cruelly killed by them.

The Orthodox Christians remaining in the Russian cities the Tatars counted and took tribute from them.

The great prince Michael learned of this and then returned from the Hungarian land with all his people to Kyiv; and those of ours who had scattered to foreign lands came back to their land.

The Tatars began forcing the princes to travel to Batu and to the khan, saying to them: “You must not live on the land of Batu and the khan and not bow to them.” And many went and bowed to Batu.

Then the impious Batu, after the cruel devastation of the Russian land, also wished to conquer pious souls, and to defile the Most Pure Church, and to distort the holy Orthodox faith; and his foul faith, the leftover brand of the Persian lie, he wished to spread in Rus.
