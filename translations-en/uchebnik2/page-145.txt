In the same year Vladyka Ilya, Archbishop of Novgorod, with his brother Gavriil founded a stone church of St John the Baptist at the Marketplace.

In the year 6692 from Adam (1192 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich of Vladimir went to war against the Bulgars together with his nephew Prince Izyaslav Glebovich, grandson of Yury Dolgoruky and great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, and with Prince Mstislav Davidovich and Prince Vladimir Svyatoslavovich; he summoned to his aid the Ryazan and Murom princes: Prince Roman Glebovich of Ryazan and his brothers Prince Igor, Prince Vsevolod, and Prince Vladimir of Murom. They went by water in boats and along the shore on horses, and came into the land of the Bulgars, disembarked, and stood by a small town called Tugchin, and remained there two days; on the third day they approached their great city, while the guard detachments rode ahead.

From there they equipped a regiment of Belozertsy, whose commander was Foma Laskov, and sent them to the boats that stood on the water, to guard them from the Polovtsians.

And they themselves went into the Field and saw a huge regiment standing in the Field; they thought it was a Bulgar regiment, and five men came from that regiment, bowed before Grand Prince Vsevolod, and said: “The Polovtsians of Emmak bow to you, prince; they have come to the Bulgar princes to conquer the Bulgars, having heard that you too are going to war against them.”

Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich consulted with his brothers and confirmed a spiritual peace with them, he according to his faith and they according to theirs, and took them with him to their great city, and they stood there two days; on the third day they approached their great city, and the Cheremis came. On the very first day they arrayed the regiments; when the regiments were arrayed, Vsevolod Yuryevich with his brothers began to hold counsel, and then Prince Izyaslav Glebovich, grandson of Yury, hastening, took a spear and ran to the raft where the foot soldiers who had come out of the city were; the rafts and many other siege engines were already prepared. He drove them behind the rafts to the city gates, but he himself grew weary and broke his spear.

The Polovtsians struck him with an arrow beneath the heart through his armor; his own warriors caught him up—he could barely breathe—and carried him to the camp. Then the Bulgars suddenly came out and killed many Christians.

And the Bulgars from the city of Sobekul and from Chelmat set out in boats, and those from Torchesk on horses against Vsevolod’s boats. Vsevolod had left the Belozersk regiment by the boats.
