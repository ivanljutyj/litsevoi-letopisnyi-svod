Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, having summoned their nephews, Vladimir’s sons Gleb and Oleg, said to them: “You told me against your uncles, against Roman, Svyatoslav and Vsevolod (but since Vsevolod has departed this life, God will judge between me and him), that they wanted to betray me to the Olgovichi; and if you spoke truly against them, go and denounce them to their face.”

They went and began to denounce their uncles, alleging that they had indeed wanted to do this. The uncles began to swear and vow: “We do not know, we are not aware of this; Satan has commanded you to bring this slander against us. Let God judge between us and you. No one is immortal in this vain and fleeting life; we shall all die and receive the dread judgment before the Lord God, and we shall be terribly examined for the deeds we have done, and not only for deeds and words but also for thoughts. From all we, your uncles, have honour and glory, but from our own nephews — shame, mockery and cruel death.” Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich ordered them and all their boyars seized and sent them to Vladimir on the 22nd day of the month of September, on a Saturday.

And on Sunday, early in the morning, Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich with his sons and with the two Ryazan princes who had slandered their uncles, Gleb and Oleg, and with David of Murom, with the Novgorodians, with the Pskovians and with a great host, crossed the Oka River.

And the other army, the boat army, Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich sent down the Oka, and began to burn, take captive and cut down the Ryazan land.

And he went to the city of Pronsk. In Pronsk at that time the prince was Kir Mikhail Vsevolodich.

And he heard that their uncles had been captured with their boyars, slandered by their nephews, and that his father, Prince Vsevolod Glebovich, had died in Pronsk, and that Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich was coming against him to Pronsk with a great host and with the slanderers who had slandered their uncles. And hearing this, he was grieved and cried out in a loud voice, saying: “O woe to us foolish ones, we have ruined our land and our kin! How did all these accursed slanderers not take pity on their uncles? How were their wicked mouths opened? How did God’s punishment not fall upon them, as upon Cain and upon Svyatopolk? O woe to us! Our own have perished at the hands of our own!”

And he began to say to his boyars: “Tell me, what should I do? Nowhere in the commandments does the Lord teach us to surrender ourselves to calamities, but He teaches: if they persecute you out of this city, flee to another.” And setting out, he fled to Chernigov to his father-in-law, Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Oleg, great-great-grandson of Svyatoslav, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, forefather of the great Vladimir. At that time Vsevolod Chermny was reigning in Kiev.
