To Vologda, to the Grand Prince’s outpost, and there he captured the Grand Prince’s voivode Fyodor Mikhailovich Chelyadnya, Andrei Fyodorovich Goltyaev, Vladimir Andreevich Zvorykin, Mikhail Chepechkin, and many others; and he came to Kostroma, and sent to the Vyatchans, and the Vyatchans came to him. And Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, having learned of this, set out against him and, arriving, stood on the cape near the monastery of the holy wonderworker Hypatius between the Volga and the Kostroma. And they could not fight, since the Kostroma River was between them; they concluded peace, and Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich gave the town of Dmitrov to Prince Vasily Yuryevich Kosoy as a patrimony. And the spring was very cold then. And Prince Vasily Yuryevich Kosoy, having come to Dmitrov, stayed there one month.

In the same year the Novgorod posadniks Ivan Vasilyevich and Grigory Kirillovich, the Novgorod tysyatsky Feodor Eliseevich, and many other voivodes with a large army went on campaign to Rzhev, to the Popovsky frontier and to Borodova; they seized and burned volosts and villages, and carried off many people of Rzhev, and returned to Novgorod.

In the same year Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich of Moscow concluded a treaty with the Novgorodians, confirming it by kissing the cross: that the Grand Prince should renounce the Novgorod patrimony, the Bezhetsky Verkh, and the lands at Lamsky Volok and at Vologda, and the Novgorod boyars should renounce the princely land wherever it might be; and the division of lands should take place on St Peter’s Day, and at the division the Grand Prince’s boyars and the Novgorod boyars should be present. In the same year Evfimy, Vladyka of Novgorod, laid the foundation in his courtyard of a stone church of St John Chrysostom over the gates; in the same year it was built. But when the craftsmen finished the church and all had moved some distance away from it, the whole church collapsed to its foundation; and this seemed a sign that the end of the power of the Novgorod posadniks, tysyatskys, and all the boyars, and of all the Novgorod land, was approaching.

In the year 6944 from Adam (the year 1444 from Christ)

Prince Vasily Yuryevich Kosoy again went from Dmitrov to Kostroma, and sent letters of rejection to Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich; and he himself stayed in Kostroma until the winter road was established, and by the winter road went to Galich, and from Galich to Ustyug, and the Vyatchans went with him. And he stood before Ustyug for nine weeks, and took the town of Ustyug, and killed the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich’s voivode Prince Gleb Ivanovich Obolensky, and hanged the tithe-collector of the Rostov Vladyka, Iov Bulatov; he killed and hanged many Ustyug people.
