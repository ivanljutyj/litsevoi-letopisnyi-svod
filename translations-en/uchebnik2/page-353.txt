And again, on the next morning, Sunday, they laid a great piece of wood on his neck and led him after the tsar to the hunt. And he remained there in such distress for twenty-five days, and then Kavgady ordered him brought to the marketplace in such reproach and humiliation, like some robber and evildoer, and uttered much evil against him. And then he said before all the people, for there were many people there, so that they could not be numbered, saying: “The tsar’s mercy toward the guilty always comes with punishment, but this man deserves death, for the tsar’s word is known and certain, and his righteous judgment has found out that Prince Michael Yaroslavich has wholly lost mercy and has drawn near to death because of his evil deeds; and behold, now he is in hardship and distress. And after this he will soon receive death, but nevertheless, by the tsar’s mercy and his majesty, it is fitting to honor him, since he is already at death.”

And so Kavgady ordered him unshackled and untied, and water brought, and him washed; and when this had been done, he ordered a costly garment placed on him, and also ordered food and all kinds of fruits brought, and drink brought as well. But Michael touched nothing and tasted nothing; and there was a multitude of people there, like sand, gathered to see all this, and it came about that people from all lands had assembled there, like grass and like sand: men of Tsargrad, Germans, Lithuanians, Russians, and many Orthodox Christians, seeing this, shed tears. And there was no one without tears at that time.

Kavgady again ordered the costly garment taken off him and all the original burdens laid upon him.

And he remained in that distress for twenty-six days beyond the Terek River, beneath the great Yassian and Cherkassian mountains, near the city of Tityakov, on the Sivints River, near the Iron Gates, by the copper idol, by the golden head, by the heroic grave of Temir.

It was very early on Wednesday. Grand Prince Michael Yaroslavich arose and summoned his spiritual father, Abbot Mark, for with him were the abbot, two priest-monks, two priests, and a secular deacon; and he began to speak to him with tears and great weeping, saying: “Behold, already three times this night it has seemed to me that my death has drawn near today. But show me love, remember me in your holy prayers and never forget me, and may the Lord God forgive me all my sins, for I have sinned much before Him.”

He also ordered Matins to be sung and then the Hours. And he himself listened attentively with weeping, with tears, and with bitter sighing.

And then he ordered the rite of Holy Communion to begin. And he himself sang from the books, and uttered all with many tears, and confessed to his spiritual father, Abbot Mark.
