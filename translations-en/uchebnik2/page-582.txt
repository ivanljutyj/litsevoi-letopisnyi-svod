He came to the monastery of the Venerable Sergius with great possessions and wealth and gave it all to him for the building up of the monastery. And he lived there many years in humility, with a virtuous life, in strict obedience. And having reached old age, he reposed and passed to the Lord. The Venerable one buried him as was fitting. And so an eternal memory of him remained.

After this there came from the city of Moscow to Radonezh, to the monastery of his younger brother, the Venerable Abbot Sergius, Stephen, of whom we have spoken earlier, having left his monastery of the Holy Theophany, in which he had been abbot, and a spiritual father to the great prince Simeon Ivanovich, as well as to the tysyatsky Vasily, and to the eldest noble boyars and magnates, and he was held in great honor by the great prince and by all. He brought with him also his younger son, named Ivan, a boy of twelve. And entering the church, he handed his son over to his brother, the Abbot Sergius, ordering him to be tonsured into the monastic order. The Abbot Sergius tonsured him and gave him the monastic name Theodore. The name was given after the saint on whose day he was tonsured; this name was given to the one being tonsured. Since he was tonsured on the day of Saint Theodore Trichinas, he was given the monastic name Theodore. And he began to learn from his uncle Sergius the virtue of monastic life, so that all who saw him marveled. But we shall tell of him in detail later.

Many people from various cities and other places came to him. Some were already monks, some were laymen wishing to be tonsured. The Venerable one refused no one—neither old nor young, neither rich nor poor; he received all with zeal and joy. But he did not tonsure a willing candidate at once; first he ordered him to wear a long garment of black cloth and to remain with the brethren for a considerable time, until he learned the whole monastic rule. After this he clothed him in monastic dress, as one who had passed through the whole trial of monastic life. And after tonsuring him, he clothed him in the mantle and klobuk. And whoever proved to be a good monk and lived in perfect purity was permitted to receive the holy schema as well.

And his monastery, which is in Radonezh, began to be glorified everywhere. And who began life here? Formerly in this place there was forest, thicket, wilderness, where hares, foxes, wolves, and bears lived. Sometimes bears visited the saint, comforted the saint, and ate the bread which he laid out on logs and stumps near the saint’s cell.
