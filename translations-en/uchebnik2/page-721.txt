And the mounted Tatars began to attack the bridge, while the townspeople raised the bridge on ropes, and beneath it they had driven sharpened stakes; and when the bridge was filled with enemies, the townspeople cut the ropes, and the bridge fell with them onto the stakes, and so all perished; and many Tatars, Poles, and Lithuanians they captured alive and led into the city; and they cut off the Tatars’ shameful members and put them into their mouths, mocking them, so that Vytautas himself might see it and all who were with them; and they flayed the Poles, Czechs, and Vlachs. When Vytautas saw this, disgraced, he withdrew from the city, having been unable to do the city any harm.

And he came to another town, Voronoch, and encamped by it with his numerous troops. And a terrible and menacing cloud crept up with loud thunder and flashing lightning, so that Vytautas and those with him already despaired of preserving their lives. And such thunder struck with lightning that the earth shook; Vytautas grabbed the tent pole and began to cry, “Lord, have mercy!” He groaned and trembled, thinking to himself that he would now be swallowed by the earth and descend into hell. After that terrible cloud had passed, an envoy from the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich of Moscow, Alexander Vladimirovich Lykov, came to Vytautas at that small town, saying to Vytautas in the name of the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich: “Why are you doing such things, violating the treaty? How can you be at one with me if you are conquering and laying waste my patrimony?” And the Pskovians, who had come at that time, petitioned Vytautas with three thousand rubles. Vytautas, son of Kestutis, having listened to his grandson the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, withdrew from the town and took one thousand rubles from the Pskovians, and refused the two thousand rubles; and so he returned to his own land with all his troops, and he had stood with his army under Voronoch for three weeks.

In the year 6935 from Adam (the year 1435 from Christ)

There was a great pestilence in all Russian cities throughout all lands, and people died from boils: whoever was to die had a blue boil appear, and on the third day the person died; but whoever was to survive had a red boil appear, and the person lay a long time until the boil burst. And after that pestilence, as after the Flood, people began to live but a short time, and became short-lived, weak, and frail.

In the same year, during the same pestilence, Prince Vasily Vladimirovich reposed, grandson of Andrei, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Daniel Alexandrovich of Moscow, and he was laid in the Church of the Archangel Michael in Moscow. In the same year the Venerable Abbot Cyril of Belozersk reposed on the ninth day of June and was laid in his monastery on Beloozero. In the same year in Novgorod the Church of the Holy Savior was completed through the efforts of Euthymius, Archbishop of Novgorod.
