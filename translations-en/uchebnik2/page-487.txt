The Grand Prince said: “I have inquired, father, into everything in detail, and I am not guilty before him in anything; for I have an agreement with him, and according to that agreement I give him, and so I am not guilty before him in anything.” And while they were conversing thus, suddenly Tatars, envoys from Mamai, came to the Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich in Moscow, asking for tribute as it had been under Tsar Azbyak and under his son Chanibek, and not according to their peace agreement, as had been agreed with them. The Grand Prince was willing to give according to his agreement; but they asked as it had been under the ancient tsars. The Grand Prince would not give so.

Mamai’s envoys spoke proudly and declared that Mamai stood nearby, in the field beyond the Don, with a great host.

The Grand Prince told all this to his father Cyprian, Metropolitan of all Rus. And he said: “Do you see, my lord, my son beloved in the Lord, that by God’s permission, because of our sins, he comes to ravage our land. But it is fitting for you, Orthodox princes, to appease those ungodly men with gifts, increasing them fourfold, so that they may come to gentleness, meekness, and humility. But if even so he is not pacified and does not humble himself, then the Lord God will humble him. For it is written: ‘The Lord resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.’ So also it happened with Basil the Great in Caesarea, when the wicked apostate, the Emperor Julian, marched against the Persians and wished to destroy his city of Caesarea and put all its inhabitants to the sword.

Basil the Great prayed to the Lord God with all the Christians who were with him, and gathered much gold, intending to give it to Emperor Julian in order to appease and calm his wrath. The Lord, seeing Julian’s unsubdued heart, sent against him His warrior Saint Mercury, commanding that he be put to a wicked death. And so the wretched Emperor Julian was slain by God’s power. For so the Lord commanded Christians to act with humble wisdom, as it is said in the Gospel: ‘Be wise as serpents and gentle as doves.’ For the wisdom of the serpent is this: as soon as some misfortune befalls it, when it is beaten and wounded by someone, it gives its whole body to wounds and blows, but guards its head with all its strength. So also every Christian in Christ: when a time of distress and need comes upon him, persecuted and wounded, beaten and tortured, he gives all that is his—gold and silver, possessions, honor and glory; in great need he even allows his body to be wounded, but his head, which is Christ and the Christian faith in Him, he preserves with all care, for the sake of His love and the faith. For so the Lord commanded to order and guide matters wisely: if those who persecute seek lands and possessions, gold and silver, give them as much as you have.
