The venerable abbot Sergius of Radonezh will be my surety in this.” The grand prince listened to him and, believing Dionysius’s words, was abashed by his surety and released Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, taking from him a promise that he would not go to Constantinople without the grand prince’s permission, and that Sergius, abbot of Radonezh, would be surety for him. But Mityai did not believe Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, any more than he believed the venerable abbot Sergius of Radonezh; Mityai suspected Sergius of having advised Metropolitan Alexis not to bless Mityai for the Russian metropolitanate. Now Mityai considered that the venerable abbot Sergius had joined in one mind with Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, and that they did not want his installation as metropolitan. And Mityai grew angry with both of them and set himself against them.

The venerable abbot Sergius said: “I pray to the Lord God with a contrite heart that He not permit Mityai to lay waste this holy place and drive us out without cause.” But Dionysius went to Suzdal, and from there to Nizhny Novgorod.

And he waited a little in accordance with the word he had given, but soon sailed on ships along the Volga to Sarai, and from there to Constantinople. He broke his promise given to the grand prince, and betrayed his surety, the venerable abbot Sergius.

The grand prince was grieved by this; Mityai was troubled and grew angry with Dionysius and with the venerable abbot Sergius, thinking that Sergius and Dionysius had united in their intentions, not wishing his installation as metropolitan.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich loved Mityai very much and honored him as his father, more than all others, obeyed him in everything, and urged him to go to the patriarch in Constantinople for installation as metropolitan. He began to prepare for the journey and asked the grand prince for a charter, that is, blank letters sealed with the grand prince’s seal, so that when he came to Constantinople he would have these blank letters sealed with the grand prince’s seal for any eventuality, so that if need arose they could be written on. The grand prince gave him such blank letters, sealed with his seal, and said: “Take them; and if at any time there is a shortage of anything, or some need arises, and it is required to borrow from someone either a thousand rubles or more, my sealed promissory note shall stand as security for it all.” Mityai began his journey in the year 6887 from Adam (1387 from Christ) to Constantinople for installation as metropolitan. He went from Moscow to Kolomna, and from Kolomna beyond the Oka to Ryazan; he crossed the Oka on the 26th day of July.
