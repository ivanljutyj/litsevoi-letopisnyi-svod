The surviving Polovtsians fled to their host, where they knew only the message sent earlier, and told of their defeat; those, hearing this, went to meet the Russian princes, and sent messengers to other Polovtsians.

And they met them, and the archers fought one another for three days, but did not fight with lances, since the Polovtsians were awaiting their army, and did not let the Russians reach the water.

All the summoned Polovtsians came up to the fighting retinue, and a great multitude of them gathered. The Olgovichi, seeing them, were terrified and abandoned their intention, since they were already exhausted and suffered themselves from lack of water, as did their horses, and they managed to come a little closer to the water, because for already three days the Polovtsians had not let them reach the water; then the Polovtsians rushed at them and pressed them against the water.

And there was a great battle and fierce combat. Many Russian warriors, abandoning their horses, fought on foot against the Polovtsians, because their horses were exhausted. In that battle they also killed the wondrous hero Dobrynya Sudislavich.

And the wrath of God fell upon the Russian warriors; all were defeated, the Russian princes, boyars, and nobles were taken captive, and many were killed, and the glory of the Russian warriors perished with a clamor: for God’s help comes not in pride but in humility. And such a disaster occurred that not a single messenger returned from them to Rus.

A certain merchant was traveling that way, and the Polovtsians sent a message with him to the Russian princes, saying: “Come for your brothers, or we ourselves shall come to you for ours.”

Grand Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich of Kiev and the other princes, hearing that such a thing had happened to their kinsmen, wept, crying out as if they had lost their mind and reason.

Then, after many tears, Grand Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich of Kiev sent to his sons and to all the Russian princes, and having gathered a large army, he went against the Polovtsians; they came to Kanev.

The Polovtsians, learning that Grand Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich of Kiev was marching against them with all the Russian princes and a vast army, fled beyond the Don.
