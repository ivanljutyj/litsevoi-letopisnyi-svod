And Voishelk gathered many troops, and the friends and allies of his father, Grand Prince Mindovg; and he went against the pagan Lithuanians and the Chud tribe, and captured all who were there; and they stayed there a whole year, and he baptized many, and raised up churches and monasteries.

And others from there fled to Pskov with their wives and children; and they were baptized with Divine Baptism. Voishelk did all this well, and made the senior commander Andrei Danilovich great prince over them there.

And Voishelk himself came again to his monastery; and put on the monastic habit again; and struggled in heavy and virtuous labor; and after many labors, in deep old age, he died in the Lord.

In the year 6774 from Adam (1274 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vasily the Younger Yaroslavovich of Kostroma, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, was married, and Ignatius, Bishop of Rostov, wedded him in the Church of Saint Theodore in Kostroma.

In that same year Berkai, Tsar of the Horde, died; and there was relief for Rus from Tatar oppression. That same winter a star appeared in the west, and from it a long ray like a tail stretched southward; and the star remained for thirteen nights, and after that became invisible.

In that same year Dovmont, Prince of Lithuania, came to Pskov with all his kin and was baptized, and he was given the name Timofey.

And the Pskovians made Dovmont their prince; he chose for himself the best and bravest Pskovians, together with his own retinue, three hundred in all, and went and seized the whole Lithuanian land that was to have fallen to him from his father.

He seized the wife of Prince Gerden and took captive her children, the prince’s two sons, and returned to Pskov; and he crossed the Dvina River and went five versts, and halted on the Dvina River; he posted two watchmen, David and Luka, with one regiment; and he sent two hundred warriors to Pskov with the captives.

And Prince Gerden at that time came into his land and saw it devastated, and pursued Dovmont with seven hundred of his troops, and with him were the princes Gogort, Lombei, and Lyugailo, and other Lithuanian princes; and they came to the Dvina River, crossed it, and stood on the bank.
