In the same year, Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, whom they had driven from the great principality of Kiev, began to gather an army and sent to the Polovtsians, seeking again the great principality of Kiev for himself, for this principality of Kiev was dear and desirable to all, and all strove for it.

Thus also this Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich was in sorrow and grief, because he had been expelled from it. The Polovtsian princes came to him with the Polovtsians at Vyrevesk, and he went with them to Chernigov, and, arriving, halted by the Desna River.

At that time in Chernigov, on the great principality, was Svyatoslav Olegovich, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, and his nephew Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, and Prince Rurik Rostislavich. And they fought with them on the Desna River, not letting them come to them, and sent to Kiev to Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich asking for help; he sent to them Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich and Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and the Galician force as aid.

The Grand Prince Izyaslav, hearing of this, withdrew to the Field with the Polovtsians; they went after him beyond the Desna River, and not finding him, returned to their own lands.

The Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich reached Igor’s Ford, and there news from Chernigov from his friends overtook him: “Do not go anywhere; your brother Svyatoslav is in great illness, and his nephew, Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, has gone from him to Novgorod, and all the army has dispersed to their homes.”

He immediately hastened to Chernigov, and the Grand Prince of Chernigov Svyatoslav Olegovich was very unwell; and he stood encamped in tents before the city with his wife and children and boyars, knowing nothing of any danger to himself.

And Prince Izyaslav Davydovich came swiftly upon him and began to cross the Desna River, and sent the Polovtsians ahead of him to wage war, and set fire to the village of the Holy Savior.

Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich heard of this, quickly gathered his troops and arrayed his regiments, and sent for Prince Vladimir Andreevich and Prince Rurik Rostislavich, because Prince Vladimir Andreevich had come with his regiment close to Chernigov, and with him the Kiev and Galician force.

And he came quickly, having arrayed his regiments, and stood waiting for Prince Izyaslav Davydovich to come against him, to begin battle with him; against the Polovtsians of Izyaslav Davydovich he sent the Berendei, and they, going, drove them back and took back the captives. They fled to Izyaslav Davydovich, saying: “Many people have come, and a great army has gathered, while we are all few, and we cannot withstand them.”
