There was a certain servant of Lazar, king of Serbia; he was faithful, but some slandered him, saying that he served the king dishonestly and unfaithfully.

Both regiments came together, and there was a great battle and fierce slaughter.

That slandered man, wishing to show his true service, entered the regiment of the Turkish king Amurat, pretending to be a fugitive from the Orthodox Serbian king Lazar; and the Turkish regiments parted before him, giving way. And he, declaring that he was coming with love to Amurat, king of Turkey, suddenly thrust his sword swiftly into the heart of Amurat, the Turkish king.

And at that same hour Amurat, the Turkish king, died. That wondrous Christian servant was also killed by them.

And therefore the Turks fell into confusion, and then the Serbian forces with King Lazar began to prevail over the Turks.

Also the Turks soon set Amurat’s son Baazit as king among themselves.

And so the Turks began to overcome the Serbian king Lazar and his army, and they captured the Serbian king, his princes, his commanders, his boyars, his servants, and all his army; some they slew, and others they seized by hand.

And Baozit, the Turkish king, ordered the Serbian king Lazar to be beheaded with the sword. This battle was in the year 6897. So the townspeople told us. Since we were in the Turkish realm, there was great rebellion and much confusion in that country.

And fearing the rebellion, Metropolitan Pimen sent the monk Michael to Constantinople, and Michael, bishop of Smolensk, sent me, Ignatius, and Sergei, archimandrite of Azakov, sent his monk. And they set out from the city of Ostravia on the Sunday before the day of Peter.

In the morning they went from Phila and passed Riva, and came to Ustye, and passed Fonar.

The wind was very favorable, and they sailed to Constantinople with inexpressible joy.

On Monday, the eve of the day of Peter, during Vespers, the Russians living there came to us, and there was great joy for all, and that night they remained in the ship.

In the morning, on the 29th day of the month of June, on the very feast of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, giving thanks to the Lord God, they entered the city of Constantine.

In the morning of the same month, on the 30th day, they went to the church of Holy Sophia, the Wisdom of God.
