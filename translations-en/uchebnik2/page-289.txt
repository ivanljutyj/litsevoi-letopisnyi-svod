They beat him over his whole body, so that the earth there itself turned red from his blood. And they beat him long and without mercy, while the martyr endured as though someone else were suffering; and to their mockery he answered nothing. And one could see a strange sight, worthy of reverence: he to whom a multitude of soldiers submitted with fear, and whom whole lands feared, was touched by vile hands without mercy. But what of the martyr? Did he groan or utter any sound, as sufferers usually do? No, nothing. For he suffered by his own will—to speak more simply, he himself desired it—and he accepted what happened with joy. And so it seemed to those looking on that someone else was suffering, and they saw that his face was bright. Michael clearly gave thanks to the Master and Lord, and cried out distinctly: “Let it never be that a man, being a Christian, should pass through the fire of sorcery; let it never be that people who truly honor God should worship a bush; let it never be that people who worship the consubstantial Trinity should venerate the visible sun.” Meanwhile the torturers with the same force crushed the saint’s body and shed his blood, but he did not weaken or submit; and even those killers themselves marveled at this man’s endurance.

Batu understood all the valor of the saint and could not long endure, in his hardness, the courage of the martyr, and he condemned the confessor of piety to death, thereby making for him a passage to Eternal Life. The merciless servants, swift to carry out evil commands, came to the martyr, who was still clearly saying: “I am a Christian.” And there was one of them named Doman; he had formerly been a Christian, but afterward renounced the Christian faith. This transgressor of the law was very enraged against the saint, because he was an enemy of the Christian faith. And at once, having received the evil command, he stretched out his vile hands and with a knife cut off the honored head of the holy confessor and martyr, the great prince Michael; and he flung the head away from the body, while it was still uttering these words: “I am a Christian”; and so, an heir of Judas’s lot, he brought the valiant heir of Christ to glory, and himself to dishonor. Thus the divine sufferer, the great prince Michael, ascended to the Founder of his contest, the Lord Christ, shining with virtues, adorned with his own blood, which he had shed for His name, and by Him he was crowned with the crown of incorruption. And if it were not for this most radiant martyr, who refused to fulfill the impious command of the mad tormentor, the godless one would have become still more hardened, and the devil would have stirred him up to take arms against the whole Russian land, since Batu saw that the princes carried out his command. But now his impious command was annulled by Christ’s warrior Michael.
