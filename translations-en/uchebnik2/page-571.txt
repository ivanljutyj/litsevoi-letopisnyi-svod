The Tale of the Venerable Sergius

On the twenty-fifth day of the month of September, on the commemoration of the Venerable Euphrosyne, the Venerable Sergius of Radonezh and Makovsky departed this life—gentle, meek, humble, and so forth. I do not know how I can describe his life. For from his youth he loved God and constantly said: “Lord, grant me from my young years to labor for You! Lord, receive me and take me to Yourself, reckon me among Your chosen flock; I am poor before You! And from my youth deliver me, Lord, from every impurity and defilement of flesh and soul, and vouchsafe me, Lord, to keep holiness before Your face! Let my heart ascend to You, Lord, and let all the sweetness of this world not be a delight to me, and let all the beauty of life not touch me, but let my soul be carried away after You, and let Your right hand receive me. Let not worldly beauties delight me and lead me into temptation, and let me not desire to rejoice with the joy of this world, but fill me, Lord, with spiritual joy, with joy ineffable, with divine sweetness, and let Your good Spirit guide me into the righteous path!” The elders and other people, seeing such zeal in the youth, marveled, saying: “What will become of this youth, if God has endowed him with such a gift of virtue from infancy?” All that has been written up to this point took place where Cyril had formerly lived, in a certain place within the borders of the Principality of Rostov, not very near the city of Rostov. A few words should be said about the relocation, because Cyril moved from Rostov to Radonezh. Why and for what reason he moved—I could tell much, but I must write about it all the same. This aforementioned servant of God, Cyril, had formerly lived in the Rostov region, and he was the son of a boyar. Their family was one of the most glorious and renowned. He was rich, but later, in old age, he became poor, and their family declined. I will also tell why he became poor: because of frequent journeys with the prince to the Horde, because of frequent Tatar raids, which Rus endured, because of frequent arrivals of Tatar envoys, because of frequent heavy Horde tributes and exactions, because of frequent years of grain famine.
