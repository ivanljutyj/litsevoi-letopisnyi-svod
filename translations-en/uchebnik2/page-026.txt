In that same year the Yem came and ravaged the Novgorod districts. The Ladoga men gathered for battle with them and killed 400 of the Yem host.

In that same year a Swedish prince came with his bishop in sixty ships against Novgorod merchants who were coming from overseas with great wealth in five boats; and they fought much, and could do nothing to the Novgorodians, who captured three of their snekkas and killed one hundred and fifty of them.

In the year 6651 from Adam (1151 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod Olegovich of Kiev married his son Svyatoslav to the daughter of Vasilko, Prince of Polotsk, son of Svyatoslav, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, and great-great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, between Christmas and Epiphany, in Novgorod.

That same winter Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich set out to his uncle, Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomashich, and, not having made peace with him, went to Smolensk to his brother, and from there went to another brother of his, Svyatopolk, in Novgorod; and he wintered there.

In the year 6652 from Adam (1152 from Christ)

Izyaslav Mstislavich came from Novgorod, having stayed with his brothers.

In that same year Izyaslav Mstislavich gave his daughter in marriage to Prince Rogvolod Borisovich in Polotsk. Then Grand Prince Vsevolod Olegovich of Kiev also went to the wedding, with his wife and all his boyars and with the Kievans, to Pereyaslavl, and they celebrated there joyously, and all were in love and peace.

In that same year Grand Prince Vsevolod Olegovich of Kiev sent an army against Grand Prince Volodimirko in Galich; he also sent against him Prince Izyaslav Davydovich and the Polovtsian princes with the Polovtsians. And the Russian princes all gathered together with Vsevolod, and the Poles, and the Czechs, and the Hungarians; and a ban came, the king's nephew, and a multitude of troops gathered against Grand Prince Volodimirko of Galich.

And they came to Galich and could not fight, because the river had not frozen. And both armies went along the river; and so they went for twelve days, and came to Zvenigorod, and stood on the Rozhne field. And they could not fight, because Grand Prince Volodimirko of Galich stood on the Bare Mountains with a multitude of warriors.
