The Russian princes returned to their own lands with only a small retinue, for all had been slain in the steppe by the Polovtsians.

In the same year, Prince Andrew of Rostov and Suzdal, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, spoke thus to his princes and boyars: “This city of Vladimir was founded in his own name by the holy and blessed Grand Prince Vladimir, who enlightened all the Russian land with holy baptism. Now I, sinful and unworthy, by God’s grace and the help of the Most Pure Mother of God, have enlarged and exalted it still more, and have built in it a church in the name of the Most Pure Mother of God, of Her holy and glorious Dormition, and have adorned it and endowed it with property and wealth, with districts, villages, and a tithe from trade, from grain, from herds, and in all things I have given the tenth to the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God; and I wish to renew this city as a metropolitan see, that this city may be a great principality and all.”

And this was loved by all his princes and boyars, that it should be so. And he sent to Constantinople to the patriarch his envoy Yakov Stanislavich, asking that he bless the city of Vladimir with a metropolitan see and install a metropolitan in it, saying: “I have raised a great city and endowed it with much property and wealth, with villages, settlements, districts, and a tithe from trade, and tithes from herds and grain, and in all things the tenth; and we have received the statute and commandment from the holy and blessed Grand Prince Vladimir of Kiev and All Rus, and just as he received the statutes according to the Greek nomocanons, so we keep them unshaken and inviolate from generation to generation until the end of the world.”

The Most Reverend Luke, Patriarch of Constantinople, hearing of this, ordered his letter to be read before the sacred council; there was also Nestor, Bishop of Rostov and Suzdal, and the envoy of Theodore, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus. And he sent to him this letter:
