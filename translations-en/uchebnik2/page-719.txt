Then the despot’s legs began to ache often, and he summoned his nephew Gurg and appointed him despot of the whole Serbian land; soon afterward he himself departed this life in the same year, on June 19. On that day there was a mighty thunderclap in Belgrade such as had never been, and darkness covered our whole land; it seemed that night had fallen, and only at sunset did a little sun appear. Weeping bitterly, they laid Despot Stefan near Belgrade in the monastery of the Life-Giving Trinity, in the tomb that he himself had built.

The Greek Empire. In the year 6933 from Adam (1433 from Christ)

After Tsar Manuel, his son Kyr Koluyan, an Orthodox sovereign, began to reign in Constantinople. In the time of this tsar, Tsar Amurat, son of Mahmet, after the death of his younger brother Mustafa, came to Constantinople, remembering that his brother had been given an army as aid; afterward he made peace with him.

In the year 6934 from Adam (1434 from Christ)

In the month of September the plague began to end in Veliky Novgorod, but it began anew in Pskov, Veliky Novgorod, Torzhok, Tver, Volok, Dmitrov, Moscow, and in all Russian cities, and in volosts and villages throughout the whole land; and everywhere there was great sorrow and grief among the people.

Then, in Moscow, in that same autumn, Prince Andrei Vladimirovich died, grandson of Andrei, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Daniel of Moscow, in the monastic order; he was named Savva instead of Andrei and was buried at the Church of the Trinity in Sergiev Monastery.

In that same autumn the Tatars came to the Ryazan borderland and returned with captives to the Steppe.

The Ryazan men set out in pursuit after them, and having overtaken them, fought with them and took back the captives. In that same autumn the Turks were conquering the Greek land.

In that same year Prince Yaroslav Vladimirovich died, grandson of Andrei, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Daniel of Moscow. In that same year Prince Ivan Vasilievich of Yaroslavl died.

In that same year the Grand Duke of Lithuania, Vytautas Keistutovich, went to war against Pskov with a great army; and with him were the Lithuanian, Polish, Czech, and Wallachian lands, and the Tatars were on his side, and he asked Tsar Mahmet for his army, consisting of service people.
