Hearing this, Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich was troubled and sent to him, saying: “Why do you crush and insult our souls? Do you not know about Metropolitan Constantine, that he rose up against my father and cursed him; is this pleasing to you?”

But Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich of Smolensk sent to him, saying: “Do not add fierce and evil things to fierce and evil wounds, but seek healing and cure the wounds; then you will be healed and receive mercy both in the present and in the future, and you will gladden God and astonish people; for the honor of a hierarch comes from God, and so does dishonor.” And they argued much about this fiercely, and made peace, deciding to set both aside in Kiev and to send for another to Constantinople to the patriarch.

In the same year Leontius came to the bishopric in Rostov and Suzdal.

In the year 6667 from Adam (the year 1167 from Christ)

Metropolitan Constantine of Kiev and all Rus reposed in the city of Chernigov, because he had fled from Kiev from Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich because of his malice toward him, not wishing to be in disputes and turmoil with him; he fled from the city according to Christ’s commandment, who says in His Gospel: if they persecute you in this city, flee to another; for Christ our God does not want us to deliver ourselves into misfortunes, saying: take heed to yourselves, for they will deliver you up to councils, and you will be beaten in their synagogues; they will deliver you up, He says, and you will not deliver yourselves up; and you will be led to rulers and kings, and you will not lead yourselves; therefore, He says, be wise as serpents and harmless as doves. If one raises war against war, hardens himself, rages, and is stirred to fury, then indeed injurious and deadly deeds are done; but it is better to calm anger and fury by gentleness and meekness, by admonitory and sweet words, and by every kind of wise discretion, if this is possible; if it is not possible, then one must flee according to Christ’s commandment, so that we not be guilty of many human deaths. And so, having wisely and peaceably avoided this, as a bird is delivered from fowlers and as a gazelle escapes from snares, we should calm the anger and fury within us against those who offend us; because anger does not work the righteousness of God in a man, and not avenging yourselves, says he, beloved, but give place to wrath, for fury dwells in the bosom of fools, and a wrathful man is not comely, and the time of his fury is his fall. For the Lord God commanded us not to resist evil; and the apostle says: “Be angry, and do not sin; let not the sun go down upon your wrath; let all bitterness, and fury, and anger, and clamor be taken away from you with all malice; for anger and great fury work evil, and so do envy and deceit. Therefore it is necessary to flee, and to take heed to ourselves, and to watch, and to pray, that we not enter into temptation.” And the great apostle of Christ Paul was let down from the city of Damascus in a basket through the wall by night, and escaped the hands of those who sought to seize him.
