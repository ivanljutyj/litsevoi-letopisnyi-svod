And there the Tatars sent other envoys to them with this speech: “Since you listened to the Polovtsians and killed our envoys, and are coming against us with war and bloodshed, while we have done you no wrong, let God judge between us and you, for God is the Creator and Nourisher of all.”

And there all the Polovtsian people with their princes joined the Russians. Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich of Galich with a twenty-thousand-strong army crossed the Dnieper and attacked the Tatar guard regiments and defeated them; and those remaining fled with the voivode Ganibek, whom the Tatars buried alive in the ground, wishing to save him; but our men, finding him, killed him.

And learning of this, all the Russian princes crossed the Dnieper River with a countless army: Grand Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Kiev, grandson of Rostislav, with the people of Kiev; Grand Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk, grandson of Rostislav; and the princes of Chernigov, Galich, Volhynia, Kursk, Trubchevsk, and Putivl, and princes from all lands with a countless multitude of troops.

There also came the Bauty, the Gaigaly, the Vygalltsy, and the men of Galich in ships along the Dnieper into the sea: there were more than two thousand of them; then again they came out of the sea into the Dnieper, and passed the rapids, and came to the Khortitsa River, to the ford, to Protolchi; their voivodes were Yuri Domorechevich and Derzhikrai Vladislavovich.

Then news came to them there that the Tatars had come to look upon the Russian warriors of Prince Daniil Romanovich and of many other princes.

And the young sons of the great princes, mounting their horses, galloped off to look at the army, and having seen it, sent to Mstislav saying: “Do not stand still; go against them.”

And our men moved into the steppe and met the Tatars; and there the Russian archers drove them far across the steppe; and they cut them down and took their cattle; and they fled with the other herds.

And from there the Russian princes marched after the Tatars for eight days to the Kalka River, and there met the guard regiments; and there the Tatars killed Ivan Dmitrievich and two other men with him; and the Tatars withdrew.

Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich ordered Prince Daniil Romanovich to cross the Kalka River with his regiments, and he himself followed after them; and when he had crossed, he sent the guard regiments of Yarun with the Polovtsians, while he himself remained.

And Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich of Galich himself also went after the guard regiments, and saw the Tatar regiments; and he ordered his warriors to arm themselves; but the two other Prince Mstislavs were in their camps and knew nothing of it, because Prince Mstislav did not inform them out of envy; for there was enmity among them.
