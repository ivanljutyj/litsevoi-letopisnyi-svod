They fled to Tsar Takhtamysh, terrified and beaten, and told him what had happened.

The tsar, hearing that the Grand Prince was at Kostroma and his brother Prince Vladimir at Volok, began to fear, expecting an invasion against himself by the Grand Prince and his brother, and gathered his forces to himself.

And so after several days he went back from the city of Moscow with many captives and countless riches, and ahead of himself sent an army to Kolomna. They went and took the city of Kolomna.

And the tsar crossed with all his forces beyond the Oka River, and ravaged and took the land of Ryazan, and burned it with fire, and cut down people, and some fled, and others were led away captive to the Horde—a great multitude of Ryazan people.

Prince Oleg of Ryazan, seeing this, fled from the Tatar invasion.

The tsar went to the Horde from Ryazan and sent as an embassy to Rus, to Nizhny Novgorod, to Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal, his brother-in-law named Shiakhmat together with Dmitry’s son, Prince Semyon, and another of his sons, Prince Vasily, he took with him to the Horde.

And so the tsar departed for his own land with countless riches and countless captives.

And then after several days the right-believing Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich and his cousin Prince Vladimir Andreyevich, each with his senior boyars, came to their patrimony, Moscow, and saw the city taken, plundered, and burned with fire, and the holy churches devastated, and a great multitude of people cut down, and corpses lying dead. And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich grieved greatly over all this, so that he wept with great tears. And who would not weep over such destruction of the city! Or who would not pity so great a multitude of people who perished needlessly by violence! And who would not lament such captivity!

And they ordered the bodies of the slain to be buried with psalms and hymns, and gave one ruble for every eighty dead to those who buried the dead, and they counted that in all 300 rubles had been given for the burial of the dead; for many had been killed by the Tatars, and others had been killed by their own for the sake of property, for they robbed and killed one another. And so many captives were led out to the Horde—a great multitude—that it was impossible even to count them.

In the year 6891 from Adam (the year 1391 from Christ)

In the month of September, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent his army against Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan. He went out into the field with his boyars.
