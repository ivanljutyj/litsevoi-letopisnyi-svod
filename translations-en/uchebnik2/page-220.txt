And Yaroslav also came running alone to Pereyaslavl and shut himself up in it.

And the first evil was not enough for him, when he had beaten people in Novgorod, Torzhok, and Volok; but rushing in, he seized the Novgorodians and Smolenskians who had come to trade in his land, and ordered the Novgorodians to be thrown into cellars, and others he shut up in a cramped hut, and one hundred and fifty of them suffocated; and fifteen Smolenskians he shut up, and they all remained alive.

The princes stood all that day at the place of the slaughter, and in the morning went to Vladimir.

And the princes came to the city of Vladimir early on Sunday, and having surrounded it, halted.

And that night the prince’s courtyard in the city caught fire; the Novgorodians wanted to take the city, but Prince Mstislav did not allow them.

On Tuesday, at the second hour of the night, the city caught fire again and burned until dawn; the Smolenskians asked at that time to take the city, but Prince Vladimir did not let them.

Prince Yuri sent out from the city to the princes with a petition: “Bear with me today, and tomorrow I will leave the city, only do not betray me to my brother Konstantin.”

Early in the morning Prince Yuri went out of the city with his two sons and bowed to the princes Mstislav and Vladimir: “To you, brothers, I bow, and my brother Konstantin is in your will.” They let him go in peace.

And on that same day Prince Konstantin gave many gifts to the princes and boyars, and brought the Vladimirians to the oath on the cross.

Prince Yaroslav still remained in malice and, breathing anger, would not submit, having shut himself up in Pereyaslavl. The princes decided to go to Pereyaslavl on Friday of the third week after Easter.

And learning of this, Prince Yaroslav was dismayed and began sending to the princes, begging for peace.

On Tuesday of the fourth week he went out of the city and bowed in submission to his elder brother Konstantin, saying: “I am in your will, but do not betray me to my father-in-law Mstislav and to Vladimir.” Prince Konstantin reconciled Mstislav with his son-in-law Yaroslav, and, before reaching Pereyaslavl, concluded peace.

On Wednesday, at Mid-Pentecost, they came to Pereyaslavl. And there Prince Yaroslav gave many gifts to the princes and commanders, but Prince Mstislav did not go to the city, though he did accept the gifts.
