They scattered everywhere and began to search. Some found Mikhail Andreevich Brenko, the grand prince’s confidant, slain, wearing the grand prince’s cloak, armor, and helmet, and all the royal regalia.

Others found Prince Feodor Semenovich Belozersky and thought that he was the grand prince, because he resembled him in face.

Two simple warriors turned aside to the right toward the oak grove—one named Fedor Zernov, and the other named Feodor Kholopov; these were of common rank. And they came upon the grand prince, sorely beaten, barely breathing, lying under a newly cut tree, beneath its branches, as if dead. Leaping from their horses, they bowed to him.

And one returned quickly to Prince Vladimir Andreevich and told him that the grand prince was alive.

He at once swiftly mounted his horse and galloped with his warriors, and coming, stood over him, and said to him: “O my dear brother, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich! You are like ancient Yaroslav and the new Alexander! But above all—glory to Christ our God Jesus Christ, and to His Most Pure Mother, and to the great wonderworker Peter, and to the venerable abbot Sergius, and to the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb, and to all God’s holy saints, because by invisible divine help the Ishmaelites were defeated, and God’s mercy has shone upon us.” The Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich barely managed with effort to say: “Who is speaking this, and what do these words mean?” Prince Vladimir Andreevich said to him: “I am your brother, Prince Vladimir Andreevich, speaking this to you.” And they barely raised him up; his armor was all battered, and he himself was sorely wounded, yet nowhere on his body did they find a mortal wound. And he had stood before all in battle, from the first onset, and had fought face to face with the Tatars a great deal. And many princes and commanders said to him: “Lord prince, do not stand in front to fight; stand behind, or on the wing, or somewhere else in an outer place.” He answered them, saying: “How can I say to anyone: Arise, my brothers, and stand firm against the enemies, while I myself stand behind and hide my face? I cannot do such a thing—to hide and conceal myself—but I wish, both in word and deed, to be first among all and before all to lay down my head for the name of Christ and His Most Pure Mother, and for the Christian faith, and for all Orthodox Christianity, so that others also, seeing my boldness, may do likewise with great zeal.” And then he added this to them: “Brothers, you yourselves read and remember the martyrdom of the holy great martyr Arethas. When the torturer wished to cut him down, and all the soldiers, his subordinates, were brought with him to be cut down, they hurried one before another to the cutting; but the valiant martyr Arethas restrained all his army and said to them: ‘I, brothers, received gifts from the earthly king before you and more than you; now before you I wish to see the heavenly...’”
