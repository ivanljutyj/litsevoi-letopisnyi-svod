The Christians, seeing that the Tatars had fled with Mamai, pursued them, striking and cutting them down without mercy. For God, by an invisible power, terrified their regiments, and they fled, turning their backs to wounds. In that pursuit some of the Tatars fell by the weapons of the Christians, and others drowned in the river. And they drove them as far as the Mecha River, and there a countless multitude of fleeing Tatars was slain; and they chased them even to their camps, and seized much wealth, all their property and the Tatar herds. The Grand Prince, hearing this from them, lifted his hands to heaven and said: “Great art Thou, O Lord, and wondrous are Thy works, and there is no word sufficient to praise Thy wonders! O most pure Mother of God! Who can praise Thee worthily and sing of Thy great and ineffable wonders? O blessed Peter, our strong intercessor! What shall I render to thee for all that thou hast rendered to us!” And so, having rested from his labor, and having been comforted from his sweat and his pains, he gladdened his brother Prince Vladimir Andreyevich, and the other princes, and all the host with sweet words, and praised them and magnified them because they had so fought for the Orthodox faith and for all Christendom.

And he went with his brother and with the remaining princes and commanders, for few of them remained; and he saw the dead lying like haycocks, and Christians mingled with Tatars, Christian blood merged with Tatar blood, and it was very terrible and dreadful to see.

And the Grand Prince wept with great weeping and tears, and came to the place where eight slain Belozersk princes lay together. These were very courageous and strong, renowned and glorious champions; and they died one for another, with a multitude of their boyars. Then, near them, he rode up to his great commander Mikula Vasilyevich, the thousand-commander, and here fifteen princes lay dead with him, and a great multitude of boyars and commanders. Then he rode up to his bosom friend, whom he loved more than all, Mikhail Andreyevich Brenk, and near him lay a multitude of slain princes and boyars. Here also lay slain Semyon Melik with Timofey Valuevich. And he moved to another place and saw Sergius’s monk Peresvet, and near him the famous Tatar champion, and turning to those with him, he said: “You see, brothers, the one who began the battle; for he defeated his like, and through him many would have drunk the bitter cup.” And the Grand Prince wept for all with bitter weeping and great tears, saying: “Glory to God, who willed it so.” Those slain by Mamai in that battle were beyond all number. Slain were noble and great and very valiant men, whose names are as follows: Prince Fyodor Romanovich Belozersky and his son Prince Ivan; Prince Fyodor Semyonovich; Prince Ivan Mikhailovich; Prince Fyodor Torussky; Prince Mstislav; his brother Prince Dmitry Monastyrev; Semyon Mikhailovich; Mikula Vasilyevich, the thousand-commander; Mikhailo and Ivan Akinfovich; Ivan Alexandrovich; Andrey Shuba.
