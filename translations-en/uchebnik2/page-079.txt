Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky, hearing of that, sent couriers throughout his grand principality, gathering troops, but soon fell ill. For Yuri drank at the tax collector Petrilo’s on May 10, and that night he fell ill, and was ill for 5 days, and died on May 15, on Wednesday night; he ruled for 33 years. His sons: Rostislav, Andrei Bogolyubsky, Ivan, Boris, Gleb, Mstislav, Vasilko, Yaroslav, Mikhalko, Svyatoslav, Vsevolod.

And on Thursday he was laid in the monastery of the Holy Savior at Berestovo. And much evil was done that day: they plundered his Red Court, and they plundered his other court beyond the Dnieper, which is called Samoray, and they plundered the court of his son Vasilko in the city; and they beat the Suzdalians in towns and villages, and plundered their property.

And Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich of Chernigov, having come to Kiev, took all his property and seized his retinue, binding some with iron fetters and throwing others into prisons; and he sat on the throne of the grand principality in Kiev on May 18, with his nephews with him, while he left Prince Svyatoslav Vladimirovich in Chernigov. And Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich came against him, but he repulsed them; and they took their stand beyond the Svina.

And hearing of that, Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich of Kiev went from Kiev to his aid with Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, and stood against them; and they made peace, and gave the throne of the grand principality of Chernigov to Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich, and Novgorod-Seversky to Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, and so they made peace, and parted in love, each to his own place; Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich returned to Kiev.

The Novgorodians, having learned of the death of Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh of Kiev, and that after him Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich of Chernigov had sat on the grand principality in Kiev, began to expel from Novgorod their prince Mstislav Yuryevich, son of Dolgoruky.

They also sent many armed warriors against him, so that he would leave the city sooner, for, they said, “We have sent for Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich and for his brother Prince Davyd Rostislavich, and then you will be in great trouble.”

And at once they tore up the bridge over the Volkhov, and many armed regiments took their stand, some on one side and others on the other, and a fierce battle nearly began; for on that day Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Davyd Rostislavich arrived. And Prince Mstislav, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, heard of that and fled that same night from Novgorod to Rostov to his elder brother Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky.
