And some raced swiftly on horses, and to both sides, and again before them, and quickly backward, and successfully shot without missing. And others made ladders and, setting them against the wall, climbed up. The townspeople heated water in cauldrons, poured boiling water on them, and so hindered them. Then these Tatars withdrew, for they were weary.

And again others came forward, the most savage and ferocious; the townspeople fought against them, shooting, and striking them down with stones, and drawing crossbows, and using siege engines and small guns. There were even some who shot at them with cannons.

There was a certain townsman named Adam, a Muscovite, a cloth-maker, who, being above the Frolov Gate, noticed one notable and renowned Tatar, who was the son of a certain prince of the Horde, and he drew his crossbow and suddenly loosed an arrow at him, which struck his wrathful heart, and death soon came to him. And this was a great misfortune for all the Tatars, so that the Tsar himself grieved over it. While all this was happening, the Tsar stood by the city for three days, and on the fourth day he ingratiated himself with their prince Ostei, grandson of Olgerd, with false words about a false peace, and summoned him out of the city, and killed him before the city gates, and ordered all his armies to attack the city from all sides. Thus they deceived Prince Ostei and all the townspeople who were under siege. For the Tsar stood about the city for three days, and on the fourth day in the morning, in the middle of dinner, by the Tsar’s command the Tatars came up to the city, the chief men and princes of the Horde, and his mercenaries, and with them also two princes of Suzdal, brothers-in-law of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, Vasily and Semyon, sons of Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal. And they came near the city walls with caution, and began to speak to the people who were on the city walls thus: “The Tsar wishes to show mercy to you, the people of his ulus, since you are not guilty and not worthy of death; for the Tsar did not come against you to wage war, but came and took up arms against Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich. You are worthy of mercy, and the Tsar requires nothing else of you, only that you come out to meet him with honor and with gifts together with your prince; for he wishes to see this city and to enter it and visit it, and he grants you peace and his love, and you are to open the city gates to him.” Also the princes of Nizhny Novgorod, Prince Vasily and Prince Semyon, brothers-in-law of the Grand Prince, sons of Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal, spoke together with the Tatars to the townspeople: “Have faith in us: for we are your Christian princes,
