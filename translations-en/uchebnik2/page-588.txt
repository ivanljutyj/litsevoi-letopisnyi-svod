He, however, did not want to, wishing to follow the path of his elder and teacher Sergius. But they continued earnestly to persuade him, and he was forced to agree, reluctantly obeying. In his Simonov Monastery he founded a large stone church in honor of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God, and in his place he left an archimandrite. He himself was installed as archbishop in the city of Rostov.

After this he was again in Constantinople. And Anthony, Patriarch of Constantinople, showed him great honor, decreeing that he be called Archbishop of Rostov. This was in the year 6898 from Adam (the year 1398 from Christ).

After this he returned. Metropolitan Cyprian came with him. Metropolitan Cyprian was then in Constantinople. Metropolitan Pimen was also in Constantinople then, and it was there that he died. After this Theodore, archimandrite of Simonov, nephew and disciple of the venerable Sergius of Radonezh, received the archiepiscopal rank and came to be called Archbishop of Rostov. And after him, those installed in the city of Rostov were also called Archbishops of Rostov. But at first, before the year 6674 from Adam (the year 1174 from Christ), there were bishops in the city of Rostov, not archbishops. Likewise in Novgorod at first there were bishops, not archbishops, and so it was until the year 1166 from Christ. In that year Ivan, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, showed great honor to Ilya, Bishop of Novgorod, ordering him to be called Archbishop of Novgorod; this was in the year 1166 from Christ. And after this Ilya came to be called Archbishop of Great Novgorod and Pskov. And also all who after him were installed in Novgorod were called archbishops. But at first they were bishops, not archbishops. Just as the one of Rostov at first was a bishop, not an archbishop. As for white klobuks, at first all in Rus wore them: both metropolitans and bishops, and those deemed worthy of the great honor of the archbishopric, and all the hierarchs in Rus wore white klobuks. And Theodore, Archbishop of Rostov, nephew and disciple of the venerable Sergius, abbot of Radonezh, having long lived a virtuous life and cared for the flock of the city of Rostov entrusted to him by God, honorably, gloriously, and in a God-pleasing manner departed to the Lord. He loved Him from his early years, and for His sake he took the monastic tonsure in his youth.
