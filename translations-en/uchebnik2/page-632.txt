And they came and stood on the bank of the Vorskla River. And opposite them stood Tsar Temir-Kutluy with all the Tatar force, having come out to meet them from beyond the Vorskla River.

And so they met with Vitovt in the open field on the Vorskla River in the Tatar land and began negotiations among themselves. Tsar Temir-Kutluy said to Vitovt: “Why have you come to fight me? I have not conquered your lands, nor your cities, nor your villages.” Vitovt Keistutovich answered him: “God has subjected all lands to me; submit to me also, be a son to me, and I will be a father to you; pay me tribute and dues every year. If you do not wish to do so, you will become my slave, and I will put all your Horde to the sword.”

And Tsar Temir-Kutluy was frightened, and sent his envoys to Vitovt with a petition, with love and entreaty, that Vitovt Keistutovich might have mercy, show him his favor, take him as his son, and receive tribute and dues from the whole Horde each year. Vitovt, whom the tsar asked for this, agreed. But besides that, Vitovt wished that throughout the Horde, on Horde money, Vitovt’s sign be stamped. Tsar Temir-Kutluy asked Vitovt for a three-day delay for reflection and sent Vitovt much cattle, oxen, and sheep, doing him honor.

And at that time Prince Edigei came to the tsar. This Prince Edigei was known throughout the Horde for his courage, boldness, and bravery; he learned from his tsar of Vitovt’s coming and that the tsar had decided to submit to Vitovt, and he said to the tsar: “O tsar, it would be better for us to accept death than to agree to such terms.”

And he sent his envoys to Vitovt, while Vitovt stood on the other bank of the Vorskla River with a baggage train of iron-bound carts, armed with handguns, cannon, and crossbows. Edigei said: “Meet with me, O good and valiant Vitovt!”

And they began to talk, while between them was the Vorskla River. Edigei, great prince of the Horde, said to Vitovt, great prince of Lithuania: “Is it true that you have taken our free tsar of the Great Horde as your son, since you are old, while our free tsar of the Great Horde, Temir-Kutluy, is still young in years? You should understand this, since you are very wise and valiant; judge: if I am older than you, and you are younger than I, then it befits me to be your father, and you to become my son, to give me each year tribute and dues from all your principality, and throughout all your principality to stamp my Horde sign on money.” Vitovt Keistutovich, hearing such words, became enraged, cried out, and ordered his army to prepare for battle.
