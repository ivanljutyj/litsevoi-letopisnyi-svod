The princes helped the Olgovichi; but the Smolensk men, not having reached them, turned to flight.

The Polotsk men, seeing Mstislav overcoming Oleg, did not pursue the Smolensk men, but turned back and struck the rear of Mstislav’s regiment, and trampled it; Mstislav was not there at that time, for with the foremost warriors he had ridden in pursuit of Oleg’s regiment. Mstislav returned, thinking he had defeated Oleg, not yet knowing of his defeated warriors; but when he rode onto the field of battle, the Polotsk men recognized him and took him captive.

Rostislav Vladimirovich, Prince David’s retinue, and the Ryazan prince’s son, David’s son-in-law, who had been pursuing the Olgovichi, returned to their own men and, seeing their banners trampled by the Polotsk men, fled to Smolensk.

Oleg Svyatoslavich, grandson of Vsevolod, seeing that the Polotsk men had trampled Mstislav’s banners, returned to them.

And seeing Mstislav taken captive, he rejoiced and asked for him from Prince Boris of Drutsk, and sent word to his uncle Yaroslav in Chernigov and to his brothers, telling them: “I have taken Mstislav Romanovich captive and defeated their army.”

And afterward he himself returned to Chernigov, and brought with him Mstislav Romanovich, the kinsman by marriage of Grand Prince Vsevolod.

About the Novgorodians

In the same year the Novgorodians drove Prince Yaroslav, Vsevolod’s brother-in-law, away from them; he went to New Torg, and the Novotorzhians received him with a petition, and the good Novgorodians pitied him, while the wicked rejoiced.

The Novgorodians sent to Chernigov to Yaroslav for his son, but at that time there was a feud there with the clan of Vladimir, and Yaroslav did not then let his son go to them, and they had no prince in Novgorod all winter.

Yaroslav ruled in Torzhok and collected tribute in that district, and above the Msta, beyond the Portage, he collected tribute from the smerds at his own will.

On the Quarrel between Rurik and His Son-in-Law Roman over His Daughter

In the year 6705 from Adam (1205 from Christ), Roman Mstislavich decided to send away Rurik’s daughter, intending to tonsure her.

Rurik sent to Grand Prince Vsevolod, saying: “Brother and in-law! Roman has deserted us and has kissed the cross to the Olgovichi, and the Olgovichi kissed the cross to me that neither they nor we would start war until they had come to agreement with you and with me and with my brother David; but they have broken their oath, transgressed the cross-kissing, conquered the Smolensk district, and taken your kinsman by marriage Mstislav Romanovich captive and brought him to Chernigov.
