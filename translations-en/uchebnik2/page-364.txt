He gives him good counsel to build a stone church in the name of our Most Pure Lady Theotokos and ever-Virgin Mary, prophesying thus. On the Prophecy of the Saint. "If, my son, you obey me and raise up a temple of the Most Pure Theotokos, then you will give me rest in your city; you yourself will be glorified more than other princes, and you will glorify your sons and grandsons for eternal times. And this city will be the most famous of all Russian cities, and hierarchs will live in it, and hands will be upon the shoulders of its enemies, and my bones will be laid in it." The prince received these words with joy from his teacher and decided to begin building the church building. Construction began, and day by day the building grew. The saint himself labored at it every day and hastened. And both of them continually abided in spiritual joy. The prince in all things followed the counsels of his teacher and rendered honor to his spiritual father, according to the command of the Lord, who said: "He who hears you hears Me, and he who receives you receives Me." And the hierarch also cared for the prince, his spiritual son, spiritually and bodily, as Paul said: "Who shall separate us from this love in Christ?" Such was the holy love of God in them!

When the church had only just been founded, by God's revelation the saint was granted to foresee his imminent death. And he began with his own holy hands to prepare a tomb for himself near the holy altar. And when he had finished, again he had a vision announcing to him the end of his life and his departure to God, whom he had loved from youth. And, filled with joy, he awaited his departure from this difficult, vain, and swiftly passing life.

One day he entered the church and began to celebrate the divine service. Having prayed for the Orthodox tsars and princes, and for his spiritual son whom he loved—I mean the pious Prince Ivan—and for all the multitude of pious Christians of the whole Russian land, and having prayed also for the departed, he partook of the holy mysteries.

When he came out of the church, he summoned all the church clergy and the people and began to teach them, as he usually did for all who came to him. And from that hour he continually gave alms to all: to the poor, to strangers, to the indigent, and also to monasteries and churches, because he already knew of his departure from the world. And the hour came: he summons a certain man named Protasius, whom the prince had appointed elder of the city, for the prince was not then in the city.
