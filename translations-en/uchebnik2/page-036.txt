The most reverend Clement, Metropolitan of Kiev, forbade him, saying: “Do not go yourself, and do not send the army.” This displeased Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich.

And going out, he summoned to himself all the boyars and his druzhina, and all the people of Kiev, and said to them the same as to the metropolitan: that “I have gathered,” he said, “with all the princes and with a great host, both Smolenskians and Novgorodians and Berendei.” But the people of Kiev said to him: “Do not trust the Davidoviches, prince, and do not go on the campaign with them, and do not unite against your uncle Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, for we do not wish to wage war against the kin of the Vladimiroviches Monomakh; but if you wish to make war on the Davidoviches, then we will go with you.” But he did not listen to any of them, and said: “Whoever wishes, let him come with me.”

And he went himself, having gathered a great host, and crossed the Dnieper River, and, coming, encamped above the Chertoryya; and he sent a wise man faithful to him, named Uleb, to the city of Chernigov, to inquire secretly here and there about all matters and to report secretly to him, telling no one.

He himself went to Suboy, while he left his brother Prince Vladimir Mstislavich in Kiev. The wise Uleb entered Chernigov, and having secretly searched here and there, he learned that Grand Prince Vladimir Davidovich of Chernigov, his brother Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodich had kissed the Life-Giving Cross to Grand Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich and to Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, wishing to kill by treachery Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev.

The wise Uleb learned all this truly, and at once quickly ran to his lord, Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev; and he came to him at Suboy and told him of all that had happened: that “all have united,” he said, “against you: both the Chernigov princes and the Ryazan princes, and Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh of Suzdal, and they soon expect the Suzdal princes to join them, and all have agreed to be of one mind, and they wish to kill you by treachery.”

He was terrified and turned back, and sent envoys to them in Chernigov, and exposed their design, and broke the kissing of the Cross with them; and ahead of himself he sent to Kiev, to his brother Prince Vladimir Mstislavich: “Let him go to our father Clement the Metropolitan, having summoned the people of Kiev, and let our father the Metropolitan hear, and my brother Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, and all the men of Kiev, about the falsehood of the Chernigov, Ryazan, and Suzdal princes, how they plotted against me and set a snare for me, wishing to catch me in it, and how they hoped to gain much profit.
