By the mercy of God, the Ecumenical Patriarch and Archbishop of Constantinople, Sir Philotheus, in the Holy Spirit, to Sergius, fellow laborer of our humility: grace, peace, and blessing! I have heard of your virtuous life, of how you glorify God, and, as the Lord says: “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven.” I have heard of the life of you and your brethren; I praise you, but only one thing is still lacking among you: you have not established a coenobitic community. For even the prophet David himself, who comprehended all things with his mind, praises nothing so much as this, saying: “Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!” Therefore I also give you good counsel: listen to our humility and establish a coenobitic monastery, and may the mercy of God and of the Most Pure Mother of God and of all the saints be upon you, and may our blessing and prayer abide with you.

The Venerable Abbot Sergius asked Metropolitan Alexei what he thought and how he would command: should there be communal or eremitic life? Metropolitan Alexei answered thus: “Since Patriarch Philotheus commands and blesses it, I also bless it.” And he sent him away in peace.

He, having come to his monastery, “which is in Radonezh,” told the brethren about the letter of Philotheus, Patriarch of Constantinople, and about the counsel of blessed Alexei, Metropolitan of all Rus’. They joyfully said: “And we, father, desire communal life.” The Venerable Sergius rejoiced at their unanimity and common consent and understood that this was God’s decision. And he assigned the brethren to services: one as cellarer, another as sub-cellarer, one as treasurer, one as precentor, some as refectory servants, others as cooks and bakers, and others to serve the sick. And all property, and all that was in the monastery, they made common, and no one was permitted to have anything personal or to call it his own, but all possessed it in common. Some who did not wish to live thus quietly left the monastery. And from that time communal life was established in the monastery of Saint Sergius, “which is in Radonezh,” to the glory of the Most Holy Trinity.

On the Founding of the Monastery on the Kerzhach

On one of the days when Vespers was being sung in Sergius’s monastery, on a Saturday, Saint Sergius was serving as priest in the altar; the aforementioned brother of Sergius, Stephen, standing in the choir, asked the canonarch: “Who ordered you to take this book?” He answered: “Abbot Sergius ordered me.” Stephen began to say: “Who is abbot here? Was it not I who came here before everyone and built the church here?” And he spoke other absurd words. Blessed Sergius, who was in the altar, heard this, but said nothing to anyone. And when all the brethren went from the church to the refectory for supper, the saint did not go.
