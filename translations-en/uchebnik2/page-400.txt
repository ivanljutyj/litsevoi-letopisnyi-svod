In that same year the Grand Prince of Lithuania, Olgerd Gediminovich, sent to the Horde to Tsar Janibek, son of Azbyak, his brother Koriad and others to ask for help against Grand Prince Simeon Ivanovich.

Hearing of this, Grand Prince Simeon Ivanovich sent Feodor Glebovich to the Tsar in the Horde, and with him his kilichei Feodor Shubacheev and Amin, with a complaint against Olgerd, saying thus: “Olgerd has ravaged your uluses and taken captives; moreover, he wants to lead us all away into servitude to himself and to lay waste your ulus to the end. And thus having grown rich, Olgerd Gediminovich wants to oppose you.” The Tsar, hearing that Olgerd was laying waste his uluses, taking people captive, and wanted to be his adversary, blazed with the fire of fury, and he handed over the Lithuanian ambassadors, Olgerd’s brother Kiriad and those with him, to the kilichei of the Grand Prince.

The Tsar’s ambassador Totui brought them to Moscow. And by the Tsar’s command Ambassador Totui handed over Olgerd’s brother Kariad and his Lithuanian retinue to Grand Prince Simeon Ivanovich.

In that same year Magnus, King of Sweden, sent his ambassadors to Vasily, Vladyka of Novgorod, and to the posadniks of Novgorod, and to all the Novgorodians, saying: “Send your philosophers to a congress, let them dispute about the faith according to the books, and whichever side wins, we shall all be in that faith. But if you do not wish it, then, having called God to help, I want to go to war against you.” Archbishop Vasily, Vladyka of Novgorod, and all the Novgorodians answered thus: “Long ago our forefathers received the faith from the Greeks, and it is the right and true faith. Our forefathers, grandfathers, and fathers held to this faith, and we also hold to this faith, and we observe the laws according to the Greek law, which we received from the Greeks. And we do not dispute with anyone, because we know that our faith is right and true, which we received from the Greeks, and we observe it and its laws. But if you wish to dispute about the faith, send to Constantinople to the patriarch, and let him answer you. And if there is any offense to you from us, then tell us; and whatever offense there may be to us from you, we will inform you of it. And let us live among ourselves peacefully and calmly, in truth.” And they sent to the King the tysyatsky Avram and Kozma Tverdislavich. And the King was then on Berezovy Island. And when they came to Orekhovets, the people of Orekhovets did not let the tysyatsky Avram go, and Kozma went to the King. And the King said to him: “I have no offense from you, only let us unite in one faith and be in unity, in counsel, in unanimity, in love and peace. If you do not do so, then I will go to war against you.”
