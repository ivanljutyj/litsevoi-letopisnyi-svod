And Kotyak went to his son-in-law Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich of Galich with a multitude of gifts: horses, camels, buffaloes, and other things, and Prince Kotyak bestowed gifts on all the Russian princes, bowing to them with the words: “Today the Tatars have taken our land, and tomorrow they will come and take yours; we have told you this; and you, having learned of it, help us.”

Mstislav Mstislavovich of Galich sent messengers to his brothers with the words: “Let us help the Cumans; if they join with the Tatars, it will be harder for us.”

The princes began to think how to help Kotyak and go out to battle against the godless Moabites; and they undertook to help Kotyak. There was a council of all the princes in the city of Kiev, and they made this decision: “It would be better for us to meet the Tatars on foreign land than on our own.”

And they sent envoys to Vladimir to Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich for help.

Prince Yuri sent to them in help his nephew Prince Vasilko Konstantinovich of Rostov.

And all the Russian princes began to gather countless troops: Grand Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Kiev, grandson of Rostislav, and the Chernigov prince, Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich of Galich; these were the senior princes of the Russian land; with them were the younger princes: Daniil, son of Roman Mstislavovich, and Prince Mikhail, son of Vsevolod Chermny, of Chernigov, and Prince Vsevolod, son of Mstislav Romanovich, great-grandson of Rostislav, and many other princes; at that time the Cuman prince Basty was also baptized.

Many Russian princes, having united with all the Russian people, went against the Tatars; and they came to the Dnieper, to the city of Zarub, to the Varangian island.

Having learned of this, the Tatars sent their envoys to them with this speech: “We are all people, and all the race of Adam; why shed your blood in vain and without cause in quarrels and battles? For we have not come against you and have taken nothing of yours, but have come against our slaves, the Cumans, since the Cumans have long been our grooms. Why then do you go to war and bloodshed against us? If you wish good for yourselves, be at peace with us, and drive the Cumans away from you and do not accept them.”

The Russian princes did not heed this, but even killed the Tatar envoys.

And the Russian princes themselves went against the godless Moabites and, not having reached the city of Oleshye, stopped by the Dnieper.
