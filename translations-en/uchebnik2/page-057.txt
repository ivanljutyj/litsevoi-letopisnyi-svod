His retinue, and, taking his spear, he went into battle with his retinue; and he was the first of all to engage the enemy, and he broke his spear, and then his horse was wounded under him in the nostrils; he began to plunge this way and that, and his helmet fell from him, and his shield fell from him; but by God’s protection and that of the Most Pure Mother of God, and by the prayers of his parents, he was preserved unwounded.

Both regiments joined battle, and the slaughter was fierce and hard; and there they killed the good, meek, and gentle Grand Prince of Chernigov, Vladimir Davydovich, and a great multitude was slain there on both sides, and many Cumans were killed. But the Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, was gravely wounded and knocked from his horse, and his own men, not recognizing him, wanted to kill him; but he took off his helmet, and they recognized him, and mounted him on a horse, rejoicing over him.

The Grand Prince of Pereyaslavl, Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomash, seeing his misfortune, wept and fled to Pereyaslavl with his children, while the Olegovichi fled to their own lands; the Cumans also fled to their own lands.

The Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, with his uncle, the Grand Prince of Kiev, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash, returned to Kiev, while Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, taking his brother, the Grand Prince of Chernigov, Vladimir Davydovich, who had been killed in the battle, went to Chernigov, and they laid him in the holy Church of the Savior, and he himself sat on the throne of the grand principality in Chernigov.

Likewise Izyaslav Mstislavich, having learned that Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky was in Pereyaslavl, went with his uncle Vyacheslav and with his brother Svyatopolk and with the Berendei, and, coming, he halted at the village of Mazheva, and from there he came to fight beneath the city, and he burned the suburbs, and many fell on both sides.

Then the Grand Princes of Kiev, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomash and Izyaslav Mstislavich, sent to the Grand Prince of Suzdal, Yuri Vladimirovich Monomash Dolgoruky, saying: “Go to your grand principality in Suzdal; be content with your own, and do not seek what belongs to others; who taught you to commit such violence and seize what belongs to others? Go then to Suzdal, and if you wish, install your son in Pereyaslavl; for we cannot live here with you, because you bring the Cumans against us.”

He, having no help from anywhere, because some of his soldiers were killed and others captured, thus against his will kissed the life-giving cross before them, together with his children. And he went to the Alta, for then came the feast of the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb, and in Pereyaslavl he left his son, Prince Gleb.

Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky went from the Alta to Suzdal; his father, Prince Yuri Dolgoruky, tried to restrain him, but he did not obey his father.
