In the year 6664 from Adam (the year 1164 from Christ)

Theodosius, abbot of the Caves, reposed. In the same year the patriarch in Constantinople appointed Constantine as metropolitan of all Rus in Kiev and sent him from his presence in Constantinople to Kiev.

The Bishop of Novgorod, Nifont, heard from merchants who had come from Novgorod that in Constantinople the patriarch, in the cathedral church of Holy Sophia, had appointed Constantine metropolitan in the great city of Kiev and over all Rus; and hearing this, he rejoiced and did not consider himself on earth, but as if flying through the air on wings, with swiftness in the joy of his spirit he made his journey to the great and glorious city of Kiev, mother and head of all the Rus cities, to see and bow down and receive blessing from the most reverend and most holy Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed in Constantinople by the Patriarch of Constantinople. For that Bishop Nifont was a champion and zealot for holy and divine things, and was in agreement with the most reverend and most holy Patriarch of Constantinople.

There was such disorder among the great princes of Kiev, and many wars, murders, and bloodsheds took place, for all strove and aspired to the great principality of Kiev, since Kiev was the glory, and honor, and mother, and head of all the Rus land, and therefore all strove to possess it, all the princes, who were one family and of one tribe; from this much disorder occurred, and hardship for the most reverend metropolitans of Kiev and all Rus. The most reverend Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, departed to the patriarch in Constantinople because of a certain unrest, and when he was there he heard of even greater unrest in Kiev and throughout the Rus land, and therefore decided not to return to his see in Kiev.

Then, after many turmoils, Izyaslav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, sat on the great principality in Kiev. And time passed, but there was no metropolitan in Rus, and over this there was great sorrow and grief for all. Among the Rus princes there was no unanimity, but disagreement and enmity.

And the great prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, because of many turmoils did not wish to send to Constantinople, but consulted with his six bishops, and they appointed as metropolitan in Kiev and over all Rus the monk Clement the Philosopher, a man of Smolensk, who had been a silent monk; and at this many of the priests and monastics, and of the laity, and of the princes were indignant and hated the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich even more, and with them also Nifont, Bishop of Novgorod, whom Metropolitan Clement compelled to serve with him; and the bishop said to him: “Not with blessing was he chosen and appointed, but apart from the holy Patriarch of Constantinople, not as the law had been from the beginning.” In this also the prince—
