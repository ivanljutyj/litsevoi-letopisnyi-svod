…of Suzdal, Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, for the offense to Novgorod, and with him his brother, Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich of Smolensk, and all the Novgorodians, and Pskovians, and Berendei, and Galicians, and the commanders of the Hungarian and Czech kings, and the Poles with their host. And all gathered at the mouth of the Medveditsa, and from there sent their envoys to Suzdal to Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh. He sent their envoys back to them and sent his own to them; and from there they went along the Volga to Snyatin, and to Ugliche Pole, and to Mologa, even as far as Yaroslavl, warring, cutting down, burning, and took more than seven thousand captives. The Chernigov princes, the Olegovichi and Davidovichi, agreed to join them at the mouth of the Medveditsa; and not reaching the Medveditsa, they halted in Vyatichi, waiting to see what would happen between them and Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh of Suzdal, for spring had already come, and there was great weariness in the host, and their horses had gone lame. And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev dismissed all the host: the Hungarians, Czechs, and Poles, while he himself went with his brother, Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, to Novgorod; and from there he went with his brother, Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich of Smolensk, to Smolensk, and spent the autumn there.

And from there he went to Kiev, and there was great joy in the city of Kiev. Then evil men rose up and began to slander Prince Rostislav Yuryevich to Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, saying: “Prince Rostislav Yuryevich has won over many people, Kievans and Berendei, and wished to sit on the grand princely throne in Kiev; and just as his father, Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, is your enemy, so too his son Rostislav is your enemy, and you keep him to your own head.”

And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev was greatly offended, and after taking counsel with his boyars, he took from him all his cities and volosts, and wealth, and property, and weapons, and horses; and he seized all his boyars and servants, and some he bound in iron fetters and shut in stone dungeons, and set strong guards over them, while others he dispersed to other cities and shut in dungeons.

And Prince Rostislav Yuryevich he despoiled and put into a boat, and sent to his father in Suzdal, to Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, with only three others. And he, coming to his father Prince Yuri, told all that had befallen him; and his father, Prince Yuri, was greatly offended at this and said to those present: “What, do I and my children have no share in the Russian land?”

And he began to gather a host and sent to Grand Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich and to his nephew, Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, saying thus concerning him: “He is your enemy: he killed your brother and seeks your heads; he is also an enemy to me and to my children, and seeks our heads; let us go, having joined—”
