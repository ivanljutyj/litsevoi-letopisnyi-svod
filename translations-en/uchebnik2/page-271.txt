But the all-good and most merciful Lover of Mankind, God, according to His will, preserved and defended His people from the evil design of foreigners; and the foolish labored in vain, without God’s command; and even before their arrival a report came that the Swedes were going to Ladoga.

And the king sent his envoys to Alexander Yaroslavich with these words: “If you can resist me, then behold, I have already come to you, and I want to take you captive; and you shall be my slave, you and your sons.”

But Prince Alexander Yaroslavich heard these words, and his spirit rose within him, and Alexander entered the Church of Holy Sophia, and knelt before the holy altar, and prayed with tears to the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God, that they might help him, and then said: “You, Lord God, who commanded that no one violate another’s borders, be judge, Lord, for this man too, and forbid him to violate another’s borders.” Then Alexander rose and went to bow to Archbishop Spiridon, and received his blessing, and left the church.

And Alexander strengthened his army and said: “God is not in power, but in truth,” and, mounting his horse, he went against the enemies; and he did not manage to send word to his father, because the enemies had drawn near; and many Novgorodians also did not manage to gather, since Alexander set out against the enemies quickly. He came upon them on Sunday, the 15th day of the month of July, the day of the memory of the 630 holy fathers of the Council of Chalcedon, and the day of the memory of the holy martyrs Cyricus and Julitta, and of the holy great prince Vladimir, who baptized all the Russian land, receiving in holy baptism the name Vasily.

About Pelgusius the Izhorian

Among Alexander’s commanders was one man, an elder in the land of Izhora, by name Pelgusius, and in baptism Philip. To him was entrusted the duty of being a lookout on the sea.

Pelgusius had great faith in the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb; and he lived among his relatives, who were pagans; and on Wednesday and Friday he ate and drank nothing; for this God granted him to see a fearful vision, which we shall briefly relate.

This same Pelgusius saw the enemy army and went to Prince Alexander Yaroslavich to describe to him the great Varangian host.
