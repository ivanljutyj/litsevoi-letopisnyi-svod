Vladimir Andreevich became angry with him and went to Dorogobuzh, and began to conspire with Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky, and there was great turmoil in the land.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Kiev Mstislav Izyaslavich sent his son Prince Roman to Novgorod to reign, but Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, the Novgorodians did not accept. But his brothers Prince David and Prince Roman Rostislavichi, and Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky, son of Dolgoruky, wished to seat him by force in Novgorod to reign; and so strife was added to strife, and there was much disorder.

And the princes began to unite against the Grand Prince of Kiev Mstislav Izyaslavich, and confirmed themselves by the precious and life-giving cross to be all in concord and unanimity.

Prince Roman Mstislavich, grandson of Izyaslav, great-grandson of Mstislav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, forefather of Yaroslav and great-great-forefather of Great Vladimir, came to Novgorod to the throne on the fourteenth day of April, on the memory of our father among the saints and confessor Martin, Pope of Rome. And the Novgorodians rejoiced that it had come about according to their desire.

In the same year the Novgorodians went with their prince Roman Mstislavich to Polotsk, and having done much evil, returned from the city thirty versts away.

In the same year the Novgorodians also went with their prince Roman Mstislavich with an army to Toropets, and burned villages and volosts, and having taken a great multitude captive, returned home.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Kiev Mstislav Izyaslavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, drove his uncle Prince Vladimir Mstislavich out of Kiev; and he went to the Polovtsians, while Mstislav sat in Kiev.

In the same year Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, imprisoned Polycarp, abbot of the Caves Monastery, over the Lord's feasts, not permitting him to eat cheese, butter, eggs, and milk on the Lord's feasts and on those of the great notable saints when they fall on a Wednesday or Friday, that is, the Nativity of the Most Pure Theotokos, the Entry, the Meeting, the Dormition, and the Transfiguration of the Lord, and the Nativity and Beheading of St John the Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist of the Lord, and the holy Apostles Peter and Paul, and the holy Apostle and Evangelist John the Theologian, and the holy Great Martyrs George and Demetrius and other great notable saints, and from Bright Week to Pentecost on Wednesdays and Fridays to abstain from cheese, milk, eggs, and cow's butter, observing the apostolic rule.
