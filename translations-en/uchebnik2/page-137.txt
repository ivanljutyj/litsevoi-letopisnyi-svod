And so Prince Mstislav prepared for battle together with his Rostovites; Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich also went out against them to battle with the Vladimirites and approached Yuryev. And there the Pereyaslavl men came to him and reported to them the words of the Rostovites. They said: “Lord Prince! God is with you and your truth is with you; there is no guilt in you before them, neither in what the Rostovites want from you nor in what Prince Mstislav Rostislavich wants from you, heeding the counsels of Dobrynya Dolgy and Matvey Butov and other villains of Rostov; moreover, to your brother, Prince Mikhail Yuryevich—and his uncle—nine days have not passed, and he wants to shed blood.”

Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich placed his hope in the Lord God and in His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos, and went out against them, having prepared his regiments for battle. Prince Mstislav Rostislavich took position at Lipitsa; the archers of both regiments began shooting, and then both regiments advanced at a trot and filled the whole Yuryev field; they met near Yuryev between the rivers Gza and Lipitsa. The Lord God and the Most Pure Theotokos helped Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, and he defeated Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his Rostov army, and killed Dobrynya Dolgy and Ivanko Stepanov, and many other Rostov boyars and voivodes, and beat the servants, and captured some alive; and he plundered all the villages of the Rostov boyars.

And Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich returned with his army to Vladimir, while Prince Mstislav Rostislavich, grandson of Mstislav and great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, fled to Rostov, and after staying there a little, fled from Rostov to Novgorod; but the Novgorodians did not receive him, saying: “You went to conquer another principality, and you left us, only your son you left with us”; then they sent his son Svyatoslav out to him, saying: “Go wherever you wish, but live with us no longer.”

He went from Novgorod to Ryazan to his father-in-law Prince Gleb and began to weep and complain to Prince Gleb of Ryazan about Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh; Prince Gleb of Ryazan wished to send envoys concerning his brothers-in-law to Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, offering him peace and love; but they did not wish it, saying: “There will be no peace and love between us and him, because we have suffered much from him.”

Prince Gleb of Ryazan lost his mind, having listened to his brothers-in-law, and went with an army to Moscow, and burned all of Moscow—the city, districts, and villages—and seized everything; Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich went out against him together with the Rostov…
