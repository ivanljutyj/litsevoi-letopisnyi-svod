In the year 6910 from Adam (1410 from Christ)

In September Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent his commanders Ivan Andreevich Uda and Feodor Glebovich with his army to search for Prince Semyon Dmitrievich of Suzdal, to find either him himself, or his princess, or his children, or his boyars hiding in the Tatar lands.

And they came into the Mordvin lands and found Princess Alexandra, wife of Prince Semyon Dmitrievich, in the Tatar land in a place called Tsybirtse, in the church of St Nicholas; this church had been built by the Besermenin Khazi-baba. And they captured Princess Alexandra, wife of Semyon Dmitrievich, there. And, having robbed her, they brought her to Moscow together with her children.

And she remained in confinement at the Beleutov court until Prince Semyon Dmitrievich submitted to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, for at that time he was hiding in the Horde lands from Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow. But when he heard that his princess with her children had been captured and that his treasury had been seized, he sent a petition to the grand prince, submitting to him with compunction and humility and asking for a safe-conduct.

Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow gave him a safe-conduct, and he came from the Horde to Moscow and made peace with the grand prince. And he went from Moscow to Vyatka with his princess and children, being already ill.

And he remained in Vyatka five months, and, becoming still more ill, he reposed on the 21st day of December. This Prince Semyon Dmitrievich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod endured many misfortunes and troubles in the Horde and in Rus; he labored striving to obtain his patrimony, and for eight years tirelessly served four tsars in the Horde: first Tokhtamysh, second Temir-Aksak, third Temir-Kutluy, fourth Shadibek, all the while warring with Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow, wishing to regain his patrimony, the principality of Nizhny Novgorod and Suzdal and Gorodets. For this he labored much, endured many misfortunes and troubles, had no shelter of his own, found no support under his feet, but achieved nothing, for he labored in vain; vain is the salvation of man, for with God all things are possible, but with man nothing.

In that same autumn Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich of Lithuania set out with an army for Smolensk against Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk, stood before Smolensk many days but did not take it. And, having concluded a truce, he returned back, while in Smolensk a tumult began, and many rioters were killed. Then there was also a plague in Smolensk among the people.
