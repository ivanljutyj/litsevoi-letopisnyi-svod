They seized and shackled the metropolitan, and the archpriest Ivan, and the protodeacon Gregory, and the archdeacon German, and the clerk Michael; for they were in their debt. But we are perishing with them without guilt. We also asked the elder of those Franks what they intended to do with us. He answered, saying: “Do not be afraid; whatever is yours, you will all get back.”

Then, little by little, they were satisfied by Metropolitan Pimen, and, having taken an ample payment, they released all of us unharmed.

That day passed, and so on the second day we set out from there.

Then a fair and favorable wind blew, and we sailed over the sea in joy and great gladness. Then on the third day a severe contrary wind arose. And we came into great exhaustion, fearing the sinking of the ship, since even the sailors themselves could not stand and staggered like drunkards.

And we passed the mouth of the Sea of Azov and came out into the great sea. On the sixth day, Saturday, we passed the Caffa liman and Surozh, and sailing past them, we sailed on well for four days: Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday.

Then on the fifth day, Thursday, a very strong contrary wind blew, and it carried us over the sea to the left toward the city of Sinope, and we entered the liman near the city of Sinope. And some people came out from the city of Sinope, visited us, and entertained us well with food and wine. And we stayed there two days.

Then a fair favoring wind blew, and we sailed near the shore. There were very high mountains there, so that clouds moving through the air stretched down to the middle of those mountains. Then, having gone a little way from there, under the same mountains, opposite the city of Amastris, we began the fast for St Peter’s Day.

And so, sailing on Tuesday, we passed Pandoraclia. On Wednesday a strong contrary wind blew. And again we returned to Pandoraclia.

And we stayed there in Pandoraclia for nine days. There is a church of St Theodore Tiro; his martyrdom took place there, and his tomb is in it.

Then from there we set out in sandals for Tsargrad on the Nativity of St John the Forerunner.

And on the next morning, Friday, sailing, we passed the city of Diopolis; on Saturday we dined at the mouth of the Sakhar River.

On Sunday we passed the city of Daphnusius and the city of Karphia.
