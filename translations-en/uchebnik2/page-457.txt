In the same year Metropolitan Cyprian came to Rus from Constantinople, appointed to the metropolitan see by Philotheus, Patriarch of Constantinople.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich did not accept him, saying to him: “We have Metropolitan Alexis, so why are you having yourself installed in the place of a living metropolitan?” He left Moscow for Kiev and remained there to live. In the same year there was a pestilence in Kiev.

In the year 6885 from Adam (the year 1385 from Christ)

Grand Prince Olgerd Gediminovich of Lithuania died, after which his land grew weak, and disorder and revolts began in it.

Olgerd Gediminovich was not the only son of his father, nor the firstborn, but the second after the firstborn. After him there were other brothers as well. Their names were: 1. Narimant; 2. Olgerd; 3. Evnutiy; 4. Keistut; 5. Koriad; 6. Lyubort; 7. Montovit.

Among his brothers Olgerd surpassed all in power and rank, for he did not love drunkenness, was never drunk, did not drink wine, and did not love amusements or anything unbecoming to the governing of the state, but always remained in wisdom and temperance; therefore he was prudent, fond of wisdom, strong, and brave, and gained much experience. By his wisdom he conquered many countries and lands, subdued many cities and principalities, and concentrated great power in his hands. By this his principality increased, and none of his brothers did the like, nor his father Gedimin, nor his grandfather.

Olgerd Gediminovich had twelve sons: five by his first wife and seven by his second wife, Uliana of Tver. These were the sons by his first wife: 1. Andrei; 2. Dmitry; 3. Konstantin; 4. Vladimir; 5. Feodor. And by his second wife, Uliana of Tver: 1. Koribut; 2. Skorigailo; 3. Yagailo; 4. Svidrigailo; 5. Korigailo; 6. Minigailo; 7. Lugveniy.

To all his sons Olgerd gave cities and principalities as possessions, and he entrusted the throne of his grand principality to his son Yagailo, who was born of Uliana of Tver, because he loved him more than all his sons, chose him from among his brothers, and entrusted to him his throne—the Grand Principality of Lithuania.

And Yagailo ruled over the whole Lithuanian land, and then four years later he married in the Polish land, taking as his wife a certain princess who had neither father nor mother; through her Yagailo received the kingdom in the Polish land.

And therefore Yagailo was named king of the Polish land and began to rule there.
