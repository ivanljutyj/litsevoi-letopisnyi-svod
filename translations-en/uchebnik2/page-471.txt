And throughout the whole metropolis he began to collect the Petrine and Nativity dues from the churches, collected the metropolitan revenues, taxes, and quitrents, lived and ruled. And he wished to go to Constantinople to the patriarch, so that he would appoint him metropolitan of Rus, and began to consider how to achieve this.

And he began to say to the grand prince: “It is written in the apostolic canons: ‘Two or three bishops appoint one.’ Let five or six Russian bishops gather and appoint me bishop and chief hierarch.”

Hearing this, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich with the boyars supported Mityai. The Russian bishops gathered and, after their arrival, received blessing from Mityai.

Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, also came, indignant, and said to the grand prince: “Who permitted you to violate the laws?! It is not fitting to do such a thing!” Mityai, however, designated as metropolitan, seeing that all opposed him and that his plan was failing, was troubled and wavered. Moreover, certain foolish people set them at odds and informed the designated metropolitan Mityai against Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, and Dionysius against Mityai.

Then the designated metropolitan Mityai sent to Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, saying: “Why have you come to my city and not come to me to bow and receive blessing? You did not show me honor and treated me with contempt as one of the lowest people.

Do you not know who I am and what power I have over you and throughout the whole metropolis?” Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, came to him and said: “You sent to me saying that you have power over me, but you have no power over me; it is rather for you yourself to come to me, bow to me, and receive blessing from me. I am the bishop, while you are a priest. If you have authority throughout the whole metropolis and the right to judge, tell us truly on the basis of the Divine Scriptures, who is higher: a bishop or a priest?” Mityai answered: “You called me a priest, but I will not make you even a priest! Wait a little until I return from the patriarch in Constantinople.” There was much quarreling and noise between them.

Upon learning of this, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich became angry with Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal. Mityai remained at the metropolitan court in Moscow for a whole year and six months, ruling over all as a metropolitan. Dionysius, bishop of Suzdal, wished to go to Constantinople, since he himself wanted to be metropolitan; his desire did not remain hidden from Mityai, and Mityai advised the grand prince: “Forbid Dionysius to go to Constantinople, so that he does not harm my election there.” The grand prince ordered Dionysius to be forcibly detained.
