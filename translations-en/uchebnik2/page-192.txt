Many Poles approached Vladimir; and the king, having crossed the mountains, heard that the Poles were going to Vladimir, and that the Olgovichi with the Cumans had already entered their land, and the king stopped, not moving from the place.

And the Olgovichi, the Cumans, and the Poles heard that the king was standing not far from them, and they did not dare go to Galich, and they stood many days in one place; neither did the king approach Galich, nor did the Olgovichi. And they began negotiations for peace and love, and, having made peace, they returned to their own lands. The Galicians, fearing that the king would leave them for his own land, said: "Woe will be to us when the Olgovichi return to us; they will do us even more harm, and we have no prince."

And they decided secretly to send to the Olgovichi's regiment, to Prince Vladimir Igorevich; and Prince Vladimir Igorevich received the Galicians' messenger and at night slipped away with his regiment from his brothers, galloped to Galich, since the regiments were standing two days' journey from Galich.

But the king ordered them to take as their prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich from Pereyaslavl; they waited for him two weeks and did not wait, because Yaroslav Vsevolodovich delayed, gathering with his men.

And he galloped from Pereyaslavl to Galich, and learned that Prince Vladimir Igorevich had entered Galich three days before him; he was greatly offended and returned back to Pereyaslavl.

The Olgovichi and all the princes learned that their brother Prince Vladimir Igorevich had slipped away from them at night and had gone to Galich for the great reign; they were surprised at this and went to Kiev, and, having come to Kiev, Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny sat on the great reign in Kiev, and relying on his large army, he began to install his governors in all the Kiev cities.

But Grand Prince Rurik Rostislavich, seeing his misfortune, returned to his Ovruch; his son Rostislav went to Vyshgorod, and Mstislav Romanovich sat in Belgorod.

Also afterward Grand Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny sent to Pereyaslavl to Yaroslav, son of Vsevolod, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, saying: "Go from Pereyaslavl to your father in Suzdal, and do not attempt to seize Galich, which is under the authority of my brothers; if you wish to obtain it, you will destroy yourself; if you delay, remaining in Pereyaslavl, then I will go against you with an army, and then do not blame me; for it is I who show you mercy." Yaroslav Vsevolodovich was very frightened, he received help from nowhere, and he sent to him with a request, asking that he be given an unobstructed road to reach his father Vsevolod Yuryevich; and he gave him free passage.
