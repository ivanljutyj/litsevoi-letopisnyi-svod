“…you understand that our humility is better than the rudeness of the Muscovite prince Dmitry.”

Thus Oleg of Ryazan and Jogaila of Lithuania plotted among themselves: “And when Prince Dmitry hears the tsar’s name and our oath to him, he will flee from Moscow to distant places, either to Great Novgorod or to the Dvina; and we shall sit in Moscow and Vladimir. And when the tsar comes, we shall meet him with great gifts and entreat him to return home, and we shall divide the Muscovite principality between ourselves, by the tsar’s command, into two parts: one part to Vilna, one part to Ryazan; and the tsar will give us charters and to our family after us.” So they plotted in their madness, not remembering what was said: “If you do evil to your neighbor, you will receive the same yourself”; and it is also said: “Do no evil to your neighbor and do not dig a pit under him, lest God cast you into worse.”

And envoys came to Tsar Mamai from Oleg, prince of Ryazan, and from Jogaila, prince of Lithuania, with gifts and letters. And Tsar Mamai received the gifts with love, and listened to the letters, and honored the envoys and sent them away, and wrote to Jogaila, prince of Lithuania, and to Oleg of Ryazan thus: “If you desire my ulus, the Russian land, I grant you all of it, my sworn men and ulus subjects. But only keep an oath to me that is not false, and meet me with your forces wherever you are able, for the sake of honor and of my majesty; for I do not need your help, but I would wish by my own strength to capture ancient Jerusalem, as Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, and Antiochus, king of Antioch, and Titus, king of Rome. But for the sake of your grievances, and giving you honor by my majesty, I grant you, my ulus subjects, and will deliver you from violence and wrong, and soothe your sorrow, if you will have an unfeigned oath and adherence to me. And then only will my ulus subject, the Muscovite Prince Dmitry, be terrified by the name of my majesty and flee to distant and impassable places; and your names, of my ulus subjects, will be glorified in those lands, and due honor will be magnified to my name, and the fear of my majesty guards and governs my uluses, and does not permit anyone to be wronged without my royal command. But to take captive and to conquer is not fitting for me myself, the great tsar: for with my royal majesty and so many countless forces and strong and valiant warriors, it is not for me to conquer this one, for he is my ulus subject and servant, and the fear of me alone is sufficient for him. But it befits me to conquer one like myself, some great, strong, and glorious tsar, as Tsar Alexander of Macedon conquered Darius, king of Persia, and Porus, king of India; such a victory is worthy of my royal name, and my majesty will be glorified in all lands. So tell this to your princes, my ulus subjects and unfeigned sworn men.”
