And Prince Andrey left his princess in Kolyvan, while he himself went overseas to the Swedish land; and the Swedish Master met him and received him with honor.

Prince Andrey sent for his princess to Kolyvan, and remained for some time, with his princess, in the Swedish land, and then crossed back to this side.

And the Tatars pursued Prince Andrey and overtook him at the city of Pereyaslavl, which is on Lake Kleshchino; but God preserved Prince Andrey, and the Most Pure Mother of God; and the Tatars, pursuing him, darted hither and thither, while he passed between them; and he was on the grand-princely throne for three years.

And the commander Nevryuy with his Tatars took the city of Pereyaslavl, which is on Lake Kleshchino, and seized Yaroslav's princess with her children, and killed them; and some of Yaroslav's children they led away captive; and they killed their commander Zhidislav; and they led all the people away captive, and took all the property, and went to the Horde.

And the Pskovians then, in the year 6761 from Adam (the year 1261 from Christ), defeated the Germans under Pskov.

In the year 6762 from Adam (the year 1262 from Christ)

The Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich of Tver left the place that had come to him from his father, Tver, and went to Pskov; and the Pskovians received him with great honor.

...They carried the body of Prince Konstantin Yaroslavovich of Uglich to Vladimir; and his brother Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich met it, with his spiritual father, Metropolitan Kirill, and with all the priests, and with all his boyars, and with a multitude of people; and they laid him in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God in Vladimir.

In the same year, 6763 from Adam (the year 1263 from Christ), the Novgorodians took Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich of Tver from Pskov and made him their prince in Novgorod.

And the Novgorodians drove Prince Vasily Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yury Dolgoruky, out of Novgorod; and Prince Vasily quickly sent envoys to his father Alexander Yaroslavovich in Vladimir, while he himself went to Torzhok and waited there for his father.
