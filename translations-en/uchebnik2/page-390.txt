In the year 6849 from Adam (1349 from Christ)

The Most Reverend Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, went to Novgorod the Great with a multitude of church clergy and with many of his servants. And the burden was heavy—for the Archbishop of Novgorod to support them, and for the monasteries many gifts.

In that year Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich of Vladimir and Moscow sent his viceroys to Novgorod.

That same autumn Tsar Azbyak of the Horde died. He reigned in the Great Horde for 19 years. Under this Tsar Azbyak, by his command, five great Russian princes were killed in the Horde: Grand Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich of Tver, and his two sons, Grand Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich and his brother Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich, Grand Prince Vasily of Ryazan, Grand Prince Ivan Yaroslavich of Ryazan; and three appanage princes: Prince Alexander of Novoselsk, Prince Fyodor Alexandrovich, grandson of the holy blessed Grand Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich of Tver, and Prince Fyodor Ivanovich of Starodub. Under this same Tsar Azbyak, the pious Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich of Moscow was killed in the Horde by Grand Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich of Tver, without the command of Tsar Azbyak. And in all, by Tsar Azbyak in the Horde, nine great and appanage princes were killed.

And after him his elder son Tinbek sat on the throne in the Horde.

And his other son Janibek killed his younger brother Khadyrbek. And for this his elder brother, Tsar Tinbek, grew angry with him, and there was enmity between them.

In that year Grand Prince Gedimin of Lithuania died. His sons: Narimant, Olgerd, Evnutey, Kestutey, Koriad, Lubort, Montivit. After him his second son Olgerd sat on the grand princely throne of Lithuania.

This Olgerd was very wise and spoke many languages, surpassing all others in power and rank; he was temperate in all things and avoided all that is vain: amusements, games, and the like. But he cared for his realm day and night, turned away from drunkenness, and did not drink even wine, mead, beer, or other drinks, so greatly did he hate drunkenness. He had great temperance in all things, and therefore was a man of great intelligence and understanding, and courageous. And through his wisdom he conquered and acquired for himself many lands and countries, cities and principalities, and held vast power. His principality rose above all others. Neither under his father nor under his grandfather had it been so.

In that year Prince Ivan Ivanovich, grandson of David, married the daughter of Dmitry of Bryansk.
