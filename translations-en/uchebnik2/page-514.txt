And discord was found among them, and they did not wish to help, not remembering the prophet David, who says: “How good and how fair it is for brothers to live in concord,” and another, ever remembered, who said: “Those who help one another, and a brother who aids a brother, are like a fortified city.” There was among them disagreement and distrust, and the Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich learned and understood of it, that among his princes and boyars and throughout all his host there was discord and strife, and moreover a depletion of the host; for the whole Russian land was depleted by the battle against Mamai beyond the Don, and all Russian people were in great fear and trembling because of the depletion of people. The Grand Prince himself was in perplexity and reflection and did not wish either to stand against the tsar himself or to raise his hand against him. And the Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich himself went with a few to his city of Pereyaslavl, and from there he went past Rostov and further still, more swiftly to Kostroma.

Tsar Takhtamysh crossed the Oka River, and first of all took the city of Serpukhov, and from there went to Moscow, making war.

And in the city of Moscow there was a great tumult: some wished to flee, and others wished to remain in the city. And when there was tumult and great strife, evil people rose up against one another and committed robbery and great plundering; and those who wished to flee out of the city they did not let go, but killed them and took their wealth and property. Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus, forbade them; but they were not ashamed of him and despised him, and they insulted the Grand Princess Evdokia. The metropolitan and the Grand Princess began to beg them to let them out of the city, but they would not let them go; they did not fear the great boyars, but threatened everyone and stood at all the city gates with drawn weapons, and threw stones from the city gates, letting no one out of the city.

Likewise afterward, with great entreaty they were prevailed upon, and only then did they let out of the city Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus, and the Grand Princess Evdokia, and the others with them; but even these they robbed, and there was a great tumult in the city of Moscow. Likewise afterward there came to them into the city a certain Lithuanian prince named Ostey, grandson of Olgerd. He strengthened the city and the people and shut himself in the city in siege with a multitude of people, with those who remained.

And there were many refugees who had come running from the volosts and other cities, and many of them gathered at that time, and foreign boyars, and Surozhans, and cloth merchants, and other merchants, and archimandrites, and hegumens, and archpriests, presbyters and deacons, and monks, and people of every age, male and female, with infants.
