He, hearing this, fled as fast as he could beyond the Desna River; and they pursued him beyond the Desna and, not catching him, returned, because Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich with the Polovtsians had fled into the steppe.

In the same year Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich with the Polovtsians ravaged the Smolensk domains; then too he married his nephew Prince Svyatoslav Vladimirovich to the daughter of Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal Andrei Bogolyubsky, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh.

In the same year Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich came against Prince Svyatoslav Vladimirovich and besieged him in the town of Vereshchin. He fought them stoutly from the town and sent messengers to his uncle, Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, and to his father-in-law, Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal Andrei Bogolyubsky, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh.

Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, hearing of this, began quickly to gather an army, wishing to go to the rescue himself; also his father-in-law, the Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal, sent to his aid his son Prince Izyaslav, his brother-in-law, with a great host; also the Murom princes sent their forces to his aid. They, hearing of the approach against them of Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich with a great host, and also of Prince Izyaslav Andreevich with a great host, and also of the voivode of the Murom prince with a great host, were frightened and made peace with them, and quickly returned to their own lands. Grand Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, hearing that they had made peace, went to the Vyatichi, and Prince Izyaslav Andreevich returned to his father in Rostov.

In the same year the Novgorodians seized their prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich and sent him into exile to Ladoga, and sent his princess into exile in a convent, and plundered all his possessions.

Hearing this, his father, Grand Prince of Kiev Rostislav Mstislavich, was grieved and saddened, and seized all the Novgorodians who were with him in Kiev, and ordered them thrown into dungeons, and many died there; and others he ordered brought out and sent to various towns, and he sent to Novgorod to the Novgorodians, saying: “What I have done to your Novgorodians who were with me in Kiev, so will I do to you, if you do not release to me my son Prince Svyatoslav, and his wife, and his boyars.”

They, seizing his envoys, robbed them, and, having beaten them, sent them into exile; and they sent to Rostov and Suzdal to the Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal, asking him for his son — to reign among them in Novgorod according to their will. He gave them his brother Prince Mstislav, son of Yuri Dolgoruky; but they did not accept him.
