Together with several people he shut himself in the church, took the gold and silver church vessels, carried them to the very top of the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God, and hid them there. He left several people there, while he himself went down, removed the ladders, and stood in the church before the image of the Most Pure Mother of God, weeping and shedding tears. And the Tatars shouted that the church doors should be opened to them. The sacristan stood motionless before the image of the Most Pure Mother of God, paying no heed to the impious Hagarenes. They, the godless, broke down the church doors and entered the church, damaged the wondrous icon of the Most Pure Mother of God, and also plundered the other icons and the church itself.

And the presbyter Patricius, having been taken captive, was questioned about the church treasures and about the people who were with him in the church. But he answered them nothing; even after enduring terrible torments, he revealed nothing to them.

They placed him on a red-hot griddle, drove splinters under his nails, flayed off his skin, and, after breaking his legs, tied him to a horse's tail and dragged him thus. And in such terrible torments he died in the Lord, having received the crown of martyrdom.

The Tatars plundered and burned all the churches, and also burned all the houses; they seized the lands and villages around Vladimir and killed countless people.

And some people they led away into captivity, carrying off with them much wealth, gold, and silver. Those who were in captivity at that time said that they carried off so much wealth that they piled precious clothing and goods like hay into shocks and burned them, being unable to carry away everything they had seized. But they took only gold and silver, ornaments and precious vestments, and countless riches; some things they burned, and the money they divided among themselves by measures. Then, too, in the flames of the fires even the bells melted.
