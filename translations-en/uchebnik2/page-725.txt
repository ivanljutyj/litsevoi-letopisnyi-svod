Photius was afraid, but after some time, gathering his strength, the metropolitan said to him: “Who are you, O man, who has so astonished me? How did you dare enter to me when my doors are closed? For it is written: he who does not enter by the doors is a thief and a robber.” Then the one who had come answered him: “Why do you think so of me? I am not a thief or a robber, nor am I of earthly men; and nothing can hold me back—neither a stone wall nor iron fortress gates; wherever I wish to enter, I enter. I am an angel of God, sent to you from the Lord God Sabaoth, the Almighty. The Lord God commanded me to tell you this for the strengthening of your life: attend to yourself and to your flock, and Christ God gives you a week for the examination of your life and for the governing of the affairs of your flock.” The hierarch listened to the angel with great fear and trembling, and weeping fell at his feet, but he no longer saw the angel of God, for he had become invisible. And from this vision Photius came as it were into an ecstasy of mind, and fell into illness, and lay for a long time, until the cell-attendant monks close to him came and took him in their hands and laid him on a bed like a dead man, unable to say anything or see with his eyes. Then, as soon as his strength returned, the metropolitan with difficulty told the people present about the vision and about everything that had happened to him.

He lived after the appearance of the angel of God one year, three months, and 20 days, and so the week was fulfilled according to the word of the angel of God: seven weeks of seventy days each. And in those days before his end he wrote a discourse setting forth all the events that had occurred throughout his whole life, forgiving all and blessing them, and asking forgiveness of all. And he enjoined that all he had written in his testament be kept, saying: “In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-giving, and Undivided Trinity. I, the servant of God, the humble Photius, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus. By God’s judgments, which God alone knows, I was raised to the great rank of hierarch, though I did not desire it at all. Great is the task of a shepherd and teacher, since he must care not only for himself but even more for his flock, and know the fitting time, humility and height, and good gentleness, and righteous and saving wrath, and many other things that shepherds must know. Knowing all this and seeing my weakness, I did not desire this rank, but desired only a virtuous life; for this reason I did not enter into marriage, and did not bind myself to the world and its vain dreams, but most of all, to me, humble as I am, only one thing was dear and desirable: to save my soul and present myself to my Lord and God, my Creator and Maker of all creation, unstained by the sins of the world.
