When they came to the Horde, Min-Bulat, daruga of the Moscow ulus, took them into his own ulus: he showed great honor to Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, but to his uncle Prince Yuri Dmitrievich, dishonor and great oppression.

But Shirin-Teginya, great prince of the Horde, was well disposed to Prince Yuri; he came and took Prince Yuri from Min-Bulat by force, and promised to give him the great principality. And Shirin-Teginya went with Prince Yuri to winter in the Crimea; but the Grand Prince remained in the Horde with Min-Bulat.

At that time the boyar Ivan Dmitrievich was with Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, and he decided to help Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich. He began to petition the great Horde princes, Prince Aidar and Min-Bulat, and the other Tatar princes on behalf of his sovereign Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, saying to each: "Is this, my lord, your intercession with the tsar and your sure word concerning our sovereign the Grand Prince—that the tsar cannot disregard Teginya's word, but at his word will give the great principality to Prince Yuri? And if it happens thus at his word, what will become of you then? Prince Yuri will be great prince in Moscow, and in Lithuania his sworn brother Grand Prince Svidrigailo, and Teginya in the Horde, and he will have the right to choose the tsar against your will." With these words, like an arrow, he wounded their hearts.

Then all the Horde princes began to petition the tsar on behalf of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich and argued, speaking with him. And they persuaded the tsar, and the tsar said to them: "If Teginya speaks of the great principality for Prince Yuri, I command that he be killed." And when spring came, Teginya returned from the Crimea to the Horde, wishing to give the great principality to Prince Yuri. A certain Tatar named Usein, the tsar's bedchamber attendant and nephew of Shirin-Teginya, told him of the design of the tsar and all the princes: whoever should speak of Prince Yuri would be killed. Hearing this, Shirin-Teginya dared not say anything against them or oppose them.

The tsar then ordered his princes to adjudicate between the Russian princes, and there was a great dispute between them: Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich sought his throne by right of his father and grandfather, while Prince Yuri Dmitrievich, his uncle, sought it by the testimonies of the chroniclers, the old registers, and the testament of his father Grand Prince Dmitry. Then the boyar of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, Ivan Dmitrievich, said to the tsar and his princes: "Sovereign free tsar! Permit me, the slave of the Grand Prince, to speak a word. Our sovereign Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich seeks the throne of his great principality, and your ulus, by your tsarist grace, by your charters concerning tribute and your yarlyks: and here is your grace before you.
