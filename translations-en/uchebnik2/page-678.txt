Saint Athanasius of Sinai said: "Many find salvation who are separated from the body by a bitter death." One saint said: "No one can know the judgments of God; many of the righteous indeed receive a dishonorable and crooked death, while many sinners are parted from the body not by a violent but by a quiet and gentle death." Likewise, concerning such people and all others, the great apostle Peter says: "O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are His judgments and untraceable His ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been His counselor?" On the Cathedral Sunday of the holy fast all performed for the ailing bishop according to custom, and also on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday of that same Cathedral Week he suffered a slight headache, and at midnight his organ of speech fell silent, closed, and speech was extinguished.

And the month of March arrived—the first day according to the book reckoning, but the fifteenth day of February according to the lunar calendar. At dawn on Friday the great prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver came to the hierarch with his brothers, children, and boyars, and commanded the archimandrites, abbots, and priests to administer holy oil to the hierarch, after which all came up to the bishop for a blessing and forgiveness, but he could not utter anything. O fear and horror! He who lived in such glory and with such honors lies voiceless, trembling and suffering from numbness, taking hunger for food, thirst for drink, sickness for rest, sorrow for joy, dishonor for honor, and humiliation for glory. Alas, alas, human existence is so brief! Ten monks sat by him and heard nothing from him except the sighs of illness and the groans of his heart.

That same night, at half past two on the second day of March, on the eve of Saturday, he departed this life and gave up his spirit to the Lord. Much weeping and groaning was heard, but no one could help him except his good deeds. The great prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver with his brothers, boyars, and all the people decided to bury his body in the monastery of the holy fathers Anthony and Theodosius in Zheltikov, founded by the bishop. And they sang funeral hymns over his body in the cathedral church of the Holy Transfiguration of the Lord, and with honors escorted his body to the monastery he had founded, to the church of the Most Pure Mother of God in Zheltikov on the river Tmake.
