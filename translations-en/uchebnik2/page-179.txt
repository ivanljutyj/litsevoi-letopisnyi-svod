He sent a letter to his father, in which he urged him to leave the earthly kingdom and come to him.

And he left the whole earthly kingdom, set his son Stefan as autocrat, and himself took monastic vows in the monastery of Studenica and was named Simeon; he had reigned for forty-two years. He also tonsured his wife Anna in a convent, while he himself went to the Holy Mountain to his son Sava with a great quantity of goods.

Then, having asked his in-law Tsar Alexios, whose daughter Simeon’s son Despot Stefan had married, for the monastery of Hilandar with all its possessions, he adorned it with every kind of building, and sent to Despot Stefan asking him to take the monastery under his protection. Stefan sent much property and donated villages in the Serbian land to the monastery, and endowed it with breeding livestock. Despot Stefan took the monastery under his protection, and for ages the monastery of Hilandar was to belong to the Serbian despots; the tsar gave the monks a charter that no one could enter their monastery, not even the protos of the Holy Mountain, but only the tsar alone.

They themselves settled in their monastery of Hilandar, but after living there a short while the venerable Simeon departed to the Lord, and through the prayers of Saint Sava myrrh flowed from his grave. Later Saint Sava was ordained presbyter, and then archimandrite by the metropolitan of Thessalonica; afterwards, at the request of his brother Despot Stefan, he himself translated the relics of his holy father Simeon and placed them in the monastery of Studenica.

On the Bulgarian Tsardom

The Bulgarian Tsardom under Tsar Basil Porphyrogenitus was part of the Greek realm; in the reign of Alexios there ruled over them Tsar Kaloyan, who separated from the Greek realm and thereby renewed the scepter of his kingdom, and the kingdom, already withered by the violence of the Greeks, flourished, while at that time the Latins were attacking Tsargrad. This Tsar Kaloyan of Bulgaria and Zagora, choosing a suitable moment, destroyed many Greek cities and subdued some under his power; he came proudly to the city of Thessalonica, as in ancient times the Assyrian king Sennacherib came to Jerusalem, wishing to destroy it; but the holy martyr Demetrius killed him at night with a spear as he slept, and his army, taking his body, fled.

There ruled over them another tsar named Boril; he wanted to kill Streza, a kinsman of Kaloyan. He fled to Serbia to Despot Stefan, and Stefan called him his brother, gave him aid, and gave him the city of Prosek; and Stefan asked those rulers of Tsar Kaloyan who held the Greek cities to come with their cities to the aid of Streza, and he called him autocrat.
