In the same year Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, having hostility against Novgorod because of Kostroma and because of the Volga people, set out against Novgorod with an army, with his cousin Prince Vladimir Andreyevich, having gathered many forces on the eve of Philip’s Fast before the Nativity of Christ. And not reaching Novgorod, he halted thirty versts away at Yamny.

And the Novgorodians met him at Yamny with a great host, armed, and sent their envoys to him concerning peace, Ivan Avvakumovich and Ivan Aleksandrovich, with a petition. He did not accept peace.

The Novgorodians sent to him with a petition their lord Archbishop Alexius concerning peace. He came to the grand prince and said: “Lord Grand Prince, I bless you, and Veliky Novgorod petitions you, that you, lord, make peace, and that there be no bloodshed; and for the guilty people Veliky Novgorod petitions you with 8,000 rubles.” The grand prince had strong hostility against Veliky Novgorod and did not listen to the archbishop, but wanted to go against Novgorod.

The archbishop sent ahead of him to Novgorod Klimenty Vasilyevich, the son of a posadnik, saying: “The grand prince has not granted peace, but wants to go against Novgorod, and you be on your guard.”

And then the Novgorodians hastily set up a defensive fort.

And Prince Patrikei Narimantovich and Prince Roman Yuryevich with the Koporye princes were in Novgorod and rode out of Novgorod with the Novgorodians in armor almost as far as Zhilotug on Sunday before noon, and stood there.

And they returned again to the city. And the archbishop came from the grand prince without peace. Therefore there was a great alarm in Novgorod on the fourth day after Epiphany.

A report rushed through the city that Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich stood with all his force at Zhilutug. The Novgorodians, hearing this, began to fear greatly and sent to find out news. They rode about and found no army anywhere.

And then the Novgorodians, from that alarm, with the archbishop’s blessing and by their own counsel, set fire to their suburbs around the city, and 24 great monasteries, and 6 wooden churches, and the Plotnichy End, and the Lyudin End, and the Nerevsky End, and on all the streets outside the city beyond Kopanitsa they burned all the buildings; and there was great loss to Veliky Novgorod. And the monastery of St Nicholas was burned by the soldiers on Monday, but the church was not burned; and they ravaged many districts, and plundered much goods from Novgorod merchants, and led many people, women, and children into captivity.
