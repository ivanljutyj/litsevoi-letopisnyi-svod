“…to you from Christ God, and you will deliver your realm from great misfortunes.” But he did not want peace, relying on the multitude of his army, and said: “I obtained the grand principality of Kiev and Pereyaslavl by great labor and sweat, and now you bid me myself abandon such a dominion.” But Euthymius, Bishop of Pereyaslavl, spoke to him of love and peace, and not of abandoning the grand principality of Kiev.

He went against Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh to Yankino village, where Prince Yuri was standing; and they stood opposite each other from the midday meal until vespers; and then Prince Yuri withdrew, and Prince Izyaslav went after him.

When the sun was setting, both sides came together, and there was a great battle and fierce slaughter; and first the Poroshans fled, then also Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, then the Pereyaslavlians, also the Berendei, also the Novgorodians, also the Galicians, and also the Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, himself fled to the city of Kiev with only two others, on August 23, the feast of the holy martyr Lupus.

And while in Kiev, he gathered with his wife and children, and with his property, and with all his people, and fled to Lutsk. And Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, on the morning after the battle, entered Pereyaslavl, and after staying there three days, went to Kiev, and in Pereyaslavl he installed his son, Prince Rostislav, in the grand principality.

And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, sitting in Lutsk, began to gather an army, Hungarians and Czechs; King Boleslav and his brother Andrich sent him aid.

And Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh joined with his brother Vyacheslav; and a force from Grand Prince Vladimir of Galicia came to his aid, and Prince Vladimir of Galicia himself also went to his aid, and arriving, stood at Shumsk; and many Polovtsians also came to his aid. And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich set out from Lutsk, having gathered with his army, and the Hungarian and Czech aid with him.

And the Hungarians and Czechs heard of the countless army of the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, and were very frightened, and departed from Izyaslav, saying: “Prince! Not all have yet assembled, but against a foe one must assemble with all one’s strength, and now you should make peace with him.”

But the sons of the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, Prince Rostislav with his brother Prince Andrei, went ahead; and when they were at Moravitsa, that night there was a very great panic among them. And out of great fear the Polovtsians also fled with their commander…
