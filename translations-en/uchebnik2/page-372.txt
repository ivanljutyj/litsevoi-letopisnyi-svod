But Constantine, his brother by another mother, gathered a considerable army and sent it against him, ordering him to yield his kingdom to him. “Such a thing has never been since the beginning of time,” he said, “that a blind man should be king.” With these words he sought to reproach him.

Stefan first went and bowed in the cathedral church. And Archbishop Nicodemus adorned his head with the royal crown, and before all the Illyrian people crowned him king, and having gathered, he set out for battle. When the two regiments drew near, being merciful to his brother, his gracious soul sends him a message. The message: “Stefan, by the grace of God king to the Serbs and heir of his father, with the fear of God ruler over people.”

…in battle was defeated, captured by the Bulgarian tsar, and killed. What he had prepared for another, he himself suffered. The Bulgarians, having taken on shamelessness, destroyed many, set that Alexander up as king, and went off home.

Stefan returned victorious, and with all came to the monastery of the Almighty, and rejoiced in its beauty.

Then he founded another monastery, not far from this one, with many cells. And he filled it with well-arranged bedrooms, beds, clothing, and everything necessary for life. And he ordered to gather throughout his kingdom all who suffered from various illnesses, from old age, the decrepit, lepers with festering faces and other parts of the body, all who aroused pity and tears. And he appointed for them a superior from the palace, a good man, to care for them every day, for their food and wine, and to give help to the sick, to relieve their diseases, and he commanded all to serve them.

But he himself often came in the guise of a warrior and gave coins, sometimes even openly stood with them, making prostrations, kissing with tears their much-suffering wounds, conversed with them, comforted them, saying that for temporary sufferings on earth they would receive the [eternal] Kingdom of Heaven. Often he conversed with them at night. It is impossible to describe all his virtues! But let us tell of his death.
