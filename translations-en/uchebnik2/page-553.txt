Not far from there is the place where the Jews killed Archdeacon Stephen, and his blood is still visible on the stone. There too, not far from the church, is the cell of the Most Pure Mother of God, in which the Most Pure lived with John the Theologian. And that cell, quite solid, stands to this day. Behind the cell of the Most Pure Mother of God is the cell of the holy apostle and evangelist John the Theologian.

That same spring, on the tenth day of May, at evening twilight there was an eclipse of the moon for quite a long time, and then it appeared before early dawn. That same spring, on May 16, a son, Konstantin, was born to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, and he was baptized by Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, his elder brother, and by Maria Vasilyeva, the wife of the tysyatsky. Prince Dmitry himself was ill at that time.

On the Life and Repose of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, Tsar of Rus

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was born to the pious and highly revered parents Grand Prince Ivan Ivanovich and mother Grand Princess Alexandra. He was the grandson of Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich, the gatherer of the Russian land, a fruitful branch of the holy root and of the garden planted by God, and of the beautiful flower, Tsar Vladimir, who, like Constantine, baptized the Russian land, and a kinsman of the new wonderworkers Boris and Gleb. He was raised in piety and glory, in spiritual instruction, and from infancy he loved God. When his father, Grand Prince Ivan Ivanovich, departed from the life of this world and passed to the heavenly mansions, he was very young, only nine years old, and he remained with his beloved brother, Prince Ivan Ivanovich. Then he too reposed, and their mother, Grand Princess Alexandra. And he remained alone in the great reign, having received the scepter of the power of the Russian land and inherited the earthly kingdom, his patrimony, the great reign, by the grace given to him from God, in honor and glory. He was still young in age, but he loved the spiritual life, did not love empty conversations and indecent words, avoided people of evil character, and always conversed with the good. He always listened with compunction to the divine Scripture, diligently cared for the churches of God, and steadfastly guarded the borders of the Russian land. He was free from malice as a youth, but in mind like a mature man. In battles he was always fearsome to enemies, and he defeated many who rose against him. He surrounded his glorious city of Moscow with wondrous stone walls. And he became famous throughout the world, rose like a cedar of Lebanon, and flourished like a palm tree. When he turned seventeen, they brought Princess Evdokia from the land of Suzdal as a bride for him, the daughter of Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich and of her mother Grand Princess Anna. And all the Russian land rejoiced at this marriage. In marriage they lived chastely, did not gratify the flesh, with piety.
