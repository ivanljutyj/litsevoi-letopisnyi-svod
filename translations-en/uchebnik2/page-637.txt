Another of his sons, Prince Mikhail, was then nine years old, and he was sent to Novgorod to his godfather, Vasily, Archbishop of Novgorod, to learn letters. The archbishop entrusted him to his archdeacon, a God-fearing and Christ-loving man, so that he might teach him letters, all spiritual learning, and the Christian law. Soon the youth learned letters, spiritual wisdom, and the law; he was always at church, avoided childish games and amusements, and always diligently studied the Divine Scripture and listened to the conversations of wise and good people. Most of all he gained knowledge from his most holy spiritual father, the lord Archbishop Vasily; in it he delighted and nourished his mind.

When he assumed the grand principality of Tver, his patrimony and ancestral inheritance, then by God's grace the people of Tver received much good from him, for he was a God-loving and man-loving man. In the days of his reign robbers, thieves, and slanderers disappeared; tavern-keepers, tax collectors, and trade tolls were all eliminated; nowhere did violence or robbery occur; all remained in quietness, meekness, humility, and love. This prince always had the name of God on his lips and gave glory to God; he loved and honored priests and monks, built, adorned, and supplied churches with all necessities, comforted the afflicted, and gathered people everywhere.

He fortified the cities of Tver, terrified his enemies by prayer and the mercy of God, and conquered by arms. The people of Tver took pride in his name; he showed mercy to the poor and fed them, defended the oppressed, visited the sick, fed the hungry, gave drink to the thirsty, clothed the naked, and was stern to the evil, but to the good he was merciful and gentle.

He loved spiritual conversations, hated foul and obscene speech, read the Divine Scriptures and delighted in them, attended to them, and listened diligently and attentively to those who read. He was well trained in military affairs and skilled in them. He was courageous, strong, and terrible to his enemies. He strongly guarded his patrimony and was to all like a loving father. Above all he honored and loved the hierarch as Christ Himself, and he honored priests and monks more than princes. He was likewise merciful to the monastic order; many times he affectionately kissed them with tears, rejoicing with fervent desire of heart, as if they were angels of God; and to priests, whom he called princes of the church, saying that they are God's servants and more honorable than we. Especially did he show ineffable good conduct and acquire great faith in God, leaving a good example to all princes who wish to rule on earth. Therefore the Lord God loved him and granted him many blessings, crowned him with glory and honor, and glorified him in various lands. The Lord Himself said: "Those who glorify Me I will glorify." By this the Lord made him an example for many peoples.
