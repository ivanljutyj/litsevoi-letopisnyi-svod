To the episcopate of Rostov and Suzdal, since Nester, Bishop of Rostov and Suzdal, was still alive, and he seized Nester’s throne contrary to the sacred canons of the holy apostles and holy fathers; and Prince Andrew Yuryevich expelled him. He was also deceived by his own wicked household, for Prince Andrew’s household hated him and spoke with him flatteringly and deceitfully, and so they sowed enmity and set him at odds with his brothers, with Prince Mstislav Yuryevich, with Prince Vasilko Yuryevich, and with Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich, and with the former retainers of his father.

And so he expelled his brothers, wishing to be sole ruler in all the land of Rostov and Suzdal, and also the former retainers of his father: some he expelled, others he shut up in prisons; and there was fierce strife in the land of Rostov and Suzdal.

In that same year Prince Andrew Yuryevich again restored Bishop Leon to the episcopal throne in Rostov and Suzdal; and he began to teach that one should not eat meat on the Lord’s feasts, on Wednesdays and Fridays, at the Nativity of Christ and at Theophany, and there was a great dispute about this between him and the pious Prince Andrew Yuryevich and all the people; and Bishop Feodor defeated him in argument. He then went to Constantinople for correction, and, while with Patriarch Luke, he also went to Emperor Manuel; and Adrian, Bishop of Bulgaria, defeated him in the dispute about this before Emperor Manuel.

At that same time there was with Emperor Manuel an ambassador of the Grand Prince of Kiev, Ivan Yakovlev; and another ambassador, Ilya Andreev, of Grand Prince Andrew, son of Yuri Dolgoruky; a third ambassador of the Grand Prince of Chernigov, Svyatoslav Olegovich; and a fourth ambassador of the Grand Prince of Pereyaslavl.

In that same year there came from Luke, Patriarch of Constantinople, an ambassador Andrew to the Grand Prince of Rostov and Suzdal, Andrew Yuryevich, concerning Nester, Bishop of Rostov and Suzdal: “Let him be allowed,” he said, “to ascend the episcopal throne in Rostov and Suzdal, so that he does not wander through foreign lands, and the name of Christ is not blasphemed, lest the wrath of God overtake you.”

In that same year Prince Andrew, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, sent his son Prince Izyaslav and with him many other princes, and the hosts of Rostov, Suzdal, Ryazan, Murom, and Pron, and many others joined them in aid, and they went against the Polovtsians in the Field far beyond the Don, and joined battle, and there was great strife and fierce slaughter, and the Russian princes began to prevail. The Polovtsians scattered in all directions across the Field; the Russian soldiers pursued them and came to Rzhavtsy, and the Polovtsians, gathering again, struck the Russian host and killed many; but again the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God helped the Christian host, and they drove off the Polovtsians.
