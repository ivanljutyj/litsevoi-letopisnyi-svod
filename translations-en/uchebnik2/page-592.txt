And the grand prince said: “Venerable father, if God helps me, all will end well, and I will return, then I will found a monastery in the name of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God.”

And soon he set out against Mamai and his Tatars. There was a great battle, and by the saint’s prophecy the Tatars were defeated, and a multitude of them was slain. And the grand prince returned safely with his army, and asked Saint Sergius to find a place for founding a monastery. And he found such a place, showed it to the grand prince, and founded a monastery in the name of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God. He established there a cenobitic monastery, leaving as abbot in this monastery one of his disciples named Savva, who had formerly been in his great monastery the confessor of all the brethren, an honorable and wise elder. And the monastery was named, as it is still called, the monastery on the Dubenka. The venerable one himself returned to his own monastery. Saint Sergius founded not only those monasteries of which we write here, but many other monasteries to the glory of God and the Most Pure Mother of God. And he placed his disciples as superiors in those monasteries. And they tended their flock virtuously and in a God-pleasing manner, and departed to the Lord in ineffable joy.

On the Appearance of the Most Pure Mother of God

The venerable abbot Sergius remained in his monastery in silence, in stillness, and in poverty, and the number of his brethren increased. And it happened that once the monastery ran short of supplies, and the brethren grew weak. And having become lacking in the very necessities, they began to weaken in faith. But the venerable Sergius did not weaken in faith even in this poverty, trusting in God and in the Most Pure Mother of God. He hoped that they would provide for and save them in all things, and would watch over this holy place, consecrated and dedicated to His divine name. He also trusted in the intercessor before the Lord Christ—the Most Pure Mother of God—praying to her unceasingly; such was his custom: every day he sang the thanksgiving canons to the Most Pure—the Akathist. Thus, on one of these days he again sang the thanksgiving canon to our Most Pure Lady Mother of God. This was on the fortieth day after the Nativity of Christ, in the evening of the fifth day, when he had completed his usual rule and sat down, wishing to rest, he heard: “The Most Pure One is coming!” And as soon as the venerable one heard this, he immediately rose and went out from the cell into the vestibule, and saw a dazzling light shining brighter than the sun, and saw the Most Pure Mother of God and two apostles: Peter, the chief apostle, and John the Theologian, radiant in the ineffable brightness of dawn.
