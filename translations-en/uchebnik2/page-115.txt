And so the warriors began to withdraw from the weak places of the city, and left there a small and poor force, and all rushed to the strong places of the city; and so the townspeople stood against them at the strong places of the city, while all the weak places of the city were unguarded. Thus the warriors prepared a trap for the careless townspeople, and so suddenly they all advanced upon the unfortified place of the city, and took the city of Kiev, and Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich fled from the city with a small retinue, and his princess and son were captured; and when he fled, the servants of Bastia overtook him and shot at him, and many of his retinue were captured around him.

And he joined his brother Yaroslav beyond the Unov, and they went to the city of Vladimir Volynsky. Kiev was taken on March 8, Wednesday of the second week of Lent.

And they plundered the city for two days: the Podol, and the Hill, and the monasteries, and St. Sophia, and the Church of the Tithes of the Mother of God, and there was no mercy for anyone from anyone. And they took a great multitude of property, and from the churches icons and books, and vestments, and bells—they took everything, and all the sacred things. And in Kiev there was among all the people groaning and grief, and inconsolable sorrow, and ceaseless tears.

The reign of Gleb Yuryevich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, in Kiev

Mstislav Andreyevich, having taken Kiev and plundered it, installed in it his uncle Gleb Yuryevich Dolgoruky, and himself went to his father in Suzdal, and so each returned to his own place.

And Prince Gleb of Pereyaslavl, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of great Vladimir, sat upon the throne in the great principality in Kiev, and he gave Pereyaslavl to his son Vladimir.

In the same year many Polovtsians came to Rus and wrought much evil upon Christians; and the Russian princes could not stand against them, because in internecine wars many warriors had been killed, and so there was much evil from the accursed Polovtsians.

In the same year Theodore the monk, nephew of Peter Borislavich, went to Constantinople, carrying with him much property, and falsely said to the patriarch: “There is now no metropolitan in Kiev, and here I am—ordain me.” But the patriarch did not heed his words.

After some time he again began to entreat the patriarch: “There is now no metropolitan in Rus, and there is no one there from whom bishops may be ordained; ordain me bishop of Rostov.”
