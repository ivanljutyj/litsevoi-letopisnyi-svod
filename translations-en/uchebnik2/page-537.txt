We had gone far from our place, and the Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich himself met us with great love, together with his children and boyars.

And we came to the city of Pereyaslavl, and they met us with crosses. The metropolitan went into the cathedral church and performed a prayer service, and we feasted with the Grand Prince and received great honor. And so they honored us without ceasing, together with their bishop Eremiy Grechin.

Also, when we were leaving there, the Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan himself escorted us with his children and boyars, with great honor and love.

And so, having kissed, we parted. He returned to the city.

We went on ahead; and he sent with us his boyar Stanislav with a rather large company; he ordered him to escort us to the Don River with great caution because of robbers. Many bishops also escorted us then: Feodor of Rostov, Efrosin of Suzdal,

Eremiy Grechin, Bishop of Ryazan, Isakiy, Bishop of Chernigov, Danilo, Bishop of Zvenigorod, and archimandrites, and hegumens, and monks. We set out from Pereyaslavl of Ryazan on Thomas Sunday. They also sent with us three strugi and a nasad on wheels; on Thursday we came to the Don River and launched the boats onto the Don.

And on the second day we came to Chury Mikhailovy, for there is a place so called. For once there had also been a city there. And here, having taken comfort and given the kiss in the Lord, the bishops and archimandrites, and hegumens, and priests, and monks, and the boyars of the Grand Prince

Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan escorted us with joy and tenderness; all kissed with the holy kiss and returned from that place to their own homes. And we, on the Sunday of the Holy Myrrh-Bearers, all set out from there with Metropolitan Pimen: Mikhail, Bishop of Smolensk, and Sergius, Archimandrite of the Spassky Monastery, and archpriests, and protodeacons, and priests, and monks, and servants; we got into the boats and sailed down the Don River with the current.

This journey was sad and dreary, for there was wilderness everywhere; nothing could be seen there—neither city nor village; even if there had once been beautiful and very famous cities here, by what we saw only all places were empty and uninhabited; nowhere was a person to be seen, only great wildernesses and a multitude of beasts: goats, elk, wolves, foxes, otters, bears, beavers, and birds: eagles, geese, swans, cranes, and others. And all was great wilderness.

On the second day of river sailing we passed two rivers, the Mecha and the Sosna. On the third day we passed Ostra Luka. On the fourth day we passed Krivoy Bor. On the sixth day we reached the mouth of the Voronezh River.
