In Ustyug. His father, named Semion, was a cleric of the cathedral church in Ustyug; his mother was Maria.

There too he learned to read and write. And while he was still very young, he took the monastic vow. Then Arsenius, bishop of Rostov, ordained him deacon, and Gerasim, bishop of Kolomna, ordained him priest.

Then he learned the Greek language and letters, and also learned the Permian language and composed an alphabet for the Permian language, and translated books into Permian. Such Permian writing had never existed before.

He conceived a desire to go to Perm and teach unbaptized people. He took a blessing from the hierarch, and after praying to God he went to the Perm land and began to teach unbaptized people like a sheep among wolves. He taught the Christian faith, leading them away from idol worship, baptized them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and smashed idols and burned them with fire. Many he baptized, up to a thousand souls.

But at first he suffered many wounds and persecutions from them. After some time Metropolitan Pimen appointed him bishop in Perm.

And he returned again to Perm, and began to teach some Permian letters, and ordained them priests and deacons; he established churches, and in them they glorified God in the Permian language.

And it was wonderful to see this in that land where formerly there had been idol worship and service to demons, and from the beginning of the world they had prayed to idols, the sun, fire, water, trees, oxen, goats, sorcerers and magicians, and the Golden Woman.

And these are the names of the peoples living around Perm, of lands, regions, and places: Dvinyane, Ustyuzhane, Vilyazhane, Vychazhane, Penyazhane, Yuzhane, Seroyane, Gaiyane, Vyatchane, Lop, Korela, Yugra, Pechora, Vogulichi, Samoyad, Pertasy, Perm the Great, Gamal Chusovaya. The first river, called Vym, flows into the Vychegda. The second river, the Vychegda, passes through the whole Perm land, flows north, and empties into the Dvina forty versts before Ustyug. The third river, the Vyatka, flows on the other side of Perm and empties into the Kama River. This river Kama also runs through the entire Perm land. Along this river many peoples sail; it flows south into the Tatar land and empties into the Volga River sixty versts below Kazan.

Now, in the last years of the seventh thousand, in the remaining hundred, piety and knowledge of God shone there, and the Christian faith flourishes. Orthodox Christians who heard of this in all the Russian lands glorify God. Truly one should rejoice, as it is truly said in the Gospel: “There is joy in heaven even over one repentant sinner.” But this is not one, not two, but the whole Perm land was enlightened.
