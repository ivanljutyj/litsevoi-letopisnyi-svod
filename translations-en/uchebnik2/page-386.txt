Lord, Thy will be done; I am ready for all for Thy holy name!” And he commanded that everything be done as prescribed: all the rules and prayers.

After several hours had passed, he sent to the queen to learn the news. Then he himself mounted a horse and began to ride about, trying to learn from his acquaintances whether death threatened him. And having learned that it had nevertheless been decided to put him to death, he returned to his camp. Then a message also came from the queen that he would soon be put to death.

He embraced his son, took leave of the boyars, gave instructions concerning his patrimony, asked forgiveness of his son, of the boyars, and of all his servants, especially of his spiritual father, and having forgiven them, with love and shedding tears, he confessed everything to his spiritual father and finally received Communion, partaking of the holy and divine Mysteries.

His son Feodor did likewise, and all his boyars, and all who were with him, because none of them expected to remain alive.

After some time had passed, his attendants ran to him, weeping bitterly, all in tears, and said: “Lord, our beloved prince, they are already coming for you; Berkhan and Cherkas, your murderers, have been sent by Tsar Azbyak.” He, raising his hands to heaven, began to pray: “Lord, let Thy will be done, and not mine; as Thou wilt—whether I am to live or die—grant me Thy mercy and forgive me all my transgressions.” And while he prayed, tears flowed continually from his eyes, and all around stood weeping.

And behold, envoys from the tsar came to his camp. He went out to them with humility. And so he was killed together with his son Feodor: their heads were cut off. The boyars and servants fled.

Some who remained there took their bodies and carried them to Rus. And when they were in Vladimir, Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, met them with a holy council; they performed the funeral hymns and sent them on.

At Pereyaslavl his brothers, Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich and Prince Vasily Mikhailovich, met them, and Gabriel, Bishop of Rostov, and Feodor, Bishop of Tver, also met them; after performing the funeral hymns, they sent them on to Tver.
