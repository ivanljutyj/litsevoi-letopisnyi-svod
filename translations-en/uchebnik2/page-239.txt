In that battle they also killed Alexander Popovich, and his servant Torop, and Dobrynya Ryazanich, Zolotoy Poyas, and seventy great and brave bogatyrs; all were slain by the Tatars, for God was angered against us for our sins.

But Grand Prince Mstislav Mstislavich of Galich was the first of all to flee across the Dnieper; and Grand Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk, grandson of Rostislav, ran to Kiev and took the place of the grand prince, since Grand Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Kiev, with his children and his son-in-law, had been killed by the Tatars. This great calamity befell the Russian land, the Russian grand princes, the Kievan and all the other princes, and all the Russian lands on the 16th day of the month of June.

The Tatars pursued the Christians as far as Novgorod-Seversky; the Christians, not knowing the Tatars’ cunning, came out to them in a procession with crosses; the Tatars cruelly cut them down, burned their homes, and consigned their property to the fire.

The Tatars also went through other cities; they captured and burned all the regions and villages; and there was no one to defend them, because everywhere the whole land was empty; and the Tatars moved about, ravaging and fearing no one. It was said that sixty thousand Kievans alone were killed then; of the others it is impossible even to say how many were killed; only God alone knows their countless number.

But Prince Vasilko Konstantinovich of Rostov, nephew of Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich of Vladimir, was then preserved by God together with his army, since he did not manage to reach the place of battle, but had only come to Chernigov.

And having learned of this great calamity, Prince Vasilko hastily returned to Rostov, kept by God and the Most Pure Mother of God. No one knows where these Tatars came from or where they disappeared to. This was the first appearance of the Tatars in Rus; they were swift in battle; they were also light in flight, burdening themselves with nothing heavy; they would flee and then return again; and they were as malicious as demons.

In the same year Prince Michael, son of Vsevolod Chermny, of Chernigov, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Oleg, came to Novgorod; and there was relief in the cities, districts, and villages of Novgorod; and all the Novgorodians rejoiced at this; and they came to love Prince Michael Vsevolodovich.

In the same year Prince Michael of Chernigov, son of Vsevolod Chermny, went to Vladimir to his son-in-law, Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich, taking with him the plundered Novgorod merchants whom his son-in-law, Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich, had robbed in Torzhok.

And having taken from Prince Yuri all that had been plundered, Prince Michael went to Novgorod with all that wealth; and he came and stopped at the courtyard.
