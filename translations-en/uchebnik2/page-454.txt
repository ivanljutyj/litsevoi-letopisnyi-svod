He, seeing his submission, did not wish for the city’s destruction or the shedding of Christian blood, and made peace with him. And so they concluded everything, signed the charters, withdrew from the city, and dispersed each to his own principality.

At the time when Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was at Tver, Novgorod robbers with the commanders Prokofy and Smolyanich, in seventy ushkuys, two thousand in all, came to the Volga by the Kostroma River.

And they halted, preparing for battle beneath the city of Kostroma.

The townspeople came out of the city prepared for battle, and their own governor came forth as their commander. In all, more than five thousand Kostromans gathered; the Novgorod robbers numbered only two thousand. And seeing the great number of Kostromans, they divided in two and secretly sent half through the forest.

Those, having gone around the Kostroman army, struck it in the rear, while the others struck it in the front.

The Kostroman commander Pleshchey turned his back and fled; all the Kostromans, seized with fear, took to flight, and some of them were captured. And entering the city, the Novgorodians plundered everything and remained in the city a whole week.

And all the hidden riches were found; what they could, they carried off with them, and what they could not carry off they burned or threw into the Volga; many peasants were led away captive with their wives and children.

Then from Kostroma they went to Nizhny Novgorod, set the city on fire, killed people, and took some captive.

And they went down the Volga to the Kama, and committed many robberies along it.

And returning, they went to the Bulgars living in Kazan and there sold all their captives.

And from there they went down the Volga to Sarai, robbing and killing Christian merchants.

And they came to Astrakhan, and there sold all their captives.

The Astrakhan prince Salchey began to entice them with flattery and many honors, feasting them with rich dishes; and they drank their fill and collapsed as if dead.

The Astrakhanians killed them all, leaving not one alive, and took their property for themselves. Such was the end of the commander Prokofy and the other Novgorod commander, Smolyanich, with all his retinue: with the measure they themselves had measured, so it was measured out to them.
