The Novgorodians, having come to Novgorod, gathered a veche according to their ancient custom against Posadnik Dmitry and his brothers, because they had sold the people of the volosts and the merchants.

And, enraged, they went against their courts. First they set fire to the court of Posadnik Miroshka, plundered his brothers, and took their property and wealth, and sold off their villages, their people, and their servants, and searched out their hidden treasures.

In that same year they brought the Novgorod posadnik Dmitry Miroshkinich dead from Vladimir, because he had been gravely wounded at Pronsk; and many others also were brought dead, wounded at Pronsk.

And the Novgorodians rushed with great fury even against their dead posadnik, against Dmitry Miroshkinich. Some wanted to throw him from the bridge into the water, others to burn him in a pyre, having laid up wood. But Lord Archbishop Mitrofan forbade them; and they barely calmed themselves from their frenzy.

Lord Mitrofan buried him in Yuryev Monastery, beside his father.

In that same year Grand Prince Vsevolod, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, sent his son Svyatoslav to Novgorod to reign, and kept his eldest son, Prince Konstantin, with him, and gave him the city of Rostov and five other cities besides.

Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich came to Novgorod on Meatfare Saturday and sat on the princely throne in Novgorod. And the Novgorodians gave him the tablets of Posadnik Dmitry Miroshkinich, and there was much wealth on them; and the prince was with them on their terms, in all things according to the old custom and the statute of their first princes, and there was peace and great love between them.

And they began to say to their prince thus: “Know, lord Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, that we do not wish to keep among us the children of our former posadnik Dmitry Miroshkinich: Vladislav, Boris, Predslav, and Stanislav, nor Ostryata Domozhirovich, nor Ivan Danilovich, nor Mitroshka Oleksich.”

In that same year Prince Rurik Rostislavich, hearing that Vsevolod Yuryevich was warring against Ryazan, began to gather an army against the Chernigov princes; and the Cumans also came to him as aid, doing everything in secret.

And so, having joined together, he drove and pursued to Kiev, and drove Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny from Kiev, and himself sat in it, and in all the cities of Kiev he placed his governors, and, having given gifts to the Cumans, sent them back to their dwellings.
