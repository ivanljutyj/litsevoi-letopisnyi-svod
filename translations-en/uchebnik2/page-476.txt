And from Kostroma to Galich, and from Galich to Chukhloma. And he remained in Chukhloma for one year in confinement, and afterward from Chukhloma he was transferred to Tver. The earth is the Lord's, and its ends; here also is the end of the Tale of Mityai, of Pimen, and of the others.

Princess Vasilisa of Nizhny Novgorod passed away. In the same year Princess Vasilisa passed away in monastic rank, the wife of Prince Andrei Konstantinovich of Nizhny Novgorod; her monastic name was Feodora, and she was buried in the Monastery of the Holy Conception, which she herself had built. By birth she was from Tver; her father was Ivan Kiasovsky, and her mother was Anna.

She was born in the year 6839 from Adam (the year 1339 from Christ), in the reign of Tsar Andronikos of Constantinople; the patriarch at that time was Kallistos, and in the Horde at Sarai Tsar Azbyak then ruled, while in Rus there was the time of the reign of Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich Kalita, and the metropolitan in Rus then was Feognost. While still a maiden, she learned all letters and studied the whole Old and New Testament, and she wished to be tonsured into monastic rank. Her parents opposed this and gave her at the age of twelve to Prince Andrei Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod. And though she was a married woman, she paid no heed to the joys of worldly life, but spent her days in fasting and abstinence, prayer and the giving of alms; by such a harsh life she dried up her body, and beneath her bright clothing she wore a hair shirt on her body. And when at the age of thirty her husband, Prince Andrei Konstantinovich, passed away in monastic rank, he was buried in the Church of the Holy Savior, where her father Prince Konstantin had also been buried.

After her husband's death the princess was tonsured into monastic rank by Dionysius, Archimandrite of the Caves, and was named Feodora. All her wealth and property—gold and silver, pearls—she distributed to churches, monasteries, and the poor, and she dismissed all her servants. And she herself went to the Conception Monastery, which she herself had founded, and remained there in silence, living on the income from her own handiwork, spending her harsh life in fasting and prayers, in reading the divine Scriptures, in compunction and tears. Many women, widows and maidens, boyars' wives and princesses, took the tonsure in her monastery; the total number of nuns was 110, and they all lived according to the cenobitic rule, strictly and severely. And having grown old, she did not weaken bodily from the struggle, but in a harsh and wondrous life she passed away to the Lord, Whom she had loved from early years, to Whom be glory forever and ever, amen.
