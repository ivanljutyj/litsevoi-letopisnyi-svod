In that same year there was a terrible plague in Veliky Novgorod, in Pskov, in Ladoga, in Rusa, Porkhov, Torzhok, Tver, Dmitrov, throughout all lands and villages. And the plague was so fierce that the living could not bury the dead in time; there were not enough people to help the sick; one healthy person could not be in ten or twenty places at once, and people died every day; the living could not bury the dead before sunset. Many villages were left deserted; in towns and settlements scarcely one person or child remained alive. As if a sickle were mowing people down like ears of grain in a field, all the large households were left empty: of a great many people scarcely one or two survived, and somewhere only one child remained alive. These were the signs of this disease: at first it was as if a spear struck a person under the shoulder blade, or opposite the heart, or under the breast, or between the shoulders, or in the groin, or in the bosom; having fallen ill, the person began to cough blood and was seized by heat as if by fire. After that the person was thrown now into sweat, now into shivering; thus this ailment passed through all parts of the human body. The abscess on the body appeared in various places: in one on the neck, in another on the thigh, in one under the arm, in another behind the shoulder blade, in one under the cheekbone, in another in the groin or in some other places. And after suffering a little, many people died with repentance, while some took the angelic and monastic order and so departed from this world. The merciful God showed His wrath and anger together with mercy.

In Novgorod, in Pskov, in Torzhok, and in Tver people made vows, and in a single day churches were built of logs and erected in many places, consecrated, and liturgies were already being celebrated in them. People promised to keep Christ’s laws, to abide in peace and love with their brothers, and not to seize what belonged to others. But O human weakness and distracted mind! When wrath and anger come from God, all turn away from their evil deeds; but when God’s mercy and grace rest upon people, they forget, begin to do evil, fight one another, kill, and seize what belongs to others. And the merciful God again wishes to turn His creation to repentance by famine, plague, invasion of foreigners, frosts, thunder, lightning, drought, and heavy rains. Most of all God desires the salvation of man.
