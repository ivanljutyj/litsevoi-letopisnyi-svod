Stefan asked his brother, Archimandrite Sava, to go to Sreza and reconcile them.

The saint instructed him much, but could not dissuade him from violence, and by prayer at night he killed him. They found him still breathing; he only said then: “Who was the terrible one who appeared before me in the night, sent by Sava, and, taking a sword, pierced me?” And they withdrew in fear, taking his body.

After a long time Sava left his fatherland and went to the Holy Mountain, to the cells of the silent ones, and remained a long time in silence.

The Greek Kingdom of Theodore Laskaris

After Alexios Angelos, Theodore Laskaris reigned in Constantinople; he was 18 years old, and Germanos was patriarch then.

On Sava of Serbia

Saint Sava went to Constantinople for monastic needs and was joyfully received by the emperor and the patriarch, since the emperor had long wished to see him. The emperor honored him with great honors, because the daughter of Emperor Theodore had been given in marriage to Radoslav, son of Despot Stefan, nephew of Saint Sava.

God instructed Saint Sava that the time had come to ask the emperor to permit one of his disciples to be installed as archbishop in his fatherland, the Serbian land. The emperor and the patriarch wanted to install Sava himself, but he did not want this; and they refused to install another. Sava began to entreat the emperor, saying: “Although the heresy of evil belief was destroyed by my father and me, and piety is increasing, only one thing is lacking: there is no archbishop of our own in our land to instruct in the teaching of the Lord.” The emperor pressed him to agree, but did not want to install another archbishop; then Sava consulted with his disciples, took pity on the land of his fatherland, and, though he did not wish it, obeyed the emperor.

The patriarch consecrated him to the episcopal rank, and the emperor rejoiced greatly at this, and arranged a feast in his honor with abundant dishes, and gave much to the poor. Then Saint Sava began to ask the emperor and the patriarch to grant him authority, and after him also to the other bishops in his land, so that by the council of their bishops they could choose archbishops for themselves and not turn to Constantinople. The emperor and the patriarch said: “You ask too much,” but they could not disobey him and granted him such authority by imperial and patriarchal decrees; the patriarch also himself...
