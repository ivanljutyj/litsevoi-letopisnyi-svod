Photius was terrified, but after some time, gathering his strength, the metropolitan said to him: “Who are you, O man, who has so astonished me? How did you dare enter to me when my doors are closed? For it is written: he who does not enter by the doors is a thief and a robber.” Then the one who had come answered him: “Why do you think such things of me? I am not a thief or a robber, nor am I of earthly people; and nothing can hold me back—neither a stone wall nor fortified iron gates; wherever I wish to enter, I enter. I am an angel of God, sent to you from the Lord God Sabaoth, the Almighty. The Lord God commanded me to tell you, for the strengthening of your life: attend to yourself and your flock, and Christ God gives you a week to examine your life and to govern the affairs of your flock.” The hierarch listened to the angel with great fear and trembling and fell at his feet with tears, but he no longer saw the angel of God, for he had become invisible. And from this vision Photius came, as it were, into an ecstasy of mind, and fell into illness, and lay for a long time, until the monk cell-attendants close to him came and took him in their arms and laid him on a bed like a dead man, unable to say anything or see with his eyes. Afterward, as soon as his strength returned, the metropolitan with difficulty told those present about the vision and everything that had happened to him.

He lived after the appearance of God’s angel one year, three months, and 20 days, and so the week was fulfilled according to the word of God’s angel: seven weeks of seventy days each. And in those days before his death he wrote a work recounting all the events that had happened throughout his whole life, forgiving everyone and blessing them, and asking forgiveness of everyone. And he enjoined that everything he had written in his testament be kept, saying: “In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-giving, and Undivided Trinity. I, the servant of God, the humble Photius, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus. By God’s judgments, which God alone knows, I was raised to the great rank of hierarch, though I did not desire it at all. Great is the work of a pastor and teacher, since he must care not only for himself but even more for his flock, and know the fitting time, humility and loftiness, good gentleness, and righteous and saving zeal, and many other things that pastors ought to have. Knowing all this and seeing my weakness, I did not desire this rank, but desired only a virtuous life; for this reason I did not enter into marriage, and did not bind myself to the world and its vain imaginings, but above all, to me, humble as I am, one thing alone was dear and desirable: to save my soul and to present myself to my Master and God, my Creator and Maker of all creation, unstained by the sins of the world. And so I renounced the unhappy, swiftly passing worldly life, full of temptations and vanity…
