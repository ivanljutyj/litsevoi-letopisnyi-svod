That same autumn Prince Patrekey Narimantovich, grandson of Gediminas, came from Lithuania to Novgorod.

And the Novgorodians gave him for maintenance the town of Oreshek Korelsky, and another small town—half of Koporye—and the village of Luzhskoe. And afterwards, when he had stayed there a little while, the peasants—the men of Oreshek, Koporye, and Luzha—petitioned the Novgorod posadniks against Prince Patrekey Narimantovich, grandson of Gediminas, complaining of his cruel oppression.

Then the Novgorod posadniks took those towns away from Prince Patrekey and gave him the towns of Rusa and Ladoga.

In that same year a certain Lithuanian prince named Komat was killed in Moscow for some sedition and treason. In that same year the Novgorodians built a stone town at Yama, with the blessing of their Archbishop Alexius.

In that same year there was darkness for many days and nights; birds fell to the earth and into the water, for they could not see where to fly, and people dared not travel over the lakes and rivers; and there was great sorrow and grief among Christians.

In that same year Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, came from Constantinople to Kiev, having been appointed by the patriarch as metropolitan for Rus.

And he planned to go from Kiev to Moscow, but at that time Metropolitan Cyprian was in Kiev; and Prince Vladimir Olgerdovich of Kiev, grandson of Gediminas, took Metropolitan Dionysius captive and said to him: “Why did you go to Constantinople to the patriarch to be appointed metropolitan without our counsel? Behold, there is in Kiev Metropolitan Cyprian, and he is the metropolitan of all Rus; remain here, then, sitting in Kiev.” And so Metropolitan Dionysius, formerly Archbishop of Suzdal, remained in Kiev in captivity until his death.

In that same year there was a great collection of tribute throughout all the principality of Moscow: half a rouble from each village; then also they gave gold to the Horde. In that same year Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent his boyars to Great Novgorod—Fyodor Svibl, Ivan Uda, and Alexander Beleut—to collect the black tribute; and the Novgorodians paid the black tribute.

In that same year they built in Novgorod a stone church of St Philip, and another church, also of stone, of St John.

In that same year there came from Constantinople the father confessor of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, Feodor, abbot of Simonov, nephew of the venerable Abbot Sergius of Radonezh, appointed by Nilus, Patriarch of Constantinople, as archimandrite; and he was shown greater honor than other archimandrites.
