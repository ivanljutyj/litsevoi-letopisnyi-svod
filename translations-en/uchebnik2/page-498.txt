The Tatar host came out upon the hill and descended from the hill. Likewise the Christian host descended from the hill and stood on the open field, on firm ground.

The Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich often changed from horse to horse, rode through the regiments, and said with tears: “Beloved fathers and brothers, for the sake of the Lord and the Most Pure Theotokos, and for your own salvation, strive for the Orthodox faith and for our brothers! For all of us, from small to great, are one brotherhood, descendants of Adam, one clan and tribe, one baptism, one Christian faith; we have one God, our Lord Jesus Christ, glorified in the Trinity. Let us die at this hour for His holy name, and for the Orthodox faith, and for the holy churches, and for our brothers, for all Orthodox Christendom!” Hearing this, all wept, and were strengthened, and were courageous, like flying eagles and like lions roaring against the Tatar regiments.

The Grand Prince strengthened them, came beneath his black banner, dismounted from his horse, and took off his royal robe. Summoning his beloved one, whom he loved above all, Mikhail Andreevich Brenok, he ordered him to mount his horse, laid his royal robe upon him, adorned him with all the royal regalia, and ordered his esquire to carry his great black banner over Mikhail Andreevich Brenok. And he took the holy cross, on which the sufferings of Christ were depicted and in which was the life-giving wood of Christ, and weeping said: “I place all my hope in You, O Christ God! By the power of Your cross grant me victory over my enemies, as You did to Constantine in ancient times!”

Then envoys came to him from Abbot Sergius of Radonezh with a blessing, bearing this letter: “May the Lord God be your helper, and the Most Pure Theotokos, and Saint Peter the wonderworker.” And he sent him a loaf of the Most Pure Theotokos. The Grand Prince ate that holy bread, stretched out his hands, and cried aloud: “Great is the name of the Most Holy Trinity! Most Holy Lady Theotokos, help us! Through your prayers, O Christ God, and through those of Saint Peter the wonderworker, and the great hierarch Cyprian, Metropolitan, and the venerable Abbot Sergius, have mercy and save us from these infidels who have taken up arms against us!”

And he ordered his regiments to advance little by little. In his vanguard regiment were his commanders Dmitry Vsevolozhsky and Vladimir, his brother; on the right flank came Mikula Vasilievich, and Prince Semyon Ivanovich, and Semyon Melik with many forces.

It was already the sixth hour of the day when they came together at the mouth of the Nepryadva River, and behold, suddenly a great Tatar force rushed swiftly down from the hill; and then they advanced no further but halted, for there was no place for them to spread out.
