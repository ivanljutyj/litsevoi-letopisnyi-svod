“Woe is me, a sinner! Who am I? A stinking dead dog, doing such evil! Better for me, O bishop, according to your word, to die in humility than with pride and desires to perish both in this age and in the age to come.” And he also said to the bishop: “Your words are right and true, O holy father; let it be as you have said.”

And so Prince Vsevolod Olegovich with his brothers sent in submission to Yaropolk, Grand Prince of Kiev, and sought peace. And they kissed the Life-giving Cross—Grand Prince Yaropolk of Kiev, son of Vladimir Monomakh, with Vsevolod Olegovich, Grand Prince of Chernigov—and they gave many gifts to one another, and wept much together, and made humility and great love between them.

And they went their separate ways, each to his own place. That same night there was a wondrous sign in the heavens. That same autumn the right-believing and Christ-loving Grand Prince Yaropolk, son of Grand Prince Vladimir Monomakh, grandson of Grand Prince Vsevolod, great-grandson of Grand Prince Yaroslav, and great-great-grandson of blessed Grand Prince Vladimir, departed this life, on the 8th day of November. He reigned in Kiev for 7 years. He surpassed many in piety, for he was exceedingly Christ-loving and humane, and he loved the reading of divine words and honored bishops and presbyters and monks, conversed much and delighted in spiritual conversations; but to warlike enemies who would not submit he was fearsome, and all feared him and trembled before him.

And in that same year his brother Vyacheslav, son of Grand Prince Vladimir Monomakh, ascended the throne in Kiev to reign; and the most reverend Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, blessed him. And the most reverend Metropolitan Michael gathered a multitude of people, and they enthroned Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich in the great principality in Kiev, on the throne of his father Vladimir, and his grandfather Vsevolod, and his great-grandfather Yaroslav, and his great-great-grandfather the great Vladimir.

In that same year Vsevolod, Grand Prince of Chernigov, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great and blessed Vladimir, came with his brothers to Kiev against Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, and sent to Vyacheslav, saying: “Go peaceably out of the city of Kiev.” But he, not wishing for bloodshed, did not fight with him.

The most reverend Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, reconciled them and confirmed them with the precious Cross; and Grand Prince Vyacheslav of Kiev, son of Vladimir Monomakh, went to Turov to reign over the great principality, having sat only twenty days in the great principality in Kiev.
