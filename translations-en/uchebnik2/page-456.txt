And they brought the blessing of the metropolitan to Vladyka Alexis and to all Novgorod, and the Novgorodians assembled a veche at Yaroslav's Court.

And they sent to the Vladyka with a petition the viceroy of the grand prince, Ivan Prokofievich, the posadnik Yuri Onsiforovich, and the tysyatsky Alexis, together with many boyars and noble people.

The Vladyka heeded the blessing of the metropolitan and the petition of the Novgorodians.

In the same year the Vladyka of Novgorod, Alexis, came from Novgorod to Moscow to Alexis, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, and with him were the Novgorod posadniks; they stayed with Metropolitan Alexis in Moscow for two weeks.

And the metropolitan sent them away in peace and with blessing, and they returned to Novgorod with joy.

In the same year Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent his brother Prince Vladimir Andreevich with an army to Rzhev. He stood about the city for three days and burned the posad, but did not take the city.

In the same year two dignitaries came from Constantinople—protodeacons, the name of one George and of the other Ivan.

In that same winter Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal sent his sons, Prince Vasily and Prince Ivan, against the Bulgars, that is, against Kazan, and Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow sent his commander Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich Volynsky; they approached Kazan on the 16th day of March.

The Kazanians came out of the city to meet them, shooting from bows and crossbows, while others launched thunder from the city, frightening the Russian host, and some rode out on camels; the horses of the Russian riders were frightened by the camels. But God helped the Russian princes, and they drove them back into the city.

The Kazan princes Asan and Makhmat-Sultan began to petition the grand prince and gave ransom from the whole city to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow—one thousand rubles—and to Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod—one thousand rubles—and to the commanders and army three thousand rubles.

And having taken all under their authority and established the road and customs in Kazan, they returned home. In the same year a certain Metropolitan Mark came to the Russian land from Mount Sinai for alms.

In the same year a certain Archimandrite Niphon came to the Russian land from Jerusalem, from the monastery of the Holy Archangel Michael in Jerusalem, for alms, and by that alms he ascended the patriarchal throne in Jerusalem.
