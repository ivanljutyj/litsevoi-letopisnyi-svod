Evil had been done to the ulus, Christians had perished, you would not have acted thus, would not have listened to the young, but would have gathered all your old boyars and elders and held counsel with them as to how to act so that all your Christians might fare well and none perish through your pride. If you do not wish to act thus, but wish to wage war, then you should act differently. It would be well for you to act so as to live, collect dues, and live and rule without want in your ulus. And if any offenses are committed by the Russian princes or by Lithuania, you complain to us about them, send letters asking for help, seek protection from us, and we have no peace from you in this matter; you tell us that your ulus is poor and cannot pay tribute. And we ourselves have not seen your ulus before, only heard of it, but in your letters and orders that you sent us to the Horde you lied about everything; you said that in your state you had a ruble from every two sokhas in each ulus—where did that silver go? If it were really as you told us, if you gave us everything as was established in old times, then no evil would have been done to the ulus, and Christians would not have perished, and we would not be angry or at war with you.” We ourselves ought to understand why the Hagarites rise against us; is it not clear that it is for our sins that the Lord God permits them against us, so that we may turn and repent! We have much injustice, envy, pride, robbery, theft, violence, fornication, drunkenness, gluttony, extortion, lying, slander, condemnation, laughter, dancing, and demonic spectacles, every exaltation against the mind of God, disobedience to God’s law, failure to keep the Lord’s commandments; for all this the wrath of God will fall upon disobedient sons. But if we renounce evil and repent, God, the Lover of Mankind, will have mercy and save us; but if we do not turn and repent, we shall receive otherwise. If all that is written here should be displeasing to anyone, because so much has been said about the grievous things in our land, then this may stir him, serve his profit, and make him return to the good preserved in the people’s memory. We do not hinder, do not revile, do not envy honor, but establish what we found in the original Kievan chronicler, who, without hesitation, narrates all worldly temporal events; even our first rulers without anger ordered all that happened, good and unpleasant, to be described, and after them it continued as it did under Vladimir Monomakh, with that great Sylvester of Vydubychi, who narrated without embellishing reality; read this, if you wish, to be convinced. All good and salvation of the present and future age consists not in pride, anger, and ostentation, but in simplicity, compunction, and humility, since through humility all good is acquired, without which nothing can be accomplished.
