And so he sent to Kiev his spiritual father Theodore, abbot of Simonov, in the month of March for Metropolitan Cyprian, summoning him to Moscow with great honor.

And Cyprian came from Kiev to Moscow on Thursday of the sixth week after Easter, on the feast of the Ascension of Christ, and the Grand Prince met him with his children and boyars and all the people with great honor.

And here after some time news came of the invasion of the accursed Mamai. And the metropolitan said to his son: “Inquire reliably, and if it is so, gather the army, so that they do not find you unprepared.”

He began to gather a great army and mighty force, uniting with great love and much humility with the Russian princes and those local princes who were under their authority.

And he also sent to his brother, Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, asking for help. He soon sent a force and dispatched to his aid his nephew Prince Ivan Vsevolodovich Kholmsky, grandson of Alexander, great-grandson of Mikhail, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav Yaroslavich.

He also sent for his cousin, Prince Vladimir Andreevich, for he was then in his patrimony at Borovsk, whence he soon came to Moscow to the Grand Prince. And here again other tidings came, saying that Mamai was indeed coming with great fury and much force.

The Grand Prince was grieved and greatly saddened, and he stood in his bedchamber before the icon of the Lord’s image that stood at the head of his bed, and praying he spoke thus: “O Master, Lord Jesus Christ, God merciful and loving of mankind! If I, Thy many-sinning servant, dare, I pray to Thee in my sorrow, for I lift up my sorrow to Thee, merciful Master Lord! Do not do to us as Thou didst bring the evil Batu upon our forefathers: for, Lord, while that fear and trembling is still great in us, now, Lord, do not be angry with us to the end. For I know, Lord, that because of me Thou wouldest destroy the whole land: for I have sinned before Thee more than all men; but grant me, Lord, mercy for the sake of my tears!”

And after the prayer he went out from his bedchamber, and took his brother Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and went to his father Cyprian, Metropolitan of all Rus, and said to him: “Indeed, father, the impious Mamai is coming upon us with fury in great force.” The metropolitan began to comfort him and strengthen him, saying: “Be not troubled therefore about this, my lord and beloved son! Many are the sorrows of the righteous, and the Lord will deliver them from all; and though the Lord chastened me sore, He did not give me over to death. God is our refuge and strength, a helper in the troubles that have come upon us so greatly. Tell me, son, the truth: in what have you not done right toward him?”
