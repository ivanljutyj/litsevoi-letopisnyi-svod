But according to the canons, we bishops ourselves have the right to appoint a metropolitan in council, as was formerly done in Rus. Thus it is written in the sacred canons: “Two or three bishops appoint a metropolitan,” it says, as bishops also did in Rus before us: in the time of Grand Prince Izyaslav of Kiev, the bishops themselves appointed Metropolitan Clement according to the canons. The Bulgarians, baptized before us—our brothers and kinsmen in baptism—did likewise; they acted according to the canons, and their bishops appointed for themselves a chief hierarch. So too the Serbian bishops, according to the canons and the apostolic ordinance, appointed by council from among their bishops a chief hierarch for themselves, who to this day serves God. In the Serbian land many bishops are subject to the chief hierarch, but the Serbian land itself is not comparable to the Russian land in its majesty, multitude, and expanses, which are subject to you, Grand Prince Alexander, known as Vytautas; rather, the Serbian land has a small territory and few people. What shall we say of the Serbs and Bulgarians! The holy apostles established this rule: the grace of the Holy Spirit descends equally upon all Orthodox bishops. After the holy apostles had been appointed by Christ the Savior Himself, they themselves appointed others, and these appointed those after them, and so gradually the grace of the Holy Spirit has reached us humble bishops. By that grace we have the same authority as the disciples of the apostles, when we gather in council according to the canons, to appoint in our own fatherland a pastor, teacher, and worthy chief hierarch whom God will bestow on us. We also have authority from God: whomever we bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whomever we loose on earth will be loosed in heaven. Let no one say: “They are separated from the apostles because they have appointed a metropolitan for themselves.” That shall not be! For we are not separated; those who say such things are foolish. We all piously hold to the church tradition of the holy apostles and holy fathers, and we confess the faith according to apostolic and patristic tradition. We curse every heresy, heretical falsehood, divisions, and assemblies that do not come from God; we also anathematize the simoniac heresy, which sells the gift of the Holy Spirit for gold and silver and appoints men to the priesthood for bribes and promises of money. We recognize the most holy Patriarch of Constantinople as the true patriarch and father, and likewise the other patriarchs—of Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem—and we receive those metropolitans and bishops as our fathers and brothers in the Holy Spirit; with them we hold one confession and form of the holy faith, as it is written: “I believe in one God.” We confess and believe just as they do, and perform—
