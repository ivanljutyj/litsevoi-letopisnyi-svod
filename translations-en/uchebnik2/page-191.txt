And, having stood there, he grew careless, rode away from his regiment with a small retinue, and the Poles at once attacked him and killed him.

The soldiers took his body and carried it to Galich, laid it in Galich in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, and swore on the cross to his son Daniel that they would remain in peace and love, and seated him on his father’s throne.

In the same year Prince Ryurik Rostislavich, hearing of Roman’s death, took off his monastic habit, renounced his vows, and took the grand princely throne in Kiev; he also wished to have his wife tonsured, but she not only refused this, but even took the schema upon herself, saying: “There is nothing greater than the soul; it is of it that we ought to take care and think. The Lord said: What profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world and loses his soul, or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?”

The Grand Prince Ryurik Rostislavich sent to Chernigov to the Olgovichs and swore on the cross to them that they should remain in peace, unity, and love; and having summoned the Polovtsians, he went with an army against Galich.

The King, hearing of this, sent his aid to Galich to Prince Daniel Romanovich.

And the Poles came to Galich, fortified the city, and settled the people there, because Prince Daniel Romanovich was still young among them.

And they came with a great army to the city of Galich, and surrounding the city, began battle; the townspeople fought from the city walls; many people fell on both sides; and after standing there a long time, they did not take the city, but only burned the districts and villages, and taking many people captive, returned home.

In the year 6714 from Adam (1214 from Christ)

All the Russian princes gathered into one army: the Olgovichs of Chernigov, Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny, grandson of Vsevolod and great-grandson of Oleg, with all his brothers, and Prince Vladimir Igorevich with all his brothers, and Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Smolensk came to them with his nephews, and many Polovtsians also came to them.

All gathered in Chernigov, and from there went against Galich; and when they reached Kiev, Prince Ryurik Rostislavich joined them in Kiev with his sons, Princes Rostislav and Vladimir, with the Berendei, the Kovui, and the Bouty, and from there the regiments went against Vladimir and the Galicians.

The Galicians with Prince Daniel Romanovich were very frightened and sent to the King for help. When the King heard of their great misfortune, that the Russian princes with the Polovtsians were coming against them from one side and the Poles from the other, he himself went to the aid of the Galicians. But the Romanoviches were frightened and fled from Galich to their patrimony in Vladimir, without waiting for the King.
