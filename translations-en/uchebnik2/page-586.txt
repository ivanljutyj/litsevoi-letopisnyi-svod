On the Founding of the Simonov Monastery

Earlier we told you about Ivan, the son of Sergius’s elder brother Stefan, whom his uncle Sergius tonsured a monk at the age of twelve. His father Stefan brought him here and placed him in the hands of the Venerable Sergius. In monastic life he received the name Feodor instead of Ivan. Sergius, his uncle, was his guide in a virtuous life. He became renowned for his fasting and great abstinence in monastic life and was deemed worthy of the priesthood. He had the custom of telling the Venerable Sergius all his thoughts. Then the idea came to him to find a place and found his own monastery. He told Sergius about this. Sergius said to him: “Child, I had hoped that you would lay my bones in the grave and after me be abbot in this monastery. But if you wish to begin such a work for God’s sake, then may God help you and the Most Pure Mother of God.” So he blessed Feodor, his nephew, and with him his willing companions of like mind. They went forth, and after searching in many places they found a place suitable for building a monastery, which from ancient times was called Simonovo. It was on the bank of the Moskva River, not far from the city of Moscow.

The Venerable Sergius himself also came there and saw how good that place was and suitable for building a monastery. They told the blessed Metropolitan Alexei about this and received his blessing. And so, by the blessing and command of the metropolitan, they founded a church in honor of the honorable and glorious Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God, and they built the church, a refectory, and many cells.

Princes, boyars, and nobles gave much property, gold, and silver for the building of the monastery. And in the monastery they established a cenobitic community according to the rule of the great venerable fathers: no one was to have anything as property or call it his own, but all things were common. And there was in the monastery truly a common life, fasting, great abstinence in all things, constant prayers and psalmody, reading of the divine words, and fulfillment of the monastic rule. The Venerable Sergius, seeing such a virtuous life of the monks, was greatly amazed, rejoiced in spirit, and glorified God.
The venerable abbot Sergius did not wish to lag behind his disciple and nephew in holy silence and stillness, and he himself desired to remain in silence and stillness, since he himself loved silence and stillness.
