Edigey of the Horde began to fight with Vitovt; the Tatars and Lithuanians shot from crossbows and guns. But in the open field cannons and guns do little harm to the enemy, and although they did little harm, the Lithuanians fought bravely; arrows fell like rain.

And the Lithuanians began to defeat Prince Edigey of the Horde. But Tsar Temir-Kutlui came up with a great Tatar army, surrounded the Lithuanians, began killing the horses under the warriors, and long fought them desperately. And the battle was fierce, and the Tatars began to prevail.

And Tsar Temir-Kutlui defeated Vitovt and all the Lithuanian army. And Tsar Tokhtamysh, seeing what had happened, was the first of all to flee; many warriors were killed, they fell to the ground like ears of grain at harvest, and many Lithuanian lands were plundered.

And the Tatars captured the baggage train and carts bound with iron chains, and cannons, and guns, and crossbows, much wealth, gold and silver vessels. Vitovt Keistutovich, seeing such evil, fled with a small retinue; the Tatars rushed after them and overtook them five hundred versts from Kiev, and blood flowed like water.

Tsar Temir-Kutlui came himself and stood before the city of Kiev, and sent his army to conquer the Lithuanian lands. And the Tatar army reached even great Lutsk, captured many cities, conquered and devastated many lands. Tsar Temir-Kutlui took a ransom of 3,000 rubles from Kiev, and 30 rubles from the Pechersk Monastery, and went away to his Horde land.

And in the Lithuanian land there was then sorrow, lamentation, great weeping, and depletion of the people. These are the names of the slain Lithuanian princes: Grand Prince Andrei Olgerdovich of Polotsk; Grand Prince Dmitry Olgerdovich of Bryansk; their cousin Prince Mikhail Evnutevich, grandson of Gediminas; Prince Ivan Dmitrievich Kindyr; Prince Andrei, stepson of Prince Dmitry; Prince Ivan Evlashkovich; Prince Ivan Borisovich of Kiev; Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich of Smolensk; Prince Lev Koriadovich; Prince Mikhail Vasilievich and his brother Prince Semyon Vasilievich; Prince Mikhail Podberezsky and his brother Prince Alexander; Prince Dmitry Daniilovich of Volhynia; Prince Feodor Patrikeevich of Rylsk; Prince Andrei of Drutsk; Prince Vspytka of Krakow; Prince Mnot Toluntievich; Prince Ivan Yurievich of Belsk; Prince Dmitry Koriadovich; Prince Yamont, governor of Smolensk—in all, 74 noble princes were killed. And of other commanders and noble boyars, Christians and Lithuanians, Ruthenians, Poles, and Germans, a great multitude was killed; who will be able to count them? This happened on the twelfth day of the month of August, on Tuesday, in the seventh hour of the day.
