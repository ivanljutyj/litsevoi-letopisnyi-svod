And when they came to the Dnieper, they crossed over opposite Trepol, surrounded it, and there was a fierce battle. And Yaroslav, son of Volodimirich, shut himself up in it.

And they stood about it for three weeks. The people in the city were already exhausted and submitted to him. And he, having made peace with them, hastened to Kiev.

Vladimir Igorevich came from Galich to his aid. But Rurik, hearing that a countless host was coming against him from everywhere and that he had help from no one, fled from Kiev to Vruchiy.

They came to Kiev and received news that Rurik had entered Vruchiy, and that Mstislav Romanovich had shut himself up in Belgorod. They hastened to Belgorod, and when they came, they surrounded it, and there was a savage battle.

When Mstislav saw the multitude around the city and that there was no help to be expected from anyone, he began to ask for free passage to withdraw.

And Vsevolod, having kissed the cross, gave him passage. And he went to Smolensk, to his own patrimony.

From there they went to Torchesk. And Mstislav Mstislavich shut himself up in it, and they besieged him.

And he sent the pagans off to war, and they did much evil throughout that land, taking captives, cutting down people, and burning villages.

And Mstislav could not endure the siege in the city and kissed the cross to him according to his will. And Vsevolod Chermny, having come, sat in Kiev, having done much evil to the Russian land.

On the Treachery of the Ryazan Princes

When the great prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, heard that the Olgovichi with the pagans were warring against the Russian land, he was angered and said: “Is the Russian land a patrimony for them alone? Is it not a patrimony for us too?” And he said: “I want to go to Chernigov, and let God judge between us and them; let us call upon the Lord God for help. I want to go to Chernigov against the Olgovichi, because they drove my son Yaroslav with dishonor out of the city of Pereyaslavl-Russky, and inflicted great disgrace on me.”

And he sent to Novgorod for his son Konstantin, ordering him to gather an army against the Olgovichi. And Konstantin, having obeyed him, began to gather a great army from the Novgorodians, and Pskovians, Ladogans, and Novotorzhians, and went hastily with all his forces to Moscow, awaiting his father.
