And he stood beyond Pereyaslavl by the Sherensk forest, and there the Novgorodians, servants of the tysyatsky Milonega, came to him and said: “Lord Prince Vsevolod! Do not go against Prince Gleb of Ryazan without the brave sons of Novgorod, but gather all the Novgorodians and then go against him.”

He, having listened to the Novgorodians, returned to Vladimir, saying: “May the Lord’s will be done; let the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother avenge him.”

Prince Gleb of Ryazan burned the whole city of Moscow, the volosts and villages, and, seizing much property, returned back to Ryazan.

In that same winter Grand Prince Vsevolod wished to avenge himself on Prince Gleb of Ryazan and marched against him to Ryazan with the Rostovians, Suzdalians, Pereyaslavlians, and Novgorodians; the Grand Prince of Chernigov, Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, sent to his aid his nephew Oleg and his son Vladimir. His nephew, Grand Prince Vladimir Glebovich of Pereyaslavl, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, also came to him from Pereyaslavl Russky. So, having joined all those forces, Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich marched to Ryazan against Prince Gleb of Ryazan; and they stood at Kolomna, and news came to him that Prince Gleb of Ryazan was going by another way to Vladimir and was warring with the Cumans around the city, and had plundered the Bogolyubovo church and all the icons with all kinds of furnishings and appropriated them for himself; he did the same to other churches, and Prince Gleb of Ryazan caused much evil to Christians by his deeds, angered the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, and received vengeance worthy of his folly and wickedness from the Lord God and from the Most Pure Mother of God, as will be told further on.

Then Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, hearing of the swift approach of Prince Gleb of Ryazan, sighed and said: “Thou, Lord God, Lover of mankind, avenge his injustice, for by slanderous accusations against his brothers-in-law he went forth and wrought many murders and much bloodshed”; and having said this, he left Kolomna and returned to his land, and met Prince Gleb of Ryazan standing on the Koloksha River with the Cumans and with captives; and they stood one against the other for a month, neither advancing against the other, for it was impossible to cross the river because the ice on the river was not firm. Prince Gleb of Ryazan wanted peace, but Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich did not, because he did not see the Cumans with him, for he had done him no harm, but he had listened to slander against his brothers-in-law and strove for murder and bloodshed, and by this angered the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God.
