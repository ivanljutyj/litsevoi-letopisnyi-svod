The monks mentioned, with great faith and zeal, falling before him with tears, begged him to receive them. The saint marveled at their faith and humility. And he remembered the words of the Lord: “He who comes to Me I will not cast out; and where two or three are gathered together in My name, there am I in the midst of them.” And David said: “Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together.” He also remembered the words of Solomon, who said: “Woe to the one who does not turn aside to the right or to the left, but walks by the royal path, that is, to live in silence with two or three together; this is profitable and fitting, if indeed it be so.” This is good, and this would be sufficient. But if God wills that there be a multitude here, then the will of the Lord be done! Who can resist the will of the Lord! Therefore I will joyfully receive all of you who come with faith to labor for the Lord and are ready to endure in this place for the sake of your salvation and the obtaining of future blessings. Only let each build himself a cell. But first of all you ought to know that life in this desert place is hard, harsh, hungry, and you will live in poverty. Those who wish to live in this place must prepare themselves not for abundant food and drink, for comfort, merriment and joy, but for hardships, sorrows, griefs and calamities, which must be endured courageously. The Apostle says: “Through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of heaven.” In the Gospel the Lord says: “The kingdom of heaven is taken by force, and the violent seize it; and in your patience possess your souls, for he who endures to the end will be saved.” They joyfully and zealously promised to fulfill everything, saying: “We do all that you have commanded and in nothing disobey you.” And each built himself his own cell, desiring to live in silence in solitude according to the blessing of the saint. And so they lived for God, learning from the saint and following him, each as he could. His life was as follows: hunger, thirst, dry food, sleeping on the ground, vigil, purity of body and soul, silence of lips, constant labor, mortification of the flesh, gentleness, meekness, unfeigned humility, constant prayers, good thoughts, perfect love, poverty in clothing, immeasurable destitution, having in his soul unceasing fear of God. For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. Just as a flower is the beginning of a berry and of any vegetable, so the beginning of every virtue is the fear of God. He, having planted fear in his soul, fenced himself with it. And, meditating on the law of the Lord day and night, he lived like a fruitful tree, planted and fertilized, which in due season will give fruit. And since he was still young and strong in body, his bodily strength was that of two men, so the devil tried to inflame him with arrows of lust. But the venerable one, sensing the devil’s workings, restrained his body, exhausting it with fasting.
