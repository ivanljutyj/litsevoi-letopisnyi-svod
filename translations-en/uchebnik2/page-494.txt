And he heard that Oleg, Prince of Ryazan, had been greatly frightened and had trembled; and he too began to grieve and lament, saying: “Why was I enticed by my friend Oleg of Ryazan? Why did I trust him? Lithuania has never been instructed by Ryazan. And now why have I fallen into folly?” And then he began to wait to see what would happen between Mamai and the Prince of Moscow.

In the year 6889 from Adam (the year 1389 from Christ)

In the month of September, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich came to a place called Berezuy, twenty-three versts from the Don.

And there came to him Lithuanian princes to bow down and serve: Prince Andrei Olgerdovich of Polotsk with the Pskovians, and his brother Prince Dmitry Olgerdovich of Bryansk with his host. These princes, with God’s help, rendered great assistance to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

Then the grand prince sent into the Field against Mamai’s Horde his chosen boyar and stalwart commander Semyon Melik, and with him his chosen men Ignaty Kren, Foma Tynin, Pyotr Gorsky, Karp Alexandrov, Pyotr Chirikov, and many others, noble and valiant, and knowledgeable people specially appointed for this, to meet the Tatar watch and bring news quickly. And from that place the grand prince moved toward the Don, advancing quietly and awaiting news.

And then suddenly two of his watchmen, Pyotr Gorsky and Karp Alexandrovich, came to him and brought a noble captive from the tsar’s court, one of the tsar’s high-ranking men. That captive related, saying: “Now the tsar is at Kuzmin Gati; he is in no haste, but awaits Oleg, Prince of Ryazan, and Prince Jagiello Olgerdovich of Lithuania. But he knows nothing of the mustering of the Prince of Moscow, nor does he expect to meet him, according to the letter previously written to him by Oleg of Ryazan. In three days he is to be at the Don.” And they asked him about Mamai’s strength, how great it was. He said: “A vast and countless multitude.”

Then Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich summoned his brother Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and all the princes, commanders, and nobles, and began to take counsel with them: “What shall we do? How shall we set battle against these godless Tatars—on this side of the Don, or shall we cross to the other side of the Don? For behold, the day and hour comes to each.” And many began to speak variously, and in this much time passed, and great sorrow was upon all.
