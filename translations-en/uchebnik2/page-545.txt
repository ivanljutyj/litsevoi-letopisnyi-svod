Lightnings, and from the terrors of the sea waves their souls were near death. And by great winds and whirlwinds their ships were scattered over the sea, and they did not know of one another, where each one was.

Then by God’s grace the storm ceased, and a great calm came; and little by little all gathered together and were saved, and all arrived healthy at Belgrade; and by God’s mercy and that of His Most Pure Mother they sailed to Rus. We, having read these words written in the letters to us from Cyprian, Metropolitan of all Rus, and having heard them, were very glad and gave gifts for the forty-day memorial for Metropolitan Pimen in churches and monasteries. And we visited the holy places and venerated the healing tombs and wonder-working relics.

And we saw, on the eleventh day of February, on the Sunday of the Prodigal Son, how Emperor Manuel of Constantinople was crowned in the year 6899 from Adam (the year 1399 from Christ) to the kingdom, and was blessed together with his empress, and installed by their father His Eminence Anthony the Patriarch. His installation to the kingdom was thus. That night there was an all-night vigil in the great patriarchal church of Holy Sophia, which means the Wisdom of God.

When day came, they came there, and allowed us to watch the rite of installation to this rank. And a great multitude of people gathered, the male sex inside the church of Holy Sophia, and the female sex in the galleries, and this was wonderful and wise. For all the women stood in the galleries behind silk curtains, so that the adornments of their faces, alluring and subject to death, might not be seen by anyone of the people. And those men themselves, and all the male sex, were clothed according to the ancient rite in costly garments, but without any ostentation. Women could in no way be seen by men in that church. But the women standing in the galleries saw everything. The singers stood wondrously adorned: they had robes like sacred sticharia, broad and long, and their sleeves were broad and long, some of them damask, others silk, with shoulder-pieces of gold and pearl and lace. On their heads were pointed caps with gold and pearl and lace, and a great multitude of them was gathered, and everything was so orderly that it looked as if painted. Their senior man was wondrous and very handsome, and his gray hairs shone white as snow. There were also Romans from Rome and from Spain, Germans and Fryags from Galata, and some were Constantinopolitans, and some Genoese, and some Venetians, and some Hungarians, and these were seen as a wondrous order. And they stood in two choirs, and each land had its own insignia upon it and its garments, some in crimson velvets, others in cherry velvets, others in dark blue velvets, and some in black velvets.
