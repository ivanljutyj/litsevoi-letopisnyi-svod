Society of Lovers of Ancient Writing

The Illuminated Chronicle — A Source of Truth

The Illuminated Chronicle was created in the 16th century by order of the Russian Tsar Ivan the Terrible for the education of the tsar’s children. The work of compiling it was led by the most educated man of his time, Metropolitan Macarius, Metropolitan of Moscow and All Rus’. The best scribes and icon painters of the capital labored over the compilation of the Chronicle.

What they accomplished was a collection of all reliably known sources, from Holy Scripture (the text of the Septuagint) to the history of Alexander the Great and the works of Josephus—the entire written history of humanity from the creation of the world through the 16th century inclusive. All times and all peoples that possessed writing are reflected in dozens of books of this collection. No civilization of humanity—neither Europe, nor Asia, nor America or Africa—created anything like this chronicle collection, adorned with a vast number of highly artistic illustrations.

The fate of the Russian tsar himself and of his children was tragic. The Illuminated Chronicle did not prove useful to the tsareviches, but on reading the Illuminated Chronicle, part of which is devoted to the period of Ivan the Terrible, it becomes clear why.

Over the following centuries an official historiography appeared, often opportunistic and politically biased, and therefore reliable chronicle sources were doomed to destruction or correction—that is, falsification. The Illuminated Chronicle survived these centuries thanks to the fact that after the death of Ivan the Terrible, during a period of troubles and interregnum, this folio became a coveted object for “enlightened” bibliophiles. But fragments were carried off to their libraries by the most influential nobles of their time: Osterman, Sheremetev, Golitsyn and others. “Even then, high-ranking collectors understood that such a folio with sixteen thousand miniatures was priceless.” And by the time of the revolution the Chronicle had been scattered among several places and repositories.

Already in our days, through the efforts of enthusiasts, the scattered books and leaves were gathered together from various repositories. And the revived Society of Lovers of Ancient Writing has made this masterpiece accessible to all. This historical source, which has no equal, can now be obtained free of charge by many major educational institutions of the world, national libraries of various countries and, of course, our compatriots, for the upbringing of children on this treasure of experience and wisdom of millennia.

In such a wonderful way, the work that was done for the tsar’s children five hundred years ago has come down to our children, dear contemporaries, with which we congratulate you from the bottom of our hearts!
