And then a certain tsar named Temir-Kutluy suddenly attacked him, and there was a great battle between them and a fierce slaughter. And Tsar Temir-Kutluy overcame Tsar Tokhtamysh and drove him out, and he himself sat on the throne of the Volga Great Horde, while Tsar Tokhtamysh fled to the Lithuanian lands. And he came to an agreement with Vytautas Kestutovich, Grand Prince of Lithuania, and Vytautas was glad to have him, and he, with the remnants of his army, with his tsaritsas and two sons from his land, went to Vytautas in Kiev.

And he was with Vytautas in Kiev, sustaining himself there and receiving everything he needed, hoping to receive help from him, since he wanted to regain his realm, which he had lost.

In the same year the Turkish Sultan, brother of Amurat, with a great Turkish force surrounded Constantinople both by sea and by land, and stood there a long time. The patriarch and Emperor Manuel sent to Rus to ask for alms.

Then Metropolitan Cyprian turned to his sons, the great princes of Rus: to Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow, to Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, to Vytautas Kestutovich of Lithuania, and also to Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan and to many princes, boyars, and merchants, advising them to show mercy in such need and misfortune and to help those besieged by the Turks. And a fair amount of alms was collected. Both the priestly orders and the monasteries gave alms. And all that was collected was sent to Constantinople.

The emperor, the patriarch, and all the people gave great thanks and praise to God, and blessing to Rus. And they sent to the grand prince, as a blessing and a gift, a wondrous icon on which the Savior is painted in white robes. And that icon stands in his court in the Church of the Annunciation to this day, on the left side, for veneration.

In the same year Prince Vasily Ivanovich of Smolensk came to Novgorod, and Patriky Narimantovich, grandson of Gedimin of Lithuania, and the Novgorodians received them with honor.

In the same year Grand Prince Vytautas Kestutovich of Lithuania sent to Moscow, to his son-in-law Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, saying: "Let us send our envoys to Novgorod: you yours, and I mine, so that the Novgorodians break the peace with the Germans."

And as Vytautas said, Prince Vasily Dmitrievich did so: he sent his envoys and Vytautas's to Novgorod, ordering them to break the peace with the Germans. But they did not obey and answered thus: "We, lord prince, have our own peace with you, another with the Germans, and another with Vytautas. And you, lord prince, do not interfere in our affairs.
