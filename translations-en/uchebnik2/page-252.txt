In the year 6742 from Adam (1242 from Christ)

The faithful Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich built the Church of the Holy Great Martyr George in the city of Yuryev, and adorned it more than other churches; inside, around the whole church, holy figures were carved in stone most wondrously; this church stands even now.

Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich went with the Novgorodians against the Germans to the city of Yuryev, and before reaching the city, they halted.

And the Germans came out of the city of Yuryev and out of the city of Bear’s Head against the guard regiments and broke through to Yaroslav’s regiment.

And Prince Yaroslav with the Novgorodians beat the Germans as far as the Amovyzha River; and on the river the Germans broke through the ice; and many of them drowned; and others, wounded, fled to the city of Yuryev, and others to the city of Bear’s Head.

And around the city of Yuryev the Novgorodians did much harm to the Germans; they took captives; and they burned districts and villages.

And the Germans asked Prince Yaroslav for peace; and Prince Yaroslav made peace with the Germans; and he returned to Novgorod.

In the same year the Lithuanians came in war against Russa and reached even Torzhok; and Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich went against them and killed many, while the others fled.

Prince Izaslav Mstislavovich of Smolensk, grandson of Roman Rostislavovich, with the Polovtsians, and Prince Mikhail, son of Vsevolod Chermny, with troops from Chernigov, and Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with the Novgorodians went in war against Kiev and took it.

And the Polovtsians captured Grand Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Kiev and led him away to their own land; he had reigned as grand prince in Kiev for ten years.

And there the Polovtsians took a ransom from Prince Vladimir and released him to Rus; and Izaslav Mstislavovich, grandson of Roman, began to reign in Kiev.

In the year 6744 from Adam (1244 from Christ)

Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich of Novgorod became grand prince in Kiev; and his son Alexander began to reign in Novgorod.

There was a great sign: the sun vanished throughout all the land on the Lord’s Day; it vanished so completely that it became like a four-day-old moon.
