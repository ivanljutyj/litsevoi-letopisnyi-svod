…and the lighting of many candles, and the censing, and the reading of the appointed Life and miracles of the saint, Stephen offered prayer from the depths of his soul; and, having dozed a little from much labor, he sees with the eyes of his heart the great Nicholas, and falling at his feet, he entreats him, asking. The merciful one said: “I told you before not to grieve, and I showed you your eyes in my hand. And now I have been sent to fulfill everything.” And he raised him up, and made the sign of the cross on his face, and touching his eyes with the tips of his fingers, said: “The Lord Jesus Christ, who gave sight to the blind man from birth, will grant sight to your eyes also.” And then he became invisible. Stephen, waking, was trembling. O Thine ineffable mercy, O Christ! He began to see as before; and with many tears he thanked God, and he revealed it to no one until the time, but according to custom, covering his eyes with a cloth, he walked with a staff. And after some time his younger son departed from this life.

His return to his own fatherland came about in this way. The autocrat of the Greeks sent to his son-in-law, the Serbian king, asking him to send troops to his aid. And the Bulgars at that time had brought all the southern regions under their sway, and with a multitude of soldiers were heading east. With them he also sent the abbot of the Monastery of the Pantocrator, where Stephen was confined.

Summoning him, King Milutin questioned him privately about his son. The wondrous elder said: “Is it about the much-suffering second Job that you ask me, O lord? Your whole dominion is not worthy of Stephen’s poverty, even if your life should be prolonged for thousands of years; for as a precious treasure his monastery and the whole reigning city have received him.” And he told him everything, and also that he was setting out against the heretics.

When King Milutin heard this, he was moved with fatherly mercy, began to weep, and sent to the Greek emperor to send Stephen to him as quickly as possible. The emperor, with joy and having given many gifts, released him; and he gave them to the Monastery of the Pantocrator.

He came to his father. The father, kissing him with tears and joy, asked his forgiveness for his offense against him. But the imitator of Christ called himself guilty, and, having comforted his father’s weeping, did not reveal his recovery of sight to him, nor to anyone else, nor to anyone in the church; but according to custom, covering his eyes with a cloth, he walked, lest from envy the sight granted him by God should be extinguished again. Then his father commanded him to live in the holy place in Diocletia. There too he continued to live an even more virtuous life.
