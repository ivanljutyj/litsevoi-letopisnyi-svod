In the same year the Grand Prince of Kiev, having gathered many forces, went against Prince Izyaslav Davydovich and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich and Prince Oleg Svyatoslavich; and when they met, they waged a war of words among themselves. But Christ God did not let them commit slaughter and bloodshed, and so they parted each to his own place.

In the same year Theodore the metropolitan came from Constantinople to Kiev and to all Rus, appointed by the patriarch of Constantinople, because the Grand Prince of Kiev Rostislav Mstislavich had sent to the patriarch about him; and the Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich went out of Kiev to meet him, and met him with great honor, and there was great joy in Kiev.

In the same year Prince Izyaslav Davydovich with Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich and with Prince Oleg Svyatoslavich of the Olegovichi, and with a multitude of Polovtsians, went against Prince Rostislav Mstislavich to Kiev, and having come, they stood along the Desna River opposite Vyshgorod.

But Prince Rostislav Mstislavich, seeing the great multitude of their host, was frightened and trembled, for he had help from nowhere except from Prince Andrei Yuryevich and from the people of Kiev; and he wished to flee from Kiev. And his druzhina said to him: “Do not flee, prince, for we can wage battle with them from the city; we have all weapons—stones, stakes, and boiling tar, and all the fighters from the city wall.”

The Grand Prince of Kiev Rostislav Mstislavich listened to them and began battle; and there was a fierce battle between them, and a great host surrounded the whole city, and it was terrible to see, for they covered the whole city with arrows; and so the people in the city grew faint. And Prince Izyaslav Davydovich began to prevail. And the Kiev tysyatsky Zhyroslav Andreevich said to Prince Rostislav Mstislavich: “Lord prince! Flee from the city, then you will be free; but if you remain inside the city, prepare to be taken captive; if you are outside the city, on horseback with your druzhina, you are like a terrible lion, and your druzhina are like bears and wolves, or you are like an eagle flying beneath the clouds, and your druzhina are like hawks—then no one will be able to overcome you.”

He listened to his words and, taking his princess and children, fled from the city of Kiev with all his soldiers to Belgorod.

And entering Belgorod, he shut himself in and sent with entreaty and tears to Prince Vladimir Andreevich and to Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich—that they help him.
