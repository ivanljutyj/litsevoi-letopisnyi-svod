Prince Mstislav had then a small army; all were in great need: some had left earlier, while others had weakened from the hardships of winter; but the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother put the pagans to flight, showing by this an ineffable miracle, defended them and saved them from the hands of the pagans; the Russian host barely returned with great difficulty, because in winter it is hard to fight the Bulgars.

In the same year the Grand Prince Roman Rostislavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir, named Andrew in holy baptism, Monomakh, departed from Kyiv of his own will, because he greatly feared the treachery of the Kyivans and of Prince Mikhail Yuryevich.

In the same year Prince Mikhail Yuryevich took his seat on the great principality in Kyiv.

In the same year Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, took his seat on the great principality in Kyiv.

In the year 6681 from Adam (1181 from Christ)

Prince Mstislav Andreevich, son of Andrew Bogolyubsky, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, reposed in the month of March on the 28th day; he was laid in the Church of the Most Holy Theotokos in Vladimir, which his father had founded.
