He went to Mtsensk, and went to Kromy against Prince Izyaslav Davydovich. And there envoys overtook him with a petition from Prince Vladimir Davydovich and from his brother Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, and from their cousin Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, nephew of the Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich and grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, and they kissed the life-giving Cross before him, that they should be of one accord; and having come to the city of Chernigov, they agreed that all should be of one accord, and they also secretly communicated with Grand Prince Yuri of Suzdal, and after taking counsel they confirmed their unity.

In the same year Prince Andrei Rostislavich came from Ryazan, from Yelets, to the Davydoviches in Chernigov, and after taking counsel they all confirmed their unity.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Chernigov Vladimir Davydovich and his brother Izyaslav Davydovich sent to Izyaslav Mstislavich, Grand Prince of Kiev, saying flatteringly: “Svyatoslav has taken our volosts, the Vyatichi and others; and if we keep silent about this before him, he will encroach on the rest too; and what then can we do, having been utterly impoverished by him? Let us not delay, but gathering our host, let us go against him.”

At that time also Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich came from Chernigov to Kiev to Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, for Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich loved him.

And Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich began to deliberate with his counsellors and with his nephew Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, saying: “What shall I do—shall I go with an army against Svyatoslav Olegovich or not go?” And so, having taken counsel with his counsellors, he sent ahead of himself his nephew Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich to the Grand Prince of Chernigov Vladimir Davydovich and to his brother Prince Izyaslav Davydovich.

Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, having come, told them all that had happened, what he had heard; and they, all gathered, sent again to Kiev to him, saying: “Why do you delay? You yourself have many times wished to go against Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh and Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich for their injustice; do not delay, but go, now is the time.”

And Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich went to his father Clement, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus’, with his brother Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, and said to him: “I wish,” he said, “to go against Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich and Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh with Prince Vladimir Davydovich and with his brother Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, and with Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich and with his brother Rostislav Vsevolodovich.”
