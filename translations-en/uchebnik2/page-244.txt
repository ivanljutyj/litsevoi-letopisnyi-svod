"They do not want to go to war with you against Riga; so we too, prince, will not go; and you, prince, send your army home, and make peace with the Pskovians."

And the men who were in Pskov with Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, those the Pskovians robbed and drove out in disgrace; and these men came and told Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich everything.

Prince Yaroslav was grieved at this and asked the Novgorodians: "What is this?" They said: "You know, prince, the Novgorodians and Pskovians: they do whatever they want."

Then Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich set out with his princess to Pereyaslavl, and in Novgorod he left his two sons, Feodor and Alexander, with his boyar Feodor Danilovich and with other men, preserving all the liberties of Novgorod.

In the same year rain fell without ceasing, day and night, from the Feast of the Transfiguration to St Nicholas's Day; and the Novgorodians began to show discontent with their archbishop Arsenius, saying that because of him the rain had continued so long, since he had driven Archbishop Anthony out to the Khutyn Monastery, having given a large payment to Prince Yaroslav.

And then Arsenius was taken to the Khutyn Monastery, and Anthony was brought from the Khutyn Monastery to the archbishop's house; and there was a great tumult in the city; some were robbed, and some even tasted death.

That same autumn there was a great flood beyond all measure; lakes and rivers overflowed; this brought many disasters to people. And frosts struck; and the great water in Lake Ilmen froze; and again strong, great winds blew, and broke the ice, and turned the water back, and carried into the Volkhov ice larger than usual, and broke out nine sections of the great bridge.

In the same year the Novgorodians sent envoys to Pereyaslavl to Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with these words: "Lord Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, come to us in Novgorod; do not hold evil or anger, do not send judges into the districts; observe all our Novgorod liberties, and live among us according to all the charters of Yaroslav and the ancient customs of Novgorod; and then be our prince, and we will be yours; but if not, then you by yourself, and we by ourselves."

In the same year the sons of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, Feodor and Alexander, with their boyar Feodor and with other men of their father, fled by night from Novgorod to their father in Pereyaslavl.

In the same year the Novgorodians, according to their ancient custom, held an assembly and said thus: "Behold, the princes have fled from us without cause; they know it."
