Blessed Alexander with his father Yaroslav were reigning in Veliky Novgorod; a certain Divine power prevented the pagans from reaching there.

And that power did not allow the Tatars to approach in the least, not only the borders of Veliky Novgorod, but wherever else it happened that princes Alexander and Yaroslav were then warring against the hostile Lithuanians and Germans, there too, by God’s will, these wild Tatars did not attack them at all.

Of the battle and victory on the Neva, of the appearance of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb and of angelic help, and of the six brave men

Afterwards certain people came from the western lands, who in the writings of Methodius of Patara are called “servants of God.” From these people came a famous man named Andriash, desiring to see the wisdom, courage, and wondrous stature of blessed Alexander Yaroslavich, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, forefather Yaroslav, and great-great-forefather of great Vladimir. As once the queen from the south came to Solomon to see his wisdom, so likewise this Andriash came to see the holy and great Prince Alexander; and he marveled greatly at the beauty of his face and his wondrous stature. Most of all Andriash marveled when he saw the wisdom and steadfast understanding God had granted Alexander; he did not know how to express it and was in great bewilderment.

When Andriash returned from Alexander and came to his own land, he began to tell of him with wonder: “I have been in many lands and among many peoples, and I have seen many kings and princes, but nowhere have I met one equal in beauty and courage to the great Prince Alexander, neither a king among kings nor a prince among princes.”

These words of praise about the courage and understanding of blessed Alexander reached the king of the Roman land, who ruled in its northern part; and he was filled with envy and hatred. In his pride the king thought that the time had come, for he knew that Batu had then conquered Rus from all sides. The king, for his part, hoped to swallow up what remained of Rus and in his pride spoke thus: “I will go and seize Veliky Novgorod and the other cities, and I will make all the Slavic people slaves to myself, and I will strike down the great Prince Alexander himself or take him alive.”
