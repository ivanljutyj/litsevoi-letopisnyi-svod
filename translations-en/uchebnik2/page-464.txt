And I made a vow to God to found a church in honor of the saint on whose memorial day we would reach the shore. And at that very moment the wind and the sea swell calmed, stillness came, and the ship peacefully put in to shore on the sixteenth day of the month of August. And according to my vow I decided to build a church of the Image of Our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ Not Made by Hands and to establish a cenobitic community there,” so said the hierarch to those close to him.

The hierarch came to the blessed Sergius and said to him: “I wish,” he said, “now to fulfill my promise.” The blessed Sergius answered the hierarch thus: “You wish to do a good and blessed deed, and if you require anything of us, it will all be permitted to you.” The metropolitan replied: “I wish to place your disciple Andronicus as head of that monastery.” The venerable Sergius answered him: “Do as you wish.”

Saint Alexius gave the monastery a large alms, established the brethren, and, taking with him the monk Andronicus, set out from the monastery to Moscow.

And he found near Moscow on the Yauza River a place suitable for building a monastery, and with the help of the Lord God and the Most Holy Mother of God he erected a church in the name of the Image of Christ Not Made by Hands, which he had brought with him from Constantinople, adorned with gold and pearls. And in that monastery he set up churches, which stand to this day; the hierarch gave the monastery everything necessary, and villages, and waters, and lands.

The venerable abbot Sergius came to that monastery, praised and blessed that place, and returned to his monastery in Radonezh.

After some time had passed, Saint Alexius, Metropolitan of All Rus, set out for Nizhny Novgorod, founded there a church in the name of the Honorable and Glorious Annunciation of the Most Pure Mother of God, established a cenobitic rule in the monastery, granted the monastery villages, waters, and lands, supplied everything necessary, and adorned the Church of the Annunciation of the Most Pure Mother of God.
