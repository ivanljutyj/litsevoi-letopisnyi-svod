A year later the aforementioned abbot Mitrofan, who had tonsured blessed Sergius and afterward departed, came again to blessed Sergius, his own tonsured disciple, and remained there to live and to serve as priest. Then he fell ill, and after being ill for some time he departed, leaving this fleeting life. That man was like a saint, distinguished and covered with gray hair. Venerable Sergius grieved over his death and prayed to the Lord God to send him an abbot to that desolate place; he sorrowed and besought the Lord God for an abbot.

Then the brothers assembled, took counsel, and coming to one mind, they went to the venerable one, saying: “Father, we cannot live without an abbot. Accept the priestly rank and be our abbot in this place, our pastor and father, so that we may come to you with repentance, confessing our sins, and receive blessing and forgiveness from you. And every day we wish to see you celebrating the liturgy and to receive communion from your hands.” But he refused. The brothers asked him twice and three times. Sometimes calmly and affectionately, sometimes with threats and cruel words they threatened him, with bitterness. But he, strong in soul, firm in faith, humble in mind, did not yield to affectionate words and did not fear threats, but considered himself sinful and unworthy. “Who am I, that I should dare such a thing? Even the angels cannot comprehend this with fear, trembling, and awe. How then can I, sinful and unworthy, dare such a thing? My words do not agree with your words, because you too persistently compel me to the abbacy, and I too persistently refuse. I want to learn myself rather than teach others, and to live under authority rather than rule over others.” Then the brothers decided to disperse. And only after this was he constrained to agree, saying: “That to which you compel me is beyond my measure, but the will of the Lord be done!” And he obeyed their will, saying: “If God wills it, then there will be a bishop. I will no longer resist and will go to the city of Pereslavl to Bishop Afanasy Volynsky.” Metropolitan Alexei was at that time in Tsargrad, and in Pereslavl he had left Bishop Afanasy Volynsky in his place. Sergius came to him, taking with him two elders, and bowed to the bishop as is proper. Seeing Sergius, he spoke with him about the founding of the monastery, and having conversed with him about spiritual matters, he asked him the reason for his coming. He began to ask for an abbot for his newly founded monastery. Afanasy said: “It is fitting for you to be abbot in your monastery.” Sergius said: “Bishop of God, I am unworthy, but I dare not disobey you. If it is so pleasing to God, then let your holy prayers help my unworthiness.”

The hierarch entered the church, vested himself in the sacred robes, and commanded Sergius to recite: “I believe in one God.” When it was finished, he made the sign of the cross over him.
