And they shot arrows at him. He fled, saying: “God forbid that one should trust a pagan; and now I have done this by my simple mind, and I have perished in soul and body.” The Berendei killed his retinue and captured others, while he fled to Dorogobuzh, for his princess was there, having fled to Vladimir Andreevich; but he did not let him in.

And he went against the Ratmichi to Suzdal. Prince Andrei Yuryevich sent to meet him, saying: “Go to Prince Gleb Rostislavich in Ryazan.” He went to Ryazan, leaving his wife and children in the town of Glukhov.

In the same year Metropolitan Constantine came from the patriarch in Constantinople to Kiev and all Rus, and Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich of Kiev met him with the boyars and the people, and so entered with him into the city of Kiev, and entering the church, they offered prayer and thanksgiving to the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God.

In the same year the Novgorodians assembled a council and held a veche, and decided to seize their lord Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich; but he, learning of it, fled by night to Luki.

The Novgorodians, filled with fury and wrath, gathered and went to drive him from Luki; he heard of this and quickly fled from there to Toropets; and from there he went to the Volga.

Prince Andrei, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, gave him help; and he burned New Torg, which belonged to the Novgorodians, and the Novotorzhians fled to Novgorod, and much evil was done to the Novotorzhians, their volosts and villages laid waste.

And his brother Prince Roman Rostislavich and Prince Mstislav burned Luki, and the Lukians fled—some to Novgorod, and others to Pskov.

In the same year the Novgorodians sent to Kiev to Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, asking him for his son to reign among them in Novgorod.

In the same year Prince Andrei Yuryevich agreed with one mind with the Smolensk and Polotsk people against the Novgorodians, and blocked their roads, and intercepted their envoys and merchants, not allowing news to reach Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich in Kiev.

Prince Svyatoslav, son of Rostislav, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, and of the line of Great Vladimir, stood with his force and sent into the city to the Novgorodians, and urged them, wishing to be prince among them, and secretly communicated with their posadnik Zakhar; for this Zakhar loved Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich and served him, keeping it secret from the Novgorodians. Svyatoslav Rostislavich spoke thus to the Novgorodians: “You have no prince other than me, Prince Svyatoslav Rostislavich.” But the Novgorodians did not wish to hear, not only him but also his words, and after deliberating they killed their posadnik Zakhar Yakovlevich, and Nerevin, and Nezda the birich—
