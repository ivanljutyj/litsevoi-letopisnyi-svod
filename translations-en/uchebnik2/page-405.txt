In the same year the office of posadnik in Novgorod was given to Antsifer Lukin. In the same year Vladyka Vasily of Novgorod built a stone chamber.

In the same year the Novgorodians expelled from Novgorod to Pskov the posadnik Theodore and his brothers Michael, George, and Andrian, and they plundered their houses and plundered all of Prusskaya Street. They left Pskov for Koporye.

Then Prince Yuri Vitovtovich was killed near Izborsk. In the same year a plague fell upon the people by the will of the Lord God, who permitted punishment upon His creation, who at all times cares for the human race and grants salvation, always seeking our turning to Him and repentance for our evil deeds, which we commit, sinning ceaselessly.

He, caring for our salvation, sometimes brings upon us famine, sometimes plague, sometimes wars, sometimes various diseases, sometimes misfortunes: disasters, sorrows, and various griefs. For it is written: “Through many tribulations we must enter the Kingdom of Heaven. Many are the wounds of the sinner, and many the sorrows of the righteous, and the Lord will deliver them from all.” If we turn away from our lawlessness and repent of our sins, then may He turn away His wrath from us, forgive our transgressions, and grant us blessedness, and by His mercy destroy our enemies, if we live according to His commandments and do His will—the Lord is near to all who call upon Him.

In the year 6860 from Adam (1360 from Christ)

Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich with his brothers, Princes Ivan and Andrew, and with other princes, having gathered a great force, went to war against Smolensk. When they reached Vyshgorod on the Porotva, envoys came to him from the Grand Prince of Lithuania Olgerd Gediminovich with many gifts, proposing peace. He did not disregard Olgerd’s word and, agreeing to peace, returned to Moscow and sent the envoys away in peace.

But he himself set out for the Ugra, intending to go to Smolensk. And then Smolensk envoys came to him. He stood on the Ugra for eight days, and sent his envoys to Smolensk, and, having concluded peace, returned to Moscow.

In the same year an envoy Akhmat came to Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver from the Horde, from the tsar, and brought him a yarlyk in his name. And Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver began to be angry with his nephew Prince Vsevolod Alexandrovich Kholmsky, recalling his Bezdezh plunder.
