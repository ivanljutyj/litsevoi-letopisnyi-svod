All the Pskovians marched out to Izborsk and joined stubborn battle with the Germans; and there was a great engagement between them; there they killed Gavriil Gorislavovich, the Pskov voivode; and the Germans defeated the Pskovians and, pursuing them, killed many and took some alive; and the Germans drove the Pskovians back to Pskov.

The Germans burned the settlement around the fortress of Pskov completely; and much harm was done: churches, venerable icons, and all church property burned.

The Germans also laid waste many villages in the outskirts of Pskov; they stood by the city for a week, but did not take the city.

The Germans also seized many children of good people and led them away into captivity, and departed again, and did not conclude peace. The Germans suborned the Pskovians; Tverdilo Ivankovich became a traitor, and some others with him.

Tverdilo himself began to hold Pskov together with the Germans, ravaging Novgorodian villages.

And some Pskovians fled from German oppression to Novgorod, with their wives and children.

In the same year, after the return and after the victory of Grand Prince Alexander, in that same winter, troops of Germans and Chud again came from the western land and attacked the land of the Vod, and conquered it all, and imposed tribute upon them.

And the Germans built a town at Koporye, in a village, in the homeland of Grand Prince Alexander.

But that was not the only evil; the Germans also took Tesovo, and pursued them thirty versts to Novgorod, beating merchants; and thence even to Luga and to Sabl.

And with great booty the Germans returned to their own land, and imposed tribute on some towns as well.

Then the Novgorodians sent ambassadors with a petition to Grand Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, asking him for a son to rule over them; he gave them his son Prince Andrei; then they asked for another son of Yaroslav, Grand Prince Alexander; and he did not give him to them.

The Novgorodians went to Novgorod and reported all the words of Yaroslav, that he had not given them Grand Prince Alexander to rule in Novgorod.

In the same year the Novgorodians, according to their custom, held an assembly; and they sent Archbishop Spiridon, with boyars and with leading men, again to Grand Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with a petition, asking for Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich for themselves.

Archbishop Spiridon, with those boyars and with the Novgorodian people, came to Grand Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich in Vladimir.
