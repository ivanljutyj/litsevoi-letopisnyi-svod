And so he now leaves the reign and comes out of the church by the Red Gates. A multitude of people had gathered, countless in number! He, standing on the high step before the church, bowed to all the people, asked their forgiveness, and said to them: "My brethren, good retinue, sons of Tver! Until now I have ruled over you; now forgive me, for I leave you my beloved eldest son Ivan; let him be prince to you in my place. Love him as you love me, and he will care for you as I have." The people who heard him began to weep, sobbing bitterly and saying: "Why are you leaving us, and where are you going? O great freedom of Tver and honorable glory of the sons of Tver, great guardian of the city of Tver, who always guarded us like an eagle its nest, and through you the sons of Tver were held in honor in other lands, and they did not dare to offend us!" And a great weeping arose among the people. He bowed humbly to all and gave them a farewell exhortation. After this his gaze dimmed, and the radiance of his face turned to pallor from great exhaustion and grave illness.

He descended the church steps. His sons and boyars thought he was going to his own court, but he, motioning with his hand, ordered them to lead him to the monastery of Saint Athanasius. And his princess Evdokia, with the other princesses and her daughters-in-law, and also his sons and grandchildren, the boyars and all the people, hearing this from him, raised a great lamentation. And so he went to the lavra of Saint Athanasius. They all accompanied him with weeping. O touching sight, worthy of tears! Who would not marvel at this! And how could one not shed tears, seeing how the great prince, who was famed in all lands and whom enemies feared, leaves such a glorious reign and humbly, on his own feet, goes to the monastery, and tenderly asks the bishop for tonsure. The venerable Bishop Arseny, with tears thanking the Lord God, tonsures him and clothes him in the holy angelic monastic image, naming him Matthew. And the people said: "Truly the Lord does not deprive the generous of gentleness," and "These are the eyes of the Lord upon those who fear Him," and also "Blessed is God, who has done the will of those who fear Him."

And Matthew, good in soul, now without authority, comes to his brother, the monk Gregory, and humbly asks his permission to settle in his cell, giving a gift in the name of the wise Creator, the Lord God, who orders all things in love for mankind. Then he began to be ill. His noble sons sat beside him the whole time.

The steadfast Matthew on the fourth day summons the bishop and says that he wishes to receive the schema. The bishop, rejoicing spiritually, tonsures him into the schema and for many days anoints him with holy oil, and communes him with the most pure mysteries of Christ.

By God's grace his illness was not very severe, so that he did everything sitting. He ordered that abbots from all over the city come to him.
