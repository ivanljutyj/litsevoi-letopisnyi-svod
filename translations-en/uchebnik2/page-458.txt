Mstislav, great-grandson of the blessed great-martyr Michael of Chernigov, whom the impious Tsar Batu killed for the Orthodox faith, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Svyatoslav, forefather of Oleg, great-great-forefather of Svyatoslav, great-great-great-forefather of Yaroslav Vladimirovich.

And another son of Titus, Andrian Zvenigorodsky, took as his wife Elena, daughter of the Lithuanian prince Gamant, grandson of Mstislav of Korachev, great-grandson of the great-martyr Grand Prince Michael of Chernigov, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Svyatoslav, forefather of Oleg, great-great-forefather of Svyatoslav, great-great-great-forefather of Yaroslav Vladimirovich.

And the third son of Titus, Prince Ivan Kozelsky, grandson of Mstislav of Korachev, married the daughter of Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan, great-grandson of the great-martyr Grand Prince Michael of Chernigov.

In the same year Prince Feodor, son of Andrian Zvenigorodsky, defeated many Tatars. Prince Feodor Zvenigorodsky, son of Andrian, grandson of Titus, great-grandson of Mstislav of Korachev, great-great-grandson of the great-martyr Grand Prince Michael of Chernigov, was a man of heroic build, brave in battles with enemies, and possessed great courage and strength.

Olgerd gave his son the glorious great city of Kiev, but after Olgerd's death his brother Skirigailo took Kiev from him, seized him and sent him away, and there he died; and Skirigailo ruled in Kiev.

In the same year, on the twenty-third day of the month of April, on the feast of the holy great-martyr George the Victory-Bearer, there was a sign in the sky: the moon was hidden, and rays in the form of a cross issued from it.

In the same year Tsarevich Arapsha fled from the Blue Horde beyond the Volga to Mamai's Volga Horde. That Tsarevich Arapsha was of a very fierce disposition, a great warrior, courageous and strong, young in age but great in valor; he defeated many warriors and wished to march with an army against Nizhny Novgorod. The Grand Prince of Nizhny Novgorod and Suzdal, Dmitry Konstantinovich, sent a messenger to Moscow to his son-in-law, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich gathered many warriors and came to Novgorod to aid his father-in-law, Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich, with a large army, but there was no news at all of Tsarevich Arapsha. And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich returned to Moscow, but left his commanders there together with the Vladimirians, Pereyaslavians, Yuryevians, Muromians, and Yaroslavians.

And afterward people began to say that the Tatars were in the Field, and that Tsarevich Arapsha was hiding in secret places, but this did not become known to the Russian princes and commanders, and they allowed an oversight.
