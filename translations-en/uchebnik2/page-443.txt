And His Grace Metropolitan Alexius escorted him to the Oka River, prayed for him, blessed him, and sent him, his boyars, and his servants away in peace, while he himself returned to Moscow.

At that time envoys came from the Grand Prince of Lithuania proposing love and peace, offering Olgerd’s daughter Elena as a wife to Prince Vladimir Andreevich.

In the same year Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow came to Mamai’s Horde, to Tsar Mamai, and showed honor to Prince Mamai and to the tsar, the tsaritsa, and the princes; he was again granted the great principality of Vladimir, received honors from the tsar and the princes, and they sent him back to Rus with honors and gifts for the great reign.

And they ordered Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich to be told: “We gave you the great principality, we gave you help and troops to seat you in the great principality, but you did not accept our help and troops, and said that you would seat yourself in the great principality. Now rule with whomever you please, but do not turn to us for help.”

In the same year Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver went with an army and took Kostroma, Mologa, Uglich, and Bezhetsky Verkh, and installed his governors in them.

In the same year Metropolitan German came from Jerusalem to the Russian land asking for alms to pay debts to the Saracens.

In the same year Novgorodians from Veliky Novgorod, the Ushkuinik robbers, seized Kostroma.

In the same year there was a sign: black spots like nails appeared on the sun, and such darkness descended on the earth that one could see nothing a sazhen ahead, and many people collided in the darkness and struck against one another. Birds could not fly through the air because they saw nothing; they fell to the earth, and some even struck people’s heads.

Likewise animals, seeing nothing, came into villages and towns and collided with people: bears, wolves, foxes, and various other beasts. Then there was a severe drought and heat, so that people were frightened; many rivers dried up, and lakes and marshes, and forests and pinewoods burned, dried marshes caught fire, and the earth itself burned; fear and trembling seized people. And then bread became very expensive, and famine spread throughout the whole land.

In the same year Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod, grandson of Vasily, great-grandson of Mikhail, built the church of St Nicholas the Wonderworker in Nizhny Novgorod.

In the same year there was a fire in Novgorod.
In the same year the Novgorodians concluded peace with the Germans.
