About Temir-Aksak, How He Wanted to Go to Rus to Wage War, but the Lord Destroyed Him with Frost and Preserved the Russian Land Unharmed

Before this, Temir-Aksak, as was said above, had overcome and destroyed many kings and rulers, and ruled their kingdoms, returning to his homeland, Orar.

And again he gathers an even greater army, a godless host, and again to his own destruction he rages against the Christian race, the new Israel. Like the ancient Pharaoh, whose heart God hardened to pursue Israel and work evil. But the Lord led them through the sea as on dry land, and Pharaoh drowned in it with all his host. In like manner Temir-Aksak also became hardened; the wretched man forgot the fearful and terrible threat of the holy angels, whom he had seen in the form of holy hierarchs with golden staffs, and above whom in the air he had also seen the Most Pure Mother of God with a great host, sternly forbidding him any designs against Christians. And he forgot, lawless one, the first apparition to him with a prohibition, how he then trembled and how immeasurably terrified he was, shaking and groaning and crying out wildly, refusing to fight against the Russian land, and how the power of God drove him back to his own home—he forgot all this.

And rising up, the despised one first wished with all his host to go to war against Tsargrad; so exalted was he, because his countless strength had increased. And the accursed Temir sent his countless host against Tsar Digman. They went, captured and burned that land, and gave the people over to the all-devouring sword.

Tsar Digman himself, with the other men of his synclite, was brought to the godless and accursed Temir. When he saw him, the wretched one became even more proud.

And coming from there to Oktay, he wanted to go to the Horde lands and against the Russian land. But in those places winter began.

The merciful God not only did not allow him to harm the Russian land, but brought it about that the blood-devouring Temir-Aksak himself ended his life with a villainous death. From the frost that struck, a multitude of his warriors died, and they reported to him that 120 tents remained empty. The insane Temir-Aksak wished to outwit God's vengeance and rashly thought to overcome the fury of the frost, not knowing, the bold one, that the divine David said: "Who can stand before His frost?" Temir-Aksak put on many garments, up to twenty fur coats, and went himself to look upon his frozen host. But he could not look upon them because of the unbearable cold and returned to his camp. And from this severe cold his vitals were damaged.
