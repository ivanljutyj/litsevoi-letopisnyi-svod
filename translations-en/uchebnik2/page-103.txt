Beloved in the Lord spiritual son, to your God-loving bishop: for when a feast of the Lord, the Nativity of Christ and Theophany, falls on a Wednesday or Friday, the bishop permits the laity to eat meat and everything, but monks milk, cow’s butter, cheese, and eggs; on the Nativity of the Most Pure Mother of God, the Meeting, the Dormition, and the Transfiguration of Christ, on Wednesday and Friday, both monks and laity may have olive oil, vegetables, fish, and wine; on the Annunciation until Palm Week, whatever day it may be, oil, vegetables, and wine are permitted. In Holy Week until Good Friday, oil and wine; on Good Friday itself, wine only. And the two feasts—of John the Forerunner and of the holy apostles Peter and Paul—and of the beloved John the Theologian, and of other eminent saints, if they fall on Wednesday or Friday, if the bishop blesses and commands, let them eat olive oil, vegetables, fish, and wine, and so do. On the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, olive oil and wine; and during the twelve days from the Nativity of Christ to the Baptism, and on the week of the Publican and Pharisee, and on Cheese-fare Week, and on Bright Week, and on the week after Trinity Day. But from Bright Week even to Pentecost, on Wednesdays and Fridays we do not keep the fast that lasts until the ninth hour; yet even if we abstain from cheese, cow’s butter, and meat according to the prohibitions of the apostolic rule, we nevertheless receive olive oil and fish, because on these days we make remembrance of the Lord’s Crucifixion and sing troparia proper to the Crucifixion, but we also sing the resurrection troparia together, just as on other days we mingle in compunctionate troparia; if a feast of the Lord falls on Wednesday or Friday, or that of some particular saint, even until Pentecost itself, ask your head, that is your bishop, and whatever he says, do so, believing that the Lord God speaks through the mouth of your bishop: if he does not permit, then fast. For no one among all people—whether hierarch, presbyter, monk, or angel, even an angel from heaven—has such power to bind and loose, but only your one God-loving bishop, whom the Lord God has set as head over all your land and over you. Henceforth keep yourself from the teachings of wicked, flattering, and crafty people; drive this same Theodore from you and compel him to go to the bishop, and if he turns and repents, thanks be to God; but if he still begins, while remaining there, to disturb the church, to speak reproaches, and to bring vexations against the bishop, doing what is contrary to the sacred and divine rules, let the sacred and divine great council with our humility count him excommunicated. We decree this by the command and authority of the Holy Spirit, and by the ordinance and word of the great High Priest our Savior Jesus Christ.
