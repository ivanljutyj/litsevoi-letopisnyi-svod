But Roman did not listen either to him or to his counsellors: Prince Roman’s men desired peace, but he went out with his regiment against him, and the Poles fought the Rus, the Poles trampled the Rus, Mieszko defeated Prince Roman, and many Rus warriors were killed in his regiment; and Prince Roman himself, wounded, fled to the Kazimiroviches in the city, and from there his druzhina took him and carried him to Vladimir.

And he sent an envoy to his father-in-law Rurik, bowing to him, asking forgiveness and laying all the blame on himself; he also sent to Metropolitan Nikephoros, asking him to intercede for him with his father-in-law, repenting before them of his guilt.

The metropolitan interceded for him with Rurik, that he make peace with him and forgive his guilt, because he repents and takes all the blame upon himself.

And Grand Prince Rurik Rostislavich of Kiev listened to his father Nikephoros, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, and forgave him his guilt, made peace with him on the holy and life-giving cross, and gave him the volosts of Polotsk, one and a half times larger than Russian Pereyaslavl.

In the same year Grand Prince Rurik Rostislavich of Kiev sent to his brother Grand Prince David Rostislavich of Smolensk, also sent to Grand Prince Vsevolod of Vladimir, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, also sent to the princes of Ryazan, telling them all about the deceit of the Chernigov princes, and, having taken counsel together, they all sent their envoys to Grand Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich of Chernigov and to all the Olegovichi, saying: “Kiss the cross, all of you, that you will not seek our patrimony, neither Kiev nor Smolensk, nor strive to take it from our children and from the whole line of Vladimir; let all be as our great forefather Yaroslav divided it: the lands as far as the Dnieper he gave to you, and you have no need of Kiev.”

The Olegovichi complained about this to Vsevolod, saying: “If you have brought us here so that we should keep Kiev for you and for your in-law Rurik, then on that we shall stand; but if you propose that we renounce it forever, then we are not Hungarians, not Poles, but grandchildren of one grandfather; while you live we shall not try to seize it, and after you—to whom God gives it.”

The Olegovichi sent to him, saying: “You are crafty, and by craft you seek to get your way.”

And again they sent embassies; this happened repeatedly, but nothing was decided.

But Vsevolod wished to rule over the whole line of the Vladimirovichi and began gathering warriors against the Olegovichi.
