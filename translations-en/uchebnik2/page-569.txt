The metropolitan asked them that within a month he might establish his metropolitan court. And the Novgorodians all answered with one voice: “We have all kissed the cross that we should not be summoned to the metropolitan court, and that the metropolitan should not judge us. And concerning this we signed charters, affixed seals, and sealed them.” And Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus’, said to them: “It is Satan, by his wiles, who has advised you to take away the authority, the court, and the sacred and divine dues that were appointed to God from the beginning. How is it that you are not ashamed to say this to me? Repent of this sin and be merciful in your lawlessness, because Satan has led you astray. Turn to God with repentance from your whole heart, and the Lord God will forgive you this sin and grant you mercy. And I will release you from your oath, and tear your seals, and tear up your charters, and bless you, and absolve you by the grace given to me from God the Father and his only-begotten Son our Lord Jesus Christ and the Most Holy and Life-giving Spirit. But you must give me the court and the dues, as it was from the beginning under other metropolitans—to render what is appointed to God. What was from the beginning appointed to be given to God in the great catholic apostolic metropolitan church has been fulfilled by us; it is inalienable and shall remain unchanged from generation to generation.” But they did not listen to the metropolitan or heed his words and his teaching. And they made nothing of the letters of Patriarch Anthony and his teaching. But they spoke, puffed up with pride, led astray by demonic teachings. Metropolitan Cyprian said to them: “I too am a sinner, but I will pray for you to the Lord God, to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to all the saints. I instructed and taught you and gave you God-pleasing counsel that could save you. But you have hardened your hearts and resist the Holy Spirit, and you despise the apostolic and patristic teachings. And what is appointed to God—the metropolitan courts, properties, and dues—you have seized for your own use; and you revile with blasphemous words contrary to the Holy Spirit and the Orthodox Christian faith. I have done all I could for your salvation, and the only and most grievous punishment that remains is excommunication.”

And Metropolitan Cyprian departed from Novgorod without blessing their bishop Ivan and all the Novgorodians, because of their disobedience, hardness of heart, and resistance. Because they had trampled what was appointed to God and laid blasphemy upon Orthodoxy.
