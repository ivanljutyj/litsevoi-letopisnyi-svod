Musulman himself rose against the eastern lands, seeking his ancestral authority, and pursued and killed his brother Azbek; and he subdued the eastern lands and the Turkish land.

After peace was concluded with Musulman, the Serbian land remained in submission. But the devil could not endure this for long: he stirred up his brother Vuk, and Vuk obtained from Tsar Musulman a great host: “Let my brother Despot Stefan give me half of the patrimony,” he said, “and I will serve you from it; if not, I will plunder it and make it desolate.” But Despot Stefan did not want the pious flock, which the Lord had freed, to be enslaved again by the Turks. And Vuk with the Hagarenes went through the whole land, taking captives, burning, and cutting down like wild beasts.

Then he came a second time with an even greater army; but Stefan did not go out against them, so as not to be a partaker in his brother’s blood, and he also feared betrayal by those who remained with him, because Vuk had drawn all to himself—some by promise of gifts, others by threats—sending letters to those who were with the despot.

The despot, holding his letters in his hands, wept in his house in Belgrade before the image of the Savior: “Look, O Christ,” he said, “how unjustly they plot against me, and my servants have become traitors to me, like Your disciple Judas of old; preserve to the end the small number left to me.” And Vuk with the Hagarenes ravaged the whole land.

And Musa began to possess all the scepters, and made peace with Despot Stefan. And Stefan again became the sole autocrat of the whole Serbian land.

Tsar Musa plundered many lands and began to arm against Despot Stefan in order to deliver the Serbian land to final destruction, and he also assigned towns to his nobles.

And Sultan Mehmed came from the East, and the despot from the West, and with him also the Hungarian voivodes and the Bosnian rulers. Musa came into the mountains, intending to block the way, so that no one might escape the soldiers of the sultan and the despot.

The despot sent his nephew Gurg with an army, and when battle took place, Musa was defeated, and they drowned him in the river.

On Mehmed

Mehmed was tsar of the East and the West, and of all the Turks; he was kind and meek. Then Lord Gurg came. Despot Stefan honored him with many gifts and thanked God for everything; and a great tranquility came.
