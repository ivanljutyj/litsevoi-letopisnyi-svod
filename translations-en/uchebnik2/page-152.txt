The Grand Prince of Kiev, Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, and all the Russian princes, learning that the Polovtsians had fled, returned to Kiev and dispersed to their principalities.

About the Polovtsians

The Polovtsians heard that the princes had returned to their lands, and secretly galloped to Pereyaslavl, and captured all the towns along the Sula; at Pereyaslavl they fought for a day.

Vladimir Glebovich, seeing that the Polovtsians might take the outer fort, went out to them from the city with a small retinue, fought them valiantly, but the prince was surrounded; when the townspeople saw their defenders growing faint, they quickly ran out of the city, began to fight the Polovtsians valiantly, and as soon as they rescued the prince, wounded by three spears, they ran back into the city with him and shut the gates. The Polovtsians returned to their own borders with many captives.

Soon Igor Svyatoslavich, grandson of Oleg, fled from the Polovtsians: for the Lord will not leave the righteous in the hand of the sinner; the Polovtsians pursued him but did not find him, for God saved him from the hands of the pagans; the other captives they began to guard strictly and bound them in iron fetters. All this happened because of our sins.

In the same year the Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich sent his commanders with the townspeople against the Bulgars, and after taking many captives they returned home.

About the strife among the Ryazan princes

In the same year the Ryazan princes, the Glebovichs — Roman, Igor and Vladimir — rose against their younger brothers, Vsevolod and Svyatoslav, and a wicked internecine war began in their land; brother wished to kill brother, and they deceitfully summoned them to a council in order to take them captive; but they, learning of this, began to fortify the city. The brothers heard that the princes had begun fortifying the city, gathered many warriors and went to Pronsk, began seizing the towns and villages belonging to the brothers, while those shut themselves up in Pronsk.

The Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich heard what was happening and sent his envoy to the Glebovichs — to Roman, Igor and Vladimir — wishing to pacify them, so that bloodshed between brothers should not begin, and he said to them: “Why, brothers, do you act thus, ruining your land? It would not be surprising if pagans came to wage war on your land, but now you yourselves wish to destroy your brothers.” Hearing this, they were indignant, began to rage against him, stir up great enmity, and plot war.
