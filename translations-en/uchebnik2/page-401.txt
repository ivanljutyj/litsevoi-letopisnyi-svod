The people of Oreshek shut themselves up in the town, and the king surrounded the town; those whom he caught he had baptized into his own faith, and he sent out all his host to make raids.

The Novgorodians, however, sent Onisifor Lukin against the raiders, and in that campaign they killed five hundred Germans.

King Magnus, during the internal strife, took Oreshek and baptized many into his faith, but some did not submit to him. He took a ransom, and carried off the tysyatsky Avram and eleven noble men with him overseas. He released the namestnik Narimant and the others from the town, but left his host in the town, strengthening the fortress.

And the Novgorodians sent a request to Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich that he come to their aid. The Grand Prince reached Torzhok and returned, because kilichei from the Horde overtook him, announcing to him the Tsar's word concerning a gift: the Tsar then handed over to him Olgerd's brother Kariad. And then he himself returned to the city of Moscow, and to Novgorod he sent his brother Prince Ivan Ivanovich, and Prince Konstantin Borisovich of Rostov, and Ivan Akinyfovich, and with them voivodes and a great host. When the princes came to Novgorod, they learned that the Germans had taken the town of Oreshek, besieged it, and fortified it; but they did not go against the Germans, and instead went from Novgorod to the Niz, without taking the Vladyka's blessing and without heeding the Novgorodians' petition.

The Novgorod posadnik Feodor Danilovich, and the namestniki of Prince Semyon Ivanovich, and all the Novgorodians, and the Pskovians, and all the Novgorod land, having united, went to the town of Oreshek, defeated the Germans, and killed Lyudka, their voivode.

And the Novgorodians decided to stand long before the town, but the Pskovians did not wish to stand long. The Novgorodians said to them: "It was agreed between us that our posadnik should neither sit with you in Pskov nor judge, but the Vladyka shall judge your Pskovians, and you are not to be taken or invited from Novgorod as Novgorod noblemen, nor as Novgorod guards, nor as men of St Sophia, nor as informers, nor as heralds. The Pskovians are brothers to us, and Pskov is a younger brother to Novgorod. But you have quickly forgotten love and mercy, and you do not wish to endure a little in order to terrify the enemies. And if you are so stubborn, then go home by night, so that the enemies may not see and rejoice, saying: 'They are at variance.'" The Pskovians, however, left at midday, sounding trumpets and beating drums, playing pipes and fifes. The Germans, seeing this, began to mock. The Novgorodians, enraged, gathered all the Novgorod land, collected many supplies, and, having strengthened themselves, prepared to stand long before the town. The Germans in the town began to weaken, for they had no grain supplies, and because of this they suffered grievously.
