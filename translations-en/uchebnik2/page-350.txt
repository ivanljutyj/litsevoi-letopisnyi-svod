In the same year Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich of Moscow sent Prince Feodor Rzhevsky to Novgorod. And he, having come to Novgorod, seized the namestniks of Prince Mikhail of Tver who were sitting in Novgorod.

And he went with the Novgorodians to the Volga, and Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich, grandson of Yaroslav, came out against him with the men of Tver, for his father, Prince Mikhail, was then in the Horde, and he stood against them on his own side of the Volga River. And they stood thus for six weeks, until the frosts, and so the Novgorodians made peace on their own terms.

And they sent to Moscow for Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich, summoning him to them in Novgorod. And he came to them in Novgorod and sat on their throne, together with his brother Afanasy.

In the year 6823 from Adam (1323 from Christ)

Tsar Azbyak of the Horde sent from the Horde for Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich of Moscow, that he should come to him in the Horde without delay.

He, leaving his brother Afanasy Danilovich in Novgorod, went from there to Rostov, and from there went to the tsar in the Horde, on the fifteenth day of March, on Lazarus Saturday.

In the same year Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Starodubsky died, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Vsevolod.

In the same year Grand Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich of Tver came from the tsar in the Horde, and with him the ambassadors Taitemir and Imar-Khozha, and Indiya.

In the same year Prince Afanasy Danilovich, grandson of Alexander, great-grandson of Yaroslav, went from Novgorod to Torzhok with the Novgorod boyars but without the common folk.

Grand Prince Mikhailo of Tver heard of this, and gathered all the lowland host and the Tatars, and went to Torzhok.

And Prince Afanasy Danilovich and Prince Feodor Rzhevsky came out with the Novgorodians against them, and there was a great battle and a fierce slaughter, and they beat many men of Novgorod: thousanders, posadniks, boyars, and other notables, and other Novgorodian forces fell, beyond number, and Prince Afanasy Danilovich and Prince Feodor Rzhevsky barely fled with a few survivors to Novgorod.

And Prince Mikhailo Yaroslavich sent to the Novgorodians, saying: “Hand over to me Prince Afanasy Danilovich and Prince Feodor Rzhevsky.” The Novgorodians handed over Prince Feodor Rzhevsky, but for Prince Afanasy Danilovich they stood firm. And for themselves they gave a ransom—five thousand grivnas of silver. And having made an agreement, they made peace.
