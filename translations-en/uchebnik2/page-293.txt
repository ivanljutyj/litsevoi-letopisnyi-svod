Alexander

Alexander did not delay in Vladimir, but stayed a little while, took the blessing of Bishop Cyril, and set out on his way.

Alexander came to Tsar Batu, and everywhere the grace of God illumined him. Tsar Batu saw him and was amazed, and said to his nobles: “They told me the truth, that there is no prince like this one,” and he received him with great honor and endowed him with many gifts. Thus God distinguishes His chosen ones, putting it even into the minds of the ungodly that they should respect and honor them.

Later Batu sent Alexander with his brother Andrew Yaroslavich to the khan’s sons.

In the same year the ungodly Tsar Batu with many armies went to war against the western Hungarians, to the western lands which he had not reached before, and he destroyed and laid waste many cities.

No one could withstand Batu; he took many princes and commanders alive and sent them to his son Sartak; and he remained there a whole year, laying waste and taking captives, giving over to sword and fire all who would not submit to him.

In the same year Batu’s grand prince named Berdebek was killed among the Hungarians.

In the year 6756 from Adam (1256 from Christ)

There was a sign: the moon became entirely blood-red and disappeared. In the same year Tsar Batu reached with his army even the Hungarian city of Varadin; and he destroyed many cities in that region and captured all; and no one could withstand him.

Batu also approached the city of Varadin, which stands in the middle of the Hungarian land; around it there were few ordinary trees, but all were garden trees, abounding in all kinds of fruits and berries. The city was surrounded on all sides by water and very well fortified, and therefore they feared no one; in the middle of the city stood a stone pillar, very high. At that time the ruler of that land was King Vlaslav, over the Hungarians, Czechs, Germans, and all Pomorie as far as the Great Sea. The Hungarians had formerly been in Orthodoxy, since they had received holy Baptism from the Greeks; they had not managed to create writing for their language; but the Romans were nearby and converted the Hungarians to their heresy; from that time, even to our days, the Hungarians remain in unity with the Romans.

King Vlaslav, mentioned earlier, was also in obedience to the Roman Church, until Saint Sava, Archbishop of Serbia, came to him and again persuaded Vlaslav to turn to the Greek Law, to the Christian faith, not openly but secretly, for fear that the Hungarians would rise against them.
