Their envoys returned and conveyed to them everything Mamai had said. And they, being foolish, rejoiced at this vain greeting of Mamai, not knowing that God gives power to whom He wills. They did not call to mind what the Lord had said: “What does it profit a man if he gains the whole world and loses his soul—that is, destroys it?” For this life passes away, and kingdom passes from generation to generation and from people to people, but a man who has done evil is tormented forever, having no help from the gain of the whole world. And they were swallowed up by their folly, seeking earthly and corruptible gain, like cattle.

Then news came to Moscow to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich that: “Prince Mamai of the Volga Horde is no longer called merely a prince, but a great and mighty tsar, and he stands on the Voronezh, moving about with a great host, and intends to march against you with an army.”

Hearing this, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was grieved and greatly saddened, and went to the cathedral church, and fell with tears before the image of the Most Pure Mother of God painted by Luke the Evangelist and before the tomb of the great wonderworker Peter, Metropolitan of all Rus, and received the blessing of his father Cyprian, Metropolitan of all Rus, and told him of Mamai’s advance.

For then, in that year, Metropolitan Cyprian first came from Kiev to Moscow, having previously come to Kiev; many years before, while Alexis was still alive, he had been appointed in Constantinople as metropolitan for Rus. And he sent to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich in Moscow, saying: “The patriarch appointed me metropolitan for Rus.”

But the grand prince refused him, saying: “We have Metropolitan Alexis, and we accept no other besides him.” Cyprian also sent to Novgorod and Pskov, and those answered him likewise. And he lived in Kiev even until the repose of the blessed Metropolitan Alexis.

After the repose of Alexis, Grand Prince Dmitry wished to see Mityai, archimandrite of the Savior, on the metropolitan throne in Moscow, and Mityai was installed in the metropolitan’s court.

Then afterwards he went from Moscow to the patriarch in Constantinople to be appointed metropolitan, and the grand prince himself honorably escorted him with all his boyars. But he, not quite reaching Constantinople, reposed.

Pimen, archimandrite of Pereyaslavl, from Goritsy, had then been sent among Mityai’s attendants, and seeing that Mityai had reposed, he began to think of ascending the metropolitan see of Rus, having ready all that had been sent with Mityai; and so he was appointed in Constantinople by the patriarch as metropolitan for Rus.

And news of this soon came to the grand prince in Moscow, but the grand prince did not want him, saying: “I sent Pimen as one serving Mityai, and not as a metropolitan.”
