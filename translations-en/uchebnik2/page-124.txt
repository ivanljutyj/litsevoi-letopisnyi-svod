In the same year, Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich died in Vladimir-in-Volhynia, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, forefather of the great Vladimir, and he was buried in Vladimir, where he died. His sons: Roman, Svyatoslav.

The Novgorodians heard that Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich had died, and they drove away his son Prince Roman, and received Prince Rurik Mstislavich in Novgorod on their own terms.

In the same year a son was born to Prince Mstislav Andreevich, and he was given the name Vasily in baptism. In the same year Vladyka Ilya, Archbishop of Novgorod, with his brother Gavriil founded a monastery and a church in it in the name of the Annunciation of the Most Pure Theotokos.

In the same year in Novgorod they bought a kad of rye for four grivnas, and bread for two nogatas, and a pud of honey for ten kuns. In the same year the posadnik Vaska Buslaevich died in Novgorod.

In the year 6680 from Adam (the year 1180 from Christ)

The pious and Christ-loving Grand Prince of Kiev Gleb died, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Andrei, called Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir; he reigned in Kiev for two years, and he was buried at St Savior on Berestovo, where his father Yuri was buried, on the 6th day of the month of September. His son: Vladimir.

That same autumn Grand Prince Andrei, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, sent the Smolensk prince Roman Rostislavich to the great reign in Kiev, and Prince Yaropolk, son of Roman, sat at the great reign in Smolensk.

That same winter Grand Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, sent his son Prince Mstislav against the Volga and Kama Bulgars, and the Ryazan prince sent his son, and the Murom prince sent his son, and their journey was very difficult, because it is hard to make war on the Volga and Kama Bulgars in winter; they acted unwisely and by this act brought great sorrow upon themselves, many wept as they went, and, going, they did not go.

Prince Mstislav was at Gorodets and joined with his brothers—the Ryazan and Murom princes—at the mouth of the Oka River, and they stood there two weeks, waiting for the voivode Boris Zhidislavich, since he had all the military equipment and rules.
