Izyaslav did not even wish to look at all that, but under compulsion from the king and his men he began to make peace with him; and the king said to Vladimir: “You must kiss the cross on this: whatever Russian cities are held by you, you must return them all, and you must be at one with Izyaslav and not depart from him either in good or in ill, but always be with him”; and he joyfully agreed to all of it.

Izyaslav, the king, and Vladimir, Izyaslav’s brother, and his son Mstislav all went into the king’s tent and began sending their men to Vladimir with the cross; he kissed the cross on all that, and so they made peace.

And the king went back to his own land, while Izyaslav went to Kiev to his uncle Vyacheslav with great glory and great honor, and they praised God and His Most Pure Mother, abiding in joy and great love.

Then Izyaslav sent to his brother Rostislav and told him how he had met with the king, and how they had defeated Vladimir of Galich, and how they had come safely into their own land; hearing this, Rostislav thanked God and rejoiced.

In the same year Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomash of Suzdal, hearing that his Gorodok had been dug up and burned and all its people led away, sent to the Ryazan princes, summoning them to his aid.

Izyaslav Mstislavich heard the same and sent to his brother Rostislav to say: “Guard, brother, your land from Yuri with the Novgorodians and the Smolensk people; and if Yuri comes against you, I will come to you; but if he passes by your land, then you come to me.”

And Yuri set out with his sons, and with the Rostovites, and with the Suzdalians; and the Ryazan princes Rostislav Yaroslavich with his brothers and with their regiments were with him, and also the Polovtsians of Orplui, and the Toksobichi, and all the Polovtsian land, as many as there are between the Volga and the Dnieper.

And they went against the Vyatichi and took them, and also against Mtsensk, and Spash, and Glukhov, and there they halted.

At that time Grand Prince Vladimirko of Galich heard that Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky of Suzdal was going into Rus, and he went toward Kiev against Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev; and he came out of Kiev against him.

But Grand Prince Vladimirko of Galich did not stand for battle against him and returned to Galich, wishing to join with Prince Yuri Dolgoruky and, having come together, go against Kiev.
