The Tatars withdrew from the Golden Gates and rode around the whole city, inspecting it; and the Tatars set up their camps before the Golden Gates and around the whole city; and there was a very great multitude of them.

The sons of Yuri, Vsevolod and Mstislav, grieved for their brother Vladimir; and they said to all the retinue and to Peter the Voivode: “Brothers, it is better to die before the Golden Gates for the Most Holy Mother of God and for the Orthodox faith than to accept the Tatar will.” And Voivode Peter said: “God has permitted all this to us because of our sins, according to the word of the prophet: ‘There is no wisdom in man, nor courage, nor counsel against the Lord.’”

And they began to sing supplicatory services, and they wept and shed many tears, turning to the Lord God and to His Most Pure Mother, the Mother of God. The Tatars, however, set up their camps around Vladimir.

And the Tatars went and took the city of Suzdal, and plundered the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God, and burned everything else with fire.

The Tatars plundered and burned the churches and monasteries; and the people—old and young, abbots, priests, deacons, monks, nuns, the blind, the lame, and the deaf—they cut down all of them.

The remaining people the Tatars led away with them, barefoot and uncovered; and people perished from the cold; and one could then see a very great horror; and the Tatars led a multitude of captives to their camps.

And the Tatars came to the city of Vladimir on Meatfare Saturday; and they began to set up timber works and to install siege engines from morning until evening; and by night they enclosed the whole city with a palisade.

In the morning the princes, and Bishop Mitrofan, and Voivode Peter Oslyadyukovich, and all the boyars and people saw that their city was about to be taken, and they wept with loud weeping.

And they all entered the cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God and took the holy angelic monastic habit; and Bishop Mitrofan tonsured the Grand Princess, her sons and daughters, her daughters-in-law, and all who wished; and there was shouting, and groaning, and loud weeping in the city.

On the next day, Meatfare Sunday, on the eighth day of the month of February, at the first hour of the day, the feast day of the holy martyr Theodore Stratelates, the Tatars approached the city from all sides and began to strike the city with siege engines. And huge stones poured down, flying from afar by the will of God like rain, and fell within the city; and there was a multitude of dead people in the city; and great fear and trembling fell upon all.

And the Tatars breached the wall at the Golden Gates; likewise from the Lybed River at the Irininy Gates; and at the Copper Gates; likewise from the Klyazma River at the Volga Gates; and finally the Tatars broke through the whole city and piled up stones within the city.
