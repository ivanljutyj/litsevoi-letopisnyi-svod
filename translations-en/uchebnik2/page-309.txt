In the same year all the princes mentioned earlier gathered in Novgorod: Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich; Prince Svyatoslav, son of Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, and his brother Mikhail; and Yuri, son of Andrei Alexandrovich; and Konstantin, Alexander’s son-in-law; Prince Dovmont of Pskov with the Pskovians; and Yaropolk with a multitude of warriors; and many other princes. And they set out with a great host for the German city of Rakovor, on the twenty-third day of February.

And when the Russians came into the German land, they divided and went along three roads, and they ravaged much. And they found a great cave, into which there was no way to enter, and there were many people of the Chud tribe there; and it was impossible to take them; and they stood by that cave for three days.

But the Master, by a cunning device, let water into those in the cave; and they ran out of the cave, and then they were all cut down, and all their wealth was taken.

And the Russians went on to Rakovor and came to the river Kiyula, and there German regiments met them. The army was as numerous as sand, for people from all the German land had gathered. And the princes began to draw up their regiments: the Novgorod host in front of the German host, opposite the great iron wedge; such a formation was called “the swine.” And Prince Dovmont with the Pskovians took the right side, while Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich and Prince Svyatoslav, son of Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, stood farther from the right side; and Prince Mikhail, son of Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, took the left side with the other princes; and the Russian princes had a great host beyond counting.

And the regiments came together for battle, and small skirmishes began; then they seemed to grow furious and enraged, and joined in a great battle; and the slaughter was fierce, and blood flowed like water; and the horses could not gallop, for the dead lay everywhere, heaped like stacks.

The Germans, formed in an iron wedge, killed many Novgorodians, and many boyars and servants of the Russian princes perished, and many Pskovian and Ladogan warriors were killed, on the eighteenth day of February, on Saturday of Cheesefare Week. And many Orthodox people wept, and the princes cried out in a loud voice, and prayed with tears to the Lord God and the Most Pure Theotokos, that they might help them, for they had already utterly lost their strength; and great fear and trembling fell upon all.

And behold, suddenly the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother helped the Russian princes, and the Germans fled, with no one pursuing them, only the wrath of God; and the Russian princes pursued them and slew them as far as Rakovor along three roads for seven versts. And then they returned and remained on the battlefield.
