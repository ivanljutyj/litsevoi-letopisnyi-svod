Grand Prince of Kiev Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Manomash sent to Smolensk to his brother Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Manomash. But he had not yet come; and then, having learned of the death of Izyaslav, the Grand Prince of Chernigov Izyaslav Davydovich, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, came and stood on the Dnieper below Kiev and sent into the city of Kiev to Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Manomash and to Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Manomash, saying thus: “I was not present at the burial of our kinsman, Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich; grant me leave to enter this city of Kiev, so that, standing at his tomb, I may lament him and by tears set at rest my inconsolable grief for him, since all of us must walk this path; brief indeed is this path, and whatever we live, it is nothing, and having been, to be not; for all things pass into non-being.”

But Grand Prince Vyacheslav and Prince Mstislav did not allow him to enter the city of Kiev, because the Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Manomash, had not yet come from Smolensk; and he returned to Chernigov.

Then Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich set out from Smolensk, and came to Novgorod, and after staying there a short while, went on to Kiev, while he left his son Mstislav in Novgorod. But the Novgorodians, having gathered, were indignant at Prince Rostislav Mstislavich and did not want his son Mstislav among them in Novgorod; and having led him out of the city, they showed him the path of his father, Grand Prince Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Manomash.

And having gathered, the Novgorodians began to take counsel, saying thus: “Since we have offended the Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich, then when he becomes Grand Prince of Kiev, much harm will come to us from him, because we drove out his son Mstislav”; and they entreated Bishop Nifont to go with great men to the Grand Prince of Suzdal Yuri Dolgoruky to ask for his son, Prince Mstislav, to come to Novgorod as prince.

Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky was then on a progress with his princess, and he stood on the river Yakhroma; and there his wife bore him a son, and he was named Dmitry, but his father called him Vsevolod; and in that place his father founded a city in the name of his son Dmitry, and named it the city of Dmitrov.
