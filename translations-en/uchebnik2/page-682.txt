He went to Tver and installed Anthony as bishop of Tver on the second day of February.

In the same spring, Grand Princess Evdokia, wife of Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver, second daughter of Prince Dmitry Eremeevich, died on the thirteenth day of April, a Monday, the day after Easter, and was buried in the cathedral church of the Transfiguration of the Lord in Tver.

In the same year, Prince Alexander Ivanovich of Tver went from Tver to Lithuania and met with King and Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich in Kiev; and Tokhtamysh’s son, Tsarevich Zeleni-Saltan, was there too, with Vytautas Keistutovich.

In the same year, Tokhtamysh’s son Saltan attacked the Horde’s uluses and plundered them.

In the same year, the Most Reverend Photius, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, went from Moscow to Vladimir and stayed there for several days, knowing nothing of anything—neither of battles nor of rebellion—since at that time all was very quiet and calm in the principality.

And Prince Daniil Borisovich of Nizhny Novgorod, hiding from everyone, brought Tsarevich Talych to himself.

And he sent with him on a raid his boyar Semyon Karamyshev, with two hundred and fifty Tatars and two hundred and fifty Russians.

And secretly they approached Vladimir from beyond the Klyazma River at midnight, when all were asleep; and the city was then undefended, nor was Governor Yuri Vasilyevich Shcheka there then. And the Most Reverend Metropolitan Photius, after vespers, set out from Vladimir for his metropolitan domain in Senezh in the month of July.

A second day passed, and he was staying at Holy Lake in the church of the Holy Transfiguration of the Lord, which Metropolitan Cyprian had founded and where he had often lived, since he loved forest wilderness; there were many metropolitan lakes and impassable places there.

And thus news reached Metropolitan Photius from Vladimir: “Tsarevich Talych has come by raid to Vladimir with a great host, and with him is Semyon Karamyshev, voivode of Prince Daniil Borisovich of Nizhny Novgorod, on the day after your departure, and they will soon come to you.” And Photius went into the forests to his lakes, to the hidden Senezh places. The Tatars did not find Metropolitan Photius and turned back, mercilessly killing people everywhere.

And they took captive the population of the city, seized and burned the suburbs, killed countless people, and took their wealth for themselves.

Then the keykeeper, the priest Patrikios, a Greek by birth, who had come together with Photius, shut himself in the cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God.
