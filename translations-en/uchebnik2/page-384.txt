That year Prince Vasily Semenovich departed this life, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Daniel, great-great-grandson of Alexander, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, ancestor of Vsevolod, great-great-ancestor of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-great-great-ancestor of Vladimir Monomakh, son of Vsevolod, son of Yaroslav, son of Vladimir.

In the year 6847 from Adam (1347 from Christ)

In Novgorod, in the Church of Saint Lazarus, there was a sign from the icon of the Most Pure Mother of God: tears flowed from both eyes. And two wax basins were placed beneath.

That year the entire Vladimir district burned down. That year there was a sign in the heavens.

That year Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich Kalita, grandson of Alexander, great-grandson of Yaroslav, went to the Horde with his two sons, Semen and Ivan. And his third son, Andrei, he sent to Novgorod. When Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich Kalita came to the Horde to Tsar Azbyak with his sons, he received great honor from the Tsar.

And then Tsar Azbyak soon sent other Tatars again after Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver, and after Prince Vasily Davydovich of Yaroslavl, grandson of Feodor, great-grandson of Rostislav, and after other princes.

Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver first sent his son Feodor to Tsar Azbyak in the Horde. Feodor Alexandrovich knew of the Tsar’s wrath against his father. And he told his father everything he had heard. He said: “I am alive now, but later I will die all the same; but if I do not go now to the Horde, I will provoke the Tsar’s great wrath, and much Christian blood will be shed.” And he began to prepare for the Horde.

Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich Kalita came from the Horde, from Tsar Azbyak, to Rus, to his patrimony, with great grant and honor.

That year Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver went to the Horde, while his son, Prince Feodor, was already in the Horde. But his mother, the boyars and merchants, all the inhabitants and the whole city wept for him and long tried to persuade him not to go to the Tsar in the Horde. “You know that the Tsar is angry with you and wishes to put you to death. But divine Scripture does not bid one hand oneself over to death.” He answered: “The Lord says in His Gospel: ‘Do not fear those who kill the body but can do nothing to the soul.’ And again He said: ‘Greater love than this no one has, than that someone lay down his life for his friends,’ and the apostle says: ‘Through many tribulations we must enter the Kingdom of Heaven, and all who desire to live godly...’”
