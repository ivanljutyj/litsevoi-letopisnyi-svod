On the Death of Stephen

Stephen’s son Dušan had long been consumed by the flame of desire to reign. And, unable to restrain his ambitions, taking with him many soldiers and noble lords, he went into the Arvanitian land and began to wage war, seizing that region from his father’s domains.

Hearing of this, the gentle Stephen sent to him many times asking him to cease his impious deeds and live in peace. For in time, by God’s blessing, Dušan would be heir to the kingdom. But, unwilling to heed his father’s words, his soul turned aside to evil. Stephen, leaving everything to God’s judgment, turned himself to virtuous deeds and endured his son’s plots like David himself. And, like Job, he said: “If we receive good from the Lord’s hands, shall we not endure evil?”

The commanders urged him to send an army against his son, saying, “to instill fear in his soul, and so that he would not wage war against you.” But he did not listen to them; he awaited the end foretold to him and cared for strangers and the poor.

Finding a favorable time, the son, the instigator, came with a great army, captured his father together with his wife and children, and ordered them led to the city of Vyachen. And after some time he delivered him to a bitter death, strangling him. O merciless and inhuman one! How could he not pity a father’s heart? How did he not give rest to his parent’s old age, how did he not remember the words: “Honor father and mother.” How dared the wicked servants of the ruler look upon his beautiful sacred face, and the murderers touch the saint’s neck with their hands? How did their foul hands not wither, and their eyes not go blind? But how can one be a martyr without passing through this? For one it is the lot of martyrdom, and for many an example to imitate. Thus the blessed soul was committed to the Lord.

And he was laid in his own monastery, where he wrought many miracles. After some years he appeared to the ecclesiarch and commanded that his body be taken from the earth.

And they found his body whole and giving forth fragrance, and a miracle occurred: the blind gatekeeper was healed.

And one of the commanders, who had been entrusted with guarding the saint’s monastery, began to insult the abbot and torment the brethren living in the monastery of the holy martyr and tsar Stephen, and plundered the monastery. And when he, in his madness, was once riding to the monastery, the saint appeared, threw him from his horse, and drove two nails into his throat as far as his chest; and that same day he died in torment.
