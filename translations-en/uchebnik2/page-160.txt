He himself did not want to accept the kingdom and hid from them, having dressed in black robes.

Then they took his wife, brought her to the great church of Holy Sophia, and for a long time urged her: “Tell us where your husband is,” but she said nothing to them about her husband.

Afterwards they took a man named Nicholas, a soldier, and placed on him the crown without [the blessing of] the patriarch, and they remained with him in the church of Holy Sophia for 6 days and 6 nights.

Tsar Isaacovich was in Blachernae and wanted secretly to bring the Latins and the boyars into the city, but the boyars entreated the tsar and did not let him admit the Latins into the city, saying: “We are with you.”

At that time, frightened by the arrival of the Latins, the boyars conspired with Mourtzouphlos, seized Tsar Isaacovich, and placed the crown on Mourtzouphlos; but Mourtzouphlos released Isaacovich from prison and promised not to depose Isaacovich from the kingdom, but to rule according to his will.

Mourtzouphlos sent to Nicholas and to the people in Holy Sophia, saying: “I have captured your enemy Isaacovich; I am your tsar, and to Nicholas I give the first place among the boyars.” Nicholas wanted to lay aside the crown, but all the people not only did not let him lay aside the crown, but moreover swore: “Whoever turns away from Nicholas will be cursed.”

On that same day, when night came, all fled, and Nicholas and his wife were taken captive, and Mourtzouphlos put them in prison, and he imprisoned Alexios Isaacovich.

And Mourtzouphlos himself became tsar, hoping to defeat the Latins; but the Latins, having learned that Isaacovich had been taken captive, began battle around Constantinople and asked Mourtzouphlos to release Isaacovich to them: “We will return to the German tsar and to the Pope of Rome, by whom we were sent, and his kingdom will remain to you.”

But Mourtzouphlos and all the boyars did not spare his life and put Isaacovich to death, saying to the Latins: “He is dead; come and see for yourselves.” Then the Latins were grieved by the crime that had been committed, since the German tsar and the Pope of Rome had not so instructed them that they should do harm to Tsargrad, and they said among themselves: “If Isaacovich, with whom we came here, is not with us, it is better for us to die at the walls of Tsargrad than to depart in fear and shame.”

And they began to prepare for battle with Tsargrad, and decided, as before, to use rams on the masts on the ships, and on other ships they prepared siege engines and ladders, and on still others they decided to suspend barrels filled with pitch and kindling over the railings, and, having set them alight, to throw them toward the city and the houses in order to set the city on fire.
