In the same year there was a severe pestilence in Pskov and throughout all the land of Pskov. Rapid death came in this way: a person begins to spit blood and dies on the third day. The dead were everywhere. And people began to think about their souls: rich people began to give to holy churches and monasteries villages, lakes and fishing rights in the lakes, and their shares in rivers, and their fishing grounds, and their estates, desiring to cleanse themselves of their sins and to obtain eternal memory according to what is written: “By alms and faith sins are cleansed.” And so, having cleansed their souls by confession, repentance, and tears, they departed, ending this fleeting life.

At that time the priests could not keep up with burying the dead, but in one night they brought twenty or thirty dead to the church and sang the funeral service over them all at once. Only the prayer of absolution, which is read from the manuscript, was read to each one separately, man or woman. They laid five or ten people in one grave. And so it was in all the churches. And there was already nowhere to bury the dead; ever new graves were dug, and even far from the church there were no places left. Everywhere there was weeping and wailing, because people saw how many were dying and themselves awaited death. They distributed their estates to the poor and beggars, and no one took anything from anyone: as soon as anyone took anything from someone, at that very hour he died incurably.

Many wanted to help the dying, but soon fell ill themselves and died, and therefore many avoided helping the dying. But those who had the fear of God and the remembrance of death, remembering that no one can escape death, helped the sick and dying in faith, carried them and buried them, thereby doing all for their spiritual salvation. Neither rank nor eminence helped anyone escape death; into all it thrust its many-toothed fangs. People said that this pestilence had come from the Indian land, from the City of the Sun.

At that time the Pskovians sent their envoys to Novgorod, tearfully summoning Archbishop Vasily, lord of Novgorod, to them, so that he might bless them. Lord Vasily heeded their entreaties, went to them in Pskov, and blessed them. And then he went to Novgorod, and reposed on the way on the Uza River, on the third day of the month of June. They brought him to Novgorod and buried him at St Sophia in the large side-chapel.

He was bishop for 21 years, 4 months, and two days.

The Novgorodians sent to Moscow to the most reverend Metropolitan Theognostus with a request that he bless their old bishop Moses for the rule in Novgorod. He blessed their old archbishop for them.
