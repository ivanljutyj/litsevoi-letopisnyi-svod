Monks and nuns, and all the boyars, and simple people did not wish to see Mityai as metropolitan; only the grand prince wished it.

And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich was grieved over the death of Mityai and did not wish to receive Pimen, saying: “I did not send Pimen to be consecrated metropolitan, but I sent him as one of Mityai’s servants. What happened to Mityai I do not know; God alone knows and will judge the wrong, for I have heard much about what happened, and I do not accept Pimen and do not wish to see him.” Metropolitan Pimen with his companions was at that time waiting in Constantinople, and then the grand prince sent to Kiev his spiritual father Theodore, abbot of Simonov, to Metropolitan Cyprian, inviting him to Moscow. And the envoys came to Kiev to Metropolitan Cyprian before Great Lent.

On the Coming of Metropolitan Cyprian from Kiev to Moscow.

And Cyprian came from Kiev to Moscow on Thursday of the sixth week after Easter, on the very day of the feast of the Ascension of Christ, in the year 6888. And they began to ring all the bells, and people gathered; a great multitude assembled, and from everywhere came archimandrites, abbots, priests, and monks, for many had gathered for the feast of the Ascension. The whole city stirred with wives and children, with infants and even with babes at the breast; all went out to meet the metropolitan far beyond the city limits. The grand prince himself met the metropolitan far from the city together with his children and all the boyars, with great honor and love, faith and humility.

And they returned back to the city, gathered in the church, and Metropolitan Cyprian venerated the icons, and a service of supplication was held, and all delighted in spiritual love.

And there was great celebration that day for the grand prince and the metropolitan; all rejoiced and were glad with joy. Much alms were distributed to travelers and the poor, and they glorified Christ God, to whom be glory forever and ever, amen.

And then, after seven months had passed, when the year 6889 came (1389 A.D.), a report came to the grand prince that Metropolitan Pimen had set out from Constantinople for the Russian metropolitanate, crossing the Horde. The grand prince did not wish to receive him and became angry with his senior boyar Yuri Vasilyevich Kochevin Oleshinsky.

When Pimen was in Kolomna, the grand prince sent to him and ordered the white klobuk to be taken from him, and he himself to be put in prison, and his retinue and advisers, the clerics, to be dispersed one by one and shackled in iron fetters. They took from him all his vestments and treasury, and assigned as his overseer a boyar named Ivan Grigoryev, son of Churilov, nicknamed Dranitsa. And they led Pimen into confinement from Kolomna to Okhna, bypassing Moscow.
