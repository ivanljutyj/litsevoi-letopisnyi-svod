Then the cleric sent by Patriarch Athanasius presented to all the patriarch’s letter and Andrew’s letter against Peter. All those present were indignant at the false and crafty slander against Saint Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus. Such a great outcry arose that it is unknown how it all would have ended if God’s saint himself had not stopped their cries, imitating his teacher Christ, who once said to Peter: “Put your sword into its sheath.” The meek disciple follows the meek teacher in all things. And he said, turning to all: “My brothers and beloved children in Christ, I am no better than the prophet Jonah. If this great disturbance is because of me, depose me, and the disputes will subside. Why do you go to such lengths for my sake?” But they stood up for their teacher and good shepherd. And all the disputants wanted to know who had brought these false words against our father and teacher. An evil deed does not remain hidden, and soon it became known to all that Andrew had slandered Saint Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, and before all he was put to shame and humbled. But Saint Peter did not hold a grudge against him; rather, instructing him before all with words of comfort, he said to him: “Peace be with you in Christ, child, Bishop Andrew; it was not you who did this, but the enemy of the human race, the envious devil. From now on be on your guard, and may the Lord God forgive you what has passed.” Then, after sufficiently instructing the princes, all the sacred clergy, and the people, he sent them all away in peace.

He himself, adding labors to labors, desired to increase a hundredfold the talent given him, joining humility to humility, and without laziness he walked through cities and villages, instructing the flock entrusted to him by God, feeling neither weariness nor bodily illness. And so he labored with long-suffering until deep old age. To the poor, the destitute, wanderers, orphans, and widows he was constantly like a father.

At the same time the heretic Seit appeared again, bringing discord into the Church of Christ by his speculations about the Orthodox faith. Then Saint Peter again disputed with him, but since he did not submit, he anathematized him, and he soon perished. Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, passing through all God’s places and cities, engaged in God-pleasing work, preaching and instructing all.

He came to the glorious city called Moscow, which at that time was still small and sparsely populated compared with how we see it now. That city was ruled by the pious and great prince Ivan, son of Daniel, grandson of blessed Alexander.
