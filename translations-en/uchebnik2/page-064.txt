The townspeople, however, came out of the city every day and fought. Then Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky said to his father, Prince Yuri Dolgoruky: “You see, father, your army is not of one mind and does not fight strongly; how is it that these men, coming out of the city against such a multitude of our army, prevail and bring us great reproach and shame?” And having gathered with all the princes and taken counsel, they said: “Truly so; they do not fight strongly because we ourselves do not ride against them.” Then Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky said: “Let us do this: day by day we shall come to the city and fight; and behold, I myself will make the beginning first, and this day is mine.”

And mounting his horse, he went to the city; and they came out of the city against him, and he rushed upon them with his army and with the Polovtsians, and many were killed, and others were taken alive, and others barely fled into the city.

Then the other princes also envied him and began to come to the city to fight; but the townspeople did not dare to come out against them because of fear, for they had already suffered from Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky.

They stood, however, beneath the city of Chernigov for twenty-two days. Then the great princes of Kiev, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh and Izyaslav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, began hastily to gather an army and set out to aid Chernigov against the Polovtsians, and against the great Prince Yuri Dolgoruky, and against Rostislav Yaroslavich, and against Svyatoslav Olegovich, and against Temir, prince of the Polovtsians, and against all the princes and their army.

When the great Prince Yuri Dolgoruky heard of this, he was terrified. The Polovtsians also, hearing it, were frightened and returned to the Field, to their dwellings; and Prince Yuri Dolgoruky with his children, and with all the princes, and with all his army, quickly withdrew from the city of Chernigov and went to Novgorod Seversky.

But the great Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, came to Chernigov when all the warriors had already fled, and found not one, and after staying a short time in Chernigov, returned to Kiev.

And the great Prince of Suzdal, Yuri Dolgoruky, left his son Vasilko in Novgorod Seversky with the great Prince of Novgorod Seversky, Svyatoslav Olegovich, and himself went to Suzdal, to his great principality.

In that same year the great Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, set out with his brother Svyatopolk and with his son Mstislav to Novgorod Seversky against the great Prince of Seversk, Svyatoslav Olegovich, and against the young prince Vasilko Yuryevich Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh.

And having come, he halted at Vsevolozh, and from there, having detached his son Prince Mstislav, he sent him against the Polovtsians, giving him a great force of his: the Pechenegs.
