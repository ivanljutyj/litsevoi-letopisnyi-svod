Yuri learned of Svyatoslav’s arrival and met him at Bogolyubovo on the Surama River with his son Vsevolod.

And they greeted each other with great love and went into the city; and there was great joy in Vladimir over Svyatoslav’s arrival.

And Grand Prince Yuri held a great three-day feast for his brother Svyatoslav and all the warriors, and gave many gifts to his brother: gold and silver, various garments, horses, weapons, and various precious cloths; he also richly rewarded the warriors, each according to his rank.

Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich sent his brother Svyatoslav with great honor to his city of Yuryev, to reign there.

In the same year Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich himself began preparing to go to war against the Bulgars; but the Bulgars sent their envoys with entreaty and petition; he did not heed their entreaties and sent them away.

And Prince Yuri began gathering troops, and sent messengers to his nephew Prince Vasilko Konstantinovich of Rostov, commanding him to go to Gorodets.

And Grand Prince Yuri himself went with his troops to Omut; and while he was at Omut, other Bulgar envoys came to him with entreaty.

But Prince Yuri did not listen to the envoys and sent them away without peace; and Prince Yuri came to Gorodets to Vasilko Konstantinovich.

The Bulgars were frightened and sent other envoys with many gifts and with a petition; and Prince Yuri heeded their entreaty and accepted their gifts; and they settled everything according to the former peace, as it had been under his father Vsevolod and his grandfather Georgy Vladimirovich.

And Prince Yuri sent his men with the Bulgar envoys to administer the oath to the Bulgar princes and their lands according to their law; and Grand Prince Yuri himself returned to Vladimir.

In the same winter the Lithuanians came and ravaged the Chernigov region, and led many away captive.

Mstislav Svyatoslavovich pursued them from Chernigov, and, overtaking them, killed them all, and, recovering the captives, returned to Chernigov.

In the same winter Mstislav Romanovich from Kiev, and Mstislav Mstislavovich from Torchesk, and other princes with them, and with the Cumans, went against Galich; when they came, the king’s son shut himself up in the city of Galich; and they fought by the city for half a day; and they scattered to ravage the land, and did much evil, burned towns and villages, and, taking many captive, returned to their own lands.

In the same year the widow of Prince Konstantin, Princess Agafya, departed this life as a nun; and she was laid in the cathedral church of the Most Holy Mother of God in Rostov, on the twenty-fourth day of January.
