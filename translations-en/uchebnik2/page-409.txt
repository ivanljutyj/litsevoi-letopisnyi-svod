And then, thirty years later, I, the sinful King Magnus, having forgotten all this, went to war against them with the whole Swedish realm, wishing to take them captive, because they did not want to accept our faith. And I took many captive and baptized them into my faith.

I took their fortress Orekhov, installed my governors in that city, and left some troops there, while I myself returned beyond the sea.

And then the Novgorod people came, took back their city Orekhov, and killed the governors themselves and the Germans who were in the city.

And I, without settling that matter, in that same year again went to war against Orekhov. And it became known to me that a Novgorod force was standing before Orekhov. I went to the town of Koporye and spent the night there. There news came to me that the Novgorodians were already at the borders of the land. And I, hearing of this, fled beyond the sea.

And a mighty storm arose; such billows and waves began to rise that they could not even set the sails because of the waves. Great fear and trembling came upon me when I saw the sea waves tossing the ships with my host, and they were sinking. And many of my host were drowned at the mouth of the Nerova River. And I returned to my land with a small remnant of my host.

And from that time the wrath of God fell upon our Swedish land: famine, plague, and internecine wars. And for my sins God took away my reason from me, a sinner. And I sat in my palace for a year, chained to the wall with an iron chain, in confinement. Then my son Sakun came to me from the Murmansk land, and, taking me from the chamber and looking at me, he saw me blackened and withered. And my eyes had sunk as into a pit. My arms and legs had become thin, my skin and sinews had clung to my bones, and I could scarcely speak. And my voice was like the buzzing of emaciated bees. And all who saw me wept piteously and lamented. And my son, out of grief, beat his head against the ground, lamenting, grieving, and weeping for me.

And my son took me to his Murmansk land. And when I went aboard the ship, a mighty storm arose again, so that our ships with their sails could no longer be seen in the waves. And so all my people were drowned.

But I myself, having bound myself to a plank of the ship’s bottom, floated for three days and three nights. And the Lord carried me by the wind into the Polnaya River, to the Monastery of the Holy Savior. And the monks who were there lifted me from the plank and carried me into the Monastery of the Holy Savior.

And taking the black vestments, they tonsured me into the monastic order and into the schema. And for three days and three nights the Lord preserved my life. Nevertheless, all this was sent upon me by the Lord God for my arrogance, my presumptuous hopes
