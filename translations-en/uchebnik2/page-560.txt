Your glory has faded, and your face will soon decay. My life, I shall no longer rejoice with you! Instead of precious purple robes they have clothed you in simple, poor garments; instead of a royal crown they have covered your head with a simple cloth; instead of a beautiful palace they will lay you in a wooden coffin. My bright light, why have you grown dim? If God hears your prayer, pray for me, your princess. I lived together with you, and I wish to die with you. Youth has not yet left us, and old age has not overtaken us. To whom are you leaving me and your children? I rejoiced with you but a short time: after gladness came weeping and tears, and after comfort and joy—lamentation and weeping. Why did I not die before you, so that I might not see your death and my own ruin! Do you hear, my lord, my sorrowful words? Why do you not take pity on my bitter tears! The beasts go into their dens, the birds of the air fly to their nests. But you, my lord, leave your house so suddenly. What am I now? I am left without my tsar. Old widows, comfort me; young widows, weep with me: a widow’s lot is the bitterest. It remains for me to weep and cry out: “Great God, King of Kings, be my protector! Most Pure Lady Theotokos, do not forsake me and do not forget me in my sorrow!” And they brought the right-believing Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, Tsar of all Rus, into the church of the holy great Archangel Michael, where stand the tombs of his father, grandfather, and great-grandfather.

And after singing the funeral service over him according to custom, they laid him in the coffin on the 20th day of May, on the commemoration of the holy martyr Thalelaeus. And princes and boyars, archbishops, and all the people wept over him. And there was no one who did not weep, and no singing could be heard because of the loud weeping.

At the burial was his guest—Theognostus the Greek, Metropolitan of Trebizond—and Daniel, lord of Smolensk, and Savva, Bishop of Sarsk, and the venerable Abbot Sergius, Abbot Sebastian, and many others, and the whole sacred council. And they buried him with funeral hymns on the 20th day of May. But Pimen, Metropolitan of all Rus, was at that time in Constantinople. O terrible wonder, brothers, worthy of amazement! O trembling vision, filling us with horror! Hear, O heaven, and tell it to the earth! How can one describe and how can one tell of the passing of this great prince? From grief of soul the tongue is bound, the lips are closed, the throat grows dry, the mind fails, the sight grows dim, strength leaves. But how can one keep silent? My tongue compels me to speak more clearly than before. When the great prince of the Russian land, Dmitry Ivanovich, fell asleep in eternal sleep, the air grew dark, the earth shook, and the people fell into confusion. That day of grief, sorrow, tears, and sighs can rightly be called a day of great grief and sorrow. Among the people they said: “O woe to us, brothers! The king of kings has died, our lord and ruler! The sun has been darkened, the moon has been covered with clouds, the star that shines upon the whole world is sinking toward the west!” This is what compelled me to write the life.
