That night they had great alarm, and stood all night without sleep under arms, and shouts and murmuring rose throughout all their many camps.

Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich with his brothers, hearing of this, were afraid.

And a boyar came to them, the very one who had spoken of peace, and said to them: “Having seen nothing, why do you wish to flee? Did I not say in my foolishness that it would be better for you to make peace? And now you have not yet seen one dead or bloodied man, and you are already so frightened that you are ready to take to flight. When you see yet greater things done to you, how will you not suffer then?” And they, ashamed, sent him away dishonorably.

In the morning the princes came to Lipitsa, where they were to meet in battle. But those men had crossed through the thickets that night, entered the fortification, set up abatis, and driven in stakes.

And the sons of Vsevolod with their host stood on Mount Avdova. Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov, Prince Mstislav Mstislavich of Novgorod, Prince Vladimir of Smolensk, and Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich Romanovich with their regiments stood opposite them on another hill, which is called Yuryeva Gora. And between the hills was a stream called Tugen.

And they sent to Yuri Vsevolodovich, saying: “Either agree to peace, or withdraw to level ground so that the matter may be better fought out, or we will withdraw to Lipitsa and you come to us,” because Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich with his brothers and all his forces stood in a fortification lined with logs, woven with wattles, and surrounded with stakes.

Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich with his brothers sent to them, saying: “We do not want peace, and we will not leave here; if you are so bold and have traveled so far, yet today cannot even cross the thickets to us, then go through the swamp and through these thickets, for it is customary for pigs to walk through thickets and for crucian carp to wallow in the mud.”

They sent young men into battle. They fought all day and did not prevail, for they stood in a great fortification and fought without zeal. And that day there was great rain and great cold.

And at that time Prince Vladimir of Pskov came to them from Rostov, and they began to consider with him what to do.
