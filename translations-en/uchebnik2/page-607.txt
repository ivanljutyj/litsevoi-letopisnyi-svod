About Temir

In the chronicle it is written that there is a certain country named Arar; it is also called Mesopotamia, that is, it is surrounded by two rivers. It lay to the northeast of India. And it was from that country that this Temir came, the son of a certain elder of the Trans-Yaik Tatars, who were beyond the Iron Gates. His trade was blacksmithing. By temperament and custom he was ruthless and villainous: a man of violence and a thief. Previously he had been a slave of a certain master. Because of his evil nature he was driven out by his master.

When he was young, having nothing to eat or wear, he lived by theft and robbery.

Once he stole a sheep from a certain man; they caught him and beat him severely, breaking his leg at the thigh. Thinking he was dead, they threw him to be eaten by dogs.

After some time he recovered with difficulty and bound his broken leg with iron. That is why he received the nickname Temir-Aksak. In the Tatar language Temir means iron, and Aksak means lame man. Thus it comes out “the Iron Lame”: from the thing and from his deeds he received a name according to his deserts.

Having healed of his wounds, he not only did not change his evil character, but, raging still more, became a ferocious robber. And there gathered to him cruel and merciless young men, robbers and shedders of blood.

And he went to the place where his leg had been broken, so that he became lame, and killed the shepherd from whom he had stolen the sheep. And from this he gained property.

When about a hundred of them had gathered, they called him their ataman. And when he had gathered a thousand men, they already called him prince.

With this thousand men he went raiding against Karamadin, who had ten thousand men under him and was ruler of a certain region; Temir defeated him and seized his country.

With those ten thousand he went raiding into Persia, captured the ruler of the Persians himself, and came to possess all the Persians. And his host became one hundred and fifty thousand.

After this he subdued all the surrounding countries, acquiring vast riches; he devastated the whole East and nearly subdued all Hellas.

He also threatened to go west, wishing to take captives and burn, and to put the people living there to the sword without sparing them. And then his warriors already called him tsar.
