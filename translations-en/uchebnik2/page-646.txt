The Turkish Bayezid was killed in that battle, and his son fled with a small retinue. And he sent to the Emperor of Constantinople an offer of love and peace, since his father Tsar Bayezid had earlier captured Greek cities, but he renounced them and made peace with the Greeks across the sea.

And Tsar Temir-Aksak wished to go with an army against Constantinople; he became proud, since the number of his troops had multiplied, and he set out against Tsar Digman and captured his land.

And Tsar Digman was brought to him. Then Tsar Temir-Aksak fell gravely ill; his son began to rule in his place, and the Lord by His mercy delivered Constantinople and all Christians from the ungodly.

In the same year Vladyka John, Archbishop of Novgorod, came from Novgorod to Moscow to Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, since he had been summoned for a council concerning ecclesiastical matters, and he stayed with Metropolitan Cyprian in instruction and humility, so that not only he might learn to think and act humbly, but others too might receive benefit — not from pride, since benefit and salvation come not from submissiveness but from compunction and humility.

In the same year there was a council in Moscow in the month of July under the most reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus. At the council there were present nine hierarchs: Ivan, Archbishop of Great Novgorod; Isaac of Chernigov; Gregory, Archbishop of Rostov; Nathanael, Bishop of Suzdal; Euphrosynus, Bishop of Ryazan; Arsenius, Bishop of Tver; Theognostus, Bishop of Sarsk and Podonsk; Gregory, Bishop of Kolomna; Savva, Bishop of Lutsk. At that sacred council Archbishop John of Novgorod and Bishop Savva of Lutsk renounced their bishoprics. And the most reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, ordered them not to leave Moscow, since the most reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, had instituted a trial for them concerning certain ecclesiastical matters, so that not only they might find benefit and salvation for themselves, but this might also serve for the benefit and salvation of others. Nothing destroys the snares of the devil so much as obedience and humility.

In the same year Prince Ivan, son of Vladimir, grandson of Andrei, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Daniel of Moscow, married Vasilisa, daughter of Prince Feodor Olegovich of Ryazan.

In the same year Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan, together with his son-in-law Yuri Sviatoslavich of Smolensk and with his brothers, the princes of Pronsk, Murom, and Kozelsk, went with an army to Smolensk, choosing a suitable time, since at that time Vytautas Kestutovich had lost many soldiers in battle with Temir-Kutluk in the Tatar land on the Vorskla River; because of this there was great sorrow and loss of people in Lithuania, and unrest began in Smolensk: some wanted Vytautas to rule, while others wanted their hereditary lord, Grand Prince Yuri Sviatoslavich.
