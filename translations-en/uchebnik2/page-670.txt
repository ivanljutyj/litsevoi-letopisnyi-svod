In that same autumn, in the month of November, a Tatar came from the Horde to Moscow and told Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich that “the Grand Prince of the Horde, Edigei, wants to conquer your land.”

In that same autumn, in the month of November, the Germans began to conquer the Pskov lands.

In that same winter, in the month of December, on the 20th day, the Horde prince Edigei, by command of Bulat-Saltan, Tsar of the Great Horde, came with an army against the Russian land, and with him were four tsareviches and many Tatar princes, whose names were: Tsarevich Buchak, Tsarevich Tegriberdiy, Tsarevich Altamir, Tsarevich Bulat, Grand Prince Edigei, Prince Makhmet, Prince Yusup, son of Sulumen, Prince Tegina, son of Shikh, Prince Sarai, son of Urusakh, Prince Obragim, son of Temiryaz, Prince Yakshibi, son of Edigei, Prince Sentyalibiy, Prince Burnak, Prince Erekliberdey. And when Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich learned of this, he was saddened and troubled by his affection and closeness toward him. And it is never strange to think thus about the Tatars, since from the beginning the Ishmaelites conclude a false peace with the Russian princes, first of all with Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, cunningly negotiating with him.
