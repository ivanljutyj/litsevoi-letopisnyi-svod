In the same year Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, together with the people of the Tver and Novotorzhsk volosts, dug a moat around Tver and raised a rampart from the Volga to the Tmaka River.

In the same year died Prince Mikhail Vasilievich of Kashin, grandson of Mikhail, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, forefather of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-great-forefather of Vladimir Monomakh, and great-great-great-forefather of Vsevolod, Yaroslav, and Vladimir.

And his son Prince Vasily, with his princess Elena and with the Kashin boyars, came to Tver to Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver with a petition and submitted to his authority. Then, by God's mercy, in the same year peace was concluded between Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver and Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow. And his son Prince Ivan Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich lovingly released from Moscow to Tver, and Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver recalled his governors from the grand principality. And silence and calm came for all Christians; all rejoiced, and their enemies were put to shame.

In the year 6882 from Adam (1382 from Christ)

His Grace Alexius, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed Dionysius, Archimandrite of the Caves, as bishop in Suzdal.

In the same year the people of Nizhny Novgorod killed Mamai's envoys, and with them one and a half thousand Tatars; their chief named Sarai they took captive and brought with his retinue to Novgorod.

In the same year the ushkuyniki robbers went to Vyatka, gathered ninety ushkuy boats (river vessels), and plundered Vyatka.

On the way they captured the Bulgars called Kazanians; they wanted to set the city on fire, but took a ransom of three hundred rubles from it.

And there they divided into two parts: 50 ushkuys went down the Volga to Sarai, and 40 ushkuys went up the Volga and, reaching Obukhov, plundered all Zasurye and Markvash.

And having crossed beyond the Volga, they burned all the vessels, and themselves went on horseback to Vyatka, plundering many villages and settlements along the Vetluga on the way.

In the same year Prince Vladimir Andreevich founded the oak city of Serpukhov in his principality, granted the people and all merchants concessions and privileges, and appointed as governor of the city his okolnichy Yakov Yuryevich, called Novosiltsev.
