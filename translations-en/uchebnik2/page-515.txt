And then a small detachment rode up to the city and asked about the grand prince, saying: "Is Prince Dmitry in the city?" They answered them from the city walls, saying: "He is not in the city."

The Tatars rode around the whole city, examining it, and withdrew; and around the city all was clear, because the townspeople themselves had burned their suburbs and left not a single stake or tree, fearing an unnoticed approach to the city.

Prince Ostey of Lithuania, grandson of Olgerd, with the multitude of people remaining in the city, who had gathered from all sides into the city, strengthened their defenses and prepared for battle against the Tatars.

At that time the good people in the city prayed to God day and night and stood in fasting and prayer, awaiting bitter death, preparing themselves with repentance, communion, and tears.

Some wicked people began to go about the courtyards and carry out from the cellars their masters' meads and silver vessels and costly glass ones, and drank themselves into great drunkenness, and added insolence to their swaggering, saying: "Let us not fear the coming of the impious Tatars, having so strong a city, whose walls are stone and gates iron. They will not endure standing beneath the city for long, having a double fear: within the city, from the fighters; and outside the city, from our princes and the army being gathered." And again they climbed onto the city walls, being drunk, and swaggered, and reviled the Tatars, shamelessly vexing them, and uttered certain words full of reproach and blasphemy, spitting on and reproaching them; and exposing their shameful parts, they showed them to them in both directions; and they barked at their tsar and reviled him, and gathering phlegm and spit, threw it at them, saying: "Take this and carry it to your tsar and your princes." The Tatars waved their bared sabers straight at them toward the city, as if cutting them down, showing them from afar.

And that day toward evening the Tatar regiments withdrew from the city. The townspeople began to drink and make merry, thinking that the Tatars had been frightened.

In the morning the tsar himself with all his force came against the city; and they advanced on the city from all sides, but did not shoot, only looked at the city.

The townspeople shot an arrow at them; they began to wave their sabers at them, as if wishing to cut them down.

The townspeople shot even more arrows at them and threw stones, and shot from crossbows and small cannons. The Tatars were enraged and began shooting at the city from all sides. And their arrows flew at the city like a strong rain, multiplying greatly, so that one could not see, because the air was darkened by arrows; and many townspeople in the city and on the battlements fell.
