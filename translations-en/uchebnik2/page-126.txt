Rus (1183–1212 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 3

In the year 6682 from Adam (1182 from Christ)

The right-believing and Christ-loving Prince Svyatoslav, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, reposed in the month of January on the 11th day. This prince was a right-believing, Christ-loving, and humane man; from his youth until his coming of age he suffered many illnesses, and through his illness he was deemed worthy of blessedness. He was prudent, learned in the Divine Scriptures, of good character, humble, and had love and mercy toward many. His body was laid in the Church of the Most Holy Theotokos in Suzdal.

In the same year Prince Yuri Vladimirovich of Murom reposed, in the month of January on the 19th day; his body was laid in the church that he himself had built in Murom.

In the same year the Novgorodians rose up against their prince Rurik Rostislavich; he fled from Novgorod.

The Novgorodians sent to Grand Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky, asking him to reign in Novgorod; he gave them his son Prince Yuri, and they received him with honors on the Novgorodians’ terms.

In the same year Prince David Rostislavich of Vyshgorod, not wishing to remain in unity and love with Grand Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky and with his brothers, conspired with his brother Prince Mstislav Rostislavich, and coming by night to Kiev, he captured Prince Andrei’s brother Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich, and Yaropolk Rostislavich.

Hearing of this, Grand Prince Andrei Yuryevich Bogolyubsky sent his son Prince Yuri together with the Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Novgorodians, entrusting the command to Boris Zhidislavich; twenty other princes with their regiments went with them.
