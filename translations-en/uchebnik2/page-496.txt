And they halted between the two hosts. And they turned toward the Tatar host and heard a great shout and din, as if markets were gathering, and as if cities were being built, and as if trumpets were sounding. And behind them wolves were howling very terribly; on the right side there was a great fluttering among the birds: they cried and beat their wings, ravens cawed, and eagles screamed along the Nepryadva River. And there was great fear, and among the birds there was battle and great strife, foretelling bloodshed and death for many. And Volynets said to the Grand Prince: “What did you hear?” He said: “I heard great fear and terror.” Dmitry Bobrok Volynets said to him: “Turn, prince, toward the Russian host.” He turned, and there was great stillness. Dmitry Volynets said to him: “What, lord prince, did you hear?” The Grand Prince answered: “Nothing; I only saw that a glow is gathering from the multitude of fires.” Dmitry Bobrok Volynets said: “Lord prince, give thanks to God and the Most Pure Mother of God, and to the great wonderworker Peter, and to all the saints: for these fires are a good sign. Call upon the Lord God and pray to Him often, and do not grow weak in faith toward Him, nor toward the Most Pure Mother of God, nor toward your pastor and intercessor, the great wonderworker Peter: for these signs are good.

And I have another sign besides.” And he dismounted from his horse, and fell on his right ear, pressed close to the ground, and lay there a long time, and rose, and suddenly grew downcast. The Grand Prince said to him: “What is wrong with you, brother Dmitry? Tell me.” But Dmitry did not wish to tell him and resisted as best he could. The Grand Prince again urged him, begging him to tell. And he shed tears. The Grand Prince, seeing his tears, began to be afraid and said to him: “Brother Dmitry, tell me, for my heart is very sick.” Dmitry began to comfort him and said: “Lord prince, I will tell you alone. But do not tell anyone. For there are two tidings. One is for great joy, and the other for great sorrow. I pressed my ear to the ground and heard the earth weeping in two voices, very bitterly and terribly. One side was like a certain woman, sobbing convulsively, wailing boldly and crying in the Tatar tongue about her children, beating herself and pouring out tears like rivers. And the other side of the earth was like a certain maiden, weeping and groaning as if with a mournful, flute-like voice, in sorrow and great grief. For I have experienced many of those battles and signs in many fights, and they are familiar and clear to me; and trust in God’s mercy, for you are to overcome the Tatars. But a great multitude of your army will fall by the edge of the sword.” Then the Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, hearing such an account, wept greatly and shed tears for a long time, and said: “May the Lord’s will be done. As it pleases the Lord, so shall it be.
