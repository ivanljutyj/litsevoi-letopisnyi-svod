All who were in the church fled from it, and there was great thunder and lightning, and a radiant pillar stood over the church from earth to heaven, and the earth shook, and the people were terrified and wept, saying: “Lord, have mercy!” And the people grieved for a long time, saying: “What will happen?” Then they went into the church and saw that all the candles over him had been lit, and there was a great shining light and much fragrance; and all marveled.

Then, after praying, they carried him with honor to the end of the city, to the monastery of Saint Simeon, and there laid him in a new stone tomb. And when they had sung over him the customary hymn sung for the departed, that is, Eternal Memory, there was no one who did not shed tears over him, who had suffered innocently in the monastic habit and in the schema, their lord, the former Grand Prince of Kiev, the monk Igor Olegovich, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir; he was killed on Friday, and laid in the tomb on Saturday.

The news of his murder came to the city of Chernigov, and his brothers wept with great weeping, and all the people; and Prince Vladimir Mstislavich sent to his brother, the Grand Prince of Kiev Izyaslav Mstislavich, telling of the violent death of the prince-monk Igor Olegovich, and Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich was grieved and was indignant with the people of Kiev, saying: “They have made eternal, unquenchable enmity with his brothers and with his descendants.”

And he sent to Kiev for his brother, Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, and sent for another brother of his, Prince Rostislav Mstislavich, to Smolensk.

In the same year, in Ryazan, in the city of Kazar, there were miracles and many healings from the icon of Saint Pyatnitsa. In the same year the posadnik Konstantin died in Novgorod. In the same year, in Novgorod-Seversky, by the help of God and the All-Pure Mother of God, they defeated the Polovtsians.

In the year 6656 from Adam (the year 1156 from Christ)

The Most Reverend Nifont, Bishop of Novgorod, went from Novgorod to the Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh in Suzdal, reconciling the Novgorodians with him; and he received him with love and honor.

And Bishop Nifont, in his city of Suzdal, consecrated the stone church of the Nativity of the All-Pure Mother of God with the great consecration.

And he honored the bishop exceedingly, and gave him the Novotorzhians, and released all the Novgorod merchants who had been taken captive by him, unharmed and with their goods. And Bishop Nifont departed to Novgorod, greatly gifted and honored; but he did not grant peace to the Novgorodians, but intended to gather an army against them.
