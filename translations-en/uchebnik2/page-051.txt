Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich learned that Prince Vladimirko of Galich and Prince Andrey Yuryevich Bogolyubsky were pursuing him, and night came, and they hurriedly went to Kiev; the guards of Prince Vladimirko and Prince Andrey found traces of his camp on the Ros, but he had already gone far away, beyond great barriers—beyond marshes and beyond rivers.

Grand Prince Vladimirko of Galich and Prince Andrey Yuryevich Bogolyubsky were astonished, for they knew nothing of it.

Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich went to Belgorod. Prince Boris Yuryevich heard how many troops were with him, and fled from Belgorod to his father, Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh, in Kiev.

He was terrified and began to weep: “Alas for me! Where can I flee from Izyaslav Mstislavich? Where can I run? I cannot escape him; truly he is an enemy to me and to my children.” And he heard that he was near, and was coming against him with a great and countless host; he also understood concerning his Kievans that they loved the other more, and that they also loved Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh greatly for his simplicity and wished to see him as grand prince in Kiev; and, rising, he fled from Kiev and went to Gorodets.

Vladimirko of Galich and Prince Andrey Yuryevich Bogolyubsky knew nothing of this, and halted, pondering the traces of the camp, and sent guards, and learned that Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh had been driven out of Kiev by Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich and had settled in Gorodets, and that Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich had again entered upon the great reign in Kiev. Grand Prince Vladimirko of Galich was terrified and returned to his domain in Galich, and sent Prince Andrey Yuryevich Bogolyubsky back to his father, Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh, in Gorodets.

And he came to his father, and, embracing, they wept long and piteously, saying: “Alas for us! Where can we flee from our enemy Izyaslav Mstislavich?” Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, having sat upon his throne in the great reign in Kiev, rejoiced greatly, and, gathering all the Kievans to himself and embracing each one, kissed them, and spoke to them thus: “You are my joy and protection and help in Christ: you are fathers and mothers to me, and I am a servant to you, and must lay down my head for you”; and having said this, he gladdened and comforted all, and moved them to joyful weeping and tears.
