In the same year, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich frequently warred with the Novgorodians because the Novgorodians did not want to accept the metropolitan court of Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus'. They did not want the metropolitan to judge them in Novgorod, as had been the old Novgorod custom. Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent to them in Novgorod [to say] that they should not violate that ancient custom, and the metropolitan's court should judge them, and their archbishop should not judge them, and the tribute to the metropolitan should be as in old times, and the grand prince should have the black tribute, as in olden times, from all Novgorod and from all Novgorod volosts. They, not wishing this, did not obey him, and because of this there was war between them. The grand prince said that they should send to the metropolitan a letter saying that they had agreed among themselves and kissed the cross that the metropolitan should not judge you, but your archbishop should judge you; and you should send that letter to the metropolitan, and the metropolitan will remit that sin from you. But they did not obey.

And Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich gathered a large army and took from Novgorod its dependent towns: Torzhok with its volosts, and Volok Lamsky, and Vologda, and conquered many other volosts, and returned home with great booty.

Later the Novgorodians, together with Grand Prince Roman of Lithuania and Prince Konstantin Vasilyevich of Beloozero, gathered a large army and with great force went against Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich and took the towns of Klichen and Ustyuzhny.

And from Zavolochye many Novgorodians gathered and sailed in nasads and ushkuis, took the town of Ustyug and burned it with fire, plundered the cathedral church and seized the treasury and much gold, and stripped the settings from the icons.

And they stood there a month, plundering, catching people in the forests, torturing them, and seized all their property that they had not managed to hide, and devastated all the volosts and villages. The people, cattle, and all their goods they sent down the Dvina.

At the same time, having seized the town, villages, and volosts in Beloozero, they did the same as in Ustyug.

But afterwards the Novgorodians made peace with Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, submitted to him, and sent a letter to Metropolitan Cyprian in Moscow.

And Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent to them in Novgorod Fyodor Koshka, son of Andrey Kobyła, Ivan Udod, and Selivan, and they secured the peace according to the old ways and undertook to give the grand prince the black tribute from all Novgorod volosts. And to the metropolitan's envoy Dmitry the Novgorodians gave three hundred and fifty rubles because he brought them the blessing of their lord, Metropolitan of All Rus' Cyprian, and a letter of absolution.
