And having seized much, Mengukak went with a multitude of captives to Tsar Batu.

In the same year Prince Rostislav Mstislavovich of Smolensk, grandson of David of Smolensk, having learned of Grand Prince Mikhail Vsevolodovich, came and began to reign in Kiev.

And Daniil Romanovich, grandson of Mstislav Izyaslavovich, came to Prince Rostislav and seized him; and he ordered Dmitr, his tysyatsky, to guard the city of Kiev and to muster against the Tatars.

In the same year Tsar Batu came to the city of Kiev with a multitude of troops and surrounded the city; and the Tatar army besieged the city; and it was impossible for anyone to leave the city or enter the city; and those speaking in the city could not hear one another because of the creaking of carts, and the roaring of camels, and the sounds of trumpets and drums, and the neighing of horse herds, and the shouts and cries of the countless multitude of people; and the whole land was filled with Tatars.

Then the people of Kiev captured a Tatar named Tavrul, and he told of all the grand princes who were with Batu, and of his countless army. And these were Batu’s brothers, his great and mighty commanders: Urdyui, Baidar, Biryui, Kaidar, Bechar, Mengai, Kailug, Kuyuk.

This same Kuyuk returned back; and he saw the death of the khan; the khan was not of Batu’s family, but was his chief and great commander; and Tsar Batu mourned the khan, because he loved him greatly. Other commanders and grand princes were: Butar, Aidar, Kilemet, Burandai, Batyr, who captured the Bulgarian land and the Suzdal land; and there were a great countless multitude of other commanders. Batu placed many siege engines against the city of Kiev, near the Lyad Gates, because the moats converged there.

From the multitude of siege engines the Tatars struck without ceasing day and night; and the townspeople resisted the Tatars stubbornly; and there were many dead; and blood flowed like water.

And Batu sent envoys to Kiev to the townspeople with these words: “If you submit to me, I will pardon you; if you resist, you will suffer many torments and perish terribly.” But the townspeople did not listen to him at all, but reviled and cursed him.

Batu became very angry and ordered an advance on the city with great force; and then, with the help of the multitude of siege engines, the Tatars breached the city walls and entered the city, and the townspeople came out to meet them
