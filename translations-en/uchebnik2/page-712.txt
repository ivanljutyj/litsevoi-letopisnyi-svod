That same autumn, on the 30th day of August, the Novgorodians drove their elected bishop Feodosy from the archbishop’s court—elected by them themselves by lot and installed at the archbishop’s court; but the Novgorodians did not wish to have this bishop, saying, “We do not want a shestnik as lord among us in Novgorod!” And they led him away to the Monastery of the Holy Trinity at Klopsk; Feodosy remained bishop for two years.

And by lot they brought into the archbishop’s court the monk Emelyan from the Church of the Holy Resurrection in Derevyanitsy.

In the year 6932 from Adam (the year 1432 from Christ)

Tsar Kuidat again came with an army against Odoev, against Prince Yury Romanovich of Odoev.

Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich of Lithuania heard of this and sent to Moscow to his son-in-law Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, asking him to send help against Tsar Kuidat.

And he himself sent with an army Prince Andrey Mikhailovich, Prince Andrey Vsevolodovich, the Drutsk princes Ivan, nicknamed Baba, and his brother Putyata, and Prince Dmitry Vsevolodovich, and Grigory Protasyevich, voivode of Mtsensk.

They, joining with Prince Yury Romanovich of Odoev, drove off Tsar Kuidat, defeated his army, and captured two of his tsaritsas.

And one of these tsaritsas they sent to Lithuania to Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich of Lithuania, and the other to Moscow to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich. Tsar Kuidat himself fled. But the Moscow army did not manage to come to the rescue in time. Then they killed Kogchu, a Tatar bogatyr, mighty in body and strength. In that same year in Pskov the Pskovians convened a veche, minted silver coins, and began to use them in trade, while leaving mordki unused.

In that same year in the German land, in Lithuania, in Pskov and Novgorod, in Tver and Moscow, and throughout the Russian land a severe pestilence began: a person coughed blood for some time and then died. Everywhere there was sorrow and grief.
