What have the Russian princes done to me? How have they brought shame and disgrace upon me? How have they made me a reproach, a mockery, and a laughingstock before all? How can I escape this reproach and dishonor?” And he lamented much over this, grieved, wept, and did not know what to do.

And his counselors said to him, comforting him: “You see, Great Prince—and moreover, Great Tsar—your Horde has grown poor, and your strength has weakened. But you have wealth and countless possessions; hire Fryags, Circassians, Alans, and others besides them, and you will gather a great host, and you will avenge the blood of your princes, your alpauts, your friends, and your favorites, just as you did to Oleg, Prince of Ryazan: you burned all his cities and districts, laid waste all his land, and led all his people away captive; so you shall do also to Prince Dmitry of Moscow.”

When the impious and proud Prince Mamai heard this from his counselors, he rejoiced with great joy, hoping to gain much profit; and he became proud and exalted himself in his mind with great pride, and wished to be a second Tsar Batu and to enslave all the Russian land. And he began to try to learn from old histories how Tsar Batu had enslaved the Russian land and ruled over all the princes as he wished. And having questioned and learned truly from all his men, he began to pride himself and exalted himself in his folly above all with his pride.

And he imagined himself to be like the ancient Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon and Titus, King of Rome, who enslaved Jerusalem, and Tsar Batu, who enslaved all the Russian land and ruled over all countries and all hordes; so Mamai also thought in his mind—or rather, in his folly. And he began to show favor to all his men and to give many gifts, so that they would agree with him and be ready to wage war against Rus, namely against the Great Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow. And Tatars from many lands gathered to him, hoping in his favor and gifts, and he gave abundantly to all.

And he sent to many lands, hiring Fryags, Circassians, Alans, and others besides them, and gathered a great host.

And he went against the Great Prince Dmitry Ivanovich like a roaring lion, like a puffing bear, and like a proud demon. And they crossed the Volga River with all their forces.

And he came to the mouth of the Voronezh River, and there he halted with his forces, encamping. And his host was very great, and therefore he was called not Great Prince Mamai, but by all his close ones he was called Great Tsar Mamai; and his pride was great, and his expectations beyond measure.
