His disciple, and the Most Reverend Alexis, Metropolitan of all Rus, also asked him about this, for his installation as abbot of the monastery he had founded at Simonov—for from ancient times this place has been called Simonov, by the Moscow River near the city. And Theodore was installed by Metropolitan Alexis as abbot in his monastery at Simonov. He lived a virtuous life, was wise and prudent, and his monastery was held in honor and glory, and surpassed many in greatness. And he was confessor to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, the tysyatsky, the boyars and nobles, and shone with virtue and glory more than others.

It was customary to go to Constantinople concerning matters of church governance. At that time Pimen was metropolitan in Moscow, and Cyprian was metropolitan in Kiev.

After this, in the year 6891 from Adam (1391 from Christ), Abbot Theodore, nephew and disciple of Sergius, was with Patriarch Nilus in Constantinople concerning the governance of the Russian metropolitanate. And the patriarch loved him very much and appointed him archimandrite, showing him great honor, more than to other archimandrites. And the great and honorable Theodore became the first Simonov archimandrite. And his monastery shone with glory, honor, and great virtues. And so many elders shone forth in that monastery that many of them, by God’s providence, occupied episcopal thrones in other distant cities, and were appointed archimandrites and abbots in honorable monasteries, and lived virtuously, in honor and glory, shepherding their flocks in the name of the Lord.

Theodore, archimandrite of Simonov, nephew of Sergius, was in Constantinople, and Patriarch Nilus conversed with him kindly, and loved him very much, and showed him honor, and gave him many honorable gifts, and made the Simonov monastery patriarchal. And he gave his charters to Theodore, the honorable archimandrite, for the establishment of the Simonov monastery in the patriarchal name, and gave houses, monastic villages, rivers and lakes, and other things necessary for monastic life. And they were not to be subject to the metropolitan, and the metropolitan did not have authority over the Simonov monastery. And so, having honored Theodore, archimandrite of Simonov, nephew and disciple of Sergius, with great honor, and having established the monastery with great love and instructed him spiritually, he sent him with honor to Rus.

And the archimandrite returned with great glory and honor to his monastery at Simonov. Thus Theodore’s Simonov monastery was founded in the patriarchal name, and the city grew and multiplied. But Theodore lived, flourishing in virtues, and was greatly honored by the grand prince, the hierarch, and also by all the inhabitants.

After some time the grand prince and the hierarch earnestly began to entreat Theodore to agree to become bishop in the city of Rostov.
