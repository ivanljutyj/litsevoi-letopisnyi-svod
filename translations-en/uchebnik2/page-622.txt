Timofeyev was sent after Vladyka Ivan, Archbishop of Novgorod, summoning him to Moscow on hierarchal affairs. And the metropolitan’s stolnik was honored, and they gave him 60 rubles.

The archbishop set out for Moscow and stayed in Moscow two days, and Metropolitan Cyprian released him with honor and blessing.

And he came to Novgorod on Tuesday of Bright Week with joy and peace.

In the same year the Germans came in arms to the Karelian land and took five pogosts: Kiryessky, Kolyvansky, and others. And Prince Konstantin Vasilyevich Belozersky set out in pursuit of them, but did not overtake them; only one captive was taken and sent to Novgorod.

In the same year in Novgorod there was a sign from the Lord’s image in St Eupatius on Shcherkov: the icon exuded wine. In the same year Vladyka Ivan of Novgorod ordered the cupola at the Church of Holy Sophia to be covered with lead, for it had been burned in a fire.

In the same year Grand Prince Vitovt of Lithuania executed Prince Ivan Mikhailovich by death, and sent his wife and children to various places and plundered his house.

In the same year Prince Semyon Dmitrievich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod gathered his host, and with him went Tsarevich Ektyak with the Tatars; they came to Nizhny Novgorod and stood long by the city, but did not take it. And in the city then were three voivodes of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow: Vladimir Danilovich and Ivan Likhar. They stood long and could not take it; they made peace among themselves and kissed the cross, and the Tatars swore by their faith. True, afterward the Tatars broke their oath, plundered Nizhny Novgorod, wrought much evil, and returned to their own land, to Kazan.

When Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich heard of this, he sent his brother Prince Yuri Dmitrievich with a great host against Kazan. They went and took the city of Bulgary, Zhukotin, and Kazan.

That same spring, two weeks before Easter, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich went from Moscow to Smolensk to meet his father-in-law Vitovt. Having visited him, he returned to Moscow. Metropolitan Cyprian was with him at Vitovt’s, and at Easter he appointed Nason bishop in Smolensk; from there he went to Kiev and remained there a year and a half.
