In the year 6713 from Adam (the year 1213 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, took his son Svyatoslav from Novgorod, because the Novgorodians wanted to expel him from Novgorod; Prince Svyatoslav was still young in years.

And after some time Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich made peace with them, and when the Novgorodians asked him for his son Prince Konstantin, he sent him to them.

Konstantin Vsevolodovich, having received the blessing and instruction of his mother Maria, went to Novgorod; his brothers Yuri and Vladimir and all his father’s boyars escorted him with honors as far as the Kideksha River, and he came to Novgorod on the twentieth day of March.

In the same year Grand Princess Maria, the wife of Prince Vsevolod, reposed in monastic vows, and she was laid in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God in the monastery that she herself had founded, imitating the great Abraham: she bought land for herself for the building of the church and monastery, in which she was buried. She was ill and lay in infirmity for seven years, but she never spoke a bad word; always with gratitude she endured sorrow and great need, recalling the righteous Job: “If we receive good from the hand of the Lord, how shall we not endure evil?” And so she suffered for seven years from a severe illness, was tonsured into the monastic order, remained in the monastery for eighteen days, and reposed in peace on the nineteenth day of March.

Before her death she instructed her sons, saying to them: “My beloved children! I am already departing from the temporary light to my fathers; but you, while you remain in this brief life, have faith and love for God and for His Most Pure Mother, the Mother of God, and with all your strength acquire within yourselves the fear of God; and before bishops, presbyters, and all the priestly order do not be ashamed or too proud to bow your head, because they are the servants and pastors of the rational sheep of God Himself and of the Most Pure Mother of God: whoever honors God’s priest honors God Himself, and whoever wrongs God’s priest wrongs God Himself. Honor all monks and supply them with necessities, and never pass by a monk without bowing.
