And when they had thus confirmed themselves by kissing the cross and by hope in the Most Holy Mother of God, Prince Gleb of Ryazan sent his envoys to them on his own behalf, and the Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Pereyaslavlians on their own behalf.

The envoys came to Chernigov to the Rostislavichs, and the Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Pereyaslavlians said to them: “When your father reigned among us, he was a very good prince; come and reign among us, for all our land calls you, and it does not want another.” The envoys conveyed to them the retinue’s message. Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich was then with them, at Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich of Chernigov. And Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich answered them: “May God help the retinue, because they have not forgotten the love of our father”; and they said to Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich: “Do not grieve and do not say of us that we sent to them desiring to reign among them; you see, they themselves call us; but if you wish, go and reign among them; if not, then let us all four go: you two Yuryevichs, and we two Rostislavichs.” And having agreed among themselves, they gave the seniority to Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich, and, having kissed the holy and life-giving cross from the hand of the Bishop of Chernigov, they went in peace and came to Moscow.

The Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Pereyaslavlians heard of the coming of the Yuryevichs, began to be indignant, and said to Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich: “You come to us”; but to Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich they said: “And you wait a little longer in Moscow.”

Yaropolk, secretly from Mikhalko, rode to the retinue at Pereyaslavl; Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich saw this and went to Vladimir. And the retinue, seeing Prince Yaropolk, swore to him by kissing the cross, and Prince Mstislav and Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich went together with the Suzdalians and Pereyaslavlians to Rostov; the Vladimirians also set out with them, because the Rostovites had called them to themselves; there were about fifteen hundred of them in all; and having sworn by kissing the cross, they all went to Vladimir against Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich; and he shut himself up in the city. The Rostovites, Suzdalians, and Pereyaslavlians did much damage around the city and called the Ryazan and Murom princes to their aid, and they fought, burned, and cut down, saying: “We will burn everything and take the city, and we will install our own posadnik in it; for they are our serfs, stonecutters and carpenters.”

The Vladimirians fought them from the city; the Most Pure Mother of God helped them; and they stood about the city, fighting, for seven weeks, and provisions were already running short among them, and the Vladimirians said to Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich: “Lord prince, either make peace with them or think about yourself; for we are already exhausted from hunger.” The prince answered them: “You are right; may you not perish because of me,” and he went away from them to Chernigov; the Vladimirians escorted him with weeping.
