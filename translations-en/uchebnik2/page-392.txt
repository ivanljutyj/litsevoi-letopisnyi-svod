They began to speak with tears: “We never thought or imagined that our brother Luka would be killed; we did not send him out to be killed.”

Ontsifor and Matfey rang the bells, summoning everyone to a veche at St Sophia. And Fedor and Andrei began to gather a veche at Yaroslav’s Court. And Ontsifor and Matfey sent the vladyka to the veche.

And without waiting for their vladyka, they convened a veche at Yaroslav’s Court, and there they seized Matfey Kozka and his son Ignaty, while Ontsifor fled with his accomplices. This happened before noon; after noon the whole city, with Vladyka Vasily and Governor Boris, made peace among themselves.

That year the Pskovians sent to Novgorod, saying: “A German host is coming against us; help us.” The Novgorodians, having gathered forces, wished to send them aid. But they again sent word to them, saying: “There is no host against us.” And there was peace. And the Novgorodians returned and dispersed to their homes.

Then the Pskovians again renounced Novgorod and the great Russian prince. They sent their envoys to Vitebsk, to the great Lithuanian prince Olgerd Gediminovich, asking help against the Germans, saying: “Our brothers the Novgorodians do not help us.” He accepted their petition and first sent to them his commander, Prince Yuri Vitovtovich; then he himself went, with his brother Kestutis and with the Lithuanians, and with his son Andrei.

And he sent Prince Yuri Vitovtovich with Pskovian volunteers to Novy Gorodok to capture a tongue. He went to the border and there met a great German host going to Izborsk. And there, by the river Mekozhitsa, the Germans killed sixty Pskovians. And Prince Yuri Vitovtovich fled to the city of Izborsk.

The Germans, coming to the city, surrounded Izborsk with great force and with battering engines. But Olgerd and Kestutis knew nothing of this. And Olgerd ordered his host to cross the river, not knowing that a German host stood before Izborsk. They crossed the river and pitched camp at Kolomna.

And Olgerd sent his men ahead of the regiment on reconnaissance. They went, captured a tongue, and brought him to Olgerd. And he told of the great German force and the large host before Izborsk. Olgerd ordered them quickly to cross to the other side of the river, go to Pskov, fortify the city, and gather people from the towns into fortresses.

And Olgerd himself, with Kestutis and a small retinue, went to the Gradskoe Marsh and tried to learn everything about the German host. And Prince Lyubka, son of Voin, a close friend of the Prince of Polotsk, rode away from Olgerd, not knowing that enemies were there, and they killed Olgerd’s closest friend. And Olgerd grieved and sorrowed greatly for him.
