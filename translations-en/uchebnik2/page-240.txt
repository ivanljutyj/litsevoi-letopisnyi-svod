And then he said to them: “People and brothers, do not be angry with me because I do not wish to reign among you in Novgorod. I am going to Chernigov. Let merchants of all kinds come to me; let your merchants go freely to me, and mine to you, since your land is also my land.”

The Novgorodians wept at this, because they loved Prince Michael, and they long urged him, asking him to remain; but they could not persuade him.

And then they escorted Prince Michael to Chernigov with great honor and bestowed many gifts on him.

In the same year Archbishop Antony came from Peremyshl to Novgorod the Great and took his archiepiscopal seat in Novgorod.

On Yaroslav’s Victory over the Lithuanians

That same winter the Lithuanians went to war around Novgorod, around Toropets, around Smolensk, and as far as Polotsk; their army was large, seven thousand; and they killed merchants.

Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich learned of this and had great pity for the Christians.

And Prince Yaroslav set out from Pereslavl after the Lithuanians; warriors from Torzhok, from Toropets, and a few from Novgorod also came to him, and they caught up with the Lithuanians near the town of Usvyaty.

The Lithuanians, seeing the Russians, arrayed themselves against them on the lake; and Prince Yaroslav advanced against them;

and when they began to fight, the accursed fled; Prince Yaroslav pursued them and took all the spoil, and slew them; and he took two thousand captives; and he seized their prince; and there was great joy in those lands, for he had freed them from the pagans. There the Lithuanians killed David, Prince of Toropets, and also Vasily, who was Yaroslav’s sword-bearer.

After that pursuit Prince Yaroslav went to Novgorod and began to reign, since before this the Novgorodians had come for him and invited him to reign among them; he defeated the Lithuanians on Cheese-Fare Sunday.

In the year 6734 from Adam (1234 from Christ)

Blessed Simon, Bishop of Suzdal and Vladimir, reposed; before death he received the monastic rank of schema; and he was laid in Vladimir in the church of the Most Pure Mother of God of the Golden Tops.

In the same year Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich went with his nephews, Vasilko and Vsevolod, sons of Constantine, to help his brother-in-law Prince Michael, son of Vsevolod Chermny, of Chernigov, in a campaign against Prince Oleg of Kursk.
