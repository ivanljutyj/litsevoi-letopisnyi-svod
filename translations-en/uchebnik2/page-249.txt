These three came with the metropolitan, asking peace for Michael from Yaroslav: for Michael had not kept the promises to Yaroslav that he had made when kissing the cross; Yaroslav wanted to go to war against him. The metropolitan and the bishop spoke much to Yaroslav about peace, citing words from Divine Scripture and teaching him according to the Gospel word not to resist evil. His brother Yuri also began to speak to him, and his other brothers, that he should not leave the metropolitan's labor in vain, but be reconciled with Michael.

Yaroslav listened to his elder brother, and to his spiritual father the metropolitan, and to his brothers, and to Bishop Porfiry, and made peace with Michael.

Both princes, Yuri and Yaroslav, showed great honor to the metropolitan, and also to the bishop and to the abbot of the Monastery of the Holy Savior, and, having received their blessing, sent each of them back to their own princes in their own land.

In the same year there was a severe pestilence in Smolensk; they made four large graves, and laid 16,000 in two, and 7,000 in the third, and 9,000 in the fourth; and this continued for two years.

In the same year Archbishop Spiridon came to Novgorod from Kiev, appointed by Cyril, metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus.

In the same year in Novgorod Stefan Tverdislavovich and Ivan Timofeevich quarreled with Posadnik Vodovik, and Vodovik's servants beat Ivan Timofeevich.

And on the next day they held an assembly against Posadnik Vodovik; and they plundered his house. Posadnik Vodovik was greatly angered, and gathered all Novgorod against Ivan Timofeevich, and against Yakim Lukianovich, and against Prokopy Yakovlevich; and they condemned them, and plundered their houses; and they killed Ivan Timofeevich and threw him into the Volkhov; and they killed many others.

And God became angry and laid waste the land; and rain fell from the Day of the Annunciation to St. Elijah's Day, day and night; and cold set in, and there were severe frosts; and all the grain froze.

And they bought bread at eight kunas, and a quarter of rye for 20 Novgorod grivnas; and in some desolate rural places they bought a quarter of rye for 30 grivnas, and wheat for 40 Novgorod grivnas, and millet for 50 Novgorod grivnas, and a quarter of oats for 12 grivnas; and there was great mortality among the people from famine, so that they could not bury them.
