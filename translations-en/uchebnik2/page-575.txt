The monk Stefan, his elder brother, obeyed his younger brother Varfalomey, who even while in the world had lived a monastic life; they went into the wilderness forest and found a secluded place, and took a blessing from the Most Reverend Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, to build a church. They also took an antimension and a priest, and consecrated the church in the name of the Most Holy and Life-Giving Trinity. This happened under Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich, still at the beginning of his reign.

Then Stefan, his elder brother, left this solitary place and his brother Varfalomey, and went to the monastery of the Holy Epiphany, not far from Moscow; he found a cell there and began to live in that monastery of the Holy Epiphany in the settlement in the city of Moscow, greatly prospering in virtuous life, because he too loved to live in labors and asceticism. He lived in his cell, constantly praying, in severe fasting and great abstinence: he did not drink beer and wore plain clothes. At the same time there lived in the monastery the monk Alexei, the future metropolitan, not yet appointed metropolitan, but conscientiously walking the path of a monk, living virtuously in that monastery. Stefan lived with him in spiritual closeness, and in the church they stood side by side in the choir, singing psalms to the Lord with humility and long-suffering. There also lived a certain well-known, honored, and glorious elder, Gerontius. And in that monastery of the Holy Epiphany he lived with Stefan and Alexei in great spiritual love for the Lord, with humility and long-suffering. Metropolitan Theognost loved them and often summoned them to himself; communion with them brought him consolation and joy, since he greatly honored them. Then the monk Stefan, Varfalomey’s elder brother, was appointed by Theognost, Metropolitan of All Rus, as abbot in that monastery of the Holy Epiphany. Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich also loved him very much for his virtuous life. He often conversed with him alone and consulted him, and there was great spiritual love between them for the Lord. And Stefan, abbot of the Epiphany, elder brother of the hermit Varfalomey, became confessor to Grand Prince Semyon Ivanovich, grandson of Daniil and great-grandson of Alexander, and also to Vasily the tysyatsky, and to Feodor his brother, and to other senior boyars. And he was dear to all and honored by all.

But Stefan’s brother Varfalomey, the hermit—then still living as a layman—was engaged in building the church and monastery, living in the wilderness and accustoming himself to monastic life. And he summoned to himself into this solitary place a certain priest, an abbot named Mitrofan, and humbly asked him to tonsure him into the monastic order in this same wilderness.
