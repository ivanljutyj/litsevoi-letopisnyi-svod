In that same autumn the tsarevich Arapcha came in a raid upon Ryazan, caused much harm, and returned back.

In that same winter there were severe frosts and cold; many people and livestock froze. And wherever there was water, it froze through from the severe frosts—in the marshes, lakes, and rivers.

In that same winter the Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod sent his brother Prince Boris Konstantinovich and his son Prince Semyon with an army against the Mordvins.

And the Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow sent with them his own army and his commander Feodor Andreevich Sviblo.

And they began to ravage the Mordvin land; they plundered the districts, villages, churchyard communities, and winter settlements, killed people, captured their women and children, and made all their land desolate.

And those whom they brought back alive to Novgorod, they executed, setting dogs upon them on the ice of the Volga.

The Tale of Alexius, Metropolitan of All Rus'

In that same winter, on the twelfth day of the month of February, on the commemoration of our father among the saints Meletius, Patriarch of Great Antioch, on Friday at Matins, the most reverend Alexius, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', departed this life, having lived to old age. This blessed Alexius, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', was born into the family of prominent Chernigov boyars. At that time, by God's permission, enemy raids often occurred against Chernigov and all its surroundings. Because of this his father Feodor and mother Maria left their homeland of Chernigov for the glorious city of Moscow, where they lived when the blessed one was born.

At that time the great principality of Vladimir belonged to the Grand Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich of Tver, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, under the most reverend Maximus, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', before the murder of the Tver boyar Akinf.

Concerning Akinf. Akinf set out with an army to Pereyaslavl against Prince Ivan, son of Daniel, grandson of Alexander, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod; he himself drank the cup of death; Akinf and his son-in-law David were killed near Pereyaslavl.

Previously this boyar Akinf had been subject to Prince Andrei Alexandrovich of Gorodets, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod; the boyar served his lord Prince Andrei Alexandrovich of Gorodets until the prince's very death, and after his death he went to Tver to enter the service of Prince Mikhail Yaroslavich.
