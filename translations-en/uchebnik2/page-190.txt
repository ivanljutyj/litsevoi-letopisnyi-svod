Izyaslav and the Pronskians heard that the Ryazan regiment had been defeated, and they and their livestock were already dying of lack of water. They came out of the city with their prince Izyaslav Vladimirovich and submitted to Grand Prince Vsevolod.

He brought them to the kissing of the cross on the eighteenth day of the month of October, and installed among them in Pronsk Prince Oleg, son of Vladimir, grandson of Gleb. He took Princess Kir Mikhailova and led her away with him, and took their property and all their countless wealth. He made peace with Prince Izyaslav Vladimirovich, and having installed his own governors in all their cities, he went to the city of Ryazan. And when he was at Dobry Sot and intended in the morning to cross the Pronya River in order to go to the city of Ryazan, the Ryazanians sent to him with a plea that he not go to the city of Ryazan.

Their bishop Arseny often entreated him about this, saying: “My lord prince, Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich! You are a Christian; do not shed Christian blood, do not lay waste these places of honorable Orthodox Christians, do not burn churches and honorable monasteries, in which the name of God is glorified and prayer for the whole world is offered to the Lord God, and do not stir up the wrath of God against yourself.” Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich was frightened by his words, and, having heeded the plea of Bishop Arseny and the townspeople, he departed from them to Kolomna and thence to Moscow. And when he was at the mouth of the Mersk River, there Bishop Arseny of Ryazan overtook him with a plea and with obeisance from all the people.

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich went from there to Vladimir, and he ordered Bishop Arseny to go with him, and they came to the city of Vladimir on the twenty-first day of the month of November, a Wednesday, on the feast of the Entry of the Most Pure Mother of God into the Temple.

And the Ryazanians sent their remaining princes to Vladimir.

And Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich released the Novgorodians and Pskovians to Novgorod, having bestowed on them many countless gifts. He gave them their liberty according to ancient custom and the statutes of the old princes; everything they wanted he granted them, saying to them: “If you need anything, ask of me, for I must grant you every good thing, since you have labored much, having overcome my enemies the Ryazanians. And I render you great honor; keep for yourselves a prince according to your will; and whoever is good with you, love and honor him; and whom you wish to favor, favor; and whom you wish to punish, punish. Do as was established by the former princes among you.” And having comforted them in many other ways and given them gifts, he released them.

The Novgorodians, rejoicing, went to Novgorod. Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich kept with him his eldest son, Prince Konstantin, who had reigned in Novgorod, and the Novgorod posadnik Dmitry, wounded at Pronsk, and another posadnik, Andrey, likewise wounded at Pronsk.
