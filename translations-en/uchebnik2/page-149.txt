All burned down because of our sins. Thus God permitted that He spare not even His sacred houses, but gave over to destruction and desolation His holy churches, divine things, and consecrated places because of human sins, so that [people] might learn and turn; as the prophet Isaiah said: when you turn and sigh, then you will be saved; turn to Me, and I will turn to you.

In the same year the Russian princes went against the Polovtsians: the Grand Prince of Kiev Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, grandson of Oleg; Prince Ryurik Rostislavich; Prince Vladimir Glebovich, grandson of Yuri; Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich; Prince Gleb Yuryevich of Turov, grandson of Svyatopolk, great-grandson of Izyaslav; Prince Mstislav Romanovich; Prince David Vsevolodovich; and help from Galich, Vladimir, and Lutsk; and all together united, they went against the Polovtsians.

And after crossing the river Bosg (Bug), they sought them for 5 days. Vladimir Glebovich, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, rode ahead with the guard regiments; with him were 2,200 warriors of the Pereyaslavl men and Berendeys.

The Polovtsians, hearing of the arrival of the Russians, rejoiced, hoping in their own strength, and said to themselves: “The Russians have come into our hands of their own accord; we shall acquire wealth for ourselves without labor,” and they came out to battle against Prince Vladimir with shouts, wishing to destroy the enemies.

But Vladimir placed all his hope in the Most Merciful God and His Most Pure Mother, the quick helper of Christians, and went out against them; the other princes did not manage to go out together with Vladimir.

The Polovtsians saw Vladimir’s regiment coming against them and fled, driven by the wrath of God. All the princes, seeing this, pursued the Polovtsians, striking them down, and with bare hands captured 7,000; of the Polovtsian princes alone there were then 417 in that battle, and they captured 16, killed many, and others fled. Such was God’s help to Prince Vladimir Glebovich on the 31st day of the month of July.

And the Russian princes returned with great joy [and plunder] back, thanking the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos.

In the same year the Polovtsians came against Rus, caused much evil, burned volosts and villages, captured many prisoners, and went back to their own lands.

In the year 6694 from Adam (the 1194th year from Christ)

The Greek emperor Alexios Porphyrogenitus, son of the Greek emperor Manuel, came to Great Novgorod.
