Anthony, Bishop of Chernigov, and Theodore, Bishop of Pereyaslavl, also assisted Metropolitan Constantine.

Anthony, Bishop of Chernigov, greatly reproved his prince of Chernigov and his boyars, that they should not eat meat on the Lord’s feasts; but Prince Svyatoslav and his boyars did not listen to him. Bishop Anthony reproved them strongly, and Prince Svyatoslav of Chernigov, son of Oleg, could not endure the offense from his bishop and expelled him from his city of Chernigov and from all his principality. He went to Metropolitan Constantine in Kiev and remained there with those who were with him.

In the same year the Polovtsians came in arms against Rus. Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich of Kiev went out against them, and when the regiments met there was a fierce battle and a cruel slaughter, and a multitude of the dead fell on both sides; in the end Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich of Kiev prevailed and returned to Kiev with victory.

In the same year the Polovtsian prince Aydar came to Kiev to serve Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, and afterward, having stayed a little while, was baptized in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.

In the year 6677 from Adam (the year 1177 from Christ)

The Polovtsians came in arms, gathered in great multitude, and making war they reached the city of Plona, belonging to the Tithe Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, which the holy and blessed Grand Prince Vladimir once gave to the Most Pure Mother of God and her holy church.

And when these barbarians reached it, at that very hour some lost their minds, others were blinded, and still others became weakened; the Christians gathered and killed some and captured others.

And many of them were baptized in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; afterward many of them also took the holy monastic angelic habit and led a virtuous and austere life. Such a miracle the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother wrought.

And thus the most blessed Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev, with his son, the faithful and Christ-loving Grand Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich of Kiev, as at the exodus of Israel, loudly and with great joy sang in thanksgiving: “Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been gloriously glorified.” And so with prayer services and thanksgiving praises they glorified the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos.
