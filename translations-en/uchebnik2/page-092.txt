Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich entered the city of Kiev and sat again on his throne, on the grand principality of Kiev. Having also heard that Rostislav Mstislavich had shut himself up in Belgorod, he went against him with the Kievans and the Polovtsians and the Berendeis; and when they came, they besieged Belgorod, while the Polovtsians scattered, ravaging the villages.

Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich and his brother Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich, and Prince Yaropolk Andreevich went out from Vladimir with the Vladimirans and the Luchans, and with the Poles, and with the Czechs, to the aid of Rostislav Mstislavich against Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, to Belgorod.

The Berendeis heard that Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich was coming with a great multitude of troops, and they went over from Prince Izyaslav Davydovich to Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich; and he received them with joy. They began to urge him to go quickly against Prince Izyaslav Davydovich, saying: “We have gone over to you, while his troops have scattered through the villages, and now he has only a small retinue.”

Prince Mstislav heard this and rode swiftly against him; and when he came, he sounded the trumpet. Hearing and seeing this, Izyaslav and his small retinue were shaken with great fear and fled in various directions.

Seeing this, Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich himself fled with his few retainers. Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich and Prince Rostislav Mstislavich overtook him and met him at the Zhelan River, and there was between them a great battle and fierce slaughter, so that the Zhelan River ran with blood from the multitude of the slain. And they halted by the river and began to send messages about peace and love, but there was no love between them, and they stood there eight days.

Then there was a fearful portent in the moon: it moved across the sky changing, and began to wane little by little, and became like red cloth, and then like blood, and it seemed to have two faces, one green and the other yellow; in the middle of it were, as it were, two warriors hewing at each other with swords, from one blood flowed from his head, and from the other something like milk.

Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich sent to Vladimir for fresh forces to aid his uncle Rostislav Mstislavich.

Then came a great force: the Galician host with the commander of their prince, and from Torchesk Prince Ryurik, son of Rostislav, with Prince Vladimir Andreevich and Prince Vasilko Yuryevich, with the Berendeis, the Kovuis, the Black Klobuks, the Dolmats, the Torks, and the Pechenegs; and they joined Mstislav Izyaslavich and Rostislav Mstislavich, and Yaroslav Izyaslavich, and Yaropolk Andreevich, and their host became a countless multitude.
