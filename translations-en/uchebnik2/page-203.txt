And Metropolitan Matthew went from Kiev to Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich in Vladimir to reconcile him with the Olgovichi.

And when he arrived, he began to speak to him of guilelessness, meekness, humility, and love, and that all were entangled in misfortunes in this present life, in the snares and precipices of the enemy. And it would be right for all, while there is time in this present life, to strive and think about their souls and to forgive those who sin, that “the Chernigov princes wish to live in concord with you,” and he said many other such words to him. Vsevolod Yuryevich, hearing this, was moved in heart, and seeing the submission of the Chernigov princes, he loved them, did not remember their malice, and kissed the cross to them.

And he gave gifts to Matthew, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, and sent him away with great honor, and escorted him himself with his children and all the people, with much honor.

In the same year a son Ivan, called Vsevolod, was born to Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich.

In the year 6719 from Adam (1219 from Christ)

Prince Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny took his seat in Kiev, and Rurik in Chernigov.

In the same year the Cumans came to Pereyaslavl, and ravaged many districts and villages, and did much evil, and returned home with many captives.

In the year 6720 from Adam (1220 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich married his son Prince Yuri to a daughter of the Grand Prince of Kiev, Vsevolod Svyatoslavich Chermny, and Prince Yuri and Prince Kir Mikhail Vsevolodovich of Ryazan became kinsmen by marriage; Bishop Ivan married them in the Monastery of the Most Pure Mother of God. And Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich himself was there with his children.

In the same year the city of Rostov burned almost entirely; 15 churches burned, and also on the bishop’s court the Church of John the Forerunner burned completely to its foundation; only the icon of the Holy Great Martyr Theodore the Recruit and a wax vessel with wine remained amid the fire, which they said had belonged to Bishop Leonty, formerly in Rostov.

In the same year the Galicians hated their lord Roman Igorevich and his brothers and began to plot evil against him, how to kill him: they wanted to poison him, but it did not succeed; they wanted to destroy him while hunting, but even so it did not succeed.

And they secretly sent to the Hungarians and secretly brought them to themselves.
