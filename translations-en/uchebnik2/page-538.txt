On the morning of Sunday, on the commemoration of St Nicholas the Wonderworker, Prince Yuri of Yelets came to us with his boyars and many people.
For Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan had sent a messenger to him.
He did what had been commanded and showed us honor and joy and great consolation.

From there we sailed to the Tikhaya Sosna and saw white stone pillars, wondrously and beautifully standing in a row, like small haystacks, very white and bright above the River Sosna.

We also passed the River Cherleny Yar, the Betyuk River, and the Pokhor River.

On the fifth Sunday, of the Samaritan Woman, as we sailed we passed the Medveditsa River, the Gory Vysokie River, and the Bely Yar River. On Monday, as we sailed, we passed the Red Stone Mountains.

On Tuesday, as we sailed, we passed the town of Terkli—not a town, in fact, but only a fortified settlement. We also passed a ferry.

And there for the first time we met very many Tatars, like leaves and sand.
On Wednesday, as we sailed, we passed Velikaya Luka and the Tsar’s Sarykhozin ulus.

And so from there fear began to seize us, for we had entered the Tatar land; and their multitude on both sides of the Don River was like sand.

On Thursday, as we sailed, we passed Bek-Bulat’s ulus. We saw Tatar herds in such numbers that they surpass the mind: sheep, goats, oxen, camels, horses.

On Friday we also passed the Chervlenye Mountains. On the sixth Sunday, of the Blind Man, as we sailed, we passed Bugin’s ulus, and here was a great multitude of Tatars, and many herds of every kind of livestock without number. But none of the Tatars harmed us; they only questioned us everywhere. We answered, and they, having heard, did us no harm and gave us milk. And so we sailed in peace and quiet.
