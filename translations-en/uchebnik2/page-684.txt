And Photius remained at Senega for four weeks and three days in silence and stillness, in compunction and prayer. He remembered his former life in the desert, when he lived in obedience to the great and blessed elder Acacius, living in spiritual labor and teaching, in purification of the mind, in reading the divine Scriptures, and in compunction. And here he found a deserted place, founded a church, often came here and beheld the vast forests, deserted and impassable wilds, the stillness and deep silence, sighing, lamenting, feeling compunction, shedding tears, and he said in tears: “Woe is me, woe is me, a sinner! When I thought of my last breath, when I leave the silence dear to me, end my lamentable and humble life, and leave off thoughts of my transgressions? Woe is me! What is happening to me? From what repose shall I depart into another repose, having labored beyond measure? From what stillness shall I fall into confusion and murmuring? How shall the ship of my soul rush from such stillness into the abyss of the passions? What will be, I do not know.” And with such weeping, lamentation, and sorrow he grew faint, suffering; everything in this present vain and transient life was unlovable to him.

And hearing of what was happening with the metropolitan, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent to him, inviting him to come to Moscow. The grieved metropolitan set off for Moscow, sorrowing both for himself and for the calamities inflicted by the Tatars on Christians. Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich met him with honors and conversed with him about the invasion of the impious Hagarites; they placed their sorrow on the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God, since with God all things are possible, but man can do nothing: “Unless the Lord builds the city, in vain does the watchman keep vigil.” The grand prince began to comfort Metropolitan Photius in his sorrow, saying: “Do not grieve, father, for the Lord God has chosen you and appointed you to fulfill His divine purpose, that you might care for His people for whom He shed His blood; He has placed you in His stead, that you might guard them and care for them. Endure and strive courageously, that you may be saved and receive a great reward; pray for us, that we may be shown mercy and saved through your holy prayers.” And so, setting aside his sorrow, he was comforted in the Lord. And afterward there was a council of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich with his father Metropolitan Photius concerning the fate of his daughter Anna, whom the prince wished to marry into the Greek land, in Constantinople, to Tsarevich Ivan, son of Manuel. Metropolitan Photius agreed to this and blessed the prince.

In the same year Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich gave his daughter Princess Anna in marriage in Tsargrad to Tsarevich Ivan Manuelovich.
