He had love and mercy without pretense; and he was very attentive to the holy churches, and revered bishops in every way as rulers and pastors, and he greatly loved and revered all priests and monks. Basil was also unforgetful of wrongs and forgave those who sinned against him. He lived forty years in all from his birth; after his brother Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich he was Grand Prince of Vladimir and Novgorod for five years.

After him his nephew, Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, became Grand Prince in Vladimir.

In the same year the Novgorodians sent envoys from Novgorod to Vladimir to the Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, with obeisance, and invited him to come to them in Novgorod.

In the same year Prince Boris Vasilkovich of Rostov, grandson of Constantine, married off his eldest son, Prince Dmitry.

In the year 6785 from Adam (1285 from Christ)

Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, set out for Novgorod; and the Novgorodians received him with honor.

In the same year the princes went to the Horde: Grand Prince Boris Vasilkovich of Rostov, grandson of Constantine, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, with his princess and children; and his brother Prince Gleb Vasilkovich of Beloozero, with his son Michael; and Prince Fyodor Rostislavovich of Yaroslavl, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of David, great-great-grandson of Rostislav, great-great-great-grandson of Mstislav, descended from Vladimir Monomakh, from Yaroslav, and from Great Vladimir.

It should be known why Prince Fyodor Rostislavovich is called of Yaroslavl; for by birth he was not of Yaroslavl but of Smolensk. Listen then: the Grand Prince of Kiev was Vladimir Monomakh Vsevolodovich, and he had an eldest son, Mstislav, to whom Smolensk was given.

And although Mstislav was Grand Prince in Kiev after his father, where he also reposed, he is called of Smolensk; Mstislav had a son Rostislav, Grand Prince of Smolensk, and Rostislav had a son David, Grand Prince of Smolensk, David had a son Mstislav, and Mstislav had a son Rostislav. Rostislav had sons: Fyodor, of whom we are now telling, and his brothers Gleb and Michael; these brothers wronged Fyodor and gave him the single town of Mozhaysk; and his brother Gleb became Grand Prince in Smolensk. And Prince Fyodor Rostislavovich, of whom we are now telling, ruled in Mozhaysk before he had a wife.
