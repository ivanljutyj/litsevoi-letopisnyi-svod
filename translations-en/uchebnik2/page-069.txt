With Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh they seated him on the throne of the great principality in Kiev; and so it came about, as before, that there were two great princes in Kiev. And the princes and boyars and all the people said to the great prince Rostislav Mstislavich: “As your brother, the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, honored his uncle, the great prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, so do you also honor him, and until your death Kiev is yours.” He wept much and shed many tears over such love, unanimity, and unity, and firmly promised to honor the great prince Vyacheslav, just as his brother, the great prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, had done.

In the same year Prince Gleb Yuryevich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, came with a multitude of Polovtsians to Pereyaslavl against Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, forefather of the great Vladimir.

The people of Pereyaslavl fought them stoutly, and Prince Gleb with the Polovtsians accomplished nothing and withdrew in shame; then, going with the Polovtsians, they took the town of Piryatin, killing some and taking others captive.

In the same year the great prince of Kiev, Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, with his nephew Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, and with Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, grandson of Oleg, with the Kievans and the Torks, and with a small force, went to Chernigov against the great prince of Chernigov, Izyaslav Davidovich, saying: “Let us make haste to come upon him before Prince Yuri Dolgoruky of Suzdal comes to his aid; either we shall drive him away or make peace with him.” They crossed the Dnieper River and stood by Vyshgorod; and there a messenger came galloping to him from Kiev and said: “Your uncle, the great prince of Kiev, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, yesterday made merry, and ate and drank with the boyars, and lay down to sleep, and fell asleep and did not rise, and now lies dead; what do you command concerning him?”

When the great prince of Kiev, Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Monomakh, heard this, he fell face down on the ground, striking his head against the earth, and wept long; and he summoned the brothers and boyars and said to Prince Svyatoslav and Prince Mstislav: “You remain here with the army, and I will go to Kiev and bury my uncle, the great prince Vyacheslav.”

And coming to Kiev, he prepared his body becomingly and buried him with great honor at the Church of Holy Sophia; and he went into his treasury and examined all his property, and distributed it all to monasteries and churches, and to the poor, the needy, widows, and strangers. Having arranged everything, he went back to his army, taking with him Vyacheslav’s regiment; and coming to Chernigov, he encamped at Boloves.
