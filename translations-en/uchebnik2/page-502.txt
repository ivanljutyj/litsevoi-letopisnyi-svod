Mamai saw that new, unwearied Christian regiments had come out against his Tatars, and by such mercy of God and of His most pure Mother shown to Orthodox Christianity, it seemed to Mamai and his Tatars that Christian regiments had come out of the oak grove like darkness in number, and none of the Tatars could stand against them.

And Mamai fled with his princes and a small retinue. And many Tatars fell by the weapons of the Christian host, through the aid of the Mother of God and the great wonderworker Peter, and others drowned in the river. And they pursued them as far as the river Mecha.

And the princely regiments chased the Tatars even to their camps, and captured their wealth and much property.

And the Christian warriors turned back and saw that dead men lay everywhere; and such was the victory and help of God, and the miracles and signs of the Mother of God, and of the great wonderworker Peter, and of the venerable abbot Sergius, and of the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb, and of all the saints, and the prayer of parents for Orthodox Christians, that wherever the Christian regiments had not even reached, there too Tatars lay dead, slain by the invisible power of God, and of His most pure Mother, and of His saints. Prince Vladimir Andreevich returned and stood upon the bones and saw a multitude of the slain Christian host: princes, and boyars, and commanders, and servants, and foot soldiers, whose number was beyond counting, and everywhere rivers of blood flowed. And Prince Vladimir began to seek his brother, the great prince Dmitry Ivanovich, and did not find him, and beat his head and tore at himself from great sorrow.

And he ordered the gathered trumpets to be sounded, and as many Christian warriors as remained alive assembled, and he asked them: “Who has seen the great prince Dmitry Ivanovich, my brother, anywhere?” And some began to say to him: “We saw him severely wounded; perhaps he is among the dead bodies?” Another said: “I saw him fighting hard and running, and I also saw him fighting four Tatars, but he fled from them, and I do not know what became of him.” Prince Stefan Novosilsky said: “I saw him on foot, scarcely walking from the battle, for he was very severely wounded, and I could not help him, since I myself was pursued by three Tatars.” Then Prince Vladimir Andreevich, cousin of the great prince Dmitry Ivanovich, gathered all and said to them with weeping and many tears: “Lords, and brothers, and sons, and friends! Search diligently for the great prince Dmitry Ivanovich. And if anyone finds him alive, not falsely, but truly I tell you: if he is renowned, great, and honored, let him be glorified still more, and magnified, and honored; but if he is of the common folk, poor and in the last poverty, let him become first, and be magnified with wealth and honor and glory.”
