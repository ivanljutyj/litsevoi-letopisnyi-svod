In the year 6880 from Adam (the year 1380 from Christ)

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow returned from Mamai’s Horde with great gifts and a charter for the great reign, strengthened his great reign, and put his enemies to shame.

The tsar’s envoys came with him, together with many Tatars, and they brought Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver from the Horde, having ransomed him from the Horde and paid ten thousand rubles for him, that is, a myriad of rubles.

And they brought him with them to Moscow; Metropolitan Alexis received him, and the prince remained for a long time at the court of Metropolitan Alexis until he was ransomed.

In the same year the Novgorodians seized the city of Yaroslavl. In the same autumn Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent troops against Bezhetsky Verkh; there they killed the deputy of Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich, Nikifor Lych, and plundered the Tver volosts.

In the same winter, on the fourteenth day of the month of December, before the Nativity of Christ, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent troops against Ryazan and against Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan, sending Dmitry Mikhailovich Volynsky as commander, and with him many other commanders with warriors.

Prince Oleg of Ryazan gathered a large army and came out to meet them. The Ryazanians were harsh, fierce, haughty, and proud; they exulted in their own wit and grew arrogant, in their conceit like foolish and senseless people.

And they said to one another: “Do not take with you any armor, shields, spears, sabers, or arrows; take only lassos, straps, and ropes, for with them you will be able to bind the Muscovites, since they are very weak, cowardly, and frail.”

Our men, in their humility, trusted in the help of the Lord God and the Most Pure Mother of God and the great wonderworkers, since help from God is given to the humble: He grants victory not by strength but by righteousness, and God will not despise a humble and contrite heart. And the Lord God, seeing the humility of the Muscovites, exalted them, and seeing the pride of the Ryazanians, cast them down, as the wise Solomon says: “The Lord resists the proud but gives grace to the humble,” and “Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.” And David says: “A king is not saved by much strength, nor a giant by the multitude of his might,” and elsewhere he says: “He has cast down the mighty from their thrones and exalted the humble.” And the Muscovites, with humility, sighing, and contrite heart, called upon God for help and met the Ryazanians at Skornishchevo; a fierce battle began. The Ryazanians fought, brandishing lassos, straps, and ropes, but, accomplishing nothing, they fell dead like sheaves and were stabbed like pigs.
