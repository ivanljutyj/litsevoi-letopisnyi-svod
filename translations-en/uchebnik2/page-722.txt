In the year 6936 from Adam (1436 from Christ)

Prince Pyotr Dmitrievich passed away, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Ivan, and great-great-grandson of Daniil Alexandrovich of Moscow.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Lithuania, Vytautas Kestutovich, set out with a great host against Veliky Novgorod. The soldiers took with them cannons, mortars, and arquebuses; one cannon named Galka was so large that they hauled it on forty horses from morning until dinner, and from dinner until midday on another forty horses, and until evening on yet another forty horses.

And when they came before the town of Porkhov, Nicholas, the master of that cannon, boasting, said to Vytautas: “Prince, with this cannon I shall not only smash the tower but also destroy the stone church of St Nicholas in the town.” That tower and church were of stone and very strong.

He fired the cannon, and the shot destroyed the tower to its foundation, and in the church of St Nicholas it pierced the front and rear walls of the altar and knocked down the town’s stone battlements. And then the townspeople came out against Vytautas’s regiments, and they killed Vytautas’s Polotsk voivode, and killed many of Vytautas’s soldiers, and killed a great many horses. This happened during the liturgy; the priest serving in the church of St Nicholas was not harmed; but that German, the cannon master, who had boasted that he would destroy the church of St Nicholas the Wonderworker, was blown apart and scattered no one knows where, and nothing was found anywhere, neither body nor bones, only half of his shirt remained.

Vytautas ordered an assault on the town and firing from the cannons; the posadnik Grigory Kirillovich Posakhno and Isaak Boretsky, and others with them, came out of the town and with tears began to petition Vytautas, offering a ransom for the town. And from Novgorod came Bishop Emelyan, named Euthymius by Metropolitan Photius, and with him the Novgorod posadniks and tysyatskys, and they petitioned Vytautas: they paid a ransom of ten thousand Novgorod rubles. And Archbishop Euthymius of Novgorod ransomed all the captives from Vytautas, paying three thousand Novgorod rubles; and that silver he collected from all the Novgorod volosts and in Zavolochye from all the volosts, one ruble from every ten people. And Vytautas said to them: “This is your reward for calling me a traitor and a drunkard.” And so with all his troops he returned home.

In the year 6937 from Adam (1437 from Christ)

Tatars came secretly to Galich against Prince Yuri Dmitrievich and remained in Galich for a month, and, coming at Epiphany, they took Kostroma, Plyos, and Lukh and withdrew down the Volga to the Niz.
