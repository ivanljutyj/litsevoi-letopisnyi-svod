And now he was leaving the principality and went out of the church through the Red Gates. A multitude of people had gathered, countless in number! Standing on a high step before the church, he bowed to all the people, asked their forgiveness, and said to them: “My brothers, my good retinue, sons of Tver! Until now I have ruled over you; now forgive me, for I leave you my beloved eldest son Ivan; let him be your prince in my place. Love him as you loved me, and he will care for you as I did.” The people who heard him began to weep, bitterly lamenting and saying: “Why are you leaving us, and where are you going? O great freedom of Tver and honorable glory of the sons of Tver, great guardian of the city of Tver, who always guarded us like an eagle its nest, and through you the sons of Tver were honored in other lands, and no one dared to wrong us!” And there was great weeping among the people. But he humbly bowed to all and gave them a farewell blessing. After this his gaze dimmed, and the brightness of his face changed to pallor from great exhaustion and from his grave illness.

He came down the church steps. His sons and boyars thought he was going to his own court, but he showed them with his hand and commanded that he be led to the monastery of Saint Athanasius. When his princess Evdokia, with the other princesses and her daughters-in-law, and also his sons and grandchildren, the boyars and all the people heard this from him, they raised a great lament. And so he went to the lavra of Saint Athanasius. They all accompanied him with weeping. O moving sight, worthy of tears! Who would not marvel at this! And how could one not shed tears, seeing how the great prince, who was famed in all lands and whom enemies feared, left such a glorious principality and humbly went on his own feet to the monastery, and with compunction asked the bishop for tonsure. The venerable Bishop Arseny, thanking the Lord God with tears, tonsured him and clothed him in the holy angelic monastic habit, giving him the name Matthew. And the people said: “Truly the Lord does not deprive the generous of gentleness,” and “The eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear Him,” and also “Blessed is God, who has done the will of those who fear Him.”

Good in soul, Matthew, now without power, came to his brother, the monk Gregory, and humbly asked his permission to settle in his cell, giving a donation in the name of the all-wise Creator, the Lord God, who orders all things in His love for mankind. Then he began to be ill. His noble sons sat beside him all the time.

On the fourth day the strong Matthew summoned the bishop and said that he wished to receive the schema. The bishop, rejoicing spiritually, tonsured him into the schema, and for many days anointed him with holy oil, and communed him with the most pure mysteries of Christ.

By God’s grace his illness was not very severe, so that he did everything sitting. He ordered that the abbots from all over the city come to him.
