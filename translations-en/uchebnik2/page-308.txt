And he prayed to the Lord God, to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to the holy martyr Leontius, whose memory was being celebrated then, and went against the enemies, and defeated them, and killed their great prince Gogort, and other princes, and killed many Lithuanians.

Some Lithuanians drowned in the river; others the Dvina cast onto the island of Gandov, and there they were killed; Prince Gerden with a few warriors barely escaped; and one Pskovian was killed then; and Prince Dovmont came to Pskov with many captives and much wealth.

In the same year Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich set out from Vladimir with a great host to Novgorod and wished to go to war against Pskov, against Prince Dovmont; but the Novgorodians did not want this; he returned back, and in Novgorod he left his nephew, Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky.

In the year 6775 from Adam (the year 1275 from Christ)

There was a revolt and civil strife in Novgorod. In the same year the Nerevsky End burned in Novgorod.

In the same year the Novgorodians with Elerferius, and Prince Dovmont of Pskov with the Pskovians, defeated the Lithuanians and killed Prince Gerden.

In the year 6776 from Adam (the year 1276 from Christ)
A son, Vasily, was born to Prince Boris Vasilkovich.

In the same year the Novgorodians with Prince Yuri Andreevich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, laid waste the lands beyond the Narva River.

And the Novgorodians sent envoys to Yaroslavl for Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, summoning him to their aid; they also sent envoys to Vladimir to Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, summoning him to Novgorod, gathering an army for a campaign against the Germans.

But Prince Yaroslav sent to them in Novgorod in his place his sons Svyatoslav and Mikhail, and other princes; and they began to make throwing engines in the archbishop’s courtyard.

And the Germans sent their envoys—the Veliadians, Riga men, Yuryev men, and men from other cities—and spoke deceitfully: “Why do you wish to shed blood in vain? We are at peace and in love with you; your merchants come to us and ours go to you, and they suffer no opposition or injury whatsoever. But if you wish to make war, then here are Kolyvan and Rakovor, and we have nothing to do with them. 20}
