That winter was very warm, and all the snow melted as early as Tuesday before the last Sunday before Great Lent; no snow remained anywhere. In autumn much grain had been left under the snow, and people harvested grain during Great Lent, when the snow melted, in those places where the Lithuanian army had not been; and all the snow melted on Tuesday of St Theodore’s Week.

That winter there were severe frosts with snow after a thaw.
That same spring Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich went to the Horde.

In the same year Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver returned to Tver from Mamai’s Horde, from Tsar Mamai, with a yarlyk for the great reign, and with him came the envoy Sary-Khoja, and they went from Tver to the Volga.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow brought boyars and common people in all the towns to oath against Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, “so that they would not let him into the reign of Vladimir,” and he himself with his brother Prince Vladimir Andreyevich stood with an army at Pereyaslavl.

Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver wished to enter the capital city of Vladimir, calling himself grand prince, granted by the Horde tsar, and to sit there in the great reign. The people of Vladimir did not receive him and did not permit him to sit on the throne.

He withdrew, and the Tatar Sary-Khoja sent his Tatars and Prince Mikhail’s boyar to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, inviting him to Vladimir for the great reign; but he replied: “I will not go to the great reign, and I will not let him into my land for the reign of Vladimir; but for you the way is open.” And he sent to him, lovingly inviting him to himself; but he did not wish to go to him.

The envoy took to heart the honors and gifts from the prince, and he gave the tsar’s yarlyk for the great reign to Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, while he himself went in peace and love from Mologa to Moscow to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

And Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich went back from there to Bezhetsky Verkh, fighting, and returned to Tver on the 23rd day of May.

In the same year Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver sent his son Prince Ivan to the Horde. And the envoy Sary-Khoja received honors and gifts in Moscow from Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich and returned to the Horde, praising Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, extolling his good character, his humility, and the way he had received him, for which the envoy was grateful to him.

And then Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich himself went to the Horde together with Prince Andrey of Rostov on the 15th day of July, and they crossed the Oka River.
