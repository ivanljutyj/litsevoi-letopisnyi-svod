Rostov, and the Ustyug princes, and many other princes and commanders with many forces.

The Grand Prince desired to go to the monastery of the Life-Giving Trinity and to the Venerable Abbot Sergius, and, having set out, he received a blessing from his father Cyprian, Metropolitan of All Rus. And he came to the monastery on the 18th day of the month of August, on the feast of the holy martyrs Florus and Laurus, and wished to return quickly, because many messengers reporting Mamai’s invasion began to hurry him. But the Venerable Abbot Sergius persuaded him to eat bread with him at the table: “May the Lord God and the Most Pure Theotokos grant you help,” he said, “and it is not yet time for you to wear the crown of victory together with eternal sleep; while for many others beyond number crowns with eternal remembrance are being prepared.”

And he ordered holy water to be prepared, and after rising from the table he blessed him with the cross and sprinkled the Grand Prince with holy water, and said to him: “Honor the impious Mamai with gifts and honor, so that the Lord God may see your humility; and He will exalt you, and will cast down his untamable fury and pride.” He said: “I have done all this for him, father. But he exalts himself still more with great pride.” The Venerable one said: “If it is so, then final destruction and desolation await him; but to you from the Lord God, and the Most Pure Theotokos, and His saints there is help, and mercy, and glory.”

And the Grand Prince began to ask him for Peresvet and Oslyabya, because of their courage and their skill in equipping regiments, saying thus: “Father, give me two warriors from your monastic regiment, two brothers—Peresvet and Oslyabya. For these are known to all as great warriors and mighty champions, and they well understand military affairs and equipment.”

The Venerable Sergius ordered them to prepare quickly for battle. And with all their soul they rendered obedience to the Venerable Sergius and in no way refused his command.

He gave them, instead of perishable weapons, the imperishable—the Cross of Christ sewn upon their schemata—and he commanded them to place this upon their heads instead of helmets and to fight firmly for Christ against His enemies.

And he gave them into obedience to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, saying to him: “Here, prince, are my warriors and your helpers, whom you desired to be with you in the misfortunes that have come, in this wretched and needful time.”

And he said to them: “Peace to you, my beloved brothers in Christ, Peresvet and Oslyabya! Strive as good soldiers of Christ, for the time of your purchase has come.”
