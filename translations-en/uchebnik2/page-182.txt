Then Saint Sava went to the holy city of Jerusalem to venerate the holy places, taking with him much wealth; he distributed alms to the poor and to monasteries, redeemed relics of saints and honorable vessels and vestments, wishing to bring them to his fatherland.

On the Capture of Constantinople by the Latins

In the same year 6712 from Adam (1212 from Christ), Constantinople was captured by the Latins; whether Emperor Theodore Laskaris was not there at that time, or he could not offer them resistance, because he had the Bulgarian king as his enemy.

The Latins carried off to Rome much property, books, statutes, honorable vessels, sacred vestments, wonder-working icons, the Image Not Made by Hands, the Robe and Belt of the Most Pure Mother of God, and relics of many saints; in Constantinople they installed their own bishops and presbyters, and everything became according to Roman custom.

The Greek kingdom was divided into two parts: in all Thessaly and Illyria, in Thessalonica, Theodore Laskaris reigned, and in all Pontus, Galatia, and Bithynia, and in Asia, the pious Emperor Kal John Vatatzes reigned.

On Sava of Serbia

To him Saint Sava sailed upon his return from the holy places, and was received by him with joy; for a long time the emperor and empress showed him honor, and he constantly sought out relics of saints, bought them, and asked them of the emperor. The emperor gifted him a part of the Honorable and Life-Giving Wood and relics of saints, and sacred garments, and church vessels; he gave him much gold as well, so that as he traveled he would have means for alms to the poor, the needy, and monasteries, saying to him: “Pray for us sinners.” The saint blessed the emperor and empress, and the emperor gave him his ship, filling it with all necessities and armed soldiers, so that they might escort him to the Holy Mountain.

They sailed past holy Constantinople, which the Latins then held.
