There was a certain servant of Lazar, King of Serbia; he was faithful, but certain people slandered him, saying that he served the king unjustly and unfaithfully.

Then both hosts came together, and there was a great battle and fierce slaughter.

The slandered man, wishing to show his true service, entered the regiment of the Turkish king Amurat, pretending to be one who had fled from the Orthodox Serbian King Lazar; and the Turkish regiments parted before him, giving way. And he, declaring that he was coming with love to Amurat, King of Turkey, suddenly thrust his sword swiftly into the heart of Amurat, King of Turkey.

And at that same hour Amurat, King of Turkey, died. That wondrous Christian servant was also killed by them.

And therefore the Turks fell into confusion, and then the Serbian forces with King Lazar began to prevail over the Turks.

Soon the Turks also set Amurat’s son Baazit as king among themselves.

And so the Turks began to overcome the Serbian King Lazar and his army, and they captured the Serbian king, and his princes, and his commanders, and his boyars, and his servants, and all his army: some they killed, others they seized by hand.

But Baozit, King of Turkey, ordered the Serbian King Lazar to be cut down with the sword. This battle was in the year 6897. So the townspeople told us. Since they were in the Turkish realm, there was a great rebellion and much confusion in that land.

And fearing the rebellion, Pimen the metropolitan sent the monk Michael to Constantinople, and Michael, Bishop of Smolensk, sent me, Ignatius, and Sergius, archimandrite of Azakov, sent his monk. And they set out from the city of Ostravia on the Sunday before Peter’s Day.

In the morning they set out from Phila and passed Riva, and came to Ustye, and passed Phanar.

The wind was very favorable, and they sailed to Constantinople with inexpressible joy.

On Monday, the eve of Peter’s Day, during Vespers, the Russians living there came to us, and there was great joy for all, and that night they remained in the ship.

In the morning, on the 29th day of June, on the very feast of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, giving thanks to the Lord God, they entered the city of Constantine.

In the morning of the same month, on the 30th day, they went to the Church of Holy Sophia, the Wisdom of God.
