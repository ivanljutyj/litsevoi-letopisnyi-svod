Vyacheslav said: "You have seven sons, and I do not drive them away from you, but I have only two sons, Izyaslav and Rostislav, and the others are younger; but for the sake of the Russian land and for the sake of Christians, go, brother, to your Pereyaslavl and to Kursk with your sons, and besides that you have Rostov the Great and other cities there; and let the Olegoviches go, and we will come to terms ourselves, and we will not shed Christian blood; will you follow your intention, as you have already set out?" And, looking at the image of the Holy Theotokos painted on the Golden Gate, he continued: "O Lady and Mistress, our Theotokos, with her Son and our God, will judge between us in this age and in the age to come"; and, having said this, he sent his envoy back to him.

On the next day came Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev and Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, and Rostislav with his son Roman, and Prince Izyaslav Davidovich, and Prince Vladimir, and with a multitude of soldiers they stood by the city, and archers shot across the Lybed River; at that time the Berendei arrived with their wagons and with the Torks, and all the Black Klobuks, and wrought great havoc: they burned districts and villages, and cut down and laid waste orchards.

Then word came to Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh of Pereyaslavl that his kinsman by marriage, Grand Prince Volodimirko of Galich, was coming to his aid; and he withdrew back to meet him.

Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich and Grand Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, hearing that news, set out, having gathered with all the princes and all their forces, after him, fearing that he would join with Volodimirko, and on the third day they overtook him at Perepetov, before he had joined with Volodimirko. And they stood opposite, and wanted to fight on Thursday, but there was a strong rain with wind, and the soldiers got so wet as if drowning in the sea; and they wanted peace among themselves. But the Olegoviches and the accursed Polovtsians did not let them make peace, because they wanted bloodshed. They stood until evening, and in the morning, at dawn on Friday, having arrayed his troops, Izyaslav Mstislavich advanced against him; but Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky of Pereyaslavl did not yet want to fight, awaiting Grand Prince Volodimirko of Galich to come to his aid.

Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev, knowing that Volodimirko of Galich was coming to his aid, began to advance on him and set his regiments against him.
