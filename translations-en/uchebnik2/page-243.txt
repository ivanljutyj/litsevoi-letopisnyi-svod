When he arrived, Prince Yaroslav said to the archbishop and the Novgorodians: “I had set out for Pskov in peace and love, intending to bestow gifts on the Pskovians, but they showed me dishonor; and now I grieve and am greatly sorrowful over this.”

In the same year Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich secretly sent envoys to Pereslavl, gathering an army and intending to go to war against the Pskovians; and he said to the Novgorodians: “I wish to go to war against Riga, and I am sending to Pereslavl for troops; you too must all be ready.”

In the same year a multitude of warriors came to Yaroslav in Novgorod from Pereslavl and from other cities.

Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich then summoned the Novgorodians also to muster, saying: “Let us go to war against Riga.” But the Novgorodians feared that Yaroslav would go to Riga and there would set about ravaging Pskov too, and therefore they did not hasten to carry out Yaroslav’s command.

The Pskovians learned that Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich wished to go to war against Riga and to ravage them. And then the Pskovians, in fear, joined with the Rigans and gave ninety of their best men as hostages to the Rigans, while the Rigans gave ninety of their best men to the Pskovians; and thus they agreed to be of one mind and to stand for one another even unto blood.

Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich learned that the Pskovians had united with the Rigans, and sent envoys to the Pskovians with these words: “We are going with the Novgorodians to war against Riga; come with us.”

The Pskovians sent their envoys to Yaroslav with these words: “Lord Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, you are a wise and most prudent prince, and you know that we are all of one Adam’s race, all brothers to one another, and uncles, and kinsmen, and sisters, and aunts, and we are all one kind: both believers and unbelievers. But it is not fitting to make war on unbelievers without cause; rather one should be at peace with all. Only we should not touch their unbelief and lawlessness, but be at peace with them, so that these unbelievers too, seeing our life and humility and love, may come to the knowledge of God, and turn, and be baptized, and all be saved by the grace of Christ and of His Most Pure Mother. And you, prince, are so reasonable, yet you order war against the Rigans without cause.”

Hearing this answer, Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich was grieved, and reported it to the Novgorodians, and said: “The Pskovians mock me, and will not go with us against the Rigans, and have joined with the Rigans in one mind.”
