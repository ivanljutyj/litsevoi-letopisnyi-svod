By our sins: if one Tatar appears anywhere, our men dare not resist him; if two or three Tatars appear anywhere, a multitude of Christians flee, abandoning their wives and children. Thus our pride was revealed, and what had been foretold was fulfilled through the sign: the blood that flowed from the icon of the Most Pure Mother of God in Pokhra, in Kolomna district. The Tatar army, sent out by Edygey, continued to fight and captured many.

They also took the city of Pereyaslavl and burned it, and Rostov, and Dmitrov, and Serpukhov, and Vereya, and Nizhny Novgorod, and Gorodets; they plundered and burned many lands and villages, and many Christians died during that winter: for the winter was very cold and harsh, with fierce blizzards and winds. But the proud prince Svidrigailo with his brave Lithuanians conquered none of the foreigners; their weapons and all their military cunning were broken, while Edygey captured all the princes.

And after the Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich he sent Tsarevich Begiberdey and his son Yakshibey, and Prince Sentilibey with thirty-four thousand Tatar troops; but they, not reaching the prince, turned back. Edygey seized and laid waste all the surroundings of Moscow; only Moscow alone was preserved by God through the prayers of His Most Pure Mother, the Mother of God, for the sake of Her life-giving icon and for the sake of the great wonderworker Peter, Metropolitan of all Rus. Edygey himself did not approach Moscow and did not send anyone into it, wishing to winter there and seize Moscow, greatly proud and exalted. And he sent to Tver, to the Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver, Tsarevich Bulat and Prince Erikliberdey, ordering him to come to Moscow with cannons, mortars, arquebuses, and crossbows. He went with them part of the way, but went no farther; sending them on, he returned from Klin, not wishing to break his oath to the Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich and at the same time not wishing to anger Edygey.

And at that time ambassadors came from the Horde from Tsar Bulat-Saltan, ordering him to be before the tsar in the Horde without delay: when Tsar Bulat-Saltan had released all of them to Rus, one tsarevich, waiting his time, wished to seize the realm of the Great Horde and to drive out or kill Tsar Bulat-Saltan. Then Edygey was frightened that he had promised to winter under Moscow, exalting himself in pride, and now had to leave everything and return without delay. And he said to his retinue: “Soon we shall go to the Horde, so as not to lose the realm, for there someone, having come, wishes to seize our realm; but here the Grand Prince Vasily, having gathered an army, may be able to defeat us—what shall we do then?” And soon he sent into the city asking for peace, made peace with the townspeople, taking a ransom of 3,000 rubles.
