So he lived with the brothers, and although he was not ordained a priest, he nevertheless cared for the Church of God together with them. Every day he sang with the brothers in church the Midnight Office, Matins, and the hours—Third, Sixth, and Ninth—and Vespers and Compline, as it is said: “Seven times a day I praise You for the judgments of Your righteousness.” And between these they held frequent prayer services, continually entreating God both in church and in their cells, as Paul said: “Pray to God without ceasing.” And to serve the liturgy they invited some priest from the neighboring villages or an abbot from a monastery, and these served the Divine Liturgy.

Sergius himself at first, because of his great and perfect humility, did not wish to be ordained a priest or abbot, imitating in all things his Master, our Lord Jesus Christ, who said to those who wished to follow Him and imitate Him: “Come to Me, all who labor and are burdened, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and humble in heart.” And twelve monks gathered there. Among them were Basil, called Sukhoy, who came from the upper reaches of the Dubna; another was Yakim, nicknamed Yakuta. This Yakuta served as messenger: he was always sent when it was necessary to obtain what was needed for the service, without which it was impossible to live and endure. The third was Anisim, who was a deacon, and the deacon’s father, named Elisey. When the cells were built, they were enclosed with a palisade, but not a very high one, and a watchman was placed at the gate. Sergius himself built three or four cells with his own hands. He also performed other monastic work necessary for the life of the brothers: sometimes he carried wood from the forest on his shoulders, split it into logs, and carried it to the brothers’ cells; he worked and served like a purchased slave, prepared firewood for all, and, as it is said, chopped, pounded, and ground, baked bread, cooked food, and prepared every kind of food for the needs of the brothers; he cut and sewed footwear and clothing. And from the spring that was there he carried water on his shoulders in two water vessels and set it by each cell. And he spent the nights without sleep, praying and reading, and was not idle for an hour. He fed on bread and water, and even that not to satisfaction. And so he exhausted his body by abstinence and labors that he subdued the fleshly senses and quenched bodily burning, and repelled the arrows of the evil one without agitation, adding labor to labor. And he cared for the flourishing of that deserted place, so that his labor might be profitable. And whatever he did, a psalm was always on his lips, in which it is said: “I have set the Lord always before me; because He is at my right hand, that I may not be moved, always laboring and shedding sweat.” And he was always in labors, shedding sweat. And so he tormented, dried up, and put to sleep his flesh, desiring to be a heavenly dweller, that he counted all else as nothing.
