By Temir-Khozei, and all the rest of the host fled; only Prince Andrei Yuryevich held out a little with his retinue, but even they, possessed by fear, wanted to flee.

On the next day they all gathered together and retreated back, and halted at Dubna, awaiting help from their father, Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh. And word came to them that their father was coming to them with his brother Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh; and they rejoiced greatly, and advanced on Lutsk, and in Lutsk at that time was Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, brother of Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich.

And foot soldiers came out of the city of Lutsk and began shooting at them; and Prince Andrei Yuryevich went against them and broke his spear upon his adversary; and there the foot soldiers killed many of his troops, and they attacked Prince Andrei himself, and wounded his horse under him with two spears, and with a third spear in the front saddle-bow.

And from the city they threw stones at him like rain. And one of the enemy foot soldiers came at him with a boar-spear and wanted to run him through; but God preserved him, for He knows who is pious and delivers from calamity. Then Prince Andrei, being in great distress from those attacking him, prayed to God, saying: “Lord, help me! Lo, the death of Izyaslav Yaroslavich is about to be mine.” And he called on the holy Great Martyr Theodore for help, and took his sword in his hand, and all the warriors fled from him; and so he suffered no harm from anyone, only one of his attendants was killed then, for only two attendants were with him in that danger at that time. And he rode away with one attendant on his wounded horse. But his father, Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, and his uncle, Prince Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh, and his brothers, Prince Rostislav and Prince Boris Yuryevich, and Prince Mstislav Yuryevich, seeing him come to them alive and embracing him, began to weep for joy, saying: “Why, beloved, do you give yourself over to danger? For to go under the city like that is suicide, and you will meet an untimely death; this is not courage but lack of skill and training.” And after teaching him much, they comforted him and took off his armor, and found not many wounds or much blood on him; but his horse, though badly wounded, carried him only as far as his father and at that very hour fell. And he ran, forgetting his own wounds, to the horse and wept over it, because in such danger, though so badly wounded, it had carried him away from the enemy. And he ordered the earth to be dug and a wooden frame set there, and there he buried his horse.

And on the next day Grand Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh advanced on the city; and foot soldiers came out of the city, and there was a long battle, and he stood at the city gates for three weeks and cut off the water; and Prince Vladimir Mstislavich, who was in the city, brother of Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, grew weak with his men in the city.
