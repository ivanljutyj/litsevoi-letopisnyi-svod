He gave one part to his brothers, and distributed all the other among the troops, from the eldest to the youngest, and of that silver he kept not a single silver coin for himself; and he went to Kiev and dismissed the troops to their homes.

In the same year the Kanev church was founded by the Grand Prince of Kiev Vsevolod Olgovich, on the ninth day of July. In the same year in Novgorod they completed the stone church of the Holy Theotokos at Torgovishche, and gave the office of posadnik to Nezhata Tverdyatich.

In the year 6653 from Adam (1153 from Christ)

The pious and Christ-loving Grand Princess Elena transferred Grand Prince Yaropolk, son of Vladimir Monomakh, from the tomb to the Church of St Andrew, and they laid him beside Yanka, daughter of Vsevolod, granddaughter of Yaroslav, great-granddaughter of great Vladimir.

In the same year the Most Reverend Lord Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, went to Constantinople to the patriarch. In the same year Prince Igor Olgovich went with his brothers to the aid of the Poles, to fight together with the Poles.

In the same year in Kiev the greater half of Podol burned. In the same year in Smolensk they laid the foundation of a stone church of the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb on the Smyadyn.

In the year 6654 from Adam (1154 from Christ)

Grand Prince Vladimir of Galicia came and took Priluki; hearing of this, Grand Prince Vsevolod Olgovich of Kiev was greatly grieved and sent to his brothers, bidding them be ready from St Boris’s Day for a campaign against Grand Prince Vladimir of Galicia.

And he sent many messengers throughout the whole land: to Novgorod, and to the Czechs, and to the Poles, and to the Hungarians, gathering a great host against Grand Prince Vladimir of Galicia, and he ordered both Izyaslavs, Davidovich and Mstislavich, to remain at home.

And several days passed, and he fell gravely ill, and ordered himself to be taken to Vyshgorod; and after staying there a little, Grand Prince Vsevolod of Kiev, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of great Vladimir, fell asleep in eternal sleep on the thirtieth day of June, having sat on the grand principality in Kiev for seven years. And his son was Svyatoslav.

And after him his brother Igor Olgovich entered the great principality in Kiev; but it was not pleasing to the people, and they sent to Pereyaslavl to Prince Izyaslav, son of Mstislav, grandson of Grand Prince Vladimir Monomakh, saying thus: “Come to us, prince, to Kiev for the great principality, since we all wish to see you on the great principality in Kiev.” And Izyaslav Mstislavich, hearing this, rejoiced greatly.
