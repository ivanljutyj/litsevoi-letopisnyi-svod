Hearing of this, Tsar Azbyak of the Horde was inflamed with great fury and was in great grief and sorrow for his brother Shchelkan, and roaring like a lion against the Tverites, he wished to kill them all and lay waste all the Russian land.

And he sent to Rus for Prince Ivan Danilovich.

In that year, by the command of Tsar Azbyak, Prince Vasily of Ryazan was killed in the Horde.

That same autumn, Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich of Moscow went to the tsar in the Horde.

That same winter he returned to Moscow, and with him came Tsar Azbyak with a Tatar host and many commanders: Fedorchuk, Turalyk, Syuga, and other Horde princes, five great temniks, Prince Alexander Vasilyevich of Suzdal, and with him his uncle Vasily Alexandrovich.

And by the tsar’s command they went and took the cities of Tver and Kashin and the other Tver cities; they burned volosts and villages, led people away into captivity, and laid waste Torzhok and its volosts.

And they wished to go to Novgorod, but did not go. God and Holy Sophia protected it. And they sent their envoys there, to Novgorod. The Novgorodians, in fear, sent their envoys to them with great honor, with many gifts, and paid 5,000 Novgorod rubles. And so the Tatars returned from there with a great number of captives and much wealth.

And then a great calamity came upon all the Russian land: suffering and bloodshed from the Tatars. Then also they killed Prince Ivan Yaroslavich of Ryazan and killed and captured many Christians. And only Prince Ivan Danilovich was protected by the Lord God, together with his city Moscow and all his patrimony, from the invasion and bloodshed with the Tatars. And the Tatars returned with a great number of captives to the Horde.

Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver could not endure the malicious diabolical intrigues and Tatar violence; he left the grand principality and all his fatherland, and went to Pskov with his princess and his children, and lived in Pskov for 10 years.

And his brothers, Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich of Tver and Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver, grandsons of Yaroslav and great-grandsons of Yaroslav, came with their mother and boyars to Tver in great sorrow and grief. And they sat in Tver in great poverty and wretchedness, since all the land of Tver was empty, and all the forests and steppes became impassable because of the persecutions, fury, and violence of the Tatars. But little by little they began to gather people, comforting them in great sorrow and grief. In the holy churches and monasteries singing again began, and divine services began to be performed.
