He began to entreat Yuri for his nephews. And the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Dolgoruky, listened to him and ordered him to send for them at once.

Prince Vladimir Izyaslavich and Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich came to Kiev, but their brother Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich did not dare go with them to Kiev, saying: “Prince Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh will take me; why should I give myself into his hands of my own will and be my own murderer?” And the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh, received his brothers Prince Vladimir Izyaslavich and Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich with love, and sent to him with the kissing of the cross and an agreement; and so they were united in love and peace.

In the same year Galician aid came to the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Dolgoruky, since the Grand Prince of Chernigov, Izyaslav Davydovich, was planning a campaign.

In the same year the Cumans came again for peace and stood along the Lubents, even to the upper reaches of the Suboy; and Prince Yuri Dolgoruky, with Prince Rostislav Mstislavich, and with Prince Vladimir Izyaslavich, and with Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich, and with the Galician aid, went to Kanev for a meeting with the accursed Cumans.

And from there he sent to the Cumans, saying: “Behold, I have come and stand at Kanev; come to me for peace.”

But they came deceitfully, in very small numbers, as though for peace, not truly desiring peace, but observing how many Rus troops there were; and they saw that there were many, and said to him: “We are going back to our own, and tomorrow we will all come to you to conclude peace.”

And coming to their own, they told them of the great host with the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Dolgoruky Vladimirovich Monomakh; and that night all the Cumans fled to the Steppe. Prince Yuri Dolgoruky returned to Kiev.

In the same year the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yuri Dolgoruky, with his nephews, sent to the Grand Prince of Chernigov, Izyaslav Davydovich, in Chernigov, saying: “Either we come to you, or you come to us for peace.”

He, having learned of his like-minded union with his nephews, kissed the cross to them; and then Prince Yuri Dolgoruky sent his nephews away, each to his own place, and also sent the Galician aid back home.

In the same year Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, went from his great principality of Vyshgorod to his father Yuri Dolgoruky in Kiev.
