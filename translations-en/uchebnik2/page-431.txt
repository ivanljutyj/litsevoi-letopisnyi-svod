And in that same year they returned to Novgorod with great booty. But Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich became angry at the Novgorodians because of this, broke the peace with them, saying: “Why do you go about plundering and killing my merchants?”

In that same winter, by command of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, the Novgorod boyar Vasily Danilovich and his son Ivan were captured in Vologda, while he was traveling from the Dvina, knowing nothing and suspecting nothing.

In that same winter, on the 18th day of the month of January, Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich married, taking as his wife Evdokia, daughter of Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod; the wedding was in Kolomna.

That same winter Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich consulted with his cousin Prince Vladimir Andreevich and with all his senior boyars about building a stone Moscow. As they planned, so they did: that same winter they began hauling stones to the city.

A Most Touching Tale.

In those times there was in Cyprus a prince Andrew, called Pigor, under whose authority was a large army, with which he subdued all the surrounding lands. Having become very strong, he set out with his army against Alexandria in Egypt, and he beat, burned, and took captive the Saracens, Tatars, Arabians, Armenians, Turks, Franks, Dikats, Formases, Circassians, Barmens, and Jews who lived there.

And for this the Egyptian Sultan became angry at the Christians, fell into a great fury, and gathered a huge army. And he sent the army against Antioch and Jerusalem and against the other cities in his vicinity, and a cruel war began.

And a cruel persecution of Christians began in those cities; all the holy churches were plundered, closed, and filled up with stones, and the Sultan, seized with fury like a lion, ordered Orthodox Christians to be subjected to various tortures. And so he tortured all, took their property for himself, plundered the monasteries of Sinai, destroyed the dwellings of the great ascetics, the sketes and deserts, put abbots, priests, and monks to death and burned them, and subjected all the bishops to torments and cast them into prisons. He crucified the blessed Michael, Patriarch of Antioch, as well as all the most reverend metropolitans and bishops, and some after many torments he cast into prisons.

And, unable to endure this, the sun hid its rays on the 7th day of the month of August, at the third hour of the afternoon. And then the sun became the size of a three-day-old moon, waning on the southern side, and a blue and green darkness came from the west. And the darkness lasted one hour; the sun turned its rays toward the south, like a young moon, and then turned its rays toward the earth and became like the moon, and great darkness fell. And afterward the sun began gradually to emit light until it regained its full strength and revealed its light in full measure.
