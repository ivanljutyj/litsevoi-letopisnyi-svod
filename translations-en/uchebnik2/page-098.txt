Prince Mstislav had then a small army; all were in great need: some had gone away earlier, and others had grown weak from winter hardships; but the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother put the pagans to flight, showing by this an ineffable miracle, protected them and saved them from the hands of the pagans; the Russian army barely returned with great difficulty, because in winter it is hard to fight the Bulgars.

In the same year the great prince Roman Rostislavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir, named Andrew in holy baptism, Monomakh, left Kiev of his own will, because he greatly feared the treachery of the Kievans and of Prince Mikhail Yuryevich.

In the same year Prince Mikhail Yuryevich sat upon the great principality in Kiev.

In the same year Prince Yaroslav Izyaslavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, sat upon the great principality in Kiev.

In the year 6681 from Adam (1181 from Christ)

Prince Mstislav Andreevich, son of Andrew Bogolyubsky, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, reposed, on the 28th day of the month of March; he was laid in the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God in Vladimir, which his father founded.
