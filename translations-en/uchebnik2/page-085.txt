Izyaslav Davydovich, hearing this, at that same hour hastened against them with a multitude of warriors, and with the Polovtsian princes, and with the Polovtsians, and surrounded the city, and there was a fierce battle and a cruel slaughter. And on the next day there came to him the Polovtsian prince Bashkord, stepfather of his nephew Svyatoslav, son of Vladimir, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir, with twenty thousand Polovtsians.

And the Grand Prince of Kyiv Izyaslav Davydovich sent to Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich concerning peace and love. And when this was so, a demon came and by his wiles deceived and corrupted the hearts of Izyaslav’s men, and sedition arose against him among his household, and many fled from him into the city, while others openly went over to Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich.

And the Grand Prince of Kyiv Izyaslav Davydovich, seeing such misfortune and calamity upon himself, was afraid and trembled, and, weeping, quickly fled with his nephew, Prince Svyatoslav Vladimirovich, to Vyshgorod and to Gomel, and soon sent messengers after his princess to Kyiv. She fled from Kyiv to her son-in-law, Prince Gleb, son of Yuri Dolgoruky, to the city of Pereyaslavl Russky; and he escorted her to Gomel to Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich.

And Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, and Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and Prince Yaroslav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, entered Kyiv on the twenty-second day of August; and Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich took all the possessions of Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich.

Then Constantine, metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus, feared Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, because he was not in agreement with him, and soon fled from Kyiv to Chernigov.

And Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, having sat in the city of Kyiv, fortified it and supported all; and he sent to the Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich in Smolensk.

And the Grand Prince of Smolensk Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, sent to them, saying thus: “By the love of the Spirit and the counsel of unity you call me to the grand principality in Kyiv, and if so, I have grace toward you; for I have heard that you drove the Grand Prince Izyaslav Davydovich from the grand principality of Kyiv and plundered all his possessions; I have also heard concerning our father the most reverend Constantine, metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus, that he fled from Kyiv to Chernigov because of your malice, and this is not at all beneficial to us; and you wish to see Clement as metropolitan on the throne in Kyiv and over all Rus, whom Izyaslav Mstislavich, having agreed with his Rus bishops, installed, and many were indignant at this; but Metropolitan Constantine was installed in Constantinople by the patriarch and the council assembled there for Kyiv and all Rus, and I want him, the one whom you drive out.”
