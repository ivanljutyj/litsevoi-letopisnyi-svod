The Kievan guard, however, took fright and fled. When Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich heard that the Polovtsians had crossed the Dnieper, he returned to Kiev. The Polovtsians came to Kiev and stood by the Lybed.

Izyaslav took his stand against them before the Golden Gate with his uncle Vyacheslav, while Izyaslav Davidovich stood between the Golden and Jewish Gates, opposite Borislav's court, and Rostislav with his son Roman stood before the Jewish Gates, and Boris at the Lyatsky Gates; the Kievans, with all their forces, mounted and on foot, stood between the princes, and so a great multitude of them stood around the whole city.

Vyacheslav then said to Izyaslav and Rostislav: "Brothers, we are already prepared. Yuri is my brother, but younger; yet I wish to send to him and hand over my seniority to him, so that when we stand in judgment before God, God may see the wrong." And Izyaslav and Rostislav said: "So be it, father; do as you have said."

Vyacheslav said to his retainer: "Go to my brother Yuri and kiss him on my behalf; and you, my brothers and sons, listen: I send this before you. Say thus to my brother: Brother, I have spoken to you and to my nephew Izyaslav so many times—do not shed Christian blood and do not ruin the Russian land.

From that I restrained you, and said nothing for myself about how I was wronged, though I had troops and God gave me strength; but for the sake of the Russian land and for the sake of Christians I recalled none of it, and even disclosed this to you: when Izyaslav, going to fight Igor, said, 'I do not seek Kiev for myself, but for my father Vyacheslav,' and when God helped him, then he not only took Kiev for himself but also took Turov and Pinsk from me—thus Izyaslav wronged me; and you, brother, when you went to Pereyaslavl to fight with Izyaslav, also said: 'I do not seek Kiev for myself, but for my elder brother Vyacheslav, for he is as a father to me'; and when God helped you, you not only took Kiev for yourself but also took Peresopnitsa and Dorogobuzh from me. You wronged me thus, and gave me only Vyshgorod; but I sought none of that, for the sake of the Russian land and for the sake of Christians; moreover I restrained you, but you did not listen to me, and this—I say—is not for me to set right, but for my Lord God. And you also said to me: 'I cannot bow before a younger one'; yet how has Izyaslav, although he twice broke his word, now, having obtained Kiev, bowed to me and laid honor upon me, and seated me in Kiev, and called me father, while I called him son. But if you said, 'I cannot bow before a younger one,' then behold—I am older than you, for you were born when I already had a beard; and do you still wish to contest my seniority? If you have set out to do so, then God is judge of all." And he sent to him with these words; and Yuri at that time was standing at Vasilev.
