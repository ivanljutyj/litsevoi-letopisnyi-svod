And therefore, because you have violated our agreement, we dissolve the peace with you, we break the kiss of the cross, the peace and the treaty.”

Then Prince Semyon Olgerdovich, called Lugveny, dissolved the peace treaty with Veliky Novgorod, saying: “I will side with King Jogaila of Poland and Grand Duke Vytautas Kestutovich of Lithuania. As for the fact that you maintained me, that is good, since I was with you against your enemies, labored and shed blood for you. But since discord has now arisen between my elder brothers—Jogaila, King of Poland, and Grand Duke Vytautas Kestutovich of Lithuania—and you, I too will speak against you, because I am a brother to them. I cannot be separate from them, so do not hold malice against me.” Prince Fyodor Yuryevich of Smolensk, grandson of Svyatoslav, heard that because of him there would be war and bloodshed, and he turned to the Novgorodians, saying: “Brothers and friends, Novgorodians, for the fact that you sheltered me in my misfortune, God will reward you, but since strife, war and bloodshed will now begin because of me, and you will quarrel with Vytautas, then let me go wherever God directs me.” And he departed to the German lands with weeping and bitter tears.

That same winter Vladyka Ioann of Novgorod was in Moscow with His Grace Photius, Metropolitan of Kyiv and All Rus; he had come to him with rich gifts. Then in Moscow the archdeacon of the Novgorod vladyka, Ioakim, died and was buried in the monastery of John Chrysostom outside the walls of Moscow.

That same winter, in the month of March on the 19th day, Sunday, Prince Alexander Ivanovich of Tver again laid the foundation of Goroden, brought many people from Tver and Kashin to this, and the city was quickly built.

That same spring Grand Prince Ivan Mikhailovich of Tver left the Horde, released by the tsar of the Great Horde, Kerim-Berdei, with honors and gifts, and returned to Tver, to his patrimony, to the great reign of Tver, in the month of April on the 9th day, on Sunday morning. Bishop Antony of Tver and all the clergy met him with crosses, honors and great joy.

That same year in Veliky Novgorod they built the stone church of St Nicholas in Porkhov under Prince Yuri.

That same year Vladyka Ioann of Novgorod with the Novgorod voivodes built the Church of the Cathedral of the Holy Archangel Gabriel on Khrevkova Street, and the vladyka himself consecrated it on the patronal feast of the church.
