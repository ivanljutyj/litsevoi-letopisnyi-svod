A multitude of people gathered, coming into the field against the Tatars. And they began to count how many there were in all, and counted more than four hundred thousand of mounted and foot soldiery.

And, rising, the Lithuanian princes Olgerdovich, Prince Andrei and Prince Dmitry, brothers of Jagiello Olgerdovich of Lithuania, began to speak thus: “If we remain here, this Russian host will be weak; but if we cross to the other side of the Don, it will be strong and courageous. For all will despair of their lives, expecting death from hour to hour. And if we overcome the Tatars, glory be to you and to all. If we are slain by them, then we shall all die together in a common death.

And let no one be afraid before their great force: for God is not in power, but in truth, and to whom He wishes to show mercy, to that one He gives help.”

And then many messengers came, reporting the Tatar invasion. Then Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, strengthened in Christ, having prayed to the Lord God, and to the Most Pure Mother of God, and to the great wonderworker Peter, and to all the saints, courageously said to all: “Brothers, an honorable death is better than an evil life; it would have been better not to go against the godless Tatars than to come and, having done nothing, turn back. Let us all now, this day, go beyond the Don and there lay down our heads all for the holy churches and for the Orthodox faith, for our brothers, for Christianity!” And so he ordered each regiment to build bridges across the Don, and themselves to arm in armor for every eventuality.

And they went across the Don; and when all had crossed, they destroyed the bridges behind them.

Then through all the nights wolves howled terribly, and crows and eagles by night and day cawed and screamed, awaiting the terrible, God-permitted day of bloodshed, as it is said: “Where the corpse is, there the eagles will gather.” And then from such fear the heroic hearts of bold men began to strengthen and take courage, while the weak and base began to fear and despair, seeing death before their eyes. And the festal night of the Nativity of the Most Pure Mother of God arrived. Autumn then was long, and the sunny days shone brightly, and there was great warmth. And in that night too there was great warmth and stillness. With the Lithuanian princes was a noble commander and skilled general, very brave, by name Dmitry Bobrok, by birth from the Volhynian land, whom all knew and feared because of his courage. And he came to the Grand Prince, saying thus: “When deep night comes, if you wish, I will show you signs: you will learn beforehand what is to happen in the end.” But the Grand Prince ordered him to tell no one about this.
