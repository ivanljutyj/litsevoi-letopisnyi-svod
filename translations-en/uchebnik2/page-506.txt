Andrey Serkizov, Timofey Vasilyevich Akatyevich, called Valuy, Mikhailo Brenko, Lev Morozov, Taras Shatnev, Semyon Melik, Dmitry Minin, and Sergius's monk Peresvet, whose name was Alexander, formerly a Bryansk boyar; this man was a dashing warrior and a very famous bogatyr, skilled in military affairs and armament. They were all great dashing warriors and bogatyrs, whose names have already been written, while of the other princes and boyars, commanders, princelings, boyar sons, servants, and foot soldiers an innumerable multitude was killed; and who can count them or write them down, for their number is beyond measure? Their names are written in the Book of LIFE.

Then Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich began to say to all: "My lords, and fathers, and brothers, and sons! I give you thanks and thank you for having labored so much; and it is fitting for you to serve, and for me to reward you according to your worth. When the Lord God grants me to be in my patrimony, in the great reign at Moscow, then I will honor and bestow gifts upon you; but now let each strive, as much as he can, to bury our brothers, Orthodox Christians, killed by the Tatars in this place."

And the Grand Prince stood beyond the Don at that place for eight days, until the Christians could be distinguished from the impious Tatars; as many as they could and had time for—concerning the rest God knows, for so it was accomplished by God's judgments. For the sake of our sins the Lord God allowed such a calamity, but for the sake of His mercy He at last showed mercy through the prayers of His most pure Mother and the great wonderworker Peter, and by His invisible divine power the Ishmaelites were defeated.

Then Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich said: "Count, brothers, how many of us all remain." And they counted, and Mikhailo Alexandrovich, a Moscow boyar, said: "My lord prince, there remain of us all 40,000, but there were in all more than four hundred thousand, both horse and foot troops." The Grand Prince said: "The will of the Lord be done! As it was pleasing to the Lord, so it has come to pass. For who will resist His will? Or who will speak against the Lord? By His will and desire all things are ordered."

And the Grand Prince commanded the priests to sing funeral hymns over the slain, and they buried them, as many as they could and had time for, and the priests sang eternal memory to all Orthodox Christians slain by the Tatars on Kulikovo Field, between the Don and the Mecha. Also the Grand Prince himself, with his brother and with all the remaining army, cried out with a great voice eternal memory to them, with weeping and many tears. And again the Grand Prince himself said: "May eternal memory be to you all, brothers and friends, Orthodox Christians, who suffered for the Orthodox faith and for all Christianity on Kulikovo Field, between the Don and the Mecha! This place is appointed to you by God! Forgive me and bless me in this."
