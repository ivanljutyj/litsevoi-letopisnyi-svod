And the troops came together, and Prince Daniel, son of Roman Mstislavovich, rode at the front, and Prince Semyon Olyuevich, and Prince Vasilko Gavrilovich. And there Prince Vasilko was stabbed, and Prince Daniel, son of Roman Mstislavovich, was stabbed in the chest; and blood poured out; he galloped on, feeling nothing in his frenzy; he was very courageous and strong in battle; and still young, only eighteen years old; and he killed Tatars courageously with his regiment.

Prince Mstislav the Dumb saw the bloodied Prince Daniel, son of Roman Mstislavovich, that he had been pierced and blood was pouring out, and yet he galloped on; and Mstislav wept and cried out in a loud voice; for Mstislav too was very strong, and he was a kinsman of Daniel's father, and loved him greatly, and promised Daniel his principality after himself. Also Prince Oleg of Kursk fought fiercely with the Tatars.

But then the Cumans quickly fled and trampled the camps of the Russian princes, and the princes did not have time to arm themselves, and the Tatars came with a great army, and then there was confusion in the Russian regiments.

And there was a fierce slaughter because of our sins; the dead lay everywhere, and blood poured like water; and there was a defeat of the Russian princes such as had not been since the founding of the Russian land.

Grand Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Kiev, grandson of Rostislav, and Prince Andrew, his son-in-law, and Prince Alexander Dubrovsky saw this disaster and did not move anywhere; they stood by the Kalka River; the place was stony.

And these princes made a fortification for themselves with a palisade, and defended themselves from behind it for three days.

And there remained there two Tatar commanders, Cherkak and Tekshak, while the other Tatar princes and all the Tatars pursued the Russian princes and drove them, killing them, as far as the Dnieper. With the Tatars there were also warriors of a special tribe, the Brodniki, and their commander was Ploskynia.

And that accursed commander kissed the cross and promised Grand Prince Mstislav Romanovich of Kiev and those with him that he would not kill them but would take a ransom for them; and on such terms the princes surrendered to Ploskynia; but he, accursed, handed them over to the Tatars, and took the fortification, and cut down all the people.

And the Tatars laid the princes under boards and sat on top of them to dine, and so the princes suffocated.

And other princes they killed, chasing them as far as the Dnieper; six princes: Prince Svyatoslav of Kanev, Prince Izyaslav Ingvarevich, Prince Svyatoslav of Shumsk, Prince Mstislav of Chernigov with his son, Prince Yuri Neversky, and many thousand-commanders, commanders, and boyars; and so many fighting men were killed that barely one in ten of them was able to escape.
