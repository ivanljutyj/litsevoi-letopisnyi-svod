And they sent to Suzdal for Prince Rostislav, son of Yuri, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-great-grandson of the great Vladimir. Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh sent from Suzdal to Novgorod his son Rostislav to reign over them.

In that same year, on the twenty-first day of May, Prince Rostislav, son of Yuri, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, entered Novgorod and took the throne; and there was great joy for all in Novgorod, and they made peace with the Pskovians.

In that same year the Chernigov princes, the Olgovichi, summoned the Polovtsians to their aid and began to wage war along the Sula; Prince Andrei Vladimirovich Monomakh could not resist them, and there was no help for him from his brother Yaropolk, Grand Prince of Kiev; and he wished to flee from Pereyaslavl. The Olgovichi learned that Prince Andrei had help from nowhere, and then the Olgovichi ceased to worry.

Prince Andrei secretly sent word to his brother Yaropolk, Grand Prince of Kiev; and Grand Prince Yaropolk began to gather a great host from everywhere.

In that same year they took away the treasury from Prince Svyatoslav, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, who had fled from Novgorod; and because of that, from then on the Olgovichi began to wage war even more fiercely, and brought a multitude of Polovtsians, and took Priluki, and took many captives, and wanted to march against Grand Prince Yaropolk to Kiev; and they learned that the Grand Prince of Kiev, Yaropolk, was coming against them. The Olgovichi went to Chernigov, for with Grand Prince Yaropolk was a multitude of troops: Hungarians, Czechs, Poles, Kievans, Pereyaslavlians, Vladimirians with all the Volynians, Turovians, Drevlians, Polotskians, Smolenskians, Galicians, and Rostovians, and in addition to that there were forty thousand Berendeys—a countless multitude of troops.

The Chernigov princes, the Olgovichi, wished to flee to the Polovtsian land; and the bishop and the people of Chernigov cried out to them, saying: “If you flee to the Polovtsians, you will lose your authority and, having done so, may not return; for it is written: ‘God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.’ Therefore cast off from yourselves pride and presumption, and receive great help in the Lord through humility, that you may receive mercy from God.
