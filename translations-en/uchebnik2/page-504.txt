In that same year there was a great revolt in Lithuania: they rose up and killed Grand Prince Kestutis Gediminovich, and beat his boyars; for Kestutis had taken the grand principality of Lithuania, and therefore there was revolt and bloodshed among them.

And then Kestutis’s son, Prince Vytautas, fled to the Germans, and from there he did much evil to the Lithuanian land. In that same year Prince Jogaila of Lithuania married. This tale was written in the fifth year of this decade.

In the year 6890 from Adam (1390 from Christ)

There came from Constantinople to Novgorod the Great Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, from His Eminence Nilus, Ecumenical Patriarch, with a blessing and with letters, in which was written concerning the expenses for ordinations, and teachings of the law of God according to the sacred canons and the divine writings, strengthening against temptations and against the heresy of the Strigolniki; other teachings he ordered Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, to speak orally in his stead, in the presence of Alexius, Archbishop of Novgorod. So he did in Novgorod and in Pskov, and he stopped the disturbances and temptations concerning the expenses for ordinations: for one thing is a fee, and another are the expenses and costs for ordinations. And so he delivered to them the letters from Nilus, Ecumenical Patriarch, concerning this, and, having taught sufficiently according to the patriarch’s command, Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, went from Novgorod and from Pskov to Suzdal.

And he came to Suzdal to his throne on the 6th day of January. He had received the archbishopric from His Eminence Patriarch Nilus, and the Ecumenical Patriarch with the whole sacred council blessed him, and ordered that he be called and be archbishop in Suzdal, and in Nizhny Novgorod, and in Gorodets, and that after him archbishops should remain in those affairs and ranks. Nilus, Ecumenical Patriarch, with the sacred council, also gave him sacred vestments with four crosses, and gave him a sticharion with springs; for this is a sign of the great metropolitan rank—a phelonion with crosses and a sticharion with springs; and “metropolitan” is interpreted as mother of cities and head of the eparchy that is under him. Dionysius, Bishop of Suzdal, also brought with him from Constantinople the Passion of the Savior, and relics of many saints, and crosses, and miracle-working icons with relics.

In that same year Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Kashin died and was laid in the cathedral church of the Resurrection, in his patrimony, in the city of Kashin, on the 6th day of May.

That same summer a son, Prince Andrew, was born to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, on the 14th day of August, and Theodore, abbot of Simonov, baptized him.
