Blessing from God and to all his seed forever. My son cared for me so much that may the Lord God repay him a hundredfold in this world, and may he inherit eternal life. And may his descendants not fail, who possess this place, and may his memory not be forgotten. And all that he had he handed over to him, bequeathing it to be spent on the church, and, having blessed everyone, he began to sing vespers. And prayer was still on his lips, but his soul had already departed from his body. And, raising his hands to heaven, the Most Reverend Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, great wonderworker, departed this life on the 21st day of December, at the third hour of the night. He sat on the metropolitan throne and shepherded the Church of God for 18 years and 6 months. He left his body on earth, while his soul flew up to heaven to the desired Christ. And he was buried in the city of Moscow, in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, which he himself had begun to build with his son, Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich, and where he had with his own hands laid a grave for himself. Then, with God’s help and through the prayers of the Most Pure Mother of God and of the saint, the blessed Metropolitan Peter, three people afflicted with various ailments were healed. At his burial were Prince Ivan Danilovich of Moscow and Bishop Feodosius of Lutsk. And Prince Ivan Danilovich and Bishop Feodosius wept much for him, as did the whole assembly of priests and monks, the boyars, and a multitude of people, recalling his soul-profitable words and teachings. He was laid near the holy altar, in the wall, in a new stone coffin, granting many healings to all who came to him in faith.

In the year 6835 from Adam (the year 1335 from Christ)

There came to Tver from the Horde a noble envoy, Tsarevich Shchelkan Dyudenievich, from Tsar Azbyak. That tsarevich was the brother of Tsar Azbyak. And he wished to kill the Tver princes, to take the princely throne in Tver himself, and he wished to install his own Tatar princes in the Russian cities and to convert Christians to the Tatar faith. He stayed in Tver only a few days, but wrought much evil upon Christians.

And Easter arrived, and he wished to hold his own feast. Seeing this, Grand Prince Alexander Mikhailovich, grandson of Yaroslav, summoned the Tverites, and having armed themselves, they attacked him. And Shchelkan Dyudenievich with his Tatars came out against him.

They met at sunrise and fought all day, and by evening Alexander prevailed.

Shchelkan Dyudenievich fled into the palace, but the palace was set on fire beneath him, and the whole court of Prince Mikhail, Alexander’s father, and there Shchelkan burned with all his Tatars. And the Horde guests, old and new, who had come with Shchelkan Dyudenievich, although they had not fought, were all cut down; some were drowned, and others were stacked like firewood and burned in bonfires.
