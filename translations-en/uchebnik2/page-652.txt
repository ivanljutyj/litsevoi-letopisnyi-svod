If you do not agree to this, take my city for yourself; let it be you who rules it, and not impious Lithuania.” Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, knowing Vitovt’s evil disposition, did not intercede for Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk, since he remained in peace and love with his father-in-law Vitovt by treaty and kiss of the cross.

Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk was still in Moscow, while the traitors, the Smolensk boyars, formed a conspiracy and secretly sent to Vitovt, saying: “Come quickly, before Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk returns from Moscow with the great army of your Muscovite son-in-law Vasily Dmitrievich. He has gone to him to ask for help, and tidings reach us that he will receive not only the Muscovite army but help from many other lands as well, and is preparing to march against you. Consider what will become of you then; therefore make haste to us, and we will open the city gates to you, for we love you and wish to see you ruling the Grand Principality of Lithuania and all of us.” Vitovt, hearing such words, rejoiced greatly.

And having gathered an army, he soon came to Smolensk. The traitor boyars of Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk opened the city of Smolensk to Vitovt. He, entering the city, took captive the grand princess, the wife of Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich, daughter of Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan. He took princes and Smolensk boyars captive and sent them to Lithuania, and he put some to death, those who opposed him. In Smolensk he installed Poles as his governors and ordered them to keep the city in obedience, but to grant some leniency to the people of Smolensk, in order to turn them away from Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk, so that the whole land of Smolensk might come to love and desire Vitovt, and renounce Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk.
