To his body, although a countless multitude of beasts was there, God preserved it, for it is written: “The Lord keeps all their bones; not one of them shall be broken.” For the death of sinners is cruel, as it came upon the wretched Kavgady; not surviving a single year, the wretched man evilly gave up his life. Many faithful and unbelieving then saw two clouds overshadowing the body of the blessed Prince Michael, parting and coming together, shining like the sun, which they confessed to us with tears and with many oaths.

They sent the body from there to Mozhcharyk, and the Russian merchants who were there wished to place it in a church, covering it with a shroud; but they were not allowed, and it was placed in a stable under guard. Then those living there saw at night a pillar of fire from earth to heaven, and others saw what seemed like a heavenly rainbow bending down to the stable in which the body lay.

From there they carried it to the city of Bezdezh, and when they were near the city, many in the city saw around the sleigh a multitude of people with candles, and others on horses with lanterns riding through the air; and so they brought it into the city.

And they did not place it in a church but in a courtyard, and two of those guarding it lay down on the sleigh over his body. And great fear fell upon them, and by an unknown force they were thrown from the sleigh and cast far away, and barely rose and came to their senses; and going, they confessed everything that had happened to the priests who were there; and from them, having heard of this, he wrote it all down reliably.

From there they carried it through the Russian cities, and so little by little they brought it to Moscow, and brought it into the city of Moscow and laid it in a monastery, in the Church of the Holy Transfiguration; but in Tver they did not know of this.

In the year 6828 from Adam (the year 1328 from Christ)

Grand Prince Yuri Danilovich of Moscow came to the grand principality and brought with him Prince Konstantin Mikhailovich, grandson of Yaroslav, and his boyars and servants, leading them with him as if captives, and the report spread throughout all the land.

And his sons met him in boats on the Volga: Dmitry, Alexander, Vasily, and Grand Princess Anna, their mother, and Bishop Varsanofy, and the whole priestly order with candles and lamps, and a multitude of people; they met him at Archangel Michael on the Bank, and laid him in the Church of the Holy Savior, on the sixth day of September. His body was whole and unharmed by anything, carried from distant lands on a cart and on a sleigh; and in Moscow, however long it lay, it did not decay, but remained all whole and unharmed.
