And having assembled, they halted at Pashkova Gat, stood there several days, began negotiations among themselves, concluded a truce for that year, and then dispersed.

In the same year, on the 16th day of the month of September, the most reverend Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, departed this life, having been metropolitan for thirty years less one sixth of a month. And news of his death spread; bishops, priests, and monks gathered and prepared his body according to the rule of the holy fathers.

And princes, boyars, commanders, and a multitude of people quickly gathered and carried his body to Moscow, to the metropolitan cathedral church of the Most Pure Theotokos, where lies the tomb of the great wonderworker Metropolitan Peter and of the blessed Metropolitan Theognostus.

And the consecrated divine assembly performed the service over his body: Gregory, Archbishop of Rostov; Mitrofan, Bishop of Suzdal; Hilarion, Bishop of Kolomna, together with archimandrites, abbots, and the whole consecrated assembly, sang funeral hymns over his body.

And they laid him, according to his direction, in the metropolitan cathedral church of the Dormition of the Most Pure Theotokos in Moscow, on the right side by the wall. The first metropolitan in Moscow was Peter the Wonderworker; he was buried in this cathedral church according to his will. 2. Theognostus the Greek, also buried in this church according to his will; 3. Alexis the Wonderworker, buried in the monastery of the Holy Miracle of the Archangel Michael in Moscow, which he himself founded. The fourth was Metropolitan Cyprian, a Serb by birth, a man filled with love of wisdom and divine understanding, learned and spiritual, known for his virtuous life, who taught all church doctrine and learning; there was in him much diligence and ability to teach everyone continually in the word of God, and not to idle in discussing vain things and conversing with ungodly people. He lived a virtuous life to the very end of old age and fell gravely ill in the metropolitan village of Golyanishche, passing his life quietly and serenely, where his private church in the name of the Three Holy Hierarchs was situated, where he himself loved often to be and to live, since that place was quiet, silent, free from all vanity; it was situated between the two rivers Setun and Ramenka on a spit of land and above a pond, and there was much forest around.
