In the year 6634 from Adam (the year 1134 from Christ)

Nikita, metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed Mark, abbot and priest of the Church of John the Forerunner, bishop of Pereyaslavl, on the fourth day of October. In the same year Nikita, metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, reposed, on the ninth day of March. In the same year the princess, wife of Vladimir Monomakh, reposed, on the eleventh day of June.

In the same year the earth shook, on the first day of August, at the eighth hour of the night. In the same year in Novgorod the office of posadnik was given to Miroslav Goryatinich.

In the year 6635 from Adam (the year 1135 from Christ)

Prince Bryachislav, youngest son of Sviatopolk, reposed on the twenty-eighth day of March, and was buried on the fifth day of April.

In the same year Davyd Olegovich drove his uncle Yaroslav out of Chernigov, and cut down and plundered his druzhina. Mstislav and Yaropolk joined their armies, wishing to march against Vsevolod on account of Yaroslav.

Vsevolod sent for the Cumans, and seventeen thousand of them came with Seluk and Tash; and they stood at Ratmirova oak grove beyond Vyrem. Their envoys were sent to Vsevolod, but they were not allowed back. Izaslav Yaropolchich was sitting in Vyresk, and he captured their envoys on the Kn and brought them to Yaropolk. And the Cumans, having long had no news from Olegovich, being vexed, fled.

Mstislav, pressing harder and harder upon Vsevolod, said to him: “You brought in the Cumans and accomplished nothing.” But he prayed more and more, sent boyars, and gave gifts; and so the whole summer passed until winter.

Then Yaroslav came from Murom and bowed to Mstislav, saying: “You have kissed the cross; let us go against Vsevolod.” Vsevolod began to entreat Mstislav even more, but did not succeed because of the cross-kissing. In those days there was Gregory, abbot of the monastery of St Andrew, who was loved by Vladimir and honored by Mstislav and all the people. He did not allow anyone to deliberate about war on behalf of Yaroslav, saying: “The sin will be on you if you break the cross-kissing; but this is no more sinful than the shedding of Christian blood.”

And he gathered the whole council of priests, for there was no metropolitan at that time, and they said to Mstislav: “That sin will be on us.” Mstislav did their will, broke the cross-kissing to Yaroslav, and wept over it all the days of his life; and Yaroslav returned to Murom.
