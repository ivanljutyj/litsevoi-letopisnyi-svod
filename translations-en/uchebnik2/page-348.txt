"Lord, the Bryansk people will not let me go; they want to lay down their heads for me," and so he did not heed Metropolitan Peter; for as it is written: "A disobedient son will soon receive vengeance." And also it is said: "Let not the mighty man boast in his strength, for all who trusted in their strength have perished."

And so Prince Svyatoslav Glebovich with a great host, in great strength, went out in the afternoon against the Tatar host and joined battle with them; and the Tatar arrows darkened the air and were like rain, and they fought also with spears and sabers, and the battle was fierce. But the Bryansk people betrayed Prince Svyatoslav Glebovich of Bryansk, being traitors, and cast down their banners and fled. Prince Svyatoslav with only his own retinue fought long against the Tatars and in the end was killed in battle. Metropolitan Peter shut himself in the church then and remained there; God preserved him from the pagan Tatars, as it is written: "I will deliver my chosen one from cruel weapons, and I will overshadow his head in the day of battle." In that same year the former archbishop of Novgorod, Feoktist, died in Novgorod, having suffered much from an illness sent by God for his salvation, in the Monastery of the Annunciation of the Most Pure Mother of God; and he was laid there.

In that same year a son, Feodor, was born to Prince Vasily Konstantinovich of Galich, grandson of Boris and great-grandson of Vasilko.

In that same year Prince Vasily of Bryansk went with the Tatars to Karachev and killed Prince Svyatoslav Mstislavich of Karachev.

In the year 6819 from Adam (the year 1319 from Christ)

Prince Dmitry Mikhailovich of Tver, having gathered many troops, wanted to go to Nizhny Novgorod, and Metropolitan Peter did not bless him with the throne in Vladimir. He gathered with great force and petitioned Metropolitan Peter to absolve him of his sins. And so he dismissed his army, and each went his own way.

In that same year Simeon, bishop of Rostov, who had formerly been of Vladimir and whom the most reverend Maximus, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, had brought from Vladimir to Rostov, left his bishopric of Rostov.

In that same year the most reverend Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed Prokhor, archimandrite of the Savior monastery in Yaroslavl, bishop of Rostov.

In that same year there was a fire in Novgorod, and many holy churches, courtyards, and people burned.

In the year 6820 from Adam (the year 1320 from Christ)

Grand Princess Oksinia, wife of Yaroslav Yaroslavich, died, in monastic rank and in the schema, and was laid in Tver.
