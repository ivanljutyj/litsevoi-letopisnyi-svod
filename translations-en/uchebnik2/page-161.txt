Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich gave the city of Torchesk to his son-in-law Rostislav Rurikovich, and in the other cities he installed his own governors.

When Roman heard that Vsevolod had taken his volost from Rurik and given Torchesk to his brother-in-law Rostislav, he began sending envoys to his father-in-law Rurik, complaining to him about how they had dealt with him in the matter of volosts; and, thinking that he had come to an agreement with Vsevolod, he took a volost from him for his son, and began to justify himself to his father-in-law and to remind him of the cross-kissing.

Rurik answered him: “I was the first to give you that volost, but Vsevolod began sending envoys to me, angry that a part had not been allotted to him before others; I conveyed all his words to you, and you gave me those volosts of your own will. And how could we not give them to him, since we cannot do without him? For they recognize his seniority among all the brothers of the line of Vladimir, and you are my own son; and here is another volost for you, equal to that one.” Roman did not trouble himself about that volost, but tried to find a pretext for accusing his father-in-law, not wishing to remain at peace with him.

And he began sending envoys to the Olgovichs, to Yaroslav Vsevolodovich in Chernigov; he kissed the cross to him, wishing to lead him to Kiev against his father-in-law.

Rurik heard that Roman had gone over to the Olgovichs and was leading Yaroslav to seniority, and he was grieved, saying: “O truly true are the words of God: a man’s enemies are his own household!” Rurik began to consult with his brothers and counselors and sent to Vsevolod, telling him that Roman had joined with the Olgovichs and was leading them to Kiev against the whole line of Vladimir: “You, my brother, are the eldest in all the line of Vladimir; say what you think about the Russian land, about your honor and ours?” And to his son-in-law Roman he sent, rebuked him, and threw the cross-charters before him.

Roman feared his father-in-law, broke the treaty with him sealed by cross-charters, and went for help to Poland to the Kazimirovichs.

The Kazimirovichs said to him: “We would gladly help you, but our uncle Mezhko will be offended with us; he strives to take volosts from us, to rule over us, so that all the Poles may not be separate, but all with you behind one shield, and may avenge your wrongs.” Roman liked their advice; he listened to them and went against Mezhko with his nephews the Kazimirovichs. Roman said to his counselors: “If God helps me fight them, then, gathering them all in one place, I will not lose my honor and will fulfill my desire”; and they went to fight with Mezhko.

Mezhko sent envoys out to them, not wishing to fight with them; he sent to Roman to reconcile him with his nephews.
