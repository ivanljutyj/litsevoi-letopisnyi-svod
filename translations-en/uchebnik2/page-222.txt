The accursed murderers came and began to cut down mercilessly. And the accursed princes Gleb and Konstantin themselves, taking swords, began to cut down the princes and their boyars, and all their retinue, and they killed five brothers, and the sixth, their own brother, Prince Izyaslav Vladimirovich. But Prince Ingvar Igorevich, their nephew, did not manage to come to that evil feast; his time had not come. Thus God protected him and saved him from a cruel death. They killed countless other boyars and courtiers, and they themselves fled to the Polovtsians. This evil was done on the twentieth day of the month of July, on the feast of the Holy Prophet Elijah.

And Prince Ingvar Igorevich took his seat in Ryazan.

And Prince Mstislav Mstislavich went to Kiev, leaving his princess and his son Vasily in Novgorod, and he took with him Yuri Ivanovich, Buslav Stefanovich, and Alexei Putilovich.

On the Love of the Brothers Konstantin and Yuri Vsevolodovich

In that same year Konstantin Vsevolodovich sent to his brother Georgy at Gorodets, summoning him to him in Vladimir. He came to him with Bishop Simon and with his boyars.

Konstantin made an agreement with him: “After my death, Vladimir shall be yours; but today go to Suzdal.” He made him swear an oath and, having given him many gifts, sent him to Suzdal. And he came there on September 11, and Bishop Simon came with him to his episcopal see.

On Vladimir Vsevolodovich

That same winter Vladimir Vsevolodovich came from the Polovtsian land to his brothers; and they gave him Starodub and another small district. In that same year Prince Vladimir came to Novgorod on his own business.

And the Lithuanians waged war on the Shelon; the Novgorodians went in pursuit of them, but did not overtake them, and went with Prince Vladimir to Medvezhya Golova. The inhabitants of the town began to petition with bows, deceitfully, because they themselves had sent to the Germans.

The Novgorodians learned of their deceit and fell upon their camps without warning; and they fled to their town. The Novgorodians killed two of their commanders and took the third captive, and seized seven hundred horses.

And the Novgorodians returned home with a great victory.

And Prince Mstislav Mstislavich came to Novgorod from Kiev without them, and seized Stanimir with his son, and, having bound them in chains, put them in prison.

He seized much property and wealth from him and then released him.

In that same year the Trade Side in Novgorod burned, and fifteen churches burned down.
