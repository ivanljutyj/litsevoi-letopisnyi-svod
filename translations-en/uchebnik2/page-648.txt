In the year 6910 from Adam (1410 from Christ)

In September Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich sent his commanders Ivan Andreevich Uda and Feodor Glebovich with his army to search for Prince Semyon Dmitrievich of Suzdal, so that they might find either him himself, or his princess, or his children, or his boyars hiding in the Tatar lands.

And they came into the Mordvin land and found Princess Alexandra, wife of Prince Semyon Dmitrievich, in the Tatar land in a place called Tsybirtse, in the church of St Nicholas; this church had been built by the Besermenin Khazi-baba. And there they captured Princess Alexandra, wife of Semyon Dmitrievich. And having plundered her, they brought her to Moscow together with her children.

And she remained in confinement in the Beleutov courtyard until Prince Semyon Dmitrievich submitted to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, since he was then hiding in the Horde lands from Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow. But when he heard that his princess with her children had been captured and that his treasury had been seized, he sent a petition to the grand prince, submitting to him with contrition and humility and asking for a safe-conduct.

Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow gave him a safe-conduct, and he came from the Horde to Moscow and made peace with the grand prince. And he went from Moscow to Vyatka with his princess and children, being already ill.

And he remained in Vyatka for 5 months, and becoming still more ill, he died on the 21st day of December. This Prince Semyon Dmitrievich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod endured many misfortunes and troubles in the Horde and in Rus; he labored striving to obtain his patrimony, and for 8 years tirelessly served four tsars in the Horde: the first, Tokhtamysh; the second, Timur Aksak; the third, Temir-Kutluy; the fourth, Shadibek, all the while warring with Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow, wishing to regain his patrimony, the principality of Nizhny Novgorod and Suzdal and Gorodets. For this he labored much, endured many misfortunes and troubles, had no shelter of his own, found no support under his feet, but achieved nothing, for he labored in vain; vain is man’s salvation, since all things are possible to God, but nothing to man.

In the same autumn Grand Prince Vytautas Keistutovich of Lithuania set out with an army to Smolensk against Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk, stood before Smolensk for many days, but did not take it. And after concluding a truce, he returned; and in Smolensk unrest began, and many troublemakers were killed. Then there was also pestilence in Smolensk among the people.
