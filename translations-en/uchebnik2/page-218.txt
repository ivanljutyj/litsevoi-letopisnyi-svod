Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk placed his regiment on the flank, and next beyond him Prince Mstislav Mstislavich of Novgorod, and Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich Romanovich with the Novgorodians, and Prince Vladimir with the Pskovians, and next beyond him Grand Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov with his regiments.

And against Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk stood Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich with his regiments, and against Prince Mstislav Mstislavich and all the Novgorodians stood Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich himself with his regiments, and the younger brothers of Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich stood against Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov.

And the regiments began to close with one another, and a great host gathered, and there was fear and horror in all, because one kin and one tribe were shedding blood for nothing, and many who had the fear of God in them wept. And at first that day was sunny and hot. And then a wind rose, a cloud came, thunder struck without ceasing, terrible lightning, and fear fell upon all, and many stood without advancing on one another and without desiring peace, for they had become savage as beasts. Then Prince Mstislav Mstislavich began to speak to all his Novgorodians and Smolensk men thus: “Brothers, we have come into a strong, foreign land; no one will give us help except God and righteousness. Let us stand courageously and firmly and not look back, let us not think of fleeing. For if we flee, we shall not escape, for death and life belong to us all; let us die for one another in our righteousness, and God will be with us. Let us forget home, wives, children, and all our possessions, and let us go to the task zealously, both foot and horse.”

The Novgorodians answered him: “We, lord prince, those on horses are on horses, and those on foot are on foot; all are ready with all our heart and all firmness.” And the footmen went, and with them they sent the voivode Prince Vladimir Ivor and many others, and the Novgorodians dismounted and went on foot, because they had to go through the thickets.

And when they came into the thickets, the horse under Ivor stumbled, but the footmen went through the thickets with shouts, not waiting for Ivor, and attacked Yaroslav’s footmen with axes and javelins, and they opposed them likewise, and there was a fierce battle, and they cut down the banner of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, and then Ivor arrived with mounted Smolensk men, and they broke through to another banner.

Seeing this, Prince Mstislav Mstislavich said to Vladimir: “You see, brothers, these good men, how zealously they do the work; let us too go to their aid.”
