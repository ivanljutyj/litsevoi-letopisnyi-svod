And the Novgorodians petitioned the Most Reverend Cyril, and he gave them Bishop Dalmat.

In the same year Grand Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich fell ill in Novgorod, and his illness was very grave; but prayers were offered for him to the Lord God, together with His Most Pure Mother, His holy saints, the Most Reverend Cyril, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, Archbishop Dalmat of Novgorod, Bishop Cyril of Rostov, and all the priests, and the ancestors and parents of Prince Alexander.

In the same year there were frequent heavy rains, and there was a great flood, and everything drowned, both grain and hay; and the great bridge in Novgorod on the Volkhov was swept away; and in autumn all the crops perished from frost.

In the same year Prince Gleb Vasilkovich, grandson of Constantine, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, went to Belozersk, to the place that had fallen to him from his father. In the same year there was no war.

Nevryuy's Devastation

In the same year Grand Prince Alexander again went to the Horde to the new tsar Sartak; and he entrusted the glorious city of Vladimir and all the Suzdal land to the keeping of his brother Prince Andrew.

But Andrew, though adorned with nobility and courage, treated the governing of the country as a matter of little importance; he occupied himself with hunting beasts and listened to young and foolish counselors, from whom came very many disorders, impoverishment among the people, and damage to property. Because of this, by the will of God, in the same year Tsar Sartak sent his commander Nevryuy, and Prince Katiak, and Prince Alybuga the Brave, and other Tatars with them, to war against Grand Prince Andrew Yaroslavovich of Suzdal, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky.

It was before the day of commemoration of Saints Boris and Gleb; the godless Tatars crossed the Klyazma River by ford near Vladimir and went secretly toward the city of Pereyaslavl.

In the morning, on the day of commemoration of Saints Boris and Gleb, Grand Prince Andrew of Suzdal was in turmoil and said: “Lord, what is this! How long are we to dispute among ourselves and bring Tatars against one another! It is better for me to flee to a foreign land than to keep friendship with and serve the Tatars.” And Grand Prince Andrew gathered his troops, and he met the Tatars with his regiments; and both armies joined battle, and there was a great slaughter. But God was angered against us for the multiplication of our sins, and the pagans prevailed, and Grand Prince Andrew barely escaped.

And Prince Andrew came to Veliky Novgorod, but the Novgorodians did not receive him; he went on to Pskov and stayed there a short time, waiting for his princess.
