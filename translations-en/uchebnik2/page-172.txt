They brought him onto the ship and put him in a barrel that had three bottoms at one end, behind which Isaakovich sat, and at the other end water, where the plug was, since he could not leave the city by any other way; and so he departed from the Greek land.

The tsar learned of this and sent to search for him; they began searching for him in many places. They entered the ship where Isaakovich was, searched everything, and pulled the plug from that barrel, but seeing the water flowing out, they went back having found nothing.

And so Isaakovich departed and came to the German king Philip, his brother-in-law, and to his sister.

The German king sent to the Pope of Rome and began urging him that they should not go to war against Tsargrad, although Isaakovich asserted: “All Constantinople desires my rule.”

The pope, however, sent him away, saying: “If they want him, seat him on the throne and go to Jerusalem to help; if they do not want him, bring him back to me, but do not cause harm to the Greek land.”

But the Latins and all their commanders wanted to obtain the gold and silver that Isaakovich had promised them, and they forgot the instructions of the king and the pope not to cause evil to the Greek land; and first, arriving at the Sud, they broke the iron chains and advanced on the city, having set fire to churches in four places. And then Tsar Alexios saw the fire, did not go out to battle with them, but summoned his brother Isaac, whom he had blinded, and seated him on the throne, saying: “For what I did to you, brother, forgive me; this is your kingdom,” and he himself fled from the city.

And the whole city was burned, and the indescribable churches adorned with beauty—we cannot name their number—and the vestibule of Holy Sophia burned, where all the patriarchs are listed, and the hippodrome, and all the buildings located as far as the sea and as far as the tsar’s court, and as far as the Sud, burned.

And then Isaakovich, together with the Latins, pursued Tsar Alexios, but not catching him, they returned to the city.

And he drove his father from the throne and himself became tsar, and said to his father: “You are blind; how can you rule the kingdom? I am tsar.”

Then Tsar Isaac grieved much over the city and his kingdom and the plundering of the monasteries. He gave the Latins the promised gold and silver, fell gravely ill, took monastic vows, and departed from this world.
