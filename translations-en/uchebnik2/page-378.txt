Ivan, lover of God—or better, lover of monks and pilgrims—and fervent in faith, transferred the archimandrite’s seat from there to the city of Moscow and established it near himself, desiring always to see it and rejoice in it. And he truly raised up a divine treasure near himself, and a place of prayer for the whole world! He laid such a good and Christ-loving beginning and foundation that his children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren, following him and doing likewise, have the same reward and glory from generation to generation, as a pledge from God and His Most Pure Mother—from a good root a noble shoot grows.

In the year 6839 from Adam (the year 1339 from Christ)

There was a sign in the heavens: the sun darkened. And in that year there was a fire in Moscow; the whole city of Moscow burned.

In that year the grand princess Elena, widow of Ivan Danilovich, a schema-nun, reposed, and was laid to rest in the church of the holy Transfiguration of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ. In that year Vasily, designated for the Vladyka’s see in Novgorod, lived at the Vladyka’s court, but had not yet been installed as archbishop, and he founded a stone wall from the church of Saint Vladimir to the church of the Mother of God and to the church of Boris and Gleb.

In that year Prince Fyodor Vasilyevich reposed, on the 28th day of March. In the same year envoys came to Novgorod from Vladimir-in-Volhynia from Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, summoning the monk Vasily—formerly the priest Gregory at the church of Cosmas and Damian—who was designated to become the Vladyka of Novgorod, to come for consecration. They took him and tonsured him into the monastic order at the Vladyka’s court, and confirmed him as Vladyka of Novgorod.

And the monk Vasily set out from the Vladyka’s court for installation as Archbishop of Novgorod to Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, in the land of Volhynia, and with him the Novgorod posadniks Kozma Tverdislavovich and Efrem Astafyev, son of the tysyatsky. And when they were traveling through the Lithuanian land, Gediminas of Lithuania seized them, although they were not at war. And they, being in captivity, gave their sworn word to his son Narimantas that the Novgorod dependencies—Ladoga, Oreshek town, the Karelian land, and half of Kaporye—would be given to him and his children as patrimony and hereditary possession.

And with that they released them. They went from there to Vladimir in Volhynia. And when they came to the land of Volhynia, to the city of Vladimir, to Theognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, there in the church of the Most Pure Mother of God the monk Vasily was installed as Bishop of Novgorod, on the 25th day of August.
