Then all the princes gathered in Nizhny Novgorod to Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich, lamenting their principalities.

In the same year, Grand Prince Vasily Mikhailovich of Tver went with an army to Mikulin against his nephew Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich, but they reconciled again and concluded a peace treaty between themselves.

In the same year, Lithuania conquered Korsheva. In the same year, Grand Prince Olgerd Gediminovich of Lithuania conquered the Blue Water and Beloberezhye.

In the same year, Prince Vasily Vasilyevich of Kashin died, grandson of Mikhail, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, forefather of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-great-forefather of Vladimir Monomakh, and great-great-great-forefather of Vsevolod, Yaroslav, and Vladimir.

In the same year, a miracle occurred in Nizhny Novgorod. After the liturgy in the church, Bishop Alexius of Suzdal blessed with a cross Grand Prince Andrei Konstantinovich of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod and Gorodets, and in that same hour myrrh flowed from the cross, and the people marveled at this.

In the year 6872 from Adam (1372 from Christ)

There was a great pestilence in Nizhny Novgorod and in all its lands, and on the Sara and on the Kisha, and throughout all the surrounding parts. People coughed blood, and others suffered from plague for one day or two, or three, and after living a little while, they died by day and by night. The living could not bury the dead, for in a day fifty or a hundred people and more died.

In the same year there was a strong thunderstorm, and a thunderbolt struck the cathedral church in Tver, and there were many signs with terrible lightning and whirlwinds.

In the same year, Princess Anastasia came from Lithuania with her granddaughter, the daughter of Olgerd, and baptized her in Tver. And because of this baptism, the most reverend Metropolitan Alexius came to Tver.

In the same year there was a pestilence in Pereyaslavl. The disease was like this: first, as if struck with a spear behind the shoulder blade or under the chest, opposite the heart, or between the shoulders. And, having fallen so ill, a person began to cough blood, fire kindled in the body, and then sweats with chills began. And after lying ill for one or two days—and rarely did anyone lie ill for three—the person died. And if they suffered from plague, each person’s illness ran differently: one had a plague bubo on the neck, another on the thigh or in the bosom, or under the cheekbone, or behind the shoulder blade. And sometimes seventy people died in a day, sometimes a hundred or a hundred and fifty. Not only in Pereyaslavl was it so, but in all the districts, villages, and monasteries.
