That same autumn Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver, with his son Prince Alexander, went to Tsar Tokhtamysh in the Horde with honor and gifts; but they went not by direct ways but by roundabout paths, and not along the roads, fearing and hiding from Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich, seeking for themselves the grand principality of Vladimir and Novgorod.

That same autumn Prince Boris Konstantinovich of Gorodets, brother of Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich of Nizhny Novgorod and Suzdal, went to the Horde from his Gorodok to Tsar Tokhtamysh with honor and gifts.

That same autumn Cyprian, metropolitan of all Rus, was in Tver and there waited out the Tatar invasion; and Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent two of his boyars, Semyon Timofeevich and Mikhail Morozov, to summon him to Moscow.

He set out from Tver for Moscow on the 3rd day of the month of October, and came to Moscow on the 7th day of the same month of October.

That same autumn Fedor Timofeevich was killed by his wicked slave.

That same autumn Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow did not wish the Most Reverend Cyprian to be metropolitan of all Rus, and harbored dislike toward him. But Metropolitan Cyprian remembered the Lord’s word, saying: “When they persecute you from this city, flee to another.” For so it is written in the sacred canons of St Peter of Alexandria, and everywhere Divine Scripture speaks much of this, and the Lord in prayer commanded us to say to the Father who is in heaven: “Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.” For it is no sin to flee from troubles and misfortunes, but those who have fallen into sin do not stand firm for God. And the Apostle Paul instructs us not to give place to wrath, and not to bring ourselves into misfortunes and troubles boldly and foolishly. For enmity should be destroyed by patience and humility; but if this is impossible, do not give place to wrath. And so Cyprian, metropolitan of all Rus, that same autumn left Moscow for Kiev, and with him went Abbot Afanasy of Serpukhov from Prince Vladimir Andreevich.

And he came to Kiev to his metropolitan see, and in the cathedral church of Kiev, the mother of all Russian churches, the metropolitan was received by all with great honor; and they met him far from the city with crosses—princes, boyars, nobles, and a great multitude of people—with joy and great honor. And there Metropolitan Cyprian remained in the Kiev regions, governing according to church custom, and all obeyed and honored him.

About Pimen. Then Metropolitan Pimen was freed from confinement and bonds: for at this time Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent for him to Tver with much honor, summoning him to Moscow to the metropolitanate.
