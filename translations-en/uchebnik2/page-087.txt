For so the almighty Lord instructed us, and He Himself fled bodily from Herod, so that we, the humble, might learn not to rise up in arrogance and pride, nor to cast ourselves into misfortunes. So too this most reverend Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, according to the commandment of Christ fled, and gave place to wrath, and prayed for them, saying: “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.”

There was such an end to him: perceiving in himself that his departure to another life unto the Lord was approaching, he summoned Antony, Bishop of Chernigov, and said to him with a command: “After my death, O bishop, do not yourself bury me, and do not allow anyone to bury my body, but, having bound my feet with ropes, drag me out of the city and throw me to the dogs for food and to the birds for plunder.”

And after his death Antony, Bishop of Chernigov, did so. But the Grand Prince of Chernigov Svyatoslav Olegovich and all the princes and his boyars marveled and were terrified at his wondrous deed and great humility.

And in the morning the Grand Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich with Antony his Bishop of Chernigov, and with the princes and boyars, and with a multitude of gathered people, went outside the city with fear and trembling, and with great honor took his body, which emitted much fragrance, and bringing it into the city, laid it in the holy church of the Savior in Chernigov. Then also in the city of Kiev there was a very terrible sign on the day of his death, and when he still lay outside the city, and on the fourth day he was laid in the tomb in the church of the Holy Savior in Chernigov.

And throughout all those four days there were terrible signs in Kiev: the rays of the sun were not visible, but there was darkness, only lightning flashed, and the roar of thunder and a very terrible crash, and winds, and storms, and strong whirlwinds; and many houses fell to their foundations, and the prince’s court was shattered, and many people were killed.

But Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich, being then in the city of Kiev, fell into great fear and trembling, and heard that Constantine, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, had died in the city of Chernigov, and understood that it was because of his wickedness which he had done against God’s hierarch, and he began to confess and repent of his evil thought, and made an all-night vigil in the holy church with a multitude of people, and distributed much alms, and fed the poor and hungry, and made a bright and spiritual celebration; and so all was settled.

But in the city of Chernigov during those four days the weather was fair, and the air was temperate, and gentle, and calm, and there was great brightness.
