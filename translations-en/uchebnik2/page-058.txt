Remember that you swore and kissed the life-giving cross, together with us, your children; what shall we gain in this vain life? For the Lord said: what profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world and loses his soul?” And so, having wept long and poured out tears, he went to Suzdal; and Prince Yuri Dolgoruky returned again from the Alta to Pereyaslavl with his children.

The great princes of Kiev, Vyacheslav Vladimirovich Monomakh and his nephew Izyaslav Mstislavich, sent to him, saying: “You kissed the life-giving cross on this, that you should not live in Pereyaslavl, but should go to the great principality in Suzdal; and now you sit with your children in the great principality in Pereyaslavl, and you bring the Polovtsians against us, and your in-law, the great prince Vladimirko of Galich.”

Prince Yuri Dolgoruky left his son Gleb in Pereyaslavl, and himself went to Gorodok.

At the same time Mstislav Izyaslavich brought Hungarians and Czechs to his father to help him; and Vladimirko of Galich heard that he was coming with Hungarians and Czechs, and went after them secretly, but they did not know it. And Mstislav Izyaslavich stood at Sopagin, and the Hungarians and Czechs stood around him.

And then Vladimir Mstislavich sent him, and the Hungarians, and the Czechs much drink, from which they became drunk; he also sent him word, saying that “Vladimirko of Galich is coming against you with a great force.” They, being drunk, boasted and said: “We are ready to fight them.”

That night Mstislav set out guards, and he himself lay down to sleep with the Hungarians and Czechs; but the guards soon came running at midnight, saying: “The great prince Vladimirko of Galich is coming with a great force.”

Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich at once mounted his horse, and with his druzhina, and began to wake the Hungarians and Czechs; but they lay drunk as if dead.

And that night, at early bright dawn, the great prince Vladimirko of Galich struck them and beat all, and took some alive and bound them, and sent them to Galich; and Prince Mstislav Izyaslavich barely escaped to Lutsk with a small druzhina.

And hearing this, his father, Izyaslav Mstislavich, great prince of Kiev, went against Prince Yuri Dolgoruky to Gorodok, and with him the great prince of Chernigov Izyaslav Davidovich, and Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich, and a voivode with the force of Svyatoslav Olegovich and the Berendei; and they came to Gorodok.

Prince Yuri Dolgoruky shut himself up in the city with his children. But the prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, burned the posad and besieged the city; they fought from the city for many days and were exhausted to the end.
