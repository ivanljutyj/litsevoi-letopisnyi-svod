Then Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver said to the Pskovians: “Brothers, my faithful and beloved friends, brave Pskovians, I do not wish that because of me, unworthy and sinful, there should be upon you excommunication and the hierarch’s curse, and there shall be neither your kiss of the cross to me, sinful and wretched, nor mine to you. And I, wretched and sinful, am departing from you to the Germans in Lithuania; then there will be no offenses to you or your land from Tsar Azbyak or from the Russian princes. And let all my enemies who pursue me and thirst for my soul seek me.”

All the Pskovians, having gathered at the veche, with weeping and great lamentation released Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver according to his will. And he went to the Germans and from there to Lithuania. And the Pskovians sent to Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich a petition for peace and love. And he made peace with them.

And he returned to Novgorod, and from Novgorod went to Moscow. And all the other princes dispersed to their own domains.

In that year the Russian princes sent a message to the Horde to Tsar Azbyak, telling him about Prince Alexander Mikhailovich of Tver and about his departure to the Germans and to Lithuania.

In that year the Pskovians built the stone fortress of Izborsk on Zheraviya Hill.

In that year the Novgorod vladyka Moisei left his bishopric and went to the monastery at Kolomets, and took the schema upon himself. And the Novgorodians remained without a vladyka for eight days. And then they chose for themselves Gregory, priest of Cosmas and Damian from Kholopya Street, tonsured him into the monastic order, installed him in the upper chambers at the Vladyka’s court, and named him Vasily.

On the Danilov Monastery

In that year the most reverend Feognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, went from Novgorod to the Volhynian land and from there to Galich and to Zherava.

And from there he came to Kiev. And there came to him envoys from Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich of Vladimir and Moscow, asking him to bless him to build a monastery in the city of Moscow and to erect a church in the name of the Holy Transfiguration of the Savior and to transfer there the archimandria from the Danilov Monastery, from Zarechye, which the great Danila Alexandrovich had once built for himself, which, like the sun, was filled with radiant grace. He, a lover of learning and zealot of monastic life, decided to transfer it to the center of the city, near his court, not wishing to be parted from it for a day, a night, or even an hour. And having received the blessing from the most reverend Feognost, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, he set to work.
