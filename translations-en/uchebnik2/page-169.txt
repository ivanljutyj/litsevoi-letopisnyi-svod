And so he brought them all to swear on the cross: Rurik, and the Olgovichi, and Vladimirko; and he sent Rurik to Ovruch, and sent the Olgovichi beyond the Dnieper to Chernigov, and in Kyiv he installed on the grand princely throne Prince Ingvar, son of Yaroslav, grandson of Izyaslav, great-grandson of Mstislav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, forefather of Yaroslav, great-great-forefather of great Vladimir.

In the same year the Cumans came into Rus and, having done much evil, returned back.

Prince Roman Mstislavich himself set out in pursuit of them. The Cumans entered the Steppe and relaxed their guard; Prince Roman Mstislavich struck them suddenly, recovered the captives, killed many Cumans, and returned joyfully to his own land.

That same winter there were many signs in the sky; at the fifth hour of the night the whole sky flowed and became red as blood, and the snow on the ground and on the houses resembled spilled blood, and many people saw stars falling in the sky, and the stars fell from heaven to earth; and seeing this, people were terrified, thinking that the end of the world was coming, because whenever signs appear in the sky or in the stars, or in the sun, or in the moon, or in anything else, it is never for good but for evil; for evil is revealed so that people may see it and turn away from evil, and turn to the Lord God, and repent. Such signs foretell either famine, or death, or invasion by enemies, or internecine wars and battles.

That same winter the great prince of Kyiv, Rurik Rostislavich, seeking power over Kyiv, joined with the Olgovichi and summoned the Cuman princes to his aid, and raised the whole Cuman land; and coming with many forces, Rurik and the Olgovichi seized the great and glorious city of Kyiv, and the whole Cuman land. And a terrible evil occurred in the Rus land, such as had not happened to Kyiv since its baptism; many misfortunes had befallen it, many times it had been captured, but never had it happened as now: not only did they seize Podil and burn the Hill, but they also plundered the metropolitan church of Holy Sophia, and the holy Church of the Tithes of the Mother of God, and all the monasteries, and took down icons, and some they carried away, and precious crosses, and sacred vessels, and books, and the precious garments of the first princes, which those had donated to the holy churches in memory of themselves, and these they took.

Monks and old nuns, and also priests, and the blind, and the lame, and the deaf, and the laborers, all were killed, and all the children the foreigners took captive and led away to their own lands [...], and the Chernigov princes to Chernigov, and Rurik Rostislavich to Ovruch.
