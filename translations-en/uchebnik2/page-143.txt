Vsevolod Yuryevich went to Ryazan against Roman Glebovich, Prince of Ryazan. And he came to Kolomna, and there Prince Vsevolod Glebovich met him with his brother Vladimir Glebovich, and he made peace with them, and went away from Kolomna.

And Prince Roman Glebovich of Ryazan sent ahead of himself with an advance detachment Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich, grandson of Vsevolod, great-grandson of Oleg, great-great-grandson of Svyatoslav, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, ancestor of the great Vladimir; but he acted carelessly, all his soldiers were drunk, and his sentries were captured, as was Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich himself, grandson of Vsevolod, and they brought him to Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich; and he sent him to the city of Vladimir.

And he came to the Oka; there his advance detachment met Prince Roman’s vanguard; these too were drunk and asleep, and some were beaten, others captured, and some fled and drowned in the Oka River.

And Prince Roman Glebovich of Ryazan, learning that his guard regiments had been defeated, at once fled into the Steppe past the city of Ryazan, and shut his brothers, Prince Igor Glebovich and Prince Svyatoslav Glebovich, in the city of Ryazan.

And the Grand Prince of Vladimir, Vsevolod Yuryevich, went to the city of Ryazan, and, crossing the Oka River, took the town of Boris and Gleb, and also came to the city of Ryazan and made peace with Grand Prince Roman Glebovich of Ryazan and with his brothers, Princes Igor and Svyatoslav; and they confirmed the agreement by kissing the cross, that each should rule his own volosts, and no one should wrong another or encroach on another’s borders, and all should remain in peace and love; and so they were reconciled and parted in love.

In the year 6689 from Adam (the year 1189 from Christ)
