Some friends, who had enmity against Izyaslav, helped the bishop, but many and frequent letters were also sent to him from the Patriarch of Constantinople, praising him; and he became all the more strengthened. Metropolitan Clement intimidated him and forbade him this, and compelled him to serve with him; but he refused. “I dare not,” he said; “I fear the patriarch, since I was not appointed by him.” And so there was great enmity.

Then Constantine the Greek was appointed by the Patriarch of Constantinople as metropolitan over Kyiv and all Rus, and while he had not yet come to his see in Kyiv, Nifont, Bishop of Novgorod, went from Novgorod to bow to him and receive his blessing, and having come to Kyiv, he waited for him. And news came after news that Metropolitan Constantine was coming, and Nifont awaited him with special joy. Then illness came upon him, and three days before his death he had a dream: he dreamed that he was in the church of the Caves Monastery at the place of the blessed prince-monk Nicholas Sviatosha and was praying to the Most Pure Theotokos that he might see the venerable abbot Theodosius; and many brethren appeared to him, having come into the church; and one of them came out, and approaching, said to him: “If you wish to see Theodosius, go into the altar.” And he led him into the altar and showed him the venerable abbot Theodosius.

He bowed to him, and Theodosius embraced him with his arms and kissed him, and said to him: “It is good that you have come here, Bishop Nifont, and from now on you will be with us inseparably.” And then, having lived three days, he fell asleep in eternal sleep, on the fifteenth day of April, on Saturday of Bright Week, and was laid in the Caves Monastery of the holy abbot Theodosius, in whom he had great faith. This Bishop Nifont had love and concord with Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky and with Grand Prince of Chernigov Svyatoslav Olegovich, and was in concord and love with many other princes; but all of them were at odds with the Grand Prince of Kyiv Izyaslav Mstislavich, and so was this Nifont, Bishop of Novgorod. And about this Bishop Nifont some Novgorodians said that “Bishop Nifont has depleted the church property of Holy Sophia and distributed it—some to the Patriarch of Constantinople and others there, some to the Metropolitan of Kyiv and many others.” But they said this against him unjustly, and by these reproaches they wrought salvation for him and sin for themselves. For it is known to all about him that he adorned the Church of Holy Sophia and covered it with lead, and in Pskov built a stone church of the Savior Almighty, and built another church of Saint Clement in Ladoga, and fed the poor and needy.

In that same year Metropolitan Constantine came from Constantinople to the metropolitan see of Kyiv and all Rus. And the Grand Prince of Kyiv, Yuri Dolgoruky, himself met him with his children and boyars with honor; and that day there was feasting, and great honor to the hierarch, and joyful celebration from all.
