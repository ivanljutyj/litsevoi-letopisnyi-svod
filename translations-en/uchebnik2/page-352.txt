And they said to the tsar: "Prince Michael Yaroslavich is by judgment worthy of death, and your great word as a free tsar—as you command." And they spoke to the tsar of many of his offenses. The tsar again commanded them to hold the trial a second time and to examine everything truly and reliably. But Kavgady said: "Lay it upon me, tsar, and upon my soul; everything is known to me; and by their words, by which I judged concerning them, Michael is worthy of death. And if your word commands them to judge again, then let Michael stand bound at the trial and so answer, for we do not wish, great tsar, to see you wronged, nor us, your princes, reviled; for our dishonor is your dishonor, and your honor and glory are the life and preservation of us all and of all the Horde." The tsar said to them: "Do as you wish, only judge righteously by my judgment, for our royal judgment must be righteous; therefore judge righteously, and when you have finished judging, tell me."

Again, the following week the princes sat in judgment. Michael was placed bound, and they listened to all Yury's speeches, but turned away from Michael's words. They also began to say to him: "You are proud and disobedient to our tsar, and you shamed the tsar's envoy Kavgady, and fought with him, and beat his Tatars, and took the tsar's tribute for yourself and did not give it to the tsar; and you wished to flee to the Germans with treasure, and sent treasure to Rome to the pope, and you killed Yury's princess with poison, and beat princes and the tsar's Tatars." Michael answered them: "I have obeyed and do obey the tsar, and serve him bodily, and give him tribute; but the tsar's envoy came against me with an army, and I fought him not of my own will, and afterwards I honored and gifted him, and sent him away with great honor and gifts. And I did not kill Yury's princess with poison; she died herself, by her own death. And my brother Yury Danilovich should remember my friendship and love, since I helped his father in troubles and was not opposed to him; but he rises against me and wants to possess everything, not as is our custom. And therefore he has made enmity against me; and now, if he wants anything, let it be only according to the tsar's will and your judgment, as the tsar and you command. But now show mercy to me." Kavgady stood and said: "He is not worthy of mercy, but worthy of death," and he spoke much to him in anger.

And all the princes stood and said to the tsar: "Behold, for the second time, tsar, we say to you that Michael is by judgment worthy of death." The tsar said to them: "If so, do what is righteous." This was on Friday, already late in the evening.

In the morning on Saturday the princes sat at the tribunal, and Michael was placed bound, and seven guards from seven great princes were assigned to him, and of the others there was no count. And there was great sorrow for Prince Michael Yaroslavich, and many iron bonds were brought, intending to put them on him, but they only bound his hands with iron fetters and laid a great chain on his neck, and tied his arms behind him.
