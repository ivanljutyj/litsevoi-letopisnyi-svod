And with a movement of her hand she ordered an icon of the angel of the Lord to be painted in another manner. They painted another icon of God’s angel and brought it to her. The princess, seeing the icon, again bowed to it and said nothing.

And again with a movement of her hand she commanded a third icon of the angelic form to be painted in another way. When the third icon of the holy Archistrategos of God, Michael, had been painted, O great miracle! As soon as the blessed one saw this holy icon, her tongue was loosed from its bonds, and she began again to speak, thanking and glorifying God, and saying that she had seen just such an angel as had appeared to her.

This icon she began especially to honor, to bow to it, and to kiss it; she placed it in her church of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos, where it remains to this day.

On the Restoration of Sight to the Blind, on the Healing of the Sick, and on the Repose of the Great Princess Evdokia

The Christ-loving great princess Evdokia conducted her life according to God’s will, and by her own will decided to leave the worldly life and the temporal kingdom and desired to be clothed in the monastic habit; she left her princely palace for the monastery that she herself had founded in the name of the Ascension of Christ. As she was going from the cathedral church of the Most Pure Theotokos, a blind man heard that the great princess was going to the monastery, and cried out in a loud voice, saying: “O lady, our God-loving great princess, nourisher of the poor, we poor always receive from you sufficient food and clothing; now do not abandon me, who have suffered blindness for many years; show me mercy, since you promised to give me sight. I saw you this night in a dream, saying to me: ‘In the morning I will give you sight’; and now the time has come to fulfill your promise.” The blessed one continued walking, paying no attention to what he said, when through some necessity the sleeve of her chemise fell from her hand to the ground. The blind man felt her sleeve in his hand, trembled, and with faith and love dared to wipe his blind eyes with her sleeve, and immediately he received his sight, glorifying God. Then up to thirty people suffering from various illnesses received healing from the blessed great princess Evdokia during her procession to be tonsured in the monastery.

Having entered the monastery, the princess took upon herself the yoke of Christ, was clothed in the monastic habit, and was given the name Euphrosyne; and, living the monastic life, she accomplished many feats for the glory of Christ, lived her life virtuously, and pleased God.
