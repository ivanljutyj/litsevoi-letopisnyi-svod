Taught and instructed by the blessed and all-holy elder Akakios, my guide, whom patriarchs and all hierarchs and kings honored, who like a sunbeam was sweet and desirable to all. Him I followed and served from infancy, learning Divine Scripture and spiritual wisdom, coming to know the spiritual life, and I surrendered myself to this guileless and calm instructor, obeyed him, followed him, and learned from him. And I knew and understood nothing beyond this, but only desired to die to this world and be parted from a life full of temptations and to be with Christ; but by God’s providence there met me what I did not desire and had not thought of. I came from the land of Amorion to imperial Constantinople, sent by the blessed elder and wondrous teacher among hierarchs, kyr Akakios, to the most reverend patriarch kyr Matthew and to the pious emperor kyr Manuel. And there suddenly they laid upon me the words and deeds of the priesthood, burdensome to me; but by God’s judgments I could not escape this, and, possessed by sorrow, I was ordained by a council of the most reverend patriarch and hierarch kyr Matthew to the Kyiv metropolis in Rus. And I was sent there, and departed with great sorrow and grief, weeping and wailing and lamenting, recalling my silent and hesychastic life. And journeying thus mournfully, I arrived in the holy metropolis of Rus. And from that hour God alone knows how often I fell into weeping and tears, sometimes because of my own weakness, since I did not have sufficient spiritual strength, sometimes because of the human calamities of my flock, which before my arrival had been taken captive by the Hagarites and was greatly embittered by this, and then, for our frequent sins, unceasing pestilences, famine, and internecine wars fell upon it. All this sorrow and affliction connected with what happened touched me also; I grieved over it and wept greatly, burning in spirit, dissolving in tears, and my mind was plunged into all these fierce calamities. Besides all this I suffered greatly from the schism and division of the metropolis of Kyiv and all Rus, which the Lord God then permitted and again showed His mercy, uniting the church in ancient peace and splendor. And then I sank into deep sorrow at the death of my son in the Holy Spirit, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich; illnesses and sorrows overcame me, and all sorts of misfortunes attacked me—vexation, slander, crushing sorrow, and wailing—and still greater tears I, humble as I am, endured. And then again rebellions and internecine wars began, and for me sorrow upon sorrow and grief upon grief; and then famine and pestilence began; and these sorrows and afflictions tormented me and to the end crushed my soul, and my eyes grew blind from tears over Christ’s flock and the countless hardships of pestilences and lack of bread.
