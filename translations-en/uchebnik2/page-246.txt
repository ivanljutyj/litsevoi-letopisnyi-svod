They began to persuade him with flattery. But the martyr of Christ Abraham paid no heed at all to their empty counsel and did not yield to their blandishments, but with unyielding mind he confessed Christ as the true God, and cursed Muhammad and their Bulgar faith. They long forbade him with prohibitions and terrified him with tortures; but he did not wish to submit to them in any way, and renouncing everything, he chose rather to die for Christ.

And then they cut off his head on the first day of the month of April; the Christians took his body and laid it in a coffin in the place where other Christians were also buried in the Bulgar land. But God soon avenged the godless: their Great City caught fire, and more than half of it burned; the remaining part of the city kept catching fire for many days, two or three times a day. And thus little remained of the city; everything burned, and a countless multitude of goods burned. This happened to the godless because they had shed the blood of Christ’s martyr Abraham.

On Discord between the Brothers and Peace

Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, however, believed the deception of certain people and separated from Yuri the three Konstantinoviches: Vasilko, Vsevolod, and Vladimir, and intended to resist Yuri, his brother. But God did not allow the evil to come to pass, for Prince Yuri learned of it and summoned the princes to a congress; and they set right the enmity among themselves, and all bowed to Yuri and kissed the cross, promising to be with Yuri in complete love and to obey him as their father.

And then all the princes together celebrated the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos at Bishop Mitrofan’s in Suzdal.

In the same year the Bulgars, called Kazanians, sent ambassadors to the Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich to conclude peace; and there was a truce between them for six years; and there were captives on both sides.

And there they concluded peace, and both swore according to their faith, and they released the captive people: the Grand Prince released their Ishmaelites to them, and they released Christians to the Grand Prince.

In the same year Bishop Cyril died in the monastery of St Demetrius in Suzdal; he was buried there.

In the same year there was famine throughout the whole land, and it lasted three years; and they bought bread for two kunas, a kad of rye for four Novgorod grivnas, wheat for five Novgorod grivnas, and millet for seven Novgorod grivnas; and a multitude of people died.
