Your duty is to reconcile all, and above all us, your close spiritual children.”

The Most Reverend Kirill, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, sent his letter to Novgorod, saying: “The Lord God in His own stead gave His apostles authority to bind and loose, and after them to their successors; and behold, we are the successors of the apostles, bearing the image of Christ and possessing His authority; and behold, I, the chief pastor of all Rus, teach and instruct you. O my lords, fear the Lord God, honor the prince, do not stir up vain enmity, and do not shed blood. For every offense and every sin there is repentance and forgiveness; and the Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, in whatever he is wrong before you, repents of it all and asks forgiveness, and does not wish to be so any more; and I vouch for him to you; and you should receive him with proper honor. But if you, in your folly, swore with the kiss of the cross and agreed among yourselves not to receive the Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich, grandson of Vsevolod, then I remove that oath from you, and I forgive and bless you. Do not stir up enmity or shed blood; live in peace and love; if you do not listen to me, I will impose spiritual punishment upon you.”

The Novgorodians sent ambassadors to the Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich with peace, and so they made peace and agreed with the kiss of the cross to observe all the ancient liberties of Novgorod, such as they had from the beginning; and Yaroslav Yaroslavovich began to reign in Novgorod, and they received him with great honor.

In the same year Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavovich went from Novgorod to Vladimir.

In the same year, in the Horde, before the tsar, they slandered the Grand Prince of Ryazan, Roman Olegovich, grandson of Ingvar, great-grandson of Igor, great-great-grandson of Gleb, and they denounced him before the tsar: “He reviles you, great tsar, and mocks your faith.”

The tsar sent Tatars against him; they began to force Prince Roman to accept their faith. He said to them: “It is unworthy for Orthodox Christians, having renounced their Orthodox faith, to accept the faith of Muslims and pagans.” They began to beat him; he said: “I am a Christian; and truly the Christian faith is holy, but your Tatar faith is pagan.” They did not want to hear such words from him, and they cut out his tongue, and stopped his mouth with a cloth; and they began to cut off parts of his body
