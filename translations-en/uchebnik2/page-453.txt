And Mamai grew angry with all of us, so that if we let him through, he will defeat all of us.

And they came with a vast army from Volok to Tver and began to conquer the Tver lands.

On the twenty-first day of the month of August they captured the town of Mikulin and all its surroundings and burned them, while Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich shut himself up in Tver.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow stood with all his army before Tver for four weeks; they burned the whole outer settlement, conquered and burned all the districts and villages, seized all the estates, and of the people they killed some and carried off a great multitude of others into captivity.

And they surrounded Tver with a palisade wall, and across the Volga they built two great bridges.

And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent to Novgorod for the Novgorodians. The Novgorodians wished both to do honor to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich and to avenge the insult done to them at Torzhok; and, seized with fury, within four or five days they came and encamped before Tver, desiring to seize booty, and did much harm to the people of Tver.

And they advanced with all their forces against the city, set up siege towers, began to throw bundles of brushwood around the city, and set fire to the bridge and the towers at the Tmat Gates, so that the townspeople could scarcely put out the fire. And so the soldiers withdrew little by little from the city, and Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver came out of the city, hacked apart some siege towers, burned others, and killed many people.

And then they killed Semyon Ivanovich Dobrynsky, and for many days on end they fought, and they seized and burned the whole land, and took the small towns of Zubtsov and Belgorod.

Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver did not surrender, for he expected help for himself from Mamai and from Lithuania: his son-in-law Grand Prince Olgerd Gediminovich had promised to send an army.

But when they approached Tver, they saw Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich before Tver with countless troops and, frightened, fled back.

Learning of this, Grand Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver realized that he had nowhere to expect help, and saw his own weakness, since the whole Russian land had risen against him.

And he sent from Tver to Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Bishop Euphemius, his elders, and noble boyars with a petition, asking for peace and submitting to the prince.
