And so, having gathered together, they sent for the Polovtsians, and the Hungarians, and the Czechs, and the Poles; and they set out on the twenty-fourth day of July. And when he was at the city of Vlekhani, the Chernigov princes joined him there; and, having come, they stood at Belaya Vezha, and there they stood for a month, awaiting the Polovtsians and awaiting submission from the Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich.

He, however, was not in the least frightened by them, and not only did he not send envoys to them, but he did not say a single word to them. They went to Pereyaslavl, having joined with the Polovtsians, and stood there, awaiting submission from him; but he was not frightened even by that, and not only did he not send envoys, but he did not send them a single word, and he said to the boyars who were with him: “If my uncle, Prince Yuri, had come against me with his sons, I would not have opposed him for anything; but he has brought against me my enemies, the Chernigov princes, the Olegoviches, and the Polovtsians, and now I have no peace with him.” So he spoke, hoping in the multitude of his army; but help comes from God: whom He wills He humbles, and whom He wills He exalts; He raises the poor from the earth and lifts the beggar from the dunghill, sets him with the mighty, and his inheritance is on the throne of glory; for the Lord God gives grace to the humble, but resists the proud.

The Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, sent for his brother, the Grand Prince of Smolensk, Rostislav Mstislavich, and for his other brother, the Grand Prince Svyatopolk, to Vladimir. And Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, with all the princes and a great host, having come, stood at the village of Kudnovo.

Prince Vladimir Mstislavich stood at the outpost by Pereyaslavl, and Prince Yaropolk Mstislavich with the Poroshans. The Grand Prince of Kiev, Izyaslav Mstislavich, set out in fury for Pereyaslavl and entered the city, and a countless multitude of warriors joined him. Then Euthymius, Bishop of Pereyaslavl, rose and said to him: “It is better for you to humble yourself than to stir up war against your uncle: humble yourself, and every good thing will be yours”; the people of Kiev also restrained him.

Prince Izyaslav Davydovich came to his aid. The Grand Prince of Suzdal, Yuri Vladimirovich Monomakh, stood there five days, and armed himself at dawn, and went from Stryakova Gora, and stood at the so-called Volovye Mezhi.

And the Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, having gathered a great host, placed it in a fortified position in the marsh; and the next day, after hearing the Liturgy at St Michael’s, he came out of the church; and Euthymius, Bishop of Pereyaslavl, forbade him, saying: “Prince!
