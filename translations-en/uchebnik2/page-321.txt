In Yaroslavl, however, the Grand Prince formerly was Vsevolod Konstantinovich, grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh; Vsevolod Konstantinovich of Yaroslavl had a son, Vasily, and Vasily Vsevolodovich of Yaroslavl had a daughter. Vasily Vsevolodovich of Yaroslavl died in Yaroslavl, and after the death of Prince Vasily his princess and his daughter reigned in Yaroslavl. And Prince Feodor Rostislavovich of Mozhaisk began negotiations with Prince Boris Vasilkovich of Rostov, and with his brother, Prince Gleb Vasilkovich of Belozersk, and with the princess of Vasily Vsevolodovich of Yaroslavl, wishing to take her daughter as his wife.

And after this Prince Feodor took the daughter of Prince Vasily as his wife, and therefore he became Grand Prince of Yaroslavl.

And in this way Prince Feodor received the city of Yaroslavl and began to reign in it together with his mother-in-law; and he was very virtuous. Because of the custom that existed then, he also went to the Horde to the tsars and took from the tsar permission in his own name to reign in Yaroslavl.

For this same reason Prince Andrei Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of Vsevolod, also went with them to the tsar in the Horde; with them many other princes also went to the Horde to the tsar, each prince going to the Horde to ask for his own place; and having all united together, they traveled. They came to the Horde to the tsar and were received with honor.

In that same year Grand Prince Boris Vasilkovich of Rostov, grandson of Konstantin, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yuri Dolgoruky, fell ill in the Horde and wished to become a monk; his princess Maria dissuaded him, hoping that he would live; he was with his spiritual father, confessed, repented, and lamented himself for many days.
