Under the chambers on the right side there were 12 halls; their steps were two sazhens in width, and they were all covered with crimson purple, and upon them were set two tables of gold. That same night Tsar Manuel was in the gallery.

And when the first hour of the day came, he came down from the gallery and entered the holy church through the great front doors, which are called the Royal Doors, while the singers sang with a most wondrous and unusual singing, inexpressibly surpassing the mind. And the tsar’s procession was so quiet and gentle that it took three hours from the front doors to the hall. On either side of the tsar were 12 armor-bearers, and from their heads even to their feet all was iron. Before him went two standard-bearers, black-haired, and their shafts and garments and caps were red, and before those two standard-bearers went heralds. Their staffs were bound with silver and gold, and their ends were studded with pearls.

And the tsar reached the hall, and entered that most radiant hall, and put on the Caesar’s purple robe, and the Caesar’s diadem, and placed on his head the Caesar’s crown with points.

And he went out from the hall, and went up to the upper level, and the tsaritsa was brought, and both sat on golden thrones.
