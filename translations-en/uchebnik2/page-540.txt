The Greek kings grew weak, and so from those Armenian lands a king came to rule over the Greeks. His other brother reigned in his own fatherland, Armenia. Both were Christians, and the Constantinople one, who is also the Armenian one—that is, there were two brothers: one reigned over the Greeks, and the other reigned in Armenia.

Later the Greek king, persuaded by his close counselors, marched with an army against his brother, the Armenian king, who is also called the Achemian king.

When the Achemian king heard this about his brother, he was tonsured into the monastic order.

And he met his brother, the Greek king, on foot, in monastic habit. The Greek king marveled at this. And he said to him: “My lord and brother, why do you marvel at me? Do you not know that our grandfathers and fathers did not wish to shed blood between themselves? Can I, wretched and sinful, do this? Therefore I have received the holy angelic monastic habit, and let all my realm be in your hands. I have only one son, and he does not care for vain glory, but because of his youth he cannot yet accept the monastic life. There are many monasteries which our fathers established among our people. Give him your written order, that he may be supported by those monasteries; all the rest is in your power.”

His brother, the Greek king, did this for his son; and afterward he also made his son a ruler in those lands. And that son’s sons also possessed their Armenian lands, which are now called Achemian. Then the Turks prevailed there, and, taking the eastern lands, they joined the Achemian lands to themselves and turned them to their faith. The great-grandson of that king who had been tonsured into the monastic order converted to the Turkish faith; his name was Arkan, and his first son was Sulimen. This Sulimen, having received his scepter, set out for the western lands.

Even while his father Arkan was still alive, he crossed the sea to Kallipolis and opened the way for others as well, in the time of the Greek emperor Andronicus, who was struggling with his brother.

Then Arkan and his son Sulimen also died; and after Sulimen his brother, the younger son of Arkan, whose name was Amurat, took the throne. He surpassed all in strength, and in ferocity, and in cruelty, and he subjugated many kingdoms to his power in the east and in the west, and he subjugated the Turkish land to himself. Therefore he is called the Turkish king, because he possesses the Turkish lands. He also armed himself and marched against the Christian king of Serbia, Lazar. And the Orthodox king of Serbia, Lazar, gathered many forces and marched against him.
