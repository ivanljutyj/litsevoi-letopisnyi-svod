He abundantly sent him everything necessary. He also established an order of those who served him: some stood before him in the inner chambers, and with them he conversed about the governance of his kingdom, and he composed narratives from what was written and from what was heard, and urged them to imitate those who had reigned virtuously and exercised power piously, saying: “The way of the ungodly shall perish.”

He established the second order in the outer hall, where they received his commands from those within; the third order, which was outside, he sent through the middle ones to carry out his commands. He finally renounced all drumming and musical entertainments: “That,” he said, “is needed in time of war.” To all who served him he was generous as a father; and those whom he humbled for mistakes and removed from command, or those who had ruined themselves by their treachery, or by failure to perform service, or who in some other way had come to poverty, he did not deprive of their father’s, grandfather’s, and great-grandfather’s place, for “God,” he says, “does not punish a sin with two punishments”; and he had mercy on the sinner as on a poor man, as did King Solomon. But all those who served him preserved a reverent attitude toward one another, especially his intimates, and clamor, blasphemy, and laughter, or carelessness in dress, were not observed among them; therefore they also kept their eyes from all idle curiosity, and none of the great can fail to see this; and what is more extraordinary than the first, they were not overcome even by love for women.

His nephew Bolsha, lord of the Arbanasi, died; Despot Stefan went and took the Arbanasi under his authority, and while he was still in that country, Constantine, son of Sratsimir, Tsar of Bulgaria, came from Hungary to Belgrade and died in Belgrade in the year 6930 from Adam (the year 1430 from Christ).

Concerning Tsar Mehmed

Sultan Mehmed, the last son of Tsar Bayezid, lived in humility with those with whom he had first come to agreement, and died in Adrianople.

Many people of his country urged Despot Stefan to go out with an army and seize his lands; but he answered them: “I have given an oath to the Sultan that I will do good to his children.”

The son of Sultan Mehmed was Murad; the younger was Mustafa, to whom the Greek emperor gave help, and he seized several lands in the east.

Murad sent an army against him; Mustafa came out to battle from the city of Nicaea and was killed; and Murad became tsar of the East and the West, and over the Turks; and he went against the Greeks with an army, angered that they had given help to his brother; but with Despot Stefan he remained in peace and love.
