And all his host crossed the Oka River on the Sunday, and on the next day, Monday, he himself crossed. And he was grieved that there was little infantry.

And at Lopasnya he left his great commander Timofei Vasilyevich, the tysyatsky, so that if any of the infantry or cavalry went after him, he would guide them without misdirection.

And let none of those warriors, as they march through the Ryazan land, touch anything or take anything from anyone. And he ordered his force to be counted, how many there were, and there were more than two hundred thousand of them.

It was heard in Moscow by the metropolitan and by Grand Princess Evdokia, and in all cities and among all peoples, that the Grand Prince with all the princes and all his forces had crossed beyond the Oka River into the Ryazan land and had gone to battle. And all began to grieve and lament, saying with tears: “Why has he gone beyond the Oka? Even if he himself is preserved by God’s grace, many of his host will surely perish in one way or another.” And all wept inconsolably over this in their grief.

Prince Oleg of Ryazan heard of this, that Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich of Moscow had crossed beyond the Oka River and was going against Mamai with many forces, and he was troubled by this, saying: “What is he doing, and from where have such forces gathered to him? We expected that he would flee to distant places, either to Great Novgorod or to the Dvina. Yet now he marches against so powerful a tsar. But how shall I send word of this to my friend Grand Prince Jogaila Olgerdovich of Lithuania? For they will not allow us to communicate; they have occupied all the roads.”

And his boyars and nobles said to him: “We, lord, heard of this fifteen days ago and were ashamed to tell you. They say that in his domain there is a certain monk named Sergius, who has the gift of prophecy from God, and that monk armed him and ordered him to go against Mamai.” When Prince Oleg of Ryazan heard this, he was frightened and trembled greatly, saying: “Why did you not tell me of this before? I would have gone and entreated the impious Tsar Mamai that no evil be done to anyone. For by this I cannot replenish my land, nor raise the slain, nor bring back the captives. All this will be by God’s judgment; as the Lord pleases, so it will happen. Now I have destroyed my soul. To whom can I now show myself as one of their own? If to Mamai, I will surely perish, for he is lawless and faithless. If to Jogaila Olgerdovich, he is the same. But let the Lord’s will be: to whomever the Lord God, the Most Pure Mother of God, and all the saints give help, to him I too will show my kinship.”
