To You, O Queen, who brought it, You, having looked upon it, said that grace would abide in it forever. You, O Lady Theotokos, even now fill us, the unworthy, with Your grace and have mercy on us! You, O Lady Theotokos, at Your glorious Dormition promised not to abandon this world, but always to love and preserve it! You, O Lady Theotokos, give help to all tongues and all faithful peoples when they ask, and deliver them from every misfortune! You, O Lady Theotokos, did not despise our newly enlightened Russian land, and promised to be with us, the humble. You Yourself, O Queen, evidently sent craftsmen with gold from Constantinople to build You a church in the Caves Monastery in Kyiv, and You Yourself said: “I wish to live in Rus.” You, O Lady Theotokos, [sent] Your wondrous image, which many call the Second Icon, in ancient times called in Roman fashion “Lydian,” which the blessed Patriarch Germanus ordered to be copied in Lydda from the original; and because of the iconoclast Emperor Leo the Isaurian he sent it away to Rome, sending it by sea, and so the holy icon itself came to Rome within a day. After 130 years the icon itself again came out of the Roman church by sea and came to Constantinople under Emperor Michael and his mother Theodora, in the years of the reign of the Russian Grand Prince Rurik. Then it appeared in our Russian land in the air on the Tikhvin River, borne by invisible holy angels, where it has remained until now, working countless miracles and abundantly granting healings to all who come to it with faith. You, O Lady Theotokos, entrusted the throne of the entire Russian metropolis to the great hierarch Metropolitan Peter, for there was a revelation from Your honorable image, a vision at sea to Gerontius, who thought to occupy that throne. You forbade him, and to the one who painted Your image You gave the name Peter, who was to be shepherd of the Christian flock, whom Your Son, our God, glorified with miracles and gave to us as an intercessor for us. You, O Lady Theotokos, appeared visibly to the venerable abbot Sergius with the apostles, with Peter and John, and promised to abide unceasingly in his monastery, providing for it and guarding it. You, O Lady Theotokos, vouchsafed those who have unashamed hope to see this holy image of Yours and bow before it, hoping that You, as You deigned in ancient times to leave Palestine for Byzantium, thence to Kyiv, and then to Vladimir, will now remain here with us, the humble, for ages, pitying us and saving us. And we, Your sinful servants, having You as our blameless intercessor, will not fear any enemies’ snares, always and everywhere saved by You. So now deliver us from the barbarian threat of invasion and captivity, and we will joyfully glorify You and Christ our God born of Your seed; praising Him, we magnify Him and His beginningless Father with the most holy and good life-giving Spirit.
