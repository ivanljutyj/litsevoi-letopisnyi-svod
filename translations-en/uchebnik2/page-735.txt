The Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich came to Kolomna and began to summon people to him from everywhere, and many gathered to him; also princes, boyars, commanders, and all the nobles and servants began to forsake Prince Yuri Dmitrievich on account of the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, and all the people from small to great set out from Moscow to Kolomna, for they all disliked Prince Yuri’s favorite, Semen Morozov.

When Prince Yuri’s sons, the princes Vasily Kosoy, Dmitry Shemyaka, and Dmitry Krasny, saw that none of the Muscovites remained with their father, they were greatly offended and troubled by this. And then the princes Vasily Kosoy and Dmitry Shemyaka Yuryevich killed their father’s favorite, the boyar Semen Morozov, in the riverside vestibule, saying: “You, villain, troublemaker, have brought this trouble upon our father and upon all of us! From of old you have been known as a troublemaker and evildoer.” And having killed him, they left him dead and themselves went to Kostroma.

When Prince Yuri saw that his grand principality was unstable, and that all the people were going over to the Grand Prince, he sent to the Grand Prince, saying: “Come to Moscow to the grand principality, and I myself will go to Zvenigorod.” And he went to Zvenigorod, and from there to Galich.

Concerning the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich

And the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, having returned to Moscow, sat upon the grand principality, and made peace with his uncle Prince Yuri on the condition that he would not receive his children or give them aid.

Concerning the Yuryevichs

In the same year the Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich sent his commander Prince Yuri Patrikeevich, and with him his court and many people, against the Yuryevichs at Kostroma, against the princes Vasily and Dmitry Shemyaka; and with them were the Vyatchans and Galichans. And they prepared for battle on the Kusa River, and defeated the army of the Grand Prince, and took the Grand Prince’s commander Yuri Patrikeevich captive; and they returned again to Kostroma, and when the Volga froze, they came to the Turdeev Ravines.

In the same year Prince Yuri Semenovich came with his princess from the Lithuanian land to Great Novgorod.

In the same year there was a fire in Novgorod; the Zagorodsky End and the Lyudin End as far as Lukina Street burned down. In the same year in Novgorod they built the stone church of Saint George in Borkovets.

In the same year in Novgorod the monk Evfimy, elected lord, built a stone palace on his lord’s court; it had thirty doors; German masters from overseas built the palace together with Novgorodian masters.
