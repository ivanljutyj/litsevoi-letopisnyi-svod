He accepted; but he answered them thus: “O dark and foul kingdom, there is no way you can separate me from the Christian faith. Though I am in very great trouble, God has given this to me because of my sins. Or do you think God loves you when He delivers us into your foul hands? No, God loves us and gives us eternal life. But you, accursed ones, what answer will you give to God, having destroyed many souls unjustly? For this God will torment you endlessly; and the Lord will save the souls of those whom you have destroyed.” The Tatars gnashed their teeth at him, desiring to drink his blood; but he bathed in tears, remembering the sins he had committed before. Vasilko’s face was sorrowful from the long torment he had suffered from the pagans. And he looked to heaven and said: “You, O Lord, know all the secrets of my heart and all my thoughts, and so it was pleasing to Your mercy,” and, continuing to reflect in his heart, he sighed secretly, and prayed, and said: “Lord Jesus Christ Almighty, receive my spirit, that I may rest in Your glory.”

On the Death of Vasilko Konstantinovich

And having said this, Prince Vasilko Konstantinovich was immediately cruelly killed; this evil deed took place on the fourth day of the month of March, on Thursday of the fourth week of Lent; and Vasilko joined the number of martyrs.

Blessed Bishop Kirill came from Belozersk, having escaped the enemy there; and he came to the place where Grand Prince Yuri had been killed, and found his body; and he took it and brought it to Rostov.

Bishop Kirill sang the proper hymns over Prince Yuri, and laid him in a coffin in the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God, with abundant tears; but he did not find his head among the multitude of dead bodies.

After Vasilko was killed, the accursed ones threw him into the forest; a certain God-loving woman, Maria, saw this and told her husband, also God-loving, Andrian Popovich; he took Vasilko’s body, wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a hidden place.

Having learned of this, Bishop Kirill and Vasilko’s princess sent people to take his body.

And when Vasilko was carried to the city of Rostov, a great multitude of people came out to meet him, shedding piteous tears, having been left without such consolation. There was a great lamentation of the Orthodox people when they saw that the father of orphans, the nourisher, and the great consolation of the wretched had departed from them; the light-bearing star of the darkened had set. For all in the church his heart was open; to all the clergy, to the poor, and to the unfortunate, Vasilko was like a beloved father. Above all, concerning almsgiving, he remembered the words of the Lord: “Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy.” Solomon
