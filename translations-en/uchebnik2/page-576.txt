Abbot Mitrofan immediately entered the church of the Most Holy Trinity and tonsured him into the holy angelic monastic order on the seventh day of the month of October, on the commemoration of the holy martyrs Sergius and Bacchus. And the name Sergius was given to him: for at that time names were given by circumstance, not according to one’s worldly name, but after the name of the saint whose memory was being celebrated. On the day of the tonsure, that name was given to the one being tonsured.

The saint was then twenty-three years old when he received the monastic form. In that same church of the Most Holy Mother of God, the aforementioned Mitrofan, together with the tonsure, served the Divine Liturgy. And the blessed Sergius, the newly tonsured monk, after the tonsure was completed, partook of the sacred and divine Mysteries of Christ our God, as one worthy was counted worthy to receive such sanctity. And grace and the gift of the Holy Spirit came to dwell in him. How is this known? It happened that truthful witnesses were present there at that time, who told how, at the very moment when Sergius partook of the Divine Mysteries, the whole church was filled with fragrance, and not only in the church but outside the church as well. And all who lived there and perceived this glorified God, who thus glorifies His servant. This was the first monk tonsured in that church of the Most Holy Trinity, and in that wilderness, by Abbot Mitrofan.

Abbot Mitrofan lived for a time in that wilderness with the venerable newly tonsured Sergius, instructing and enlightening his new tonsure in the monastic life, in patience, and in how one should resist diabolical thoughts and hostile assaults, both visible and invisible. And after living with him a little while, he wished to leave him. But the venerable monk Sergius begged him to stay with him longer, guiding and teaching him. Abbot Mitrofan said to him: “God will enlighten you and teach you to keep His commandments and to do His will.” And after remaining a little longer with the venerable Sergius, he prepared to depart for his own place. And blessing him, he said: “The Lord bless you and keep you at the beginning of your life and at its end, from now and forever. Amen.” And having said this, Abbot Mitrofan prayed and, having blessed him, went away and returned to his own place. The venerable Sergius said to him: “Pray for me, father, that the Lord God may help me endure carnal warfare and demonic assaults, and attacks of beasts, and wilderness labors.” Mitrofan answered him: “God will preserve you and save you by His grace,” and set out on the road by which he had come.

When Abbot Mitrofan, who had tonsured him, had departed, the venerable Sergius began to live alone, without a single human being, struggling in that wilderness.
