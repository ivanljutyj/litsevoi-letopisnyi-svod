Heard of this, Izyaslav and the people of Pronsk, that the Ryazan regiment had been defeated, and already from lack of water they and their livestock were dying, came out of the city with their prince Izyaslav Vladimirovich and submitted to Grand Prince Vsevolod.

He brought them to kiss the cross on the 18th day of the month of October, and installed among them in Pronsk Prince Oleg, son of Vladimir, grandson of Gleb, and took Princess Kir Mikhailova and led her away with him, and took their property and all their countless wealth, and made peace with Prince Izyaslav Vladimirovich, and having installed his governors in all their cities, set out for the city of Ryazan. And when he was at Dobry Sot and intended in the morning to cross the Pronya River in order to go to the city of Ryazan, the Ryazanians sent to him with a plea that he not go to the city of Ryazan.

And their bishop Arseny often entreated him concerning this, saying: “My lord prince, great Vsevolod Yuryevich! You are a Christian, do not shed Christian blood, do not lay waste these places of honorable Orthodox Christians, do not burn churches and honorable monasteries, in which God’s name is glorified and prayer for the whole world is offered to the Lord God, do not raise up God’s wrath against yourself.” Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich was frightened by his words, and, having heeded the plea of Bishop Arseny and the townspeople, departed from them to Kolomna and from there to Moscow. And when he was at the mouth of the Mersk River, there Bishop Arseny of Ryazan overtook him with a plea and with obeisance from all the people.

Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich went from there to Vladimir, and ordered Bishop Arseny to go with him, and they came to the city of Vladimir on the 21st day of November, a Wednesday, on the feast of the Entry of the Most Pure Mother of God.

And the Ryazanians sent their remaining princes to Vladimir.

And Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich released the Novgorodians and Pskovians to Novgorod, having bestowed on them many gifts beyond number, and gave them their liberty according to the old custom and the statutes of the old princes; whatever they wanted, he granted them all, saying to them thus: “If you need anything, ask of me, for I must grant you every good thing, since you have labored greatly, having overcome my enemies the Ryazanians. And I show you great honor, and keep a prince for yourselves according to your will; and whoever is good to you, love and honor him; and whom you wish to favor, favor; and whom you wish to punish, punish. Act as was established by the former princes among you,” and having comforted them in many other ways and bestowed gifts on them, he released them.

The Novgorodians, rejoicing, went to Novgorod. Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich kept with him his eldest son, Prince Konstantin, who had reigned in Novgorod, and the Novgorod posadnik Dmitry, wounded near Pronsk, and another posadnik, Andrey, also wounded near Pronsk.
