At the see of the Archbishop of Novgorod, the Most Reverend John, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, in the years of the reign of the Grand Prince of Kiev Rostislav Mstislavich, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh.

The Novgorodians consulted with their prince Mstislav and with the whole sacred council and decided to summon to their episcopal see, if God wills and the metropolitan blesses and appoints him, Gabriel, brother of their former archbishop Ilya. Having so decided, they sent him with great entreaty to Kiev to Nikifor, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus.

The metropolitan blessed him and appointed him bishop according to the sacred and divine canons, and sent him back to Novgorod; and there was joy and great gladness in Novgorod, for he had already become the second archbishop in Novgorod.

In that same year in Novgorod the Novgorodians killed the posadnik Nerevin and Ivan Svinev and threw them from the bridge. In that same year, on the first day of the month of May, the memory of the holy prophet Jeremiah, on Wednesday evening, there was a sign in the sun: darkness covered the sun, and the sign lasted a little more than an hour, so that even the stars were visible, and people’s eyes turned green, and the sun became the size of the moon, and from its horns there seemed to come scorching fire; and that sign was very terrible and full of horror.

In that same year a son, Constantine, was born to Grand Prince Vsevolod Yuryevich on the eighteenth day of the month of May.

About the Olgovichi, how they went against the Polovtsians, and [the Polovtsians] did them much harm

In that same year the grandchildren of Prince Oleg decided to go against the Polovtsians, but they did not unite with the Russian princes; they went by themselves, saying: “Are we not princes? We shall win glory for ourselves as well.”

And they gathered at Pereyaslavl: Igor Svyatoslavich from Novgorod-Seversky with two sons, his brother Vsevolod from Trubchevsk, and Svyatoslav Olgovich from Rylsk, and aid from Chernigov, and they entered the land of the Polovtsians.

Hearing that the Russian princes were coming against them, the Polovtsians said: “Our brothers are killed, and others are taken captive, and now again they come against us.” And they sent messengers throughout all their land, while they themselves went to meet the princes, awaiting their retinues. The princes came near their borders, but they would not let them into their confines; instead they met the princes without waiting for their retinues, and both regiments joined battle, and the Polovtsians were defeated, and they were driven as far as their tents, and many captives were taken, women and children.
