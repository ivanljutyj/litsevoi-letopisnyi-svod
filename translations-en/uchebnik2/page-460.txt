And some were killed by the Hagarenes, and great terror and fear fell upon all; all were frightened and fled.

And the Hagarenes rejoiced greatly, captured many prisoners and placed them in their camps, trumpeted over the bones of Christians, and set off in a raid toward Nizhny Novgorod.

Grand Prince Dmitry Konstantinovich could not go out to battle against them; all his army was routed, and he fled to Suzdal. This calamity occurred on the second day of the month of August, on the feast of the holy protomartyr and archdeacon Stephen, on Sunday at the sixth hour of the afternoon, and so the Tatars came in a raid to Nizhny Novgorod.

The townspeople of Nizhny Novgorod sailed away in boats along the Volga to Gorodets.

The Tatars came to Nizhny Novgorod on the fifth day of the month of August, on Wednesday, on the feast of the holy martyr Eustignius; they captured the people remaining in the city, and burned the whole city, the churches and the monasteries; thirty-two churches burned in the city.

And they withdrew from the city on Friday, seizing and burning districts and villages, and then departed to their own land with many captives.

In the same year the aforementioned Tsarevich Arapsha came from the Volga Horde, plundered the land beyond the Sura, burned everything, and went back with captives.

In the same year, in the month of August, Prince Vasily Dmitrievich came from Suzdal to Nizhny Novgorod and sent his boyars, ordering them to take from the Pyana River the body of his brother, Prince Ivan Dmitrievich; they carried out his command.

And his brother, Prince Vasily, and the boyars and all the people wept over him, and buried him with funeral hymns in the stone church of the Holy Saviour, in the narthex on the right side, on the twenty-third day of the month of August.

In the same year the Mordvins made a raid along the Volga and plundered the district of Nizhny Novgorod; many people were killed, others were taken captive; the districts and villages that had remained after the Tatars, and the livestock, were burned; after this they departed.

And Boris Konstantinovich set out in pursuit of them with a small force and overtook them at the Pyana River.

They, terrified by the wrath of God, fled beyond the Pyana River; those who did not manage to escape were killed, and some drowned.

In the same year in Novgorod, at the Antoniev Monastery, Archimandrite Savva reposed. In the same year in Novgorod they built a stone church of the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb in Plotnitsy.

In the year 6886. There was a sign in the sun. In that same autumn Tsarevich Arapsha of the Volga killed many Russian merchants and took all their wealth for himself.
