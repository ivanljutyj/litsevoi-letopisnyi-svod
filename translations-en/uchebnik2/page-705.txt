In the same autumn Prince Andrei Dmitrievich of Tver came to Veliky Novgorod; the Novgorodians received him and made peace with the Pskovians.

In the same autumn Simeon, Archbishop of Novgorod, was in Pskov together with his close associates and, according to custom, held court for a month.

The Teaching of Simeon, Archbishop of Novgorod, to the Pskovians

“Noble and Christ-loving honorable men of Pskov! You yourselves know that if anyone renders honor to his hierarch, he renders that honor to Christ Himself, and from Him he who does so receives a hundredfold reward and inherits eternal life. And you, sons, render honor to your hierarch and to your fathers, the spiritual guides of our salvation, without reproach, but with love, desiring nothing in return from them, and speaking nothing against your teachers. But watch yourselves, reproach and guard yourselves, and weep over your sins. Seek healing for your own sickness, and do not look at others; do not rejoice in the misfortunes and calamities of your brothers; do not deceive yourselves; do not be proud, but with humility obey your spiritual fathers, as is fitting for Orthodox Christians living according to God’s law. And do not wrong the Church of God, since the Church of God cannot be wronged by anyone. And you, children of the Church, do not intrude into anything that was established long ago by the bishops, in the time of our former brothers, the bishops of Novgorod, by rightful testament for the house of God, Holy Sophia: do not encroach upon lands, waters, courts, the seal, or any church dues! But if anyone intrudes into something belonging to the Church, then turn away from him, cast him off from your soul, not waiting for guilt for this to fall upon you. According to the rules of the holy apostles and holy fathers, everyone ought to fear God’s wrath and call upon His mercy.” In the same winter Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich gave his daughter Vasilisa in marriage to Prince Alexander Ivanovich of Suzdal, on the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee.

In the same winter the most reverend Metropolitan Photius appointed Ambrose bishop of Kolomna.

Concerning Prince Constantine

In the same winter Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich wished to subject his younger brother Prince Konstantin Dmitrievich to his son Vasily, having drawn up a document to that effect. His brother Prince Konstantin Dmitrievich did not wish to submit to the will of the Grand Prince, saying: “Such a thing has never happened from the beginning! And why do you now wish to do this to me by force?” And his brother Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich became angry with him over this and took away his possessions. And Prince Konstantin went to Veliky Novgorod.
