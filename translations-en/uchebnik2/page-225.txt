This prince loved above all the reading of Divine Scripture and listened to it with great diligence; and from this there came to dwell in him the fear of God, and meekness, and gentleness, and humility, and love, and mercy; and he repaid no one evil for evil, and forgave all who sinned against him; and he offended no one, but edified all with conversations about things bodily and spiritual, and he spoke and did all things according to Holy Scripture; he was very attentive and devoted to the divine churches, and helped them in all things; to hierarchs, priests, and monks he showed great honor; and the poor and the maimed he himself washed with his own hands, and fed, and gave drink, and gave alms, remembering the words of blessed David: “Blessed is he who considers the poor and needy; the Lord will deliver him in the day of trouble. The Lord will preserve him, and keep him alive, and bless him on earth, and will not deliver him into the hands of his enemies.” The prince also remembered the words of David’s son Solomon: “By alms and faith sins are cleansed,” and the words of the prophet Daniel: “Cleanse your sins by alms, and your transgressions by mercies to the poor.” Such is almsgiving: as fire burns hay, so almsgiving burns away sins. All the years lived by Constantine were thirty-six.

At that same time Constantine’s wife, the princess, became a nun at his tomb, and they gave her the name Agafia. Constantine’s sons were Vasilko; Vsevolod, whose name was Dmitry; and Vladimir, whose name was also Dmitry.

After this, Constantine’s brother Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich became grand prince in Vladimir; and Bishop Simon returned to serve at his see in Vladimir.

In the same year died Prince Rostislav Rurikovich, son-in-law of Grand Prince Vsevolod Chermny.

In the same year died Prince Konstantin Davidovich, grandson of Rostislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, and great-great-great-great-grandson of Vladimir the Great.

In the same year Prince Mstislav Mstislavovich went to Torzhok and took Borislav Nekoshevich captive.

And Prince Mstislav took from Borislav all his wealth, and then released him.

And at that time illness struck the son of Prince Mstislav, Prince Vasily; and Mstislav began to weep and lament for his son. His son grew sicker and died.

And they brought him from Torzhok to Novgorod, and laid him in Novgorod in the church of the Most Pure Mother of God, beside his grandfather.
