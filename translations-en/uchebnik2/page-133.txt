The people of Vladimir began to murmur among themselves, saying: “We accepted the princes of our own free will, and we swore to them on the life-giving cross in all matters, but now they plunder and ruin this land as though it were not their own domain, and not only ordinary volosts but also church volosts; they have taken tributes, dues, and everything else.”

And the people of Vladimir sent to the people of Rostov and Suzdal, telling them of their grievance; those hypocritically supported them in words, but in deed gave them no help at all.

The people of Vladimir gathered together and sent to Prince Mikhalko in Chernigov, saying: “You, the eldest among your brothers, come to Vladimir; and if the people of Rostov and Suzdal plot anything against us while you are here, then all will be as God grants and the Most Holy Mother of God wills.”

A New Miracle of the Most Holy Mother of God

Mikhalko set out from Chernigov together with his brother Vsevolod, and with them also went Vladimir Svyatoslavich, grandson of Vsevolod Olegovich; they came to Moscow, where the people of Vladimir met them.

Hearing of this, Prince Mstislav Rostislavich of Rostov and his brother Prince Yaropolk took counsel with their retinue, and they ordered Prince Yaropolk to go out against them with his regiment, to meet them and fight them, not letting them through to the city of Vladimir; but by God’s providence they missed each other in the forests—Prince Mikhalko went from Moscow to Vladimir, while Prince Yaropolk went by another way to Moscow. And word came to Prince Mstislav Rostislavich from his brother Yaropolk Rostislavich: “Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich is very infirm, and they are carrying him on a litter, and few of his retinue march with him. I will follow after him and overtake his retinue; brother, go quickly to meet him, so that he does not enter the city of Vladimir.”

This news came to him on Saturday; he told his retinue of it, and on Sunday morning he galloped swiftly from Suzdal as if hunting a hare, and his retinue caught up with him.

Prince Mikhalko Yuryevich was five versts short of Vladimir when Prince Mstislav Rostislavich met him with his retinue, but not with all of it, since some warriors were riding behind and others had not yet left the city. The prince himself and his warriors rode quickly, not wishing to allow Mikhalko Yuryevich to enter the city of Vladimir, and they came out from behind the hill with their regiment and raised the banner.
