That same winter in Nizhny Novgorod much snow fell; the snow slid down from the high mountain above the Oka behind the Church of the Annunciation and buried many houses with people.

That same year Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent to Tver and renounced the treaty with Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver on the third day of the Dormition Fast. And that night there was terrible thunder, so that the whole earth shook, and after this Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver went to Lithuania.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich sent a large army to Tver and ordered Tver to be taken; he ordered all districts and villages to be burned and seized, and after the battles the army returned with great plunder.

In the year 6879 from Adam (the year 1379 from Christ)

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich on the third day of September went himself with many warriors to Tver. He captured the town of Zubtsov and burned it entirely; he also captured another town, Mikulin, and burned it; he conquered, burned, and devastated all the districts and villages of Tver, took many people captive, seized all their wealth, and drove off their livestock with him.

And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich returned to Moscow with great booty, brought much livestock into his land, and subdued the Tverites.

Prince Mikhail Alexandrovich of Tver learned of this, being then in Lithuania, was greatly offended by it, and went from Lithuania to Mamai’s Horde; he requested from the tsar the envoy Sary-Khoja and a yarlyk for himself for the grand principality of Vladimir, and went against Rus.

Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich learned of this, was greatly enraged, posted guards on all the roads, wishing to capture the prince; they searched for him a long time but did not find him, and news of this came to him from Moscow. He then returned to Lithuania to his brother-in-law Grand Prince Olgerd Gediminovich, began to ask him and his sister Uliana, daughter of Alexander, wife of Olgerd Gediminovich, for protection, asking his sister to entreat her husband, and he went with an army to Moscow against Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.

A Terrible Omen

That same autumn there were many signs in the sky: at night people saw what seemed like pillars in the sky, and the sky itself was red, as if bloody. Not only in the sky were there bloody visions, but on the earth and on the snow they saw what seemed to be blood, and there were many such cases. Even before the snow fell, they saw what seemed to be blood on the earth, in the water, and in the houses; and when snow fell on the earth, blood lay on the snow too, and all people walked as if bloodstained, as if all were in blood; but when anyone entered under a roof or into a house, no blood was visible on him.
