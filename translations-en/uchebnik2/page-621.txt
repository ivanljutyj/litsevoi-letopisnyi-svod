Thus there remained no one in the city, because everyone, each with his boyar, came out of the city to Vitovt Kestutievich. He ordered first of all to seize their elder brother, Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Alexander, great-great-grandson of Gleb, great-great-great-grandson of Rostislav, descendant of Mstislav, descendant of David, descendant of Rostislav Mstislavich, son of Vladimir Monomakh. And then his brothers also, the sons of Svyatoslav, and he ordered them at once sent to his Lithuanian land.

And he himself entered the city of Smolensk with princes and boyars. Before him they carried a cross and played pipes after their Lithuanian custom.

And after staying there several days, he burned the outer town, took many people captive and led them away with him, and returned home, taking countless goods and wealth. By such deceit he seized the city and the whole principality of Smolensk and installed in the city of Smolensk his lieutenants Prince Yakov, called Amont, and Vasily Boreikov. This was the first capture of the city of Smolensk by Grand Prince Vitovt Kestutievich of Lithuania, through deceit and flattery. It was taken on the twenty-eighth day of September, a Tuesday, on the commemoration of our venerable father Chariton the Confessor. And Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk was at that time in Ryazan with his father-in-law Oleg Ivanovich, Grand Prince of Ryazan.

In the same winter died the princess, wife of Ivan Vsevolodovich of Tver, daughter of Grand Prince Boris Konstantinovich of Nizhny Novgorod, and she was laid in the Church of the Holy Savior in Tver.

In the same winter Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan with his son-in-law Grand Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk, with his brothers, with the Princes of Pronsk, Kozelsk, and Murom, went to war against Lithuania and did them much harm.

In the same winter, on the twenty-seventh day of December, at the seventh hour of the night, the moon became blood-red. In the same winter Vitovt Kestutievich, Grand Prince of Lithuania, went to war against Ryazan and ravaged the districts. But Grand Prince Oleg of Ryazan had not yet returned to Ryazan.

And learning of this, he left the captives in a certain place, and attacking the detachment that had been sent, he killed many and captured some. Vitovt, hearing of this, was afraid, took to flight, and returned home.

And Oleg entered his land with great booty and wealth and kept with him his son-in-law Prince Yuri Svyatoslavich of Smolensk. He was then in grief and great sorrow for his brothers and for his patrimony. Such misfortune had never before befallen Smolensk as now they suffered from Vitovt, the cunning and insatiable plunderer of others’ goods. In the same winter Metropolitan Cyprian sent his steward Fyodor to Novgorod.
