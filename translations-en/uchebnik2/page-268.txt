Rus (1248–1297 from the Incarnation of Christ)
Book 6

On the Great Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich

This faithful and noble, God-adorned and worthy of all praise Great Prince Alexander Yaroslavovich was in the eighth generation a descendant of the autocratic and equal-to-the-apostles Tsar, Great Prince Vladimir Svyatoslavovich, who enlightened the Russian land with holy Baptism, and a descendant of Rurik in the eleventh generation. For his virtues he received much glory and praise not only from people but also from God Himself. From a small age, from his youth, every good deed was taught him by his pious father, God-wise and endowed with authority Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, and by his holy mother, the God-loving Great Princess Feodosia, who received the name Evfrosinia in monasticism; they raised Alexander in every good instruction. And thus the fear of God entered his heart, together with the desire to keep the commandments of the Lord and to fulfill them in all things. In every way he honored priests and monks.

Throughout the time of his youth, Alexander kept humility of mind with all his soul, abstaining and keeping vigil, preserving purity of soul and body, and he learned to be meek and avoided vain glory. He took great pains to eat moderately, knowing that satiety drives out chastity, hinders vigilance, and resists the other virtues. On his lips was unceasingly the Divine Scripture, which was sweeter to him than honey; he read it with zeal, listened to it, and strove to fulfill it in deed as well. His relatives, seeing that he prospered in these virtues, received much benefit and themselves tried in every way to please God, just as Alexander pleased God in all things and burned with longing for the divine and heavenly; and all that people consider good and honorable was nothing to him. He showed not a single virtue of the soul before people, and in every way contrived to hide his many good deeds because of his exceedingly great humility.
