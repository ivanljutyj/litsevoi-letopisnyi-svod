And for this the Most Reverend Cyril, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, suspended Bishop Ignatius from sacred service, and only Prince Dmitry Borisovich, grandson of Vasilko, of Rostov, barely prevailed upon the metropolitan on his behalf. The metropolitan forgave Bishop Ignatius and released him with the words: “Do not exalt yourself and do not think that you are without sin, and do not forbid and excommunicate so much as you release and forgive, for the forgiveness of our sins lies in the forgiveness of our brothers, and the Lord’s mercy is hidden in him who has been merciful. Weep, then, child, and repent until your death over this improper and shameless audacity, for you condemned before God’s judgment one already dead, while when he was alive you were ashamed of him, and accepted gifts from him, and ate and drank with him, and visited and rejoiced with him. And when it was possible to correct him, you did not correct him. Now, then, do you wish to correct him by forbidding and excommunicating so harshly? If you wish to help him, then help him with alms to the poor, and with prayers, and with supplications, and with the offering of sacred and divine services.” And so the Most Reverend Metropolitan Cyril instructed Bishop Ignatius at length, and forgave him, and blessed him to serve and to perform all sacred and divine things, and comforted him so that he would not grieve long.

In the same year there were terrible thunders, and strong winds, and great whirlwinds, and terrible storms, and frequent lightning; and many people were killed by thunder, and many were burned by lightning; and in some places a strong whirlwind tore houses from their foundations, with people and all their possessions, and carried them far away. Such signs the Lord showed, turning and bringing us to repentance: “For He does not desire the death of a sinner, but that he turn and live.”

In the same year Clement, Archbishop of Novgorod, came to Cyril, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, and received his blessing.

Then Clement went to Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich, grandson of Yaroslav, with a request for peace and love from the Novgorodians.

In the same year the Most Reverend Cyril, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, reposed in the land of Suzdal, in Pereyaslavl, on the seventh day of the month of December, on the day of the memory of our holy father Ambrose, Bishop of Milan.

There were also Grand Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich of Vladimir, and Clement, Archbishop of Novgorod, and Ignatius, Bishop of Rostov, and Theodore, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal; and they sang over Metropolitan Cyril and laid him in a coffin.

And they carried Metropolitan Cyril to Vladimir, then from Vladimir they carried him to Kiev.
