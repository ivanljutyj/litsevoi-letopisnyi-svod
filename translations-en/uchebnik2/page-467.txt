Neither the monks nor the nuns, nor all the boyars, nor the common people wanted to see Mityai as metropolitan; only the grand prince wanted this.

And Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich grieved over the death of Mityai and did not wish to accept Pimen, saying: “I did not send Pimen to be installed as metropolitan, but I sent him as one of Mityai’s servants. What happened to Mityai I do not know; God alone knows and will judge the injustice, for I have heard much about what happened, and I do not accept Pimen and do not wish to see him.” Metropolitan Pimen with his companions was at that time waiting in Constantinople, and then the grand prince sent his spiritual father Theodore, the abbot of Simonov, to Kiev to Metropolitan Cyprian, inviting him to Moscow. And the envoys came to Kiev to Metropolitan Cyprian before Great Lent.

On the Arrival of Metropolitan Cyprian from Kiev to Moscow

And Cyprian came from Kiev to Moscow on Thursday of the sixth week after Pascha, on the very day of the feast of the Ascension of Christ, in the year 6888. And they began to ring all the bells, and people gathered, a great multitude gathered; archimandrites, abbots, priests, and monks came from everywhere, for many had gathered for the feast of the Ascension; the whole city was stirred, with wives and children, with infants and even with nursing babies; all went out to meet the metropolitan far beyond the city limits. The grand prince himself met the metropolitan far from the city together with his children and all the boyars, with great honor and love, faith and humility.

And they returned back to the city, gathered in the church, Metropolitan Cyprian venerated the icons, and a prayer service was held, and all delighted in spiritual love.

And there was great celebration that day for the grand prince and the metropolitan; all rejoiced and were glad with joy; many alms were distributed to travelers and the poor, and they glorified Christ God, to whom be glory forever and ever, amen.

And then, after seven months had passed, when the year 6889 (1389 A.D.) came, news came to the grand prince that Metropolitan Pimen had set out from Constantinople for the Russian metropolitanate, crossing the Horde. The grand prince did not wish to accept him and was angry with his senior boyar Yuri Vasilievich Kochevin Oleshinsky.

When Pimen was in Kolomna, the grand prince sent to him and ordered that the white cowl be taken from him, and that he himself be put in prison, and that his retinue, his counselors and choir members, be separated one by one and shackled in iron fetters. They took from him all his sacristy and treasury, and assigned as his overseer a boyar named Ivan Grigoriev son Churilov, nicknamed Dranitsa. And they led Pimen away into confinement from Kolomna to Okhna, bypassing Moscow.
