Blessed Alexius was seventeen years older than Grand Prince Semyon, son of Ivan, grandson of Daniel, great-grandson of Alexander, and great-great-grandson of Yaroslav. Prince Ivan Danilovich, who was not yet on the grand princely throne, baptized him in infancy; the name given him in holy baptism was Simeon. While still a child he learned to read and write and lived in good order as he grew.

When he was twelve years old, he had a vision, for God wished to reveal His will concerning him: he had set snares for catching birds and had dozed a little, and a voice came to him saying: “Alexius, why do you labor in vain? From now on you will be a catcher of men.”

The blessed one awoke from sleep and, seeing no one around, wondered and was terrified, pondering his vision: “What does this new name mean, for I have been called Alexius?” And from that time he remained in thought, unable to solve the vision, but he told no one about it.

Father and mother and brothers questioned him, saying: “Why are you so sorrowful and stay silent? Books are always in your hands, and in them alone you are instructed, and we do not hear a single word from you. Such conduct suits a monk, not a layman. Who commanded you to live like a monk? Why have you dried yourself up with fasting and thirst? Do not torment yourself so, lest you fall ill and grieve us. Tell us, who has led you astray?” The blessed one comforted them a little, but continued to live in abstinence; he did not go to any spectacles, did not amuse himself with young people, avoided all mockery and scoffing, and all the more devoted himself to silence, fasting, and reading the Divine Scriptures, always remaining in compunction and humility.

When he reached twenty years, he loved God with all his soul, and desiring to keep His commandments, he wished to be tonsured into the monastic order. He left his father, mother, brothers and sisters, all his kin, friends, and thoughts of marriage; he renounced all worldly attachments, and desiring to labor for the glory of God alone, he said to himself the words of the prophets: “I have chosen rather to be cast aside in the house of the Lord than to live in the dwellings of sinners.”

And he withdrew from the world at about twenty years of age, or a little older, and went to the Epiphany Monastery, where he was tonsured into the holy angelic monastic habit.

And they gave him the name Alexius, as God had foretold when he was setting snares to catch birds in the wilderness.

And he began to live a strict monastic life, spending his time in fasting, vigils, prayers, tears of compunction, and study of the Old and New Testaments.
