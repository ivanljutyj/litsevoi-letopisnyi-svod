In that same winter there was a fierce plague in Kiev and throughout all its surrounding regions.

In that same winter Metropolitan Gregory Tsamblak died in Kiev. He was a Bulgarian by birth, a very learned man, trained in book learning from childhood, and he left behind many writings.

In that same spring, on the 9th day of April, on Sunday after vespers, there was a violent storm with wind and dark clouds, with rain and heavy hail; water flowed everywhere, so that people thought a second flood had come. Thunder rolled and lightning flashed, and people fell dead to the ground. In some churches the icons were scorched by lightning, and even one sexton was burned by heavenly fire. Great fear was everywhere: so powerful were the thunderclaps that people already expected the second coming of Christ! It was all wondrous: some died from the thunderbolts, while others who were in the same places and the same churches remained alive. It is said in Scripture concerning thunder and lightning: “If lightning comes from the joining of clouds, it harms no one, but passes by and vanishes. But if it touches clouds striking against one another, then heavenly light, like fire, comes forth from them, joins with the lightning, descends, and burns all that it touches.” The thunderclaps are incomprehensible and hard to explain; all this is the ineffable power of God! It terrifies some, puts others to death, wounds some and turns them back from evil deeds to repentance. Let us fear this, tremble, promise to do good, flee from wickedness, and love one another, so that in this present and future life we may enjoy the good granted to us by the Lord. This happened in Novgorod to manifest the will of God.

In that same spring there was a great fire in Veliky Novgorod: the Slovensky End and the Plotnitsky End as far as the Fedorovsky Brook burned, twenty-four churches were scorched, and the Church of the Holy Fathers burned completely; much Christian property burned, many people drowned in the Volkhov, and some burned in the fire.

In that same spring the Novgorod posadnik Ivan Bogdanovich died in the monastic order.

In that same year the Murmans came against Veliky Novgorod with an army in buses and shneks, conquered the Karelian lands in Arzuga, and took captive and burned the volosts of Zovolotsk land; they did much evil and returned to their own lands beyond the sea.
