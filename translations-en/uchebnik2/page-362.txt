He, by God’s grace, crossed the sea safely and came to the most holy metropolitan see of the Russian throne, giving peace and blessing to all.

And he began to teach the flock entrusted to him by God according to the rule of the holy apostles and holy fathers, visiting all cities and villages. He conversed with everyone who wished, with humility and toil, meekness and gentleness, teaching according to what is said: “God rests in the heart of the meek”; or “A broken and humbled heart God will not despise”; or “On whom shall I look, but on the meek and humble and trembling at my words”; and also “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” Some, however, yielding to the devil, the primordial enemy of mankind, who cannot endure anything that is for good and benefit to man, tried to harm the saint: they incited certain people to oppose his coming. But after some time they were ashamed, received the hierarch, and humbly submitted to him. He not only did not remember evil, but cared for their souls, prayed for them, and tirelessly attended to his own affairs.

Then, after some time had passed, the devil, the enemy, the source of envy, taught Andrew, bishop of the Tver region, frivolous of mind, unspiritual, and concerned with vain glory, to sharpen his tongue against Peter; and he writes a letter, weaving false and blasphemous words against the righteous man, and sends it to Constantinople to the holy and blessed Patriarch Athanasius. The patriarch was surprised at this letter, did not believe it, considering it slander. Nevertheless he sends to Rus one of the church clerics with a letter in which was written: “To the most sacred Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, beloved brother in the Holy Spirit and servant of our humility, Peter! I know that by the election of the Holy Spirit you were appointed shepherd and teacher of the rational flock of Christ. And now a letter has come from the people of your country with grave accusations against you, and, having heard it, it troubled my thoughts. Strive to purify and correct all this.” The cleric, taking this letter written by the patriarch, arrived in the Russian land. And these slanders of Andrew reached the most reverend hierarch Peter. He, placing all his hope in God, said: “With patience I waited for the Lord, and He heard me; and if God is for us, who is against us?”

And when the cleric sent by the patriarch came to Rus, a council was assembled in Pereyaslavl. The God-loving Bishop Simeon of Rostov came there, and the venerable Prokhor, who was then abbot. And they summoned Andrew, Bishop of Tver, who was the source of all the troubles of that time. Prince Michael, son of Yaroslav, was himself then in the Horde, but his sons Dmitry and Alexander came. Many other princes and nobles also came.
