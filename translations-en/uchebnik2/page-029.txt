He prayed in the Church of St Michael, received the blessing of Bishop Evfimy, and set out with his retinue from Pereyaslavl to Kiev.

He crossed the Dnieper River and, arriving, took his stand at Zhaline. But Grand Prince Igor Olegovich of Kiev, with his brother Svyatoslav, his boyars, and all his host, went out against Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich, Grand Prince of Pereyaslavl.

When they were at Oleg’s grave, before they reached it, they were seized with fear, and all their host was thrown into confusion; and Grand Prince Igor himself and his brother Svyatoslav fled back, with no one pursuing them. Then Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Pereyaslavl, seeing them fleeing, pursued them with his retinue, and many of the fugitives were captured, others were killed, and others drowned in the waters. This came about by God’s providence, by the prayers of the Most Pure Mother of God, by the power of the precious Cross, and by the prayers of all the saints, on the 13th day of December.

And on that day Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich entered Kiev and sat on the throne in Kiev in the great reign; while Igor Olegovich had sat in Kiev in the great reign for only two weeks. For such is God’s providence: whom He wills He enriches, and whom He wills He shows mercy; but other things also happen: those whom God hates, we do evil, we kill, we gather unjust wealth, we despise the poor and needy, and we care more for the body than for the spirit.

Also, after four days had passed, they brought Grand Prince Igor Olegovich to Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich in Kiev, having taken him in a marsh; and Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich sent him to the Vydubitsky Monastery and ordered him to be guarded there securely.

And from there he sent him to Pereyaslavl, and they put him in a stone cellar in the Monastery of St John, assigned many guards to him, and guarded him closely. And so ended the reign of Grand Prince Igor, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir the Great.

His brother Svyatoslav Olegovich fled from that battle to Novgorod Seversky with a small retinue, weeping bitterly and saying: “Woe to me, a sinner! What shall I do? I have lost my elder brother, Grand Prince Vsevolod Olegovich of Kiev, for he has departed to the Lord, and my other sweet and beloved brother, Grand Prince Igor Olegovich of Kiev, has been seized by our enemies. O my wretched soul! What shall we do? Where shall we go?” His retinue began to comfort him, while they themselves wept and shed tears.

In the same year the Polovtsian princes heard what had happened to Grand Prince Igor Olegovich of Kiev and made peace with Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich of Kiev.
