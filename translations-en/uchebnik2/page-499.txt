And so they stood, wall upon wall, each having laid his spear upon the shoulders of those in front; the front ones had shorter spears, and the rear ones longer. And the grand prince also, with his great Russian force, moved from another hill against them. And it was terrible to see two great forces coming together for bloodshed, for swift death! But the Tatar force appeared dark and darkened, while the Russian force was seen in bright armor, like a great river pouring forth or a billowing sea; and with the sun shining brightly upon them and casting rays upon them, they looked from afar like lamps. The ungodly tsar Mamai with five great princes went up to a high place, onto a hill, and stood there, wishing to see human bloodshed and swift death. And already for some the term of life had come, and the end had drawn near.

And first the Russian guard regiments began to engage the Tatar ones. The grand prince himself rode ahead in the guard regiments and, after staying there a little, returned again to the great regiment.

And so both forces went to meet at the place: from there the great Tatar force, and from here the grand prince himself, Dmitry Ivanovich, with all the Russian princes. And the Russian force was seen to be inexpressibly numerous, more than four hundred thousand of horse and foot. The Tatar force was also very numerous. And when both forces had already come close to each other, then a very great bogatyr rode out from the Tatar regiment, broad of frame and displaying great courage. He was very terrible to all, and no one dared to go out against him, and they said each to another that someone should go out against him, and no one went.

Then the devoted obedient of the venerable abbot Sergius of Radonezh, the monk Peresvet, began to say to the grand prince and all the princes: “Do not be troubled in the least about this: for great is our God and great is His might. With God’s help, and through the prayers of His most pure Mother, of all His saints, and of the venerable abbot Sergius, I wish to meet him.” This Peresvet, when he was in the world, was a famed bogatyr possessing great strength and might; in stature and breadth he surpassed all, and he well understood military matters and equipment.

And so, by the command of the venerable abbot Sergius, he put upon himself the holy schema, the angelic image, and signed himself with the holy cross, and was sprinkled with holy water, and took leave of his spiritual father, also of the grand prince, and of all the princes, and of all the Christian army, and of his brother Oslyabya. And the grand prince wept, and all the princes, and all the army wept with great weeping, saying with many tears: “Help him, O God, through the prayers of Thy most pure Mother and of all the saints, as in ancient times Thou didst help David against Goliath.”
