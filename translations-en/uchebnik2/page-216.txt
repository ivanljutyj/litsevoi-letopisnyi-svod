Nor under your grandfathers, nor under your fathers, did anyone who entered your Suzdal land with an army come out in good health and obtain what he desired. Such a thing has never happened! Even all the Russian land—Galich, Kiev, Chernigov, Ryazan, Smolensk, Novgorod, Polotsk, and Pskov—can in no way withstand this force. But why speak of this? If even the whole Polovtsian land came against them, then truly we would pelt them all with saddles and beat them with our fists, if only your host were zealous in battle and with all its heart and all its fury stood against the adversaries; no one can withstand us.

These words pleased Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich and his brother Yaroslav, and all his brothers, and all their men, and they began to rejoice and make merry. And having summoned the host, he began to say: “Brothers! Let us stand with all zeal against these adversaries. Fight without ceasing, and you shall have horses, armor, and garments. But let no one be taken alive; if anyone takes a man alive, he himself shall be killed. If anyone is in silver, or gold, or pearls, kill them, leave not one alive. If anyone from your regiment departs because of your negligence and mercy, let those be hanged and cut to pieces for that negligence and mercy. And as for the princes, if they fall into our hands, we will think about them later.” And having given such instructions, he sent the men forth.

Then Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich and his brother Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, with all their brothers and all their boyars and men, entered the tent and began to divide the cities, saying: to Grand Prince Yuri Vsevolodovich—Vladimir and Rostov; to Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich—Novgorod; to Prince Svyatoslav Vsevolodovich—Smolensk; and from Kiev we shall take tribute and install a prince there from our own hands; and from Galich we shall likewise take tribute. And they kissed the cross on this and signed the charters.

These charters the Smolensk men took after the battle in the princes’ camps and brought to their princes.

Having divided the cities, the sons of Vsevolod began to make merry.

And after this they sent a message to Prince Mstislav Mstislavich: “Brother Mstislav! It seems to us that, having forgotten yourself, you have come with ignorant men, wild Novgorod peasants, and foolish Smolensk men. It would have been more proper for you not to listen to them.” And they began to prepare for battle.

Prince Mstislav Mstislavich, Prince Vladimir of Smolensk, Prince Vladimir of Pskov, and Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich Romanovich spoke to the Prince of Rostov, Konstantin Vsevolodovich: “If you wish to be of one mind with us and faithful to us, confirm this by the honorable and life-giving cross of Christ!” And, having confirmed the agreement by kissing the cross, they went to battle.
