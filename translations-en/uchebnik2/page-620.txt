In the same year Kolchan, the Turkish tsar, son of Andronicus, came with a great host to Constantinople on campaign. The Greek tsar Manuel, son of John Palaiologos, went out against him from Constantinople with the Greeks, the Franks, and the Germans in one mind, in full accord. They met the Turkish tsar Kolchan, overcame him, and drove him back into Turkish land, killing many. And he suffered disgrace and great shame, for he was depleted of men and was in grief and sorrow.

In the year 6904 from Adam (1404 from Christ)

In the month of September there was an eclipse of the sun. That same autumn in Smolensk enmity began between the Grand Prince of Smolensk Yury Svyatoslavich and his brothers Gleb Svyatoslavich and the other Svyatoslavichi. They all quarreled among themselves and were in great enmity. And they warred against one another over the appanages of the inheritance of their principality. And there was great turmoil: they did not listen to one another, brother to brother. Grand Prince Yury Svyatoslavich was grieved and sorrowful, and went to Ryazan to his father-in-law, Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan.

That same autumn the Turkish tsar Kolchan killed his son Vechenyap and slew many princes and great men with him.

That same autumn Tsar Temir-Aksak was in the Horde’s possessions in the coastal region, and the wrath of God fell upon him: a pestilence began among people and cattle.

That same autumn, in the month of September, the Grand Prince of Lithuania Vytautas Kestutievich, grandson of Gediminas, having gathered a great host around him, set out on campaign past Smolensk, pretending that he was going against Temir-Aksak and not against Smolensk. And a rumor spread everywhere that Vytautas Kestutievich was going with a great host against Temir-Aksak past Smolensk. They spread these rumors in order to lull the vigilance of the Smolensk people.

But at that time there was great disorder in Smolensk: the princes Svyatoslavichi were at odds and in hatred, warring against one another, disputing, each defending his appanages of his principality; brother did not wish to listen to brother. When Vytautas Kestutievich came beneath Smolensk, making it seem that he was passing by Smolensk, Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich came out to him with boyars and a small retinue. He, having shown him honors and given him gifts, sent him away with love and peace, saying: “It would be well if all the brother princes came out to me with love, without fear. And if there are any disputes among you, then take me as arbitrator, and I will judge you rightly.”

Thus Vytautas cunningly deceived them, summoning them to himself from the city, supposedly wishing to judge them according to truth, giving each a part of the inheritance of their principality. They believed his crafty words, and all the brothers Svyatoslavichi, all the princes of Smolensk and the boyars, came out to him with gifts, with love and honor.
