In the city of Kiev there was an even greater tremor, the greatest of all. In the Caves Monastery the stone Church of the Most Holy Mother of God split into four parts; Metropolitan Cyril was there, and Prince Vladimir, and the boyars, and a multitude of people had gathered; for it was a feast that day, the day of the memory of Father Theodosius.

The stone altar was shaken too, when the Food and Drink had already been brought upon it; and stones falling from above crushed all of it; but the whole altar did not fall, nor its top.

In Pereyaslavl-Russky the Church of St Michael split in two; the vault of three arches also fell, with the roof; and it crushed the icons, the chandeliers with candles, and the candlesticks; this happened on one day and at one hour throughout the whole land, during the Divine Liturgy.

On the 10th day of the same month some saw the sun rise early in the morning, and it was triangular; then it remained a little while like a star; and in that form it vanished; then it rose in its usual form.

On the 14th day of the same month, at the third hour of the day, the sun began to vanish before all people’s eyes; only a little of it remained, and it was like a three-day-old moon; and then it began to grow again. Many thought it was the moon moving across the sky, since it was full moon then; others thought it was the sun moving backward, because small patches of cloud from the north were quickly moving toward the sun, southward.

On that same day and hour the same thing happened, and even more terrible, in Kiev: before everyone’s eyes the sun became the moon; and pillars red, green, and blue appeared on both sides of it; fire also came down from heaven like a great cloud onto the Lybed Stream; all the people took leave of their lives and thought the end had come; and they began to kiss one another and ask forgiveness; and they wept bitterly, and all cried out to God with tears. But the All-merciful God carried that terrible fire across the whole city without harm; and it fell into the Dnieper River; there it vanished. Eyewitnesses who were there at that time told us this.

In the same year they began to paint the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God in Suzdal, by the efforts of Bishop Mitrofan. The pious Prince George had built that church on the former site, destroying the old foundation, and made it more beautiful than the first.

In the same year Prince Svyatoslav destroyed the stone Church of St George in Yuryev, because it had grown dilapidated and begun to fall apart; his grandfather Yuri Vladimirovich had built it. And Svyatoslav began to build a new church, even more wondrous than the former.
