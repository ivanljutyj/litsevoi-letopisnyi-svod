And the Novgorodians grew sorrowful, wept, and gathered a veche. And there was murmuring and great outcry in the veche. And they began to weep and mourn for their prince, Mstislav Mstislavich, recalling his love, courage, and victories over enemies.

And after deliberating, they sent their envoys to him, that he might forgive their folly and all their malice, and come to them in Novgorod to reign.

And he, having heard them, came to them in Novgorod on the eleventh day of February, and there was great joy in Novgorod.

And he seized Khokhot Grigorievich, viceroy of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, and another of his noblemen, Yakov Stanislavich, and all his courtiers and all his people who had remained in Novgorod, and bound them with iron fetters and cast them into prisons.

And the Novgorodians came to love him and joined with him in unanimity: that in death and in life all should be one.

In the same year, Prince Mstislav Mstislavich of Novgorod, together with Prince Vladimir Rurikovich of Smolensk and Prince Vladimir of Pskov, gathered to march with an army against Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich at Torzhok, and sent to him, saying: “Go, brother Yaroslav Vsevolodovich, from Torzhok to your patrimony, Suzdal; if you do not go, there will be battle between us.”

And he sent to them, saying: “As Torzhok is your patrimony, so it is mine,” and he did not go.

In the same year died Grand Prince Rurik Rostislavich of Kiev, who had reigned in Chernigov.

In the same year Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich married in Pereyaslavl Russky, taking as his wife the daughter of Prince Gleb Svyatoslavich of Chernigov.

In the same year Prince Konstantin Vsevolodovich of Rostov founded a stone church of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God in the city of Yaroslavl, on his own courtyard.

In the same year Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich of Pereyaslavl went from Pereyaslavl Russky against the Polovtsians and met them on a river; they fought fiercely, and many fell on both sides.

The Polovtsians crossed the river by another route, while the Rus knew nothing of it, and they killed many Christians; they took Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich himself captive alive, killed Dmitry and Ivan, renowned bogatyrs, and slew many boyars and commanders, and returned to their own land with many captives, and led the Grand Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich himself away with them.
