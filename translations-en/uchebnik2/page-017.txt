They killed Monomakh's Vasilko Marich, and they struck down many princes; and Grand Prince Yaropolk, son of Vladimir Monomakh, barely escaped to Kiev with his brothers and with only a small retinue, on the eighth day of August.

In the same year the Olgovichi came again with the Polovtsians and took Trepol, Kholiap, and Detinesk, and came to Kiev, wishing to fight Grand Prince Yaropolk Vladimirovich and his brothers; but God did not permit them to do such evil.

But Grand Prince Yaropolk with his brothers could have fought them, yet he did not want bloodshed, for he feared God; and he made peace with them. And the Olgovichi went back again to Chernigov, while the Polovtsians went into the Field beyond the Don.

In the same year Simeon, Bishop of Vladimir in Volhynia, reposed.

In the same year the most reverend Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed Manuel the eunuch as bishop in Smolensk, on the twenty-sixth day of April. In the same year the most reverend Michael, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, appointed another bishop, Theodore the eunuch, in Vladimir-Volhynia, on the sixth day of May.

In the same year Konstantin Dashkov, posadnik of Novgorod, fled from Novgorod to Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich in Vyshgorod, and many other men with him; and they gave the office of posadnik to Yakun Miroslavich. In the same year in Ryazan, during a raid, they killed the Pecheneg warrior Temir-Khozya.

In the year 6645 from Adam (1145 from Christ).

In the year 6646 from Adam (1146 from Christ).

The Novgorodians with the Pskovians joined together secretly and began to murmur against their prince Svyatoslav, son of Oleg, grandson of Svyatoslav, great-grandson of Yaroslav, great-great-grandson of Great Vladimir; and they planned, having sworn secretly, to drive Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich out from Novgorod and to take again for themselves in Novgorod Prince Vsevolod, son of Mstislav, grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, great-grandson of Vsevolod, great-great-grandson of Yaroslav, and of Great Vladimir his forefather, whom they had earlier driven out from Novgorod. And so they planned secretly, having sworn, that no one should find out.

And from Novgorod went the tysyatsky Konstantin Ivankov and Dashko Yakshich, and from Pskov went Zhiryata Dashkov and Mikhalko Yanovich and others with them, their fellow conspirators; they went secretly to Vyshgorod to Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich, saying to him: "Come, prince, again to reign over us in Novgorod; all wish to see you again as prince among them, both the Novgorodians and the Pskovians."

And Vsevolod Mstislavich with his brother Svyatopolk and with all their retinue left Vyshgorod and came to Pskov. And this was heard in Novgorod; and Prince Svyatoslav Olegovich was terrified and trembled with great fear.
