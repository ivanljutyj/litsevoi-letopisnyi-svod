That same autumn, Vladyka Ioann, Archbishop of Novgorod, was tonsured into the schema of the great angelic image on the fifteenth day of November. And on the twentieth day of January he left the bishop’s house, having been on the see of the Bishop of Novgorod for thirty years less three.

That same winter, Prince Yaroslav Vladimirovich—grandson of Andrei, great-grandson of Ivan, great-great-grandson of Ivan, and great-great-great-grandson of Daniel of Moscow—went to Lithuania.

That same autumn there was a fire in Moscow. That same autumn in Novgorod they built the stone church of the holy martyr Euphemia in the Plotnitsky End. That same winter, during Great Lent, the Novgorod posadnik Kirill Dmitrievich reposed.

That same spring, during Great Lent, on the tenth day of March after the nefimon, a son, Vasily, was born to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich. His birth came about thus: when the day on which he was to be born arrived, his mother began to suffer greatly and was already almost at death; the grand prince was in great sorrow over this. At that time there lived a holy elder in the monastery of St John the Forerunner under Bor, beyond the Moscow River, known also to Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich. And the grand prince sent to him, asking the elder to pray for the princess. The elder answered the man sent to him: “Go and tell the grand prince to pray to the Lord God and to His Most Pure Mother, the Theotokos, and to the martyr Longinus the centurion, for he has been given by God as a helper to all your family in all good deeds. And do not grieve for your princess; she will recover and will bear you a son this very evening, your heir.” And so it all happened.

When the child was born, at that very hour the confessor of the grand prince, who was sitting in his cell in the monastery of the Transfiguration of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ, heard a knock at the door of the cell and the words: “Go and give the name to Grand Prince Vasily!” He quickly rose, took what was needed, and went out of his cell, but saw no one sent for him; he wondered that the one who had called him had gone so quickly.

And he went to the prince’s court. There a man sent for him met him and said that the priest should come and give the name to the newborn son of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich. The priest asked him whether he had come for him earlier. He answered: “No.” And when he arrived, he named the infant Vasily, as had been told him before.
