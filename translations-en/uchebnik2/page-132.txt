After that, the people of Vladimir also joined with Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich, and confirmed by kissing the cross with them that they would do them no harm, and came out of the city with crosses to meet Mstislav and Yaropolk.

Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk, entering the city of Vladimir, comforted the people of Vladimir, and, having divided the Rostov authority, sat down to reign; and Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich they joyfully installed to reign in the city of Vladimir in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, having concluded an agreement. The people of Vladimir offered no resistance to Prince Mstislav Rostislavich and his brother Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich, but they did not wish to submit to the Rostovites, because those had mocked them, saying: We shall set fire to and burn down the whole city of Vladimir and install our posadnik in it; for they are our serfs, stonecutters, carpenters, and ploughmen, and the city of Vladimir is a dependent town of our Rostov region.

Then the Rostovites installed Prince Mstislav Rostislavich, grandson of Mstislav, great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, to reign among them on the throne of his grandfathers and fathers.

That same winter Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich of Vladimir married, taking as his wife the daughter of Prince Vseslav of Vitebsk, and was wedded to her in the city of Vladimir in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God.

That same winter the people of Smolensk drove Prince Yaropolk Romanovich, grandson of Rostislav, great-grandson of Mstislav, great-great-grandson of Vladimir Monomakh, away from Smolensk; and they installed Prince Rostislav Mstislavich to reign in Smolensk on the 30th day of January, on Tuesday of Meatfare Week.

In the year 6684 from Adam (the year 1184 from Christ)

Princes Mstislav and his brother Yaropolk Rostislavich sat in reign in the land of Rostov and Suzdal, and distributed cities to posadniks, to their boyars and their druzhina.

These began to cause the people many troubles by fines and heavy tributes, imposed many duties on them, and thereby caused no little sorrow; for the princes themselves, being still young, obeyed the boyars in everything, and the boyars taught the princes to strive for the possession of greater wealth and for extortion.

And Prince Yaropolk Rostislavich was incited by his counsellors to evil and wickedness, to take away from the Most Pure Mother of God of Vladimir the cities and districts, and villages, tributes and church dues which the blessed Prince Andrew Bogolyubsky gave to the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God—all the gold and silver, and the sacred vessels, and the palace keys where the hidden church treasury is kept—he took it all away.
