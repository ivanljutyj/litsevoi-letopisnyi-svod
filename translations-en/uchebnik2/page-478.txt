At that time Ivan Vasilyevich, the tysyatsky, was in Mamai’s Horde, and many disorders occurred.

And they found on that priest a sack of poisonous herbs, began to question and torture him, and then sent him into confinement to Lake Lache, where Daniil Zatochennik lived.

Then, after that battle at the Vozha, the Tatars fled, driven by the wrath of God.

And they returned to the Horde to their tsar, but they turned not to the tsar, but to Prince Mamai, who had sent them.

Since their tsar, who at that time ruled in the Horde, held nothing and dared do nothing against Prince Mamai, and Prince Mamai possessed seniority, and Mamai ruled all in the Horde. Only the name of the tsar was heard, but all deeds came from Mamai; to tell the truth, name, glory, and deeds—all then belonged to Mamai.

On the Captivity of the Ryazan Land by Mamai. Then Prince Mamai, seeing the exhaustion of his retinue, and other princes, nobles, and alpauts killed, and many of his warriors killed, was aggrieved and greatly grieved, and became enraged with fierce wrath.

And that same autumn, in the year 6887 from Adam (1387 from Christ), in the month of September, Prince Mamai gathered his remaining force, and having gathered many troops, set out on campaign swiftly and without warning in a surprise raid against the Ryazan land. And Grand Prince Oleg Ivanovich of Ryazan knew nothing of this.

And when the Tatars came into his land, he did not have time to gather and did not stand against them in battle, because he was unprepared.

And he fled to this side of the Oka, abandoning his cities.

The Tatars, coming, took the city of Dubok and burned it, and burned Pereyaslavl, and burned the other cities.

And they ravaged and burned the volosts and villages, and having taken many captives, returned to their own lands, and made the Ryazan land desolate.

Oleg of Ryazan, after the Tatar departure, saw his land empty and burned by fire, and the Tatars had taken all his wealth and property; and he was aggrieved and greatly grieved.

And very few people, having escaped from that captivity from the Tatars, began to settle and build dwellings in the Ryazan land, since the whole land was empty and burned by fire.

In that same year, in the month of September, died Prince Daniil, son of Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich.
