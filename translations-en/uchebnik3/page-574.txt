RUS' (1562–1566 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 22

On the Sending from Mtsensk with Captives

And in August Prince Pyotr, son of Prince Vasily Gorensky, sent from Mtsensk with captives, five Crimean Tatars: fifty Crimean men had come to the Mtsensk borderlands, and Prince Pyotr routed them utterly. And those captives said that the Crimean tsar had gone against the Circassians, but had not gone against the frontier of the tsar and grand prince.

And the sovereign went to Moscow, and sent the tsars and tsareviches to their patrimonies; and he released the Circassian princes, at their request, to the Circassian land; and they kissed the cross to the sovereign on this, that they with all the Circassian land would serve the sovereign until the end of their lives: wherever the sovereign sends them for service, there they are to go. And the sovereign sent with them to the Circassian land Andrei Shchepotev to oversee the fulfillment of their oath.

And the tsar and grand prince came from Kolomna to Moscow on Friday, August 18.

On the Founding of a Town at the Shatsk Gates

In that same year a town was founded in Meshchera at the Shatsk Gates on the Shat River; and the voivodes were there with troops for its defense—Prince Dmitry Semyonovich Shastunov and Stefan Sidorov; and Boris, son of Ivan Sukin, built it.

On the Lithuanian Envoys

In that same summer, in August, envoys came to the tsar and grand prince from Sigismund Augustus, King of Poland—Pan Stanislav Stanislavovich Dovoyno, voivode of Polotsk, and Ivan Ostap Volovich, and the scribe Pyotr Semeshko—concerning a perpetual peace; and they did not reach agreement on a perpetual peace, but at the envoys' request the tsar and grand prince concluded with the king a truce for two years, from the year 7062 from Adam (1562 from Christ) from the Day of the Annunciation to the year 7064 from Adam (1564 from Christ) to the same Day of the Annunciation.
