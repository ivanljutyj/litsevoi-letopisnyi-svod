On August 18, Thursday, the sovereign came to the cathedral church of the Nativity of the Most Pure and, having sung a prayer service, fell before the icon of the Most Pure and before the wonderworking image of Sergius the Wonderworker, praying with many tears for a long time and secretly lifting up prayers known to God alone; and he received blessing from the archpriest and from all the priestly order that was present there.

And the sovereign rode beyond the Volga River, and he ordered the guard regiment and the left-hand regiment to cross upstream of him, and he sent Tsar Shigalei in boats to Gostin Island.

Also, with the artillery train, the sovereign sent in boats his boyar Mikhail Yakovlevich and his clerk Ivan Vyrodkov, and he ordered the towers and log breastworks that had been prepared to be brought, so as to set them opposite Kazan. And here the sovereign spent the day and waited until all the regiments had crossed.

And he sent to the Kazan River and ordered many bridges to be built, for the season was then rainy and the waters in the rivers were high.

And the sovereign went beyond the Kazan River

On the 20th day of the same month, Saturday, the sovereign went beyond the Kazan River; and there Tsar Ediger-Magmet sent a captive with a letter to Tsar Shigalei, and he wrote proud and foul words, reviling and reproaching the Orthodox faith and the pious tsar, and he also blasphemed Shigalei, and declared himself ready for battle.

The pious tsar, hearing the impious reviling of the Christian faith, asked mercy of God and strove himself to suffer for the faith. And he encamped at Teren-Uzyak, while all the regiments took position on Tsarev Meadow and down along the Volga.

And the sovereign ordered the military equipment to be unloaded from the boats and arranged, so that he might go to the city. On Sunday and Monday the sovereign stood there.

And then Kamai-Murza, son of Prince Usein, came to the sovereign to serve, and with him seven Cossacks; and he told the sovereign that about two hundred of them had intended to come and serve the sovereign, but the Kazanians, learning of it, had intercepted some of them. And he spoke to the sovereign about Kazan: “In the city Tsar Ediger-Magmet has taken evil counsel with the Kazanians, that they do not wish to submit to the sovereign; and his like-minded men, Kul Sharif the mullah and qadi, and Zeinesh, prince of the Nogais, and the sovereign’s traitors Prince Chapkun (this one is among his dignitaries), and Prince Islam, Alikei Narykov, Kebek prince of Tyumen, Prince Derbysh — these lead the whole land to evil; and the supplies in the city are great. And they have planned thus: they sent to the Arsk abatis your sovereign’s traitors Prince Yapancha and Chapkun’s nephew Shunak-Murza, and the Arsk prince Yavush, and ordered all the people to be gathered at the abatis,”
