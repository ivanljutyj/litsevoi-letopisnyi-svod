RUS (1560–1561, from V.Kh.) Book 21

In the year 7060 from Adam (the year 1560 from Christ)

In the month of September there came to the sovereign his boyars and voevodas from Kazan and from Novy Gorod Sviyazhsky: Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov with his companions. And Prince Yuri petitioned on behalf of Tsar Shigalei concerning the sovereign’s favor, that he had made him tsar in Kazan, and he sent word to the sovereign asking that he grant him additional yasak from the Mountain Side, and that he wished to send his noble envoys to petition about this and about other matters; and Prince Yuri brought to the sovereign sworn and concluded charters, as to how Tsar Shigalei was to reside in Kazan and how our sovereign’s town of Sviyazhsk was to stand. And the sovereign bestowed great favor upon his boyars and voevodas.

In the same year Tsar Shigalei sent from Kazan to the sovereign tsar and grand prince a courier, the Kazan man Kulai-Murza, son of Bibars Rastov, and Muraley-Murza, yesaul of Gorodets, with word that he was releasing his noble envoys: the Kazan karach Prince Shirin-Muraley, son of Bulat, and his palace official Shabas-Prince Shamov, and Abdula, a noble Kazan bakshey. And from the boyar Ivan Khabarov and the dyak Ivan Vyrodkov a boyar’s son galloped up with a letter, and they wrote that the Kazanians were not releasing the sovereign’s captives according to the sworn charter, and Tsar Shigalei tolerated this, fearing unrest.

And the sovereign showed favor to Kulai-Murza and Muraley of Gorodets and sent them back to Tsar Shigalei in Kazan; and to the noble Kazan envoys, Muraley Prince Bulatov and his companions, he ordered his boyar and voevoda Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky in the town of Sviyazhsk to assign escorts to Moscow and to provide provisions during their journey through the Mountain Side and through other towns to Moscow.

In the same year 60 the sovereign sent his envoy Sevastyan to Astrakhan, and with him he sent the envoy of Tsar Emgurchei to witness the oath of Tsar Emgurchei, concerning which his envoys had petitioned, and to bring him himself and all his land to the oath.
