On the Campaign from the Town of Sviyazhsk

On that same day the sovereign set out by the Volga for Vasil-gorod and Nizhny Novgorod. And the sovereign arrived by boat at Nizhny Novgorod, and there he was met on behalf of his tsaritsa by his boyar Prince Fyodor Andreevich Bulgakov; on behalf of his brother Prince Yuri Vasilievich by his okolnichy Vladimir Vasilievich Morozov; and on behalf of the metropolitan by boyar Ivan Kuzmin and Elizar Sobolev. They greeted the sovereign with his God-granted patrimony, the Kazan tsardom, and with many humble petitions praised his labors and feats. And the townspeople and priests met him with crosses by the boats, together with the archimandrite and all the church clergy, and also a multitude of people; and when they saw the pious tsar, all fell prostrate. And with great tears they gave great thanks to God for His ineffable gift. And they offered great praise to the sovereign and cried out to God a nationwide prayer for him: “Multiply, most merciful God, the years of his life, for he has delivered us from such poisonous serpents, from which we suffered for many years! Strengthen him, O Christ, for many years!” And such grateful weeping arose among the people that the priests even stopped singing because of their great weeping, and they praised the sovereign with many thanksgivings and called him their deliverer.

On the Journey from Nizhny Novgorod

And from there the sovereign rode on horseback to Balakhna and on to Vladimir. And there his boyar Vasily Yuryevich Trakhaniot came to the sovereign from his tsaritsa Anastasia and announced to the tsar sovereign that, by God’s sending, his tsaritsa had given birth to a son, Tsarevich Dmitry. The pious sovereign shed inexpressible tears of joy and said: “What shall I render, O Lord, for Thy blessing? Thou hast multiplied Thy mercy upon me, a sinner!” And he promised to favor Vasily. And the sovereign himself went from Vladimir to Suzdal, to the Monastery of the Protection of the Mother of God, and having served a service of supplication there, he went on to Yuryev and to the Life-giving Trinity, to the wonderworker Sergius. And when he came to the monastery, the former metropolitan Ioasaph, the abbot, and the brethren met him outside the town with crosses. And he entered the church of the Life-giving Trinity, fell before the icon, and for a long time with tears sent up prayers to God. He also came to the wonder-working relics of the venerable father Sergius and shed many heartfelt tears with prayer, and placed all his hope in God and in His most pure Mother and in the great wonderworker; to the abbot and brethren he spoke great words of petition for their labors and struggles, for by their prayers the sovereign had obtained all good things. And the former metropolitan Ioasaph, the abbot, and the brethren with tears bowed low to the sovereign for the Christian deliverance.
