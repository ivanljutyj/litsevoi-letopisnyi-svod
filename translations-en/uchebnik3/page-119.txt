But the accursed Mehmed, having learned of the killing of the eastern beylerbey, wept over him for a long time, for he loved him greatly for his bravery and wisdom.

And, enraged, he went himself through his own gates with all his forces, and ordered cannon and arquebuses to be aimed at the emperor, fearing lest he come out of the city with all his men and attack them.

And having come, the godless one took his stand opposite the ruined place and ordered that fire from cannon and arquebuses be opened first, so that the townspeople might retreat.

Then he sent Baltaule Pasha with many regiments, and against the emperor he sent three thousand chosen men and ordered them to try to capture the emperor—either they themselves would be killed, or they would kill him with arquebuses.

But the commanders and all the nobles, seeing the onslaught of the infidel who had come with a great force, led the emperor away, so that he would not perish in vain, needlessly.

But he, weeping bitterly, said to them: “Remember the word that I spoke to you and the vow I made: let me die here with you; do not hinder me!” They answered: “We will all die for the Church of God and for you, lord!”

And taking him, they led him away from the people, long exhorting him to leave the city; and having given him a last kiss, groaning and weeping, they all returned to the appointed place.

Baltaule came up with many regiments; the Greek commanders met him near the ruined place, but could not hold him back, and he entered the city with all his regiments and attacked the townspeople, and there was a slaughter fiercer than all the previous ones. And the stratagi and megistans and all the nobles fell.

Some broke away from the throng to inform the emperor, while those killed—both townspeople and Turks—were beyond number; and the three thousand fought and hurried about all parts of the city, hunting for the emperor.

But the accursed Mehmed again, quickly arming himself, began to send his army through all the streets and to lie in wait for the emperor at the gates, while he himself remained only with the Janissaries; having fortified himself in the camp with cannon and surrounded himself with arquebuses, for he feared the emperor.

And the emperor, hearing God’s will, went into the Great Church and fell to the ground, asking God’s mercy and forgiveness of his sins, and having repented, took leave of the patriarch and all the clergy, and of the empress, and bowed to the ground on all sides.

Having partaken from the patriarch of the holy and dread mysteries of Christ, he went out of the church. And immediately all the clergy and all the people who were there cried out, and women and children, whose number was beyond counting, with such weeping and groaning that it seemed the Great Church itself was shaken, and their voices, I think, reached the heavens.
