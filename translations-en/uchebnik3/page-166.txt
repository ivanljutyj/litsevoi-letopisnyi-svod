And he came into that land at the mouth of the Black River on Thursday of St Thomas’s Week; and from there he set out on rafts with his horses; and, having sailed to the town of Anfalovsky, he came down from the rafts and went from there on horseback to the Upper Land, to the small town of Iskor, while he sent Gavrila Nelidov to the Lower Land—to Uros, Cherdyn, and Pochka—against Prince Mikhail.

Prince Fedor had not yet reached the small town of Iskor when the Permians met him on the Kolva with an army, and there was a battle between them, and Prince Fedor prevailed and captured their commander Kach in that battle.

And from there Prince Fedor went on to Iskor and took it, and captured their commanders Burmot and Michkin, while Zynar came to him with a safe-conduct; he also took other small towns and burned them.

And Gavrila, setting out, ravaged those places to which he had been sent. And afterward Prince Fedor came to the mouth of the Pochka, where it flows into the Kolva.

And there he waited for all his men, and brought the captives there as well.

Having cut down timber and built a small fort there, they settled in it and brought all that land to an oath to the Grand Prince.

And from there Prince Fedor sent Prince Mikhail to the Grand Prince, together with those Burmot, Michkin, and Kach; and he himself remained there in the small town of Pochka, and what he had taken from those men—from Burmot, Michkin, and Kach—he sent to the Grand Prince: sixteen forties of sables, a sable coat, twenty-five bolts of cloth, three coats of mail, a helmet, and two damask sabers.

In the same year the wicked Tsar of the Horde, Akhmat, rose up against the Russian land with many forces, incited by the Lithuanian King Casimir.

Learning of this, the Grand Prince sent his commanders to the bank with many forces: before all he sent Fedor Davydovich with the men of Kolomna, and Prince Daniil and Prince Ivan Striga were sent with many men to the bank for the Feast of the Deposition of the Robe; and on that same day the Grand Princess Maria set out for Rostov.

After that the Grand Prince sent his brothers with many men to the bank.

In that same year, on the twentieth day of the month of July, at the third hour of the night, fire broke out in Moscow in the settlement at the Resurrection on the Moat, and it burned all night and the next day until noon; and a great multitude of households burned, and twenty-five churches alone burned. And it burned from there along the bank to the Exaltation at the Vostry End, to Vasilyevsky Meadow, and to Kulishka; and upward from the Resurrection along the moat to the Ascension on the Moat, to Yar, to the stone Epiphany, to the Resurrection on Dmitrovskaya Street, and from there to St Evpatiy, and to Kulishka as well. For there was a great storm then, and the fire was carried over eight households.
