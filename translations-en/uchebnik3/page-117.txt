A certain flaburarius with many Saracens, among whom were five men of great stature and fearsome gaze, furiously attacked the Greeks and beat the townspeople mercilessly.

Then the protostrator and his son Andrew hastened out of the city against the Turks with many men, and there was a terrible battle. Then from the wall three brothers saw these five Saracen men beating the townspeople; they leaped down from the wall and attacked them, and fought them so fiercely that the Turks marveled, seeing that they did not fear being killed by the Saracens; and the townspeople killed two of the Saracens.

And then, with a shout, a multitude of Turks rushed upon them; but the brothers, all three, escaped from them into the city unharmed.

At the breach the battle did not cease but grew still greater. The Turks with great force overcame the townspeople; the stratigi and nobles together with Zustunei fought hard, and many fell on both sides. But what God wills cannot be overcome: a cannonball flew, struck Zustunei, hit him in the right shoulder, and he fell to the ground as if dead. His boyars and all the people bent over him, crying and weeping, and carried him away; and all the Franks went after him.

The Turks, hearing the weeping and confusion of the people, rushed with shouts with all their regiments, trampled the townspeople and drove them into the city, killing and cutting them down. The commanders and all the townspeople saw the Turks’ advance and fled. And final destruction would have overtaken them and the city, had the emperor not hastened with his chosen men.

The emperor, arriving, met Zustunei still alive and wept bitterly over him, and then attacked the Turks with his chosen men and beat them mercilessly, having only one sword in his hand; and whomever he reached with the sword he cut in two; and if he struck a horse, it fell under them, for neither armor nor horse’s strength could withstand his sword. The Turks, seeing the emperor’s strength and courage, fled from him. And so they drove the Turks to the ruined place. And a multitude of people gathered there, and the townspeople killed them countless, stabbing them like pigs, until they passed the ruined place; and those who fled to the sides along the streets were killed there. And so by Divine providence that day the city was delivered; the Turks withdrew from the city, and the townspeople fell to rest, and there was nothing more that night.

The emperor with the patriarch and all the soldiers went to the Great Church and gave thanks to the Lord God and His most pure Mother; and they praised the emperor; and some said that the emperor himself was lifted up in his heart; but they also felt the departure of the impious, yet did not know God’s will.
