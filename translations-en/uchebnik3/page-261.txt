On the Battle at Trudy

In the same month, on the tenth day of July, Tatars came, Horde Cossacks; their leader was Teme, and with him were 220 Cossacks. They came to Aleksin, to the Voshana volost, and after plundering they went back; and the Grand Prince's pursuit set out after them, Fedor Koltovsky and Goryain Sidorov, and there were 64 of them in all, and a battle took place between them in the Field between the Trudy and the Bystraya Sosna; and of the Grand Prince's pursuit 40 men were killed, and of the Tatars in that battle 60 men were killed, and the other Tatars who were going to the Horde were wounded and died on the return journey.

On the Court

In the same year they built for the Grand Prince a wooden court behind the Church of the Archangel Michael on the Yaroslavl place.

On the Strelnitsa

In the same year, from the Frolovskaia strelnitsa to the Nikol'skaia, they laid the foundation, and they laid a new strelnitsa with a secret passage above the Neglinnaia.

On the Capture of the Town of Mtsensk

In the same month of August, Grand Prince Ivan Vasil'evich sent his voivode Prince Fedor Telepen' Obolensky with an armed force against the town of Mtsensk because of the wrongdoing of its inhabitants; and they captured the town of Mtsensk and conquered the land, and took their voivode Boris Semenov, son of Aleksandrov, and many others captive and brought them to Moscow.

In the Year 7001 from Adam (1501 from Christ)

In September the Grand Prince sent his envoy Konstantin Zabolotsky to the Crimean Tsar Mengli-Girei in the Crimea, and to Wallachia he sent Ivan Andreevich Pleshcheev.

On the Arrival of the Lithuanian Envoy

In that same autumn, on the sixth day of October, an envoy, the lord Stanislav Glebovich, came from Lithuania from Prince Alexander of Lithuania to Grand Prince Ivan Vasil'evich concerning border affairs.

In that same autumn, in November, Yushko Elizarov fled from the Grand Prince to Lithuania.

On the Vorotynsky Princes and the Capture of Lithuanian Towns

In the same year Prince Semen Fedorovich Vorotynsky and his nephew Prince Ivan Mikhailovich came to the Grand Prince to serve, with their patrimonial lands, and on the way Prince Semen took the Lithuanian towns of Serpeisk and Meshchevsk under the authority of the Grand Prince.
