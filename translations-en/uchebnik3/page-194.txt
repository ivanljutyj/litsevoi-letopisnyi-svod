On the next morning, Friday, at the first hour of the day, he was buried; and no one from the city or the surrounding country was at his burial, only the priests of that monastery and his disciples, the elders, monks tonsured by him; one secular priest happened to be there then, the confessor of Prince Andrei the Younger, named Nikita. This same Nikita, and others of the brethren, disciples of Paphnutius, related that there was no one to sing or read the funeral canons, but only one of his disciples, named Innocent, read the funeral service with many tears; and all the rest were bathed in tears, so that many of their tears fell and poured onto the ground. The brethren then numbered eighty-five.

By birth, they say, he was from the village of Kudinovo, from father Ivan; his teacher was Elder Nikita, and Archimandrite Markel tonsured him into the monastic rank, and he was then twenty years old, and he spent thirty years in monastic life, in the priesthood, and in the abbacy. And when he went to that place where he founded the monastery of the honorable Nativity of our most holy Lady Theotokos, and first erected a wooden church, then built a stone one, and painted it wondrously, and adorned it with icons and books and all church vessels, so that even the autocrats of the Russian land marveled. And during his life they came for blessing and prayers for them, as if to the ancient venerable fathers of our land; likewise other princes and boyars, and other Orthodox Christians, both from the Lithuanian land and from other countries, asking blessing and prayers from him. And he was in his monastery thirty-three years.

On the thirty-first day of May, from Friday to Saturday, on the eve of All Saints, there was a great frost, so that the puddles froze, and all kinds of garden and orchard vegetables were killed, and all the produce.

In the same month, while the envoys of the Grand Prince were still in Novgorod, a revolt occurred among the Novgorodians: they held a veche, and coming upon Vasily Nikiforov, seized him and brought him to the veche, and shouted: "Traitor, you were with the Grand Prince and kissed the cross to him against us." He answered them: "I kissed the cross to the Grand Prince in that I should serve him truly and wish him good, and not against my sovereign Great Novgorod, nor against you, my lords and brothers." They killed him without mercy, at the denunciation of Zakhary Ovin; and afterward they killed that Ovin too, with his brother Kuzma, in the archbishop's courtyard. And from that hour they went mad like drunkards, each saying whatever he would, and again they desired the king.

The Grand Prince, having heard of this from his envoys and from the local posadniks who were well-disposed to him, that all had scattered, and that such an evil tumult had arisen among them after their first crime, as also before their Shelon defection.
