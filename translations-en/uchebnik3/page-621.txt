And Ivan Cheremisinov exchanged messages with the tsar, and the tsar petitioned for himself, saying that he had betrayed the sovereign under compulsion, and asked that the sovereign show him mercy; and Tsar Derbysh and all the land of Astrakhan swore an oath to Ivan that they would go into the city and serve the tsar and grand prince.

And Andrey Tishkov was ransomed for women: fifteen women from the Astrakhan captivity were given for him. And the commanders strengthened themselves in the city so that they might sit there without fear, and they stationed streltsy and Cossacks along the Volga, and took away all freedom from the Nogais, and from the Astrakhan men the fishing grounds and all the ferries.

On the Dispatch from Astrakhan

In the same month, on the 23rd day, Ivan Cheremisinov and Mikhail Kolupaev sent Cossacks from Astrakhan, and they wrote that the Nogais had come, that Prince Isma'il had made peace with Yusuf’s sons, and had sent away Shikh-Mamai’s sons.

And Arslan-murza brought Shikh-Mamai’s sons against Yusuf’s sons. And Yusuf’s sons killed Arslan, and they fought among themselves for three days, and many murzas and Tatars were killed on both sides; but Tsar Derbysh did not go to them in the city, he betrayed his oath, and Ataman-Duvan, the voivode of the Crimean tsar, who had been sent to him since spring with men and cannon and handguns to guard him from the tsar and grand prince and from Yusuf’s sons, strengthens him and turns him away from our sovereign.

On the Arrival of Cossacks to the Sovereign at Taininskoye

In the same month of September, on the 29th day, Cossacks Rosloy Stepanov and Trukhan Pavlov came from Dyak Rzhevsky from the Field to the sovereign at Taininskoye. The dyak had beaten the Crimean men and had come from the Field to Putivl, and brought nine prisoners, and the prisoners said that the Crimean tsar had gathered his men, fearing the coming of the tsar and grand prince, but had now dismissed his men and had himself gone to the Crimea.

On the Arrival to the Sovereign from Vishnevetsky

In the same month Mikhail Eskovich came to Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich of all Rus' from Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky to petition that the sovereign show him favor and allow him to serve him, for he had departed from the king in Lithuania and had set up a town on the Dnieper on the island of Khortitsa opposite Konskiye Vody, near the Crimean nomad camps.
