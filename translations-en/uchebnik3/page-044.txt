And among the holy Eastern and Western teachers, that some speak of the procession of the Holy Spirit — from the Father and the Son, while others said the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and through the Son, but all spoke of one thing, although they set it forth differently. The Greeks, however, said that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, but not in the sense that the Son is separated from the Father; yet the Latins are in doubt about this. Their doubt consisted in this: since those did not say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son in two principles and two spirations; and therefore the Greeks say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. The Latins, however, presented this to them and thus decided according to their own mind and reason, that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. The Father is the source and beginning of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; therefore the Holy Spirit does not proceed from the Son in two principles and two origins, but the origin and beginning of the Holy Spirit is one, which they confess to this day. Yet all minds are united in agreement and unity — in the name of the Holy and Undivided Trinity: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. And the present sacred holy ecumenical council, convened in Florence, decrees thus: such faith is accepted as truth among all Christians and is affirmed as the only true one. We all so believe, we all so decree, we all, having agreed, sign. This faith is the faith of the holy apostles, this is the faith of the holy fathers, this is the faith of all Christians who truly believe in Christ, and they always think and confess thus, since the Holy Spirit from the Father with the Son is always consubstantial, and the Son possesses Him from the beginning, because He proceeds from one beginning and one origin: this is what the holy teachers and fathers say, who hold that the Holy Spirit is from the Father and through [..] the Son, just as the Father, and all that the Father has, He gave at the birth of His Son; is not the Father also the Holy Spirit, Who proceeds from the Son, Whom the Father possesses from the beginning. Let us also recall the above-mentioned phrase “from the Son”; it was invented out of some necessity of that time and, after examination, turned into a symbol. Let us also speak of wheat bread, unleavened and leavened, worthy for transformation into the Body of Christ: every priest acts according to the rule and custom of his church, Eastern and Western. Concerning the departed, we hold thus: those who passed into God’s peace in the true Christian faith and confession, but did not manage to bring forth fruits of repentance for their sins as their spiritual fathers enjoined, will be purified after death through torments and memorial services performed after their death, through liturgies, through alms and other offerings made for the faithful, as the holy Church commands. Pleasing are the souls which after baptism were not defiled by sins, or were purified from their sins by repentance while still in the body, and, having left the body, as was said before, will together be taken up into heaven and will be deemed worthy to see in three persons, as each will be worthy according to his deeds.
