That same spring the Grand Prince sent a river-fleet army against Kazan; the chief commander was Vasily Fedorovich Obrazets. And from Novgorod they set out for Kazan on May 26, a Tuesday.

That same spring the Grand Prince's viceroy in Novgorod the Great, Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Obolensky Striga, died; and he was brought from there and laid in Suzdal at the Church of the Saviour in the St Euthymius Monastery.

On the Strife of Metropolitan Gerontius with Vassian, Archbishop of Rostov, over the Kirillov Monastery.

In the same year there was a dispute between Metropolitan Gerontius and Vassian, Archbishop of Rostov, over the Kirillov Monastery.

For Gerontius began, instructed by Prince Mikhail Andreevich, to take it away from the Rostov bishopric; the monks of the Kirillov Monastery, exalting themselves in their vain arrogance and wealth, did not wish to be under the authority of the Rostov bishopric or to obey the Archbishop of Rostov, forgetting the word of the Lord, that everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted; they were troubled and shaken like drunken men, according to the prophetic word: all their wisdom was swallowed up; and imagining themselves wise, they became foolish, for their malice blinded them, and they prompted Prince Mikhail.

Prince Mikhail began to speak to the metropolitan about this. The metropolitan, obeying Prince Mikhail, gave him a charter that Prince Mikhail was to administer the monastery, and the Archbishop of Rostov was not to interfere in it.

Archbishop Vassian began to petition him not to encroach, against the canons, upon his boundaries; but he did not listen to him.

Archbishop Vassian petitioned Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and began to ask for a trial according to the canons with the metropolitan.

The Grand Prince sent to the metropolitan; but the metropolitan did not obey him.

The Grand Prince sent to take the metropolitan's charter from Prince Mikhail, and ordered a council of all bishops and archimandrites to be convened in Moscow, and gave the archbishop judgment against the metropolitan. And there was a great inquiry.

The metropolitan feared the council's judgment and entreated the Grand Prince; the Grand Prince reconciled the metropolitan with the archbishop and tore up the charter; and he ordered that the Kirillov Monastery be administered in all things by the Archbishop of Rostov according to ancient custom.
