And the grand prince ordered the tsar that Tsar Magmed-Girey, as had been agreed, should send against his common enemy, the Lithuanian King Zhidimont, into the Polish land his eldest son, the kalga Bogatyr-tsarevich, and other tsareviches, “and I for my part will send my commanders.”

And by the command of Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich, his brother Magmed-Girey, Tsar of Crimea, sent against the Lithuanian King Zhidimont into the Polish land his eldest son, the kalga Bogatyr-tsarevich, and other tsareviches, his brothers and his children, and many of his people with a great host.

And the kalga Bogatyr-tsarevich went into the Polish land and waged war almost as far as Krakow itself.

And Konstantin Ostrozhsky, joining with the Polish people, with the Podolians and the Volynians, went against the tsarevich.

And Bogatyr-tsarevich defeated many commanders and killed many Polish lords and men, and captured many others, while Ostrozhsky himself barely escaped. And in that battle the tsareviches killed Prince Vasily Chetvertensky, and Prince Alexander Boryamsky, and Princes Vasily, Alexander, and Lev Koretsky, the son of Ferlei, hetman of Krakow, Lord Stanislav, starosta of Kamenets, and his brother Jan Skarutsky, and they killed many other Polish people, and others they took and led away with them, and they plundered the Polish land, doing a service to Grand Prince of All Rus Vasily Ivanovich, and returned to Crimea with many captives.

The Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich Sent [His Troops] to Wage War on the Lithuanian Lands

In that same year Grand Prince of All Rus Vasily Ivanovich sent against his enemy, the Lithuanian King Zhidimont, with a host Avdovlet-tsarevich, son of Akhturt of Shiban, and his boyar and commander Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuisky, namestnik of Vladimir, and his other commanders: Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Vorotynsky, Prince Fyodor Vasilyevich Obolensky Lapata, Prince Vasily Andreyevich Mikulinsky, his okolnichy and commander Andrey Vasilyevich Saburov, Andrey Nikitich Buturlin, Yuri Ivanovich Zamyatnin.

And from the Novgorod and Pskov frontiers the grand prince ordered his commanders to march against the Lithuanian king: Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich Gorbaty, namestnik of Pskov; Prince Danilo Bakhteyar; Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Obolensky Kasha; Ivan Vasilyevich Kolychev; Dmitry Grigoryevich Buturlin; and his other commanders with many men.
