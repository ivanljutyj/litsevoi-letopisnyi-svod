And he stationed the commanders, and ordered siege fortifications to be built, and ordered an assault on the city. And there was an assault on the city (and they did not take the city, but many people on both sides were killed; in the city a tsarevich, the son of Safa-Girey’s younger wife, was killed by cannon fire, and the Crimean prince Chelban. And it would have been necessary to stand there a long time), but at that time the weather changed, strong winds and great rains began, and excessive wetness, and it became impossible to assault the city because of the wetness, and it was impossible to fire cannons and handguns.

And the Tsar and Grand Prince stood before the city for eleven days, and rains fell every day, and there was great warmth and wetness; and the small rivers overflowed.

And the Tsar and Grand Prince, seeing such disorder, departed from the city of Kazan on Tuesday of the Assembly Week, on the twenty-fifth day of February; because of the thaw it was impossible to carry the military equipment back. The Tsar and Grand Prince ordered the troops to go before him — the great regiment, the left-hand regiment, and the guard regiment — and they went ahead of the Tsar and Grand Prince.

And the Tsar and Grand Prince came to the Sviyaga, rode up onto the Round Mountain, and with him were some of his warriors, about thirty men, and he surveyed the greatness of that mountain. And God, the Lover of Mankind, seeing his piety and great faith and desire for labor for the Orthodox faith, placed in his heart the light of divine understanding by God’s grace, that he should set up a city on that mountain for the Kazan affair and thereby cause hardship to the Kazan land, and raise holy churches in the city, so that light might shine in a dark place, and the Orthodox faith might shine forth, and the infidel faith might vanish from that place, and saving grace might shine forth in a pagan place. And the pious Tsar came to that place — Round Mountain — and by God’s good pleasure he loved that place, where the city was to be and holy churches were to stand.

And he went to Nizhny Novgorod in his order, as he had gone from the city of Kazan; and the Tsar and Grand Prince came to the capital city of Vladimir in the fourth week of Holy Lent; and the Tsar and Grand Prince came to Moscow with his brother Prince Yuri Vasilyevich on the twenty-third day of March, in the fifth week of Holy Lent.

In that same spring, on the eighteenth day of May, on the sixth Sunday after Easter, Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of All Rus showed favor to Prince Vladimir Andreevich, married him, and took for him Evdokia, daughter of Alexander Nagoy; and the wedding of Prince Vladimir Andreevich took place in the courtyard of the Tsar and Grand Prince, and Macarius, Metropolitan of All Rus, married them in the cathedral Church of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God.
