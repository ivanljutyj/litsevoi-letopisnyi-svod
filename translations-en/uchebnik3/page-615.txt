Prince Pyotr Ivanovich Sent Soldiers Out of Kazan

And boyar Prince Pyotr Ivanovich sent many soldiers out of Kazan to take prisoners for interrogation; and in many places they fought and killed, and brought the Arsk and Poberezhny sides to utter distress; and all the rest came to Kazan and petitioned for forgiveness for their offenses.

On the Dismissal of the King’s Envoys

In the same month the sovereign dismissed the envoys of King Philip of England, Richard and George, and with them he sent his own envoy, Nepeya of Vologda, with a letter; and he wrote to the king of amity and negotiations, and the tsar and grand prince showed favor to the king’s people: in his realm he ordered a ship harbor to be built on the Dvina, and granted free trade throughout the realm, and also established a compound in Moscow.

On the Report of Dyak Rzhevsky

In the month of May Dyak Rzhevsky wrote from Mamaylug with his Cossacks that captives had run to him and said that the Crimean Tsar had come out to the Konskiye Vody with all his people and intended to go against the borderlands of the tsar and grand prince.

A Man of Putivl Fled from the Crimea

And in the same month a man of Putivl, Demeshka Ivanov, fled from the Crimea and said the same: that the Crimean Tsar had set out and intended to come against the Tula lands or the Kozelsk lands, and the tsar had ordered supplies to be gathered for the whole summer.

And in accordance with that news the tsar and grand prince decided together with his brothers and boyars that he must go to Serpukhov, and at once he prepared with his men to go to Tula and, going out from Tula into the Field, to await the tsar and deal with them as God should help. And therefore the sovereign decided that it was necessary, having gone out from Tula into the Field, to wait to see which borderlands the Crimean Tsar would make for—Ryazan, or Odoev and Kozelsk—so that the tsar and grand prince might be able to reach all places before he came to the borderland.

On the Tsar’s Arrival from the Trinity and the Campaign to Serpukhov

In the same month the tsar and grand prince came from the Trinity on Thursday, before the Petrine Fast, and went to Serpukhov, and with him were Prince Vladimir Andreevich and Tsar Simeon of Kazan, and the boyars, and many commanders, and he came to Serpukhov on the eve of the fast.
