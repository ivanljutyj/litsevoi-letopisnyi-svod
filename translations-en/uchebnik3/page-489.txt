In the same year, in the month of August, the town of Mikhailov was founded on the Prona River, and the commanders who founded it were Prince Alexander Ivanovich Vorotynsky and Mikhail Petrov, son of Golovin.

And when they were founding the town and began to dig the place where they had decided to build the cathedral church of Archangel Michael, they found on the spot where the altar was to stand an image of Archangel Michael of ancient painting, in no way damaged, clad in silver.

And the tsar and grand prince, and Metropolitan Macarius, sent priests for that icon and met it with honor.

And after serving thanksgiving prayers, they sent it to the same place, to the church where the icon had appeared, and gave praise to God for this.

On August 13, Tsar Shigalei and all the commanders of the tsar and grand prince came to the Kazan mouth and stood from the Volga to Bezhbolda, and two versts upstream, and down beyond Kazan to Tsarev Meadow; and Tsar Shigalei sent his chamberlain Prince Shabas and his equerry Prince Batikei with all his baggage train into the town of Kazan and ordered his court to be established.

And on the next day, August 14, there came to the tsar and the boyars Kulsharif-mullah and Maamet-seit, son of Maysyr-seit, and with him all the shaykhs and shaykhzadas, imams and mullahzadas, azis and dervishes, and Kudaygul-ulan, and with him all the ulans, and Prince Muraley, and with him many princes and murzas; and the boyars ordered the oath charter to be read to them, stating how the sovereign had granted them and how they were henceforth to live. And they all began to speak about the Mountain Side, that they could not do it in such a way as to divide the land; and the boyars answered them according to the sovereign’s instruction: “God has taught the sovereign to act thus, and in this is his righteousness: the sovereign has set a town on the Sviyaga, and the mountain people conquered you; otherwise that can never be; it will be as God has decided.” And many contentious words were spoken about this, but by God’s mercy, according to the sovereign’s instruction, his boyars did just as he commanded them.

On the Enthronement of Shigalei as Tsar

And Tsar Shigalei and the whole land of Kazan gave oath to the sovereign on this, that they would not enter the Mountain Side nor the half of the Volga; and that fishers should fish on their own halves; and the tsar sealed the oath charters with his seals, and many noble Kazan people set their hands to them.

And the Kazan princes brought the Russian captives that they had, and for the rest they gave oath that they would free them all; and if the sovereign learns of Christian captives held in infidel bondage, then whoever is found to have them shall be put to death; and if the Kazan people do not free all the captives, the sovereign is free to seek them as much as merciful God helps him.
