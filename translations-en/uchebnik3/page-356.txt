For God, the Creator of all, knows that for this the king’s heart continually burns with greatly desired zeal for prayer to God. May not only the Russian land but also all Orthodox Christians perish without a shepherd! And therefore the merciful God broke the bonds of his childlessness and granted him to bear an heir to his realm, like him in wisdom, courage, and correction of piety.

And in the year 7038 from Adam (1538 from Christ)

On the 25th day of the month of August, on the memory of the holy apostles Bartholomew and Titus, in the seventh hour of the night, a son was born to the great prince Vasily Ivanovich from the great princess Elena Glinskaya, and he was given the gracious name Ivan in honor of John the Forerunner.

And there was great joy in the city of Moscow over the birth of a son to the sovereign, and throughout all the land people rejoiced with inexpressible joy.

And not only all the Russian realm, but everywhere all Orthodox Christians rejoiced.

This most glorious royal birth was once written of by certain wisdom-loving toilers in a very colorful and ample manner; here only a part of it is shown. O, what care God has and what providence He has for us, the unworthy!

If the autocrat had been granted a daughter by God, then only our one Orthodox tsar would have rejoiced; now all Orthodox Christians in all ends of the universe have been filled with joy.

On the baptism of the tsar’s child

When the time came for this royal child to be born a second time by water and the Spirit, then his Christ-loving father, the autocrat Vasily, moved with great joy to go with him to the most renowned monastery of the great luminary Sergius the Wonderworker, the Life-Giving Trinity.

And there, as a certain sacred offering, he brought his royal child, giving him to God, where he was enlightened by holy baptism on the 4th day of September, on a Sunday, on the memory of the holy hieromartyr Babylas, archbishop of Great Antioch, through the sacred ministry of the superior of that monastery, a virtuous man, the hegumen Joasaph, who later became metropolitan of all Rus.

And the sponsors from the holy font for the tsar’s son were two diligent men of prayer, grown old in virtues; both were tonsured in the monastery of the Wonderworker Paphnutius of Borovsk.
