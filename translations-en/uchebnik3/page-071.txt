After the death of Leo, Pope of Rome, Benedict became pope in Rome; a Latin by birth, learned in Latin and Greek books. And seeing God's people corrupted by heretics, in two languages, Greek and Latin, he wrote a confession of the Orthodox faith, "I believe in One God," and commanded all Christians, Greeks and Latins, to read it thus for the exposure of the aforementioned heretics, and not to heed their lies. Then, having written an epistle, he sent it to the four patriarchs: of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem, so that after his death they should not commemorate the popes of Rome in church until they should confess in writing what they believed, since, he said, an evil heresy had taken root in Rome, and many people were perishing who slavishly venerated the Holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son and performed the service on unleavened bread, and other evil heresies were spreading. After the reign of Pope Benedict, Pope Adrian became the new pope in Rome, and then Leo, then Stephen, after him Paschal, Eugene, Valentine, Gregory, Sergius, whom they had formerly called the Swine's brood, Leo, John of the English lineage, Benedict, Nicholas, Adrian, John, Martin, Adrian, Stephen. All these were Orthodox popes and like-minded with the four patriarchs: of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem.

About Formosus

In the years of the reign of the wise Emperor Leo, son of Basil the Macedonian, there ruled in Rome the accursed Pope Formosus, who adhered to a secret heresy derived from certain heretics of Prince Charles. But pious people elected and proclaimed him pope, not knowing of his heresy; yet he wrote a deceptive confession of the Orthodox faith and sent it to the four patriarchs.

And when this accursed Formosus had been elected pope, he began to teach these heresies to God's people; and so all the Latins accepted the evil teaching and departed from the Greek kingdom and the four patriarchs.

After the first Pope Formosus, who laid the foundation of the heresy, the second was Pope Boniface, the third Stephen, the fourth Roman, the fifth Theodore, the sixth John, the seventh Benedict, the eighth Leo, the ninth Christopher; all these were like-minded with Formosus.
