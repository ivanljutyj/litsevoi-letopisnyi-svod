Once he dined with them and saw them sitting in the refectory during the meal, but their heads were not on their shoulders. Seeing this, the venerable one wept, stopped eating, and told his disciples that because of their pride their heads would be cut off. And so it came to pass.

And many others he sent to Moscow and ordered them thrown into prison; and he ordered the lesser people released to Novgorod. And Vasily Kazimir, Kuzma Grigoriev, Yakov Fedorov, Matfey Selezenev, Kuzma Gruzov, Fedot Bazin, and others the Grand Prince sent to Kolomna and ordered fetters put on them.

The Grand Prince himself went from there to Lake Ilmen, to the mouth of the Shelon, and came to a place between the shore and Korostyn on July 27, a Saturday.

On the Battle on the Dvina

And on that day God helped the voivodes of the Grand Prince, Vasily Fedorovich Obrazets and Boris Slepts, who were then with the Ustyuzhans and Vyatchans; all their host was only four thousand less thirty men. And they had a battle on the Dvina with Prince Vasily Shuysky, who had with him a host of twelve thousand.

The battle between them went thus: coming out of the boats, both hosts on foot began to fight at the third hour of that day, and fought until sunset; they wrestled hand to hand and cut at one another; and they struck down the banner of the Dvinians.

And they killed three standard-bearers under it: when the first was killed, another picked it up; and when that one was killed, then the third took it; and after the third also was killed, they seized the banner.

And so the Dvinians were broken, and by evening the regiments of the Grand Prince prevailed; they killed many Dvinians and Zavolochans, and others drowned; and their wounded prince threw himself into a boat and fled to Kholmogory.

Many they took alive with their hands. Then they also captured their towns and brought the whole land to kiss the cross for the Grand Prince. And there were killed then from the host of the Grand Prince fifty Vyatchans, and one Ustyuzhan, and Boris Sleptsov’s man Migun; but all the rest were preserved by God.

On the Installed Lord Feofil, and on the Novgorodians, How They Came to the Grand Prince with a Petition

On that same day there came from Novgorod to the mouth of the Shelon in boats by Lake Ilmen Feofil, designated for the archbishopric, with posadniks, tysyatskys, and other city people from all the ends of Novgorod. And first they began to petition the boyars and voivodes of the Grand Prince, and they asked intercession before the brothers of the Grand Prince, so that they, together with them, and the boyars themselves would intercede with the Grand Prince for them.
