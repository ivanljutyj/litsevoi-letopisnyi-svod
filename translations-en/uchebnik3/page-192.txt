In the same summer, on the 11th day of the month of July, an envoy came to the Grand Prince from the Great Horde, from Tsar Akhmat, by the name of Bochuka, summoning the Grand Prince to the Tsar in the Horde; with him were fifty Tatars, and with him merchants with horses and all kinds of goods—five hundred and fifty.

On the Taking of the Crimea

In the same year Tsar Akhmat of the Horde sent his son with Tatars and took the Crimea and the entire Horde of Agi-Zirei, and drove out Agi-Zirei’s son Mengli-Girey, whom the Turks had installed.

The Thunder Was Terrible

On the 31st day of the month of August, at the first hour of the night, there was a terrible thunderclap and great lightning, as if it would burn everything, and very heavy rain. That thunder at the Simonov Monastery struck the top of the stone church down to the windows in the neck of the drum, and it passed through the church and damaged much masonry, and it pierced the wall at the front doors through and through, and smashed the festal icons, damaged the icon of the Dormition and the prophets, and slightly scorched the Deesis with lightning; but the icon of the Most Pure Hodegetria, standing in its setting at the place of veneration on the left side, was not touched by anything, though its large stones fell out all over the setting—every one that had been attached to it, each of a grivna.

In the year 6985 from Adam (the year 1485 from Christ)

On the 3rd day of the month of September, at night, the moon was eclipsed at the August full moon.

In the same month, on the 6th day, the Grand Prince sent back the Tatar envoy Bochuka, and with him his own envoy Matvei Bestuzhev.

In the same month, on the 26th day, a church in Moscow burned, the Ascension on the Moat.

In the same month there was a fire in Novgorod the Great: it started at the Church of St Nicholas on Buevishche; several households burned, and stone churches burned out with living people inside, and some collapsed, and people burned; and they do not remember such a fire in their time.

That autumn was dry and cold; the river froze on the 12th day of November, and on the Day of the Entry there was rain, and from that time there were several great frosts, but there was no snow.

On the 9th day of January, from Thursday to Friday, snow began to fall, and a little the next day; but there had not been even a span of it all that winter.
