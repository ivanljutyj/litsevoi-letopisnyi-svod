And they did not dare to conclude the council without him, because then rumor would spread among the people.

And many times the emperor and the patriarch, by the counsel of Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, forbade Mark to act so, saying: “Obey Pope Eugene; it will be well for you, and you will not suffer for your obstinacy.” And Mark received many harsh prohibitions from the emperor and the patriarch, and also from Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia.

Also after them, Pope Eugene himself, instructed by Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, threatened Metropolitan Mark with torments. But he was not at all frightened by them; rather, he remembered the Prophet David, who said: “He who dwells in the help of the Most High shall abide in the shelter of the God of Heaven”; and again: “He shall call upon Me, and I will hear him; I am with him in sorrow; I will deliver him and glorify him, and I will satisfy him with length of days, and show him My salvation.” Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, trusted in the Lord God, who saves people, and consoled himself with the thought of God. And afterward the pope persuaded him and sent him much gold, so that he would obey him and fulfill his will; but he counted all this as nothing and said to them: “Not for gold, not for silver have I come here, but for the truth of God.” But they could not seduce him. The Greek emperor John of Constantinople, and the ecumenical patriarch Joseph, and Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, and the other metropolitans and bishops, and all who were with them, disputing and assembled from the day of Alexius, Man of God, on the seventeenth day of March, to the day of Sisoes the Great, on the sixth day of June, strove to attain what they desired. Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, did not always come to their assemblies, and without him they could do nothing, nor could they forbid him to act so.

And then the emperor, the patriarch, all the metropolitans and bishops, and Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, together with Pope Eugene, convened a great council, resolving various questions and contending, and they gathered for many days. The Latins, asserting their own position, said: “The head of all churches is the Roman Church, and the Christian faith is one, both in us Romans and in you Greeks, but the rule is not the same for all; among you Greeks too there are many rules not identical and not one in all things: the Egyptian, Alexandrian, and Jerusalem rules are not the same, but differ and establish not identical rules. Also the Constantinople, Holy Mountain, Studite, and many other rules are likewise not the same.
