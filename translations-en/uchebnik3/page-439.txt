And the Grand Prince, having received those tidings, sent to Putivl to his governor Fyodor Pleshcheev Ochin, and ordered him to send a scouting party into the Field across the road.

And Fyodor sent Gavrila the interpreter, and Gavrila came from the Field and told the Grand Prince that he had come upon tracks of a great army in the Field: many people, some hundred thousand or more, were marching toward Rus.

And on those tidings the Grand Prince sent from Moscow his boyar and commander Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky, and ordered Prince Dmitry and all his commanders to leave Kolomna and take position with all their men along the bank, in those places where commanders had previously stood against the tsars on the bank.

And the Grand Prince sent from Moscow Tsarevich Shigalei of Shiban and his boyar Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov, and with them he sent many men of his court, and ordered the Tsarevich and Prince Yuri to stand on the Pakhra.

To Vladimir he sent to his boyar and commander Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuisky and his companions, ordering them to stand in Vladimir; and to Meshchera he sent to Tsar Shigalei, that with the princes and murzas and all his people he should go to Vladimir as well; and from Kostroma he ordered his commander Prince Fyodor Ivanovich Shuisky and his companions to go to Vladimir with all their people to join the tsar.

In July the scout Aleksei Kutukov came to the Grand Prince from the Field and told the Grand Prince that he had seen many people on this side of the Don at the Snova: regiments marched all day long, and he did not wait to see their end.

And with that news the Grand Prince sent to the bank to Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky and his companions, and ordered them to send after the commanders to Ryazan and to the Utra, and to Serpukhov, and throughout all the border lands, that they should join him at once.

On the 28th day of July Tsar Saip-Girei came to the town of Osetr, and many Tatars attacked the town, while Nazar Glebov with the townspeople fought the Tatars beyond the outer settlements and killed many Tatars; and Nazar Glebov took nine Tatars alive and sent them to the Grand Prince.

And those Tatars told the Grand Prince that the Crimean Tsar Saip-Girei himself had come, and with him his son Tsarevich Men-Girei and the whole Crimean Horde, and Prince Semyon Belsky, and from the Nogais Prince Bakii with many people, and the Turkish Tsar's men with cannon and arquebuses, and many newcomers from other hordes and lands.

And with those tidings the Grand Prince sent to the commanders on the bank to guard his cause most firmly.
