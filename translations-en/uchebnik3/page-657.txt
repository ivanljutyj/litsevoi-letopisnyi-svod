He entrenched himself behind a great ditch and enclosed himself all around with a wagon train; and the voivodes come to his camps and kill people, while the Master fires from the camps with cannons and arquebuses, and does not ride out to the voivodes, but advances on Ryngol; and with the voivodes there are few men, and even those are very weary; and prisoners also say that the Yuryev ratmans and posadniks are in communication with the Master. It was at their summons that the Master came: they indicated that the fighting men had all been sent away with the voivodes and princes, with Peter and his companions, and those who remain are very exhausted.

In the same month there came from Cherkasy to the tsar and grand prince the sons of the senior prince Temryuk of Kabarda, Bulgeruk-murza and Salnuk-murza, to petition that the sovereign might favor them, his slaves, and protect them from the Shevkal sovereign; and with them came the sovereign’s envoy, the serving Tatar Baybera.

From Astrakhan Ivan Vyrodkov wrote that the sons of Prince Ismail had fallen away from him, Tanbay-murza and Inbay-murza, with their uluses, and had gone to the Crimean tsar, and many murzas had gone to Crimea to serve, and their uluses with them.

And Prince Ismail also wrote to the tsar and grand prince that his sons and many murzas with their people had gone from him to Crimea. And the tsar and grand prince ordered that his own affairs and also his person be protected from the Crimean tsar.

On the Baptism of the Cherkassian Princes

And the tsar and grand prince, in accordance with their petition, ordered Saltan-murza to live at his court and ordered him to be baptized, and he was named Prince Mikhail, and the sovereign ordered him to be taught reading and writing; and to Bulgar-murza the sovereign ordered it to be said that he would arrange defense for them against Shevkal as soon as opportunity permitted.

On the Envoys from Yurchinch

In the same month there came to the tsar and grand prince from Yurchinch, from Tsar Khadchim, the envoy Tinisha-Zey with companions, with gifts and a great petition for friendship, that the sovereign should order free passage to be given to merchants and should protect him.

On the Envoys from Bukhara

In the same month envoys came from Bukhara, from Samarkand, from the Bukharan and Samarkand tsar, Azam-Azi and Shikh, with gifts and a petition of friendship, asking for passage for merchants and for protection.

On the Envoys to the Sovereign from Tyumen from the Tyumen Prince

In the same month envoys came from Tyumen from the Tyumen prince with gifts and petitioned that the tsar and grand prince would hold them under his sovereignty.
