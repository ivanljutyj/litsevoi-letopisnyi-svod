And the Great Prince's boyar sons, Novgorod landowners, who had come at that time to Prince Andrey and had gone with Prince Andrey to Novgorod—those boyar sons, Andrey Ivanov, son of Pupkov, and Gavrila, son of Vladimir Kolychev, with companions, thirty men—the Great Prince ordered to be flogged with the knout in Moscow, and ordered them to be executed by death, hanged along the Novgorod road, not together, but at intervals up to Novgorod.

And Prince Andrey sat in that captivity for about half a year and reposed, and was laid in the Church of the Archangel Michael, on the left side, at the head of Prince Yuri Ivanovich.

In the year 7046 from Adam (the year 1546 from Christ)

On the third day of April, on Wednesday of the fifth week of Holy Lent, in the second hour of the day, reposed the blessed Great Princess Elena, daughter of Prince Vasily Lvovich Glinsky, wife of the blessed Great Prince Vasily Ivanovich; and she was laid in the Church of the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ, beside Great Princess Sophia, wife of Great Prince Ivan Vasilyevich.

In the year 7047 from Adam (the year 1547 from Christ)

There was enmity between the boyars of Prince Vasily and Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuysky and Prince Ivan Fyodorovich Belsky. And in that quarrel the Shuysky princes and other boyars ordered the Great Prince's clerk Fyodor Mishurin to be stripped in their courtyard (before the Great Prince's princelings, boyar sons, and courtiers), and they laid him naked on the block and ordered him killed.

In that same month of April the Shuysky princes and other boyars seized the boyar and equerry Prince Ivan Fyodorovich Ovchina and placed him under guard where Prince Mikhail Glinsky had been held, and they starved him to death on the sixth day.

And Prince Ivan Fyodorovich Belsky they placed under guard in his own courtyard, and the boyar Mikhail Vasilyevich Tuchkov they exiled from Moscow to his village.

In that same year reposed Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuysky. That same winter, in February, Metropolitan Daniel was removed from the metropolitan see by the boyar Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuysky and his advisers. That same winter Joasaph Skripitsyn, abbot of the Sergius Monastery, was chosen to the metropolitanate, and was installed and consecrated in February on the sixth day, on Thursday of Meatfare Week.
