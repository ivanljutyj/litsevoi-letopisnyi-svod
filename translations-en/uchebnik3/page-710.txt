And Emperor Ferdinand wrote in his charter that the tsar and grand prince, at his request, should release Fürstenberg, Master of Livonia, for whom Wolf-Jank, Master of the German Order, his counsellor, had petitioned. And Master Wolf-Jank wrote with a petition for the release of Master Wilhelm Fürstenberg, that the sovereign might show him favor, settle him in the Livonian land, and send him back to Master Wolf-Jank at Rugodiv. But he did not send a charter with him to the emperor, because before that there had been no communication between the new emperor and the tsar and grand prince. And whereas before that Master Wolf-Jank had sent his man Ivan Wagner to petition the tsar and grand prince, in the year seventy-one, to petition that the tsar and grand prince show favor to him, Wolf-Jank, since he wished to obtain the Prussian lands, and that the tsar and grand prince march against the King of Lithuania; but the envoys, when they came, petitioned not according to the master's former petition, and their sovereign Master Wolf-Jank did not wish to march against the King of Lithuania; and the tsar and grand prince ordered the master's envoys to be refused.

In that same September, on the 25th day, at night, the court of the okolnichy Afanasy Andreyevich Buturlin beyond the Neglinka, opposite the open place, caught fire, and the church at the end of Petrovskaya Street, and there burned along Petrovskaya Street on both sides thirty-one households, and the Church of the Resurrection of Christ between Petrovskaya and Dmitrovskaya streets, and twenty-seven cells were destroyed.

That same night, September 25, on the memorial of Sergius the Wonderworker, after the sovereign's departure, when the tsar and grand prince had set out from the monastery, in the Trinity-St Sergius Monastery the refectory and the monastery treasury in the chambers burned out, and many bells melted, and all the kitchens, and the guest court, and the service yards, and the cells burned: the upper row from the Red Gates to the dam behind the refectory and as far as the cellarer's. And a cell near the cellarer's treasury caught fire, and all the supplies stored for the monastery's use burned; the brethren were left without a single day's supply of food.

And the tsar and grand prince, hearing of such divine will, was greatly grieved, for he had great faith in the Life-Giving Trinity and in Sergius the Wonderworker, and gave them a thousand rubles for cells and for the building of the monastery, and gave other necessities to the monastery, and ordered the monastery to be restored as it had been before.
