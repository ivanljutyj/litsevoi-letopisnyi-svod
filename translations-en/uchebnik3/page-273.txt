The aforementioned Kazan princes, hearing that the grand prince's commander Prince Semyon Ivanovich Ryapolovsky was coming with a great host, fled from Kazan to Tsar Mamuk; and Tsar Mamuk the Shibanid, learning of the grand prince's great host in Kazan, returned to his own lands.

And the Kazan tsar Magamet-Amin sent the grand prince's commander Prince Semyon Ivanovich Ryapolovsky with all his host from Kazan back to Moscow on Semen's Day, since he no longer expected Mamuk's arrival at Kazan.

In the year 7005 from Adam (1505 from Christ)

Soon Tsar Mamuk the Shibanid learned that the grand prince's commander had left Kazan with all his host for his own lands, because in Kazan they had betrayed Tsar Magamet-Amin and sent word to Mamuk from Kazan.

Concerning the Kazan Tsar Magamet-Amin

And Tsar Mamuk quickly came with his army before Kazan, with a great Nogai host and with the Kazan princes.

And the Kazan tsar Magamet-Amin, fearing treason on the part of his princes, himself fled from Kazan with the tsaritsa and his remaining princes, and came to the grand prince in Moscow in November of the year 7005 from Adam (1505 from Christ); and the grand prince received him with honor in Moscow.

Concerning the Taking of Kazan by Mamuk

Tsar Mamuk advanced upon the city with a great host and took Kazan, because there was no one to oppose him; and he seized the Kazan princes who had betrayed their sovereign—Kalimet, Urak, Sadyr, and Agish with their brothers—and plundered all the merchants and common people.

And soon Tsar Mamuk showed favor to the Kazan princes and released them, and came with them with his army before the town of Arsk.

But the Arsk princes would not surrender their town, and fought them bravely; and at that time the Kazan princes rode away from Mamuk and fortified Kazan, and did not let Tsar Mamuk into the city; and they accused him of treachery because he had taken their princes captive and plundered the merchants and common people.
