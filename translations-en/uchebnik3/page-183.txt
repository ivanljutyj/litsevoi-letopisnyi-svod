And on that day Archbishop Theophilus and Prince Vasily, and all the previously named with them, the posadniks and boyars of Novgorod, ate and drank at the Grand Prince’s dinner; and he dismissed them from his presence.

And after them came to the Grand Prince the street elders: from Slavkova Street two elders, Ivan Kuzmin and Trofim Grigoriev, and from Nikitina Street also two, Grigory Kiprianov Arzubiev and Vasily Fomin; and they presented from both streets a barrel of red wine.

And having come out from Rydino, the Grand Prince stood on the 19th day, Sunday, at Lytna on the Meta River, fifty versts away; and there his boyars and well-to-do townsmen met him: Moisei Fedorov, Luka Afonasov, Konstantin Bogdanov Babkin, Yakim Ananyin, Demeniy Andreev Emetsky, each with a skin of wine; many also of the merchant elders, and many merchants, and well-to-do people met the Grand Prince, all with wine.

On the 20th day, Monday, the Grand Prince stood at Plashkino, twenty-five versts away; and there the posadniks met him: Foma Andreevich Kuryatnik, Fedor Akinfov Glazoemtsov, Yakov Slizen, Nikita Fedorov, Alfery Mikhailov the tysyatsky, Nikita Kirilov, son of a posadnik, and the Novgorod boyars: Klimenty Filipov, Saveliy Semenov, Luka Astafiev, Matfei Ananyin, Zakhary Grigoriev; and the well-to-do townsmen: Stepan Zheleztsov, Falely Astafiev, all with a skin of wine; and the elder of Gorodishche, Ivashko Abakumov, with all the people of Gorodishche, a barrel of white wine, one hundred and fifty apples, and a dish of grapes.

On the 21st day, the Feast of the Entry of the Most Pure, Tuesday, the Grand Prince went from Plashkino to his patrimony, Great Novgorod; and on that day many posadniks and well-to-do people met the Grand Prince on the way, and a great multitude of people.

And the Grand Prince came that day to Gorodishche and heard Mass in the Church of the Annunciation, and dined at his own place.

The Vladyka sent Nikita Savin and the brother of Timofei Lunev to the butler and the equerry to hand over fodder; and the Grand Prince did not want them, and was angry with the Vladyka because they were unsuitable for that matter, and he ordered that the fodder not be taken. The Archbishop petitioned the Grand Prince about this through his boyars; and he ordered his vicar Yuri Repekhov to hand over the fodder, and those men were with him in the tax rolls.

And the Grand Prince set aside his displeasure with the Vladyka, and pardoned him, and ordered the fodder to be taken.

And on that same day the Vladyka was with him, and petitioned him, inviting him to eat bread with him; and the Grand Prince did not grant him that on that day.
