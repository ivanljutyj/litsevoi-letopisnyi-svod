And the Novgorodians who fled to Novgorod told of what had befallen them; and there was in Novgorod sorrow and much weeping for those slain and taken captive, and over their own betrayal of the grand prince. According to their custom they began to ring the veche bell, and the whole city assembled at that veche—posadniks and tysyatskys and all the rest of the people; and, not knowing what to say, they were confused and in doubt, and, like drunken men, each said his own. When all had taken thought, they decided to petition their archbishop Evfimy, that he go and entreat the grand prince, that he have mercy on his patrimony and turn away from the wrath of his fury, so that they not perish utterly for their betrayal of him, their lord the grand prince. And the archbishop, seeing the people in trouble, said to them: “My children, because of your violation of the agreement and because of my own sin, I cannot go to my lord the grand prince. But I am merciful: I go to entreat him, that he forgive us this evil, since you not only betrayed him but also raised your hand against him.” And he went to the grand prince, and with him the posadniks and tysyatskys and the men, as was their custom.

Archbishop Evfimy came to the grand prince and began to petition him and entreat him for his patrimony, for Novgorod; but first he petitioned his brothers and boyars. And the grand prince, having heard the entreaty of his pilgrim Archbishop Evfimy, and of his brothers, and of his boyars, and of those Novgorodians who had come with the archbishop, granted them favor; and for his trouble the grand prince took from them ten thousand rubles in their Novgorod silver (this besides the brothers and boyars).

And he sent his boyars to Novgorod, and brought all Novgorod to the kissing of the cross, that they be in obedience to the grand prince and not keep his malefactors and traitors among them. And so the grand prince returned to Moscow.

On the Ryazan Prince

That same spring Grand Prince Ivan Fedorovich of Ryazan died, having been named Jonah in monastic vows; and not long before that his princess had died. His Ryazan principality and his son Vasily he bequeathed to Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich for safekeeping. And Grand Prince Vasily took his son, together with his sister Feodosia, to himself in Moscow, and sent his viceroys to Ryazan to keep it, and to his other cities and volosts. And his son was then eight years old.

On the Yaroslavich

In the same year, on the tenth day of the month of July, the grand prince seized Prince Vasily Yaroslavich in Moscow and sent him into confinement in Uglich; and his son Ivan, by his first wife, and his second wife fled to Lithuania.
