When he was at Volok, his brothers came to him, and all the princes, and his commanders with a multitude of troops. And from Novgorod to Volok came to him the posadnik Vasily Stepanov with a petition that the Grand Prince take pity on them and not march against Novgorod, and set aside his wrath; but the Grand Prince did not accept the petition and marched against them.

And having entered the land of Novgorod, he sent Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Obolensky Striga and Feodor Basenok to Rusa.

They, coming there, took a very great multitude of wealth, since the people who were there had not managed either to flee or to carry off or hide their goods anywhere.

And with that great booty they sent all their men ahead of them, while they themselves, with the commanders and boyar sons, with only a few men without whom it was impossible to be, lagged behind, having sent their own farther ahead, and intended to follow after them to the Grand Prince.

And then tidings came to them, and they themselves saw, that a great host was coming against them from Novgorod: there were five thousand of them; but of these not even two hundred remained. Seeing this, they were afraid. Then they began to say among themselves: “What shall we do? If we do not go against them to fight, we shall perish by our sovereign the Grand Prince, because we have taken booty and sent the warriors away. No, better that we die with them for the truth of our sovereign, and against their treason.”

And they went against them. But between them was a wattle fence and great snowdrifts, and they could not come together. The Grand Prince’s warriors, seeing strong armor on the Novgorodians, began to shoot arrows at their horses; and their horses, as if maddened, began to rear under them and throw them off. They, not knowing such fighting, seemed as if dead, and their hands grew weak; they had long spears and could not raise them as warriors are wont, but lowered them to the ground, while the horses struggled under them. And so they fell under their horses, unable to hold them. And the prophetic word spoken came true upon them: “Vain is the horse for salvation, and in the multitude of his strength he shall not be saved.”

And so they soon fled, driven by the wrath of God; and many of them were slain, and others taken captive.

Then their chief posadnik Mikhailo Tucha was captured, and Esip Nasonov was killed, and many others; but those captured were few because there was no one to take them, since the Grand Prince’s host was small.

The Grand Prince’s commanders and the warriors with them pursued them, killing, plundering, and leading away captive.

And so they returned and came to their sovereign the Grand Prince, all safe and sound.
