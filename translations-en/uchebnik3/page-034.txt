And there was a fierce battle, and God helped the Christians; many Tatars were killed, the tsar’s son-in-law and many princes and Tatars were killed, and they were driven back into the city. Prince Pyotr Kuzminsky and Semyon Volynets were killed then in the city; they pursued the Tatars to the middle of the city, while the other warriors returned from the city.

In the morning the tsar sent to the Russian princes and commanders his son-in-law Elyberdey and the daruga princes Usein Saraev and Usen-Khozya; and Vasily Ivanovich Sobakin and Andrei Fedorovich Goltyaev came to them for negotiations. And the Tatars said to them: “We bring you the tsar’s word: I give you my son Mamutyak, and the princes give their sons as hostages on this condition: if God grants me to be in the tsardom, then as long as I live I will protect the Russian lands, and I will not send for tributes, nor for anything else.” They did not wish to agree to this; then the Tatar princes said to the grand prince’s commanders: “Do you not want this either? Look back.” They turned back and saw their warriors running back, though no one was pursuing them. Thus for our arrogance, for our many sins, the Lord allowed the infidels to overcome the great host of Orthodox Christians; for our people lived unrighteously and destroyed their Christianity, so that a small, weak army of the godless overcame and defeated them; one Hagarene overcame ten or more of ours. The senior princes fled unharmed. This happened on the fifth day of December; killed in that battle were: Andrei Konstantinovich Shonurov, Prince Fyodor of Tarusa, Prince Andrei Loban of Starodub, Nikita Turikov, Semyon Gorstkin, Kuzma Porkhovsky, Ivan Kuzminsky, Andrei Khorobrov, Dmitry Kaka, and many others.

On Metropolitan Isidore

In that same autumn Isidore the Greek, metropolitan of all Rus, set out from Moscow to Veliky Novgorod, and with him went Euthymius, Archbishop of Novgorod; other Russian bishops escorted him with honors.

And Isidore, having come to Novgorod, blessed all; the archbishop, the posadniks, the tysyatskys, the boyars, the merchants, and all Veliky Novgorod showed him honor, and gave him judicial authority according to the old custom, [and gave him all his dues according to the old custom].

Later, in winter, Metropolitan Isidore set out for Pskov, then to the Germans in Rome for the Eighth Council, to Pope Eugene of Rome, to the Greek emperor Kir Kaloyan of Constantinople, and to the ecumenical patriarch Kir Joseph. And the people of Pskov received him with honors.

And he installed Archimandrite Gelasius in Pskov; the people of Pskov gave him judicial authority according to the old custom, and gave him all his dues according to the old custom, and presented him with many gifts.
