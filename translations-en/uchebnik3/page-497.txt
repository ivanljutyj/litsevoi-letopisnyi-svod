He inspects the places where the warriors are to join and muster a host against the godless; and the commanders in the regiments, in the great regiment and in the others, the sovereign likewise orders to prepare for battle and to review the warriors. The sovereign favors the commanders and the boyars’ sons and strengthens them with his word, that they may not be put to shame before the Hagarites, and he says: “The Hagarites have no God and expect no recompense; but we have our Lord God as Master: if we suffer for His name, we shall be crowned with martyrs’ crowns; the time is approaching for us to establish ourselves courageously in the name of the Holy Trinity and for our brothers, the Orthodox Christians.” The commanders and the boyars’ sons were strengthened in mind and rejoice in hope, seeing their pure-minded sovereign, who favors them, preparing for battle, established by grace; all with one voice answer the sovereign: “We are ready, sovereign, to suffer for the Christian faith and for you, the sovereign, even unto death.”

The sovereign arrived at Kolomna; the commanders of his regiment meet the tsar and tell the sovereign that in his regiment all the men are ready to march out against the adversaries; the sovereign tells them where he has chosen a place for the muster against the Crimean Tsar, and also about the commanders and boyars’ sons, that they answered the sovereign wisely.

The sovereign sends to Moscow to his tsaritsa and to his father Macarius, Metropolitan of all Rus, and writes to him thus:

Letter to the Metropolitan

“To the Most Reverend Father our Macarius, Metropolitan of all Rus, Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus bows his head. We have taken counsel with you concerning this: that the Crimean Tsar is marching against our borderlands, and that we, having called God to help, should ourselves go for the deliverance of Christians, and that you should bless us. And now we have come to Kolomna, and our border scouts have come to us and say: they have seen many people who are going to conquer our land. And we, placing our hope in God and His Most Pure Mother and the great wonderworkers, and trusting in your holy prayers, wish to stand against them. And do you, our father, entreat God and His Most Pure Mother and the great wonderworkers, both in council and privately, concerning our sin and all Orthodoxy, so that the Merciful God, through your holy prayers, may not remember our sin before Himself, may not deliver us to foreigners, and may God give us courage and bravery, and chastity, and concord to all Orthodox Christians, since Christ settles the like-minded in His house. And be pleased also, in all holy places, in monasteries and cathedrals, to order diligence in prayer and fasting for this present time.”
