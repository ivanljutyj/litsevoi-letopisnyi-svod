In that same month the tsar and grand prince sent to Kazan to Tsar Shigalei his boyar Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Paletsky, and with him the secretary Ivan Klobukov; and with them he sent to Tsar Shigalei his great bounty—robes and vessels, and money—and also to his tsaritsa, whom he had taken in Kazan from Tsar Safa-Kirei; and to the Kazan and town princes he sent much of his bounty, robes, money, and cloth. And he ordered a gracious word to be conveyed to the tsar and the land of Kazan for their service, and concerning the captives he ordered thus: that according to the sworn charters they should free all Russian captives, and if they did not free the captives, then the sovereign, seeing Christians in captivity, could not endure it, as much as the Merciful God should help him. With Prince Dmitry he ordered the tsar to be told that the tsar, remembering the bounty of Grand Prince Vasily and the bounty of Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan, should serve the sovereign honorably in Kazan according to the sworn charters, as he had promised, and should free all Russian captives, and should firmly secure Kazan for the sovereign and for himself, like the town of Kasimov, so that under him and after him Kazan might remain unshaken, and bloodshed on both sides might cease forever, and in that Tsar Shigalei should be firm.

And the tsar and grand prince wrote to his boyar Ivan Ivanovich Khabarov that he had sent Prince Dmitry to Tsar Shigalei, and that the tsar should release Ivan; and Tsar Shigalei, according to the sovereign’s instruction, released Ivan Ivanovich to Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich.

On the Kazan Envoys

In that same autumn, in October, there came to Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus Kazan envoys from Tsar Shigalei: the noble Kazan karacha Shirin-Muralei, Prince Bulatov, and Shibas, Prince Shamov, and Abdul-bakshey, presenting the tsar’s petition to the sovereign, and they said that the sovereign should show favor: grant the Mountain Side to the tsar; and if he did not grant it, he would not give up all of it; then the sovereign should show him favor by adding yasak from the Mountain Side, as much as he pleased, and the sovereign should show favor and order truth to be established for the tsar and the land of Kazan on that in which the land had given truth to the sovereign. And the sovereign heard their petition and ordered his boyar Ivan Vasilyevich Sheremetev, and Alexei Adashev, and the secretary Ivan Mikhailov to prepare them an answer: that the sovereign would not give from the Mountain Side to Kazan a single coin; “And as for the oath, you told the sovereign that if the Kazanians gave an oath that they would free all Christian captives to the last man, and now they still hold many captives; and when the tsar and the Kazanians free all Russian captives, then the sovereign will also establish the truth, and you will stay here until that time.”

And the envoys petitioned the sovereign concerning that, that the sovereign should send to the tsar and the land of Kazan concerning the captives, and they were waiting for the time when all the captives would be freed.
