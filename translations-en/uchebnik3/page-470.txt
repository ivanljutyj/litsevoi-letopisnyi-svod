And Prince Ivan Turuntay was taken at matins by the New City at the Neglinnaya Gate, as he was trying to enter the city with priests; and Prince Mikhail was seized by Prince Pyotr Shuysky in the settlement, in the courtyard at the Church of the Resurrection by the Good Bell Tower beyond the Neglinnaya River on Nikitskaya Street; and they brought both of them into the city on the 11th day of the same month. And the tsar and grand prince ordered them placed under guard and questioned about their flight; they petitioned that out of fear after the murder of Prince Yury Glinsky they had gone to pray at Okovets to the Most Pure (Mother of God) and had turned aside, not knowing the road.

And after that the tsar and grand prince investigated their offense, and at the intercession of his father Metropolitan Macarius he pardoned them, forgave them their guilt, and ordered them released on surety, because they had made that flight in their folly, out of fear after the murder of Prince Yury Glinsky.

On the Tsar's Campaign against Kazan

In that same autumn Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus, with the metropolitan, his brothers, and the boyars, resolved to go against his foe the Kazan tsar Safa-Girey and the oath-breaking Kazanians for their perjury.

On the 20th day of November, a Sunday, the tsar and grand prince sent ahead of him to Vladimir his commanders, Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky and his other commanders, and ordered them to assemble with their men in Vladimir.

And he ordered Tsar Shigaley to come from Meshchera, and with him the commanders Prince Vladimir Vorotynsky and others, and ordered them to join him at the mouth of the Tsivil River.

And in that same autumn, on the 11th day of December, a Sunday, Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus set out for Vladimir, and he ordered the cannon and handgun equipment to be conducted after him to Vladimir as well.

And the tsar and grand prince arrived in Vladimir on the 20th day of the same month, a Thursday; but the equipment, cannons, and muskets were sent to Vladimir after Epiphany with great difficulty, because heavy rains had fallen and there was no snow.

Then also, on the 6th day of January, the archpriest of the Annunciation, Fyodor Barmin, fell ill; fear came upon him, and he asked the sovereign's leave to become a monk and was tonsured in the Chudov Monastery.

And the tsar and grand prince set out from Vladimir on the 8th day of January, a Sunday, and arrived in Nizhny Novgorod on the 26th day of the same month, a Thursday.

And from Novgorod he set out against his foe the Kazan tsar Safa-Girey and the oath-breaking people of the Kazan land on the 2nd day of February.
