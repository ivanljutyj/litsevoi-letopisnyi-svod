The hirelings of those traitors began throwing stones at those who wished to be for the grand prince. And great turmoil began among them, and they fought among themselves, rising up against one another.

Many of them, however—old posadniks, tysyatskys, leading men, and also prosperous townsmen—said to them: “It cannot be, brothers, that it should be as you say: that we give ourselves to the king and have an archbishop installed by his Latin metropolitan. For from the beginning we have been the patrimony of those grand princes, from our first grand prince Rurik, whom our land took by its own will from the Varangians as prince for itself, together with his two brothers. Then his great-grandson Grand Prince Vladimir was baptized and baptized all our lands—both the Russian and our Slovenian, the Merya, the Krivichian Ves, called Belozersk, the Murom, and the Vyatichi, and others. And from that holy and great Prince Vladimir even until this our lord Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, we have never been under the Latins, nor have we had an archbishop installed from them, as you now wish to install an archbishop from Gregory, who calls himself Metropolitan of Rus; for he is a disciple of Isidore, in essence a Latin.” But those same troublemakers, like the former heretics taught by the devil, wishing to have their own way against Grand Prince Ivan and against his father, and having dared against piety, did not want to submit to the grand prince. Some shouted: “We want the king!”; and others cried: “We want Moscow, for its Orthodox Metropolitan Philip!”

These villains stood against Orthodoxy, did not fear God, and sent their envoy to the king with many gifts, Panfil Selifontov and Kiril Ivanov, son of Makarin, with these words: “The free people of Great Novgorod beat their brows to you, honorable king, that you may become sovereign of our Great Novgorod and lord to us, and command your Metropolitan Gregory to install an archbishop for us, and give us a prince from your realm.” The king received their gifts with love and was glad at their words, and after showing them many honors he sent their envoy back to them with all that they had requested, and he sent them as prince Mikhail Olelkov, son of the Kievan.

The Novgorodians received him honorably, but they did not drive the grand prince’s governors from Gorodishche; and Prince Vasily Gorbaty, brother of the Suzdal princes, who was with them, they sent to Zavolochye, to the outpost on the Dvina.
