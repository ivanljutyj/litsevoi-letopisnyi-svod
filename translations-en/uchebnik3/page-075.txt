The Britons, even in the time of the great Emperor Constantine, had received holy baptism and become pious Christians; they were of one mind with the Greeks and belonged to the same faith as they, but they had no books in their own language and used books in Latin; and they were always enemies of the Romans. The Pope of Rome consulted with his Romans and sent to Britain to the Almanian Prince, and concluded peace and love with him and with all Britain, and confirmed it with many oaths, so that they should always have peace and love with the Romans, fight together against enemies, and firmly keep the concluded peace and love. And he promised to set up a king in Britain for them; and they rejoiced at the opportunity to receive a ruler of royal lineage and in all things became of one mind with the pope and all the Romans. And since the Britons also used books in Latin, the pope spread his Roman laws in their country and seduced them with cunning flattery.

And he summoned their prince to himself in Rome with all his commanders and nobles, and set him up as king; and he wrote a law according to which the pope in Rome is elected by the Latins, and the king in Great Britain is elected by the Almanians instead of the Tsar of Constantinople, that is, the Greek Tsar of Tsargrad.

Also, in the great cities in the Roman realms, in place of the four patriarchs—of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem—he set up his own four patriarchs, whom they call cardinals. And so he completely departed from the pious faith and the Greek law, Christian Orthodoxy, and composed his Roman laws according to his own law and custom; and they were hostile to the Greeks, and they spread their malice, causing turmoil, not submitting to the Greek realm and law, and they inherited a curse, blinded by their pride.

In the same year in Great Novgorod, Archbishop Evfimy built a stone chamber for ecclesiastical affairs and a stone guardhouse in his courtyard. In the same year in Novgorod they built the Church of St Nicholas on Krechva.
