On the Messenger from the Crimean Tsar

In the same month a messenger came to the tsar and grand prince from the Crimean Tsar, Syuyundyuk Tulupov, and the Crimean Tsar sent his messenger Tutai and wrote that he gave an oath that the tsar and grand prince should send him great gifts in Crimea, and the tribute that the Lithuanian king pays, if there was to be truth for truth and friendship; but if the tsar and grand prince did not wish to send for an interpreter, then he would exchange ambassadors. And the tsar and grand prince ordered the ambassadors to be exchanged and ordered the ambassadors to be refused, and did not wish to fulfill all that the Crimean Tsar had proposed, because the tsar had written not as was proper; that did not befit friendship. And Fedor Zagryazhsky also wrote that the Crimean Tsar had given an oath but had stipulated what could not be.

A Messenger Came from Prince Ismail of the Nogai Horde

In the same month Prince Ismail of the Nogai Horde sent his messenger Baiterek, and messengers from other murzas also arrived, and they petitioned that the sovereign show them favor, release to them the murzas Magmet-Kasym-murza Shiidyakov and Bechi-murza, and render them aid against the Crimean Tsar.

On the Release of the Captive Murzas to Ismail

In the month of January the tsar and grand prince released the captive murzas to Ismail in the Nogai Horde, showed favor to Magmet-Kasym-murza and Bichi-murza, and to Prince Ismail’s messenger Baiterek with his companions, and to the service Tatars, and promised to give aid against the Crimean Tsar; and he ordered them themselves, once gathered, to go, or to release their brothers and kinsmen.

On the Sending of Vishnevetsky against the Uluses of the Crimean Tsar

In that same month the tsar and grand prince sent Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky against the Crimean uluses, and with him the sovereign sent the Kabardian Circassian murza Kaklych Kanukov to Kabarda and to Circassia, and ordered them, once gathered, to come all together to Prince Dmitry’s aid; and he was sent to Circassia, to Kazan, and to Astrakhan by boat, and from Circassia they were to go by campaign past Azov. And with Prince Dmitry the sovereign sent Ignaty Zabolotsky with the zhiltsy and Shiryay Kobyakov with boyar scions, and Daniil Chulkov, and Yuri Bulgakov, and other atamans with Cossacks, and sotskys with streltsy; and the sovereign ordered him to go straight, and in the Field ordered boats and supplies to be prepared and to go to the Dnieper. And the sovereign ordered Prince Dmitry to stand on the Dnieper and defend his cause against the Crimean Tsar as much as God would help him.
