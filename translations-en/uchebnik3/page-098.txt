In the thirteenth year of his reign, moved by the counsel of God, he wished to found a city in his own name and sent wise men to Asia, Libya, and Europe to seek out and choose a most glorious and distinguished place for the founding of such a city. Returning, they told the tsar about various most glorious places, but above all they praised Macedonia and Byzantium to him. He, however, was more inclined in thought toward the Troad, where had been the worldwide victory of the Greeks over the Franks.

And so, pondering this day and night, the tsar heard a voice in his sleep: “In Byzantium Constantinople is destined to be built.”

And when the tsar awoke from sleep, he sent to Byzantium masters and city craftsmen to prepare the place.

The tsar himself left in Rome as Caesars his two sons Consta and Constantine, and sent his nephew Dalmatius to Britain.

And with his mother Helena the tsar went to Byzantium; he took with him also his wife the empress Maximiana, daughter of the tsar Diocletian, and his son-in-law Licinius, and his two brothers Dalmatius and Constantius, and Dalmatius’s son Dalmatius, and Constantius’s two sons Gallus and Julian.

And, coming to Byzantium, he saw on that place seven hills and many sea bays. And he ordered the hills to be dug down and the lower places filled up, and in the bays to erect stone columns, and to set vaults upon them, and to level the place; and the tsar himself remained in Byzantium.

When the place was prepared, the tsar gathered his nobles and the megistans, that is, the local princes and masters; and they began to consider where the walls, towers, and city gates should be. And he ordered the place to be measured in a triangle, seven versts on every side; for such was the place between the two seas, the Red and the White.

And then suddenly a serpent crawled out of a hole and began to crawl, but an eagle fell upon it from above, seized the serpent and flew up on high; the serpent began to coil around the eagle. The tsar and all the people watched the eagle and the serpent. The eagle disappeared from sight for a long time and then appeared flying down, and fell with the serpent on that same place, for it had been overcome by the serpent.

The people, running up, killed the serpent and took the eagle. The tsar was in great horror. And, summoning the scribes and wise men, he told them about the sign. They, after thinking, said to the tsar: “This place will be called Seven Hills; it will be glorified and magnified throughout the whole universe more than other cities. But because it stands between two seas, and the sea waves constantly beat against it, it will waver. The eagle is a Christian symbol, and the serpent is an infidel symbol.
