On the Flight to Lithuania of Prince Nikita Lobanov, Son of Rostovsky

In that same year, in July, Prince Nikita, son of Prince Semyon Lobanov-Rostovsky, wished to flee to Lithuania, and boyar scions caught him in Toropets.

And they brought him to the tsar and grand prince. And the tsar and grand prince ordered him interrogated as to why he had fled; and Prince Nikita said that boyar Prince Semyon Rostovsky had sent him to Lithuania to the king to say concerning himself that he was going to the king, and with him his brothers and nephews; and before that Prince Semyon had sent to the king his man Bakshey to ask for a safe-conduct.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered Prince Semyon seized and interrogated; and Prince Semyon said that he had wished to flee from wretchedness and feeble-mindedness, because he was poor in mind and in every good deed, and had idly and in vain devoured the tsar’s bounty and his own household’s. And from that, he said, he began to be vexed, as though the sovereign had sent him and his kin not according to their ancestry, together with many who were lesser than they. But he said all this desiring to betray the sovereign; yet the sovereign had sent him according to his worth, and his kin with those with whom it was fitting for them to be; but they raged with malice.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered his people whom he had named in his own council to be seized. And his man Semeyka said against him that he had communicated with the Lithuanian envoys, with Dovoyna and his companions, through his man Bakshey, and had himself met with them twice and confirmed it with an oath, and had passed on the counsels of the tsar and grand prince to the envoys, saying that they should not make peace with the tsar and grand prince, that the realm had grown poor, and that the tsar and grand prince could not hold Kazan and would soon abandon it; and for that reason the envoys did not conclude an eternal peace; and many insulting words about the tsar and sovereign grand prince he spoke to them, because the sovereign did not favor them all, dishonored great families, and brought young men close to himself, and oppressed us with them, and had so oppressed us that he married his own boyar’s daughter, took his own slave, and how are we to serve our sister; and other insulting words he spoke. As Satan himself, the hater of good, wounded him; and he sent his man Bakshey to the king, and with him wrote blasphemy and reproach against the sovereign and the whole land.

And Prince Semyon himself said that he had spoken all that out of madness, and had betrayed the counsel of the tsar and grand prince to the king through his envoys, and Prince Andrey Katyrev Rostovsky and others, such madmen, the Rostov princes, the Lobanovs and Priimkovs, and other perjurers, had wished to go with him.
