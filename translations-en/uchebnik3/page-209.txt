And they began to petition, and they abolished the veche, the bell, and the posadnik, so that the sovereign would lay aside his anger and cease to be wroth, and would not carry out removals, and would not encroach on their hereditary lands, lands and waters, and property; and would show favor and set aside the Moscow summonses in Novgorod, and would not assign them to service in the Nizov land. And the Grand Prince showed favor to them in all these things.

And they began to petition that the sovereign give a charter of assurance to his patrimony, Great Novgorod, and kiss the cross; and the Grand Prince answered thus: “There shall be no kissing of the cross by me.”

And they petitioned that the boyars kiss the cross to them; and the Grand Prince refused them in that; and they petitioned further that he order his viceroy, who would be with them, to kiss the cross; but he did not do even that. And they petitioned further for a safe-conduct charter, and the Grand Prince did not give them that.

On December 28, on Sunday, Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuysky, who was in Novgorod, cousin of Prince Ivan Gorbaty, renounced his oath to the Novgorodians in Novgorod in the name of the Grand Prince; and the Novgorodians, fearing the Grand Prince’s wrath, dared not say a word to him; and he was in their city for two days after the renunciation of the oath.

On December 29, the Archbishop with his aforementioned companions petitioned the Grand Prince through the boyars: “Since the sovereign no longer shows favor—he has not only set aside the kissing of the cross, but does not even give a safe-conduct charter—how will he show favor to us, his patrimony? And we would hear that favor from the lips of our sovereign himself, without intermediaries.” And the boyars went and told this to the Grand Prince.

And the Grand Prince showed favor, ordered them to enter his presence, and said to them: “That you have petitioned me, the Grand Prince—you, our pilgrim, the Archbishop, and the posadniks with you, and the prosperous townsmen, and the common people, from our patrimony, Great Novgorod—that I should show favor, lay aside my anger, not carry out removals from the Novgorod land, not encroach on hereditary lands and property, and that there be no Moscow summonses, and that judgment be according to old custom in Novgorod, as judgment is established in the land, and that I should not assign you to service in the Nizov land; and I favor you, my patrimony, in all these things: I have set all that aside.” And they, hearing this and bowing low, went out from him.
