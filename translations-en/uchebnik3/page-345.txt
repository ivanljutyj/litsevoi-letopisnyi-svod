Entreaty of the Venerable Fathers. And these two venerable fathers, as if by agreement, affectionately fell at the feet of these great hierarchs and entreated them with tender tears, saying: “O holy pastors of this rational flock of Christ’s sheep! Why are you leaving this city, and to whom are you abandoning your flock in the time of the barbarian invasion?” Response of the hierarchs. The radiant hierarchs also answered with tears: “We have long prayed to the all-merciful God and the Most Pure Theotokos for deliverance from this present sorrow. But God has commanded not only us to leave this city, but also to carry out the wonder-working image of His Most Pure Mother, because people have forgotten the fear of God and do not care for God’s commandments; and therefore God permitted the barbarian tongue to come here to punish them, so that they might return to God with repentance.”

Entreaty of the Venerable Fathers. And these two venerable fathers, Sergius and Barlaam, earnestly entreated them with weeping, saying: “You, O holy hierarchs, who once in this life laid down your souls for your flock, can you now, in this present sorrow, wish to abandon them? You see how they go about grieving and turning to repentance. Do not disdain them, we beg you; do not abandon the flock entrusted to you by God! Now is the time for you to help them; intensify your earnest prayers for them to the Most Pure Theotokos, and perhaps she, together with you, will entreat Her Son, Christ our God, to turn His righteous wrath into mercy, while His people strive to perform deeds pleasing to God.” And the venerable fathers spoke with the hierarchs about many other things as well, addressing one another by name.

Common Prayer. And immediately all together, with one accord, rose to prayer, performed a litia, and sang supplicatory canons in harmony.

There they also read the Gospel and began the ectenia, saying “Lord, have mercy,” calling out with tears as they sang; and they said the prayer to the Most Pure Theotokos before her image, and performed the dismissal, and blessed with the cross, and censed with fragrant incense, blessing all directions in the form of a cross.

And from there they returned again to the city with the wonder-working image of the Mother of God and with all the sacred things.

Having seen this, the venerable nun found herself again in her cell. And afterward she lived two years, seeing the light with blinded eyes. This wondrous vision became known to her spiritual father David, abbot of the old monastery of St Nicholas. But not only this nun was vouchsafed to see such a wondrous vision; many others were as well.
