And they wrote to Prince Ivan Ovchina and ordered him to gather with men and go after Prince Andrew. And at that time the boyar sons, the Novgorod landholders, had gone to service, and they were ordered to be in Moscow; and the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess sent to meet them along the roads and also to the pyatinas, to the boyar sons, and ordered them to hurry after Prince Ivan Ovchina.

And Prince Ivan Ovchina, having gathered with many men, went after Prince Andrew; and Prince Andrew turned left from Zayachsky Yam toward Staraya Russa.

And Prince Ivan Ovchina caught up with Prince Andrew at Tyukholi, about five versts from Zayachsky Yam. And Prince Andrew stood for battle with Prince Ivan, armed his men, and set up regiments, and with the regiments went against Prince Ivan. And Prince Ivan appointed commanders for the Grand Prince’s men against Prince Andrew and set up regiments; and having arranged the regiments and taken God as his help, he went against Prince Andrew. And Prince Andrew saw this, the Grand Prince’s regiments, and did not wish to enter into battle with the Grand Prince, and began to send messages to Prince Ivan, and began to ask Prince Ivan for assurances that the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess would not take him captive and would not lay disgrace upon him. But Prince Ivan had no instruction from the Grand Prince or from his mother the Grand Princess to give assurances to Prince Andrew.

And Prince Ivan, without consulting the Grand Prince and the Grand Princess, gave Prince Andrew an assurance, and together with Prince Andrew went to Moscow.

And the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess for this pronounced a great disgrace upon Prince Ivan Ovchina in words, because he had given Prince Andrew an assurance without their command.

And Prince Andrew they ordered to be seized and put in a chamber, and they placed fetters on him; and his princess and his son they placed in the city under guard in the Bersenev court.

And upon his boyars—upon Prince Fyodor Dmitrievich Pronsky, Prince Ivan Andreevich Leninsky Obolensky, Prince Yuri Andreevich Menshoy Peninsky Obolensky, his dvoretsky, and his konyushy, Prince Boris Ivanovich Paletsky, and also upon the princes and boyar sons, upon those who were in his izba and knew his duma, upon Prince Yuri Andreevich Bolshoy Peninsky Obolensky and Ivan Umnoy, son of Ivan Lobanov Kolychev, and also upon Prince Andrew’s brother-in-law, Prince Ivan Andreevich Khovansky—they laid their disgrace, and ordered them to be tortured and also subjected to trade punishment.
