And he came to the Kazan posad on the 18th day of that same month, a Monday, on the feast of the Descent of the Holy Spirit, at the first hour of the day, but not with all his men: many people lost their way, for a thick mist had then descended on the Volga. And he came secretly to the posad and, by God’s mercy, killed many people and took some alive, and recovered the Russian captives, and killed more than 100 princes and chief murzas.

And Prince Peter withdrew with all his men to the mouth of the Sviyaga and halted on Round Hill to await the tsar and the commanders.

Sign at the Sviyaga

Also on the Sviyaga River, near the great Volga, on the site where the town of Sviyazhsk now stands, the Tatars living near that place many times saw a man walking in monastic garb; sometimes they saw him and began shooting at him, but he became invisible; those who saw this were seized with fear and dared not approach that place; sometimes they also heard a bell and God-voiced singing of many voices.

Sometimes they saw priests singing and censing at that place. Seeing this, the Tatars marveled and grieved, and said to themselves: “It seems that Rus and Christian churches are to be on this place, and Russian people are to live here,” which indeed came to pass by the grace of Christ.

On the 24th day of May

On the Sunday of All Saints, Tsar Shigalei and the commanders came to the Sviyaga, and the Grand Prince’s commanders disembarked from the boats and began to cut timber where the city was to be, and, having cleared the hill, they served a prayer service and blessed the water, and walked with crosses around the site of the future city walls.

And they laid the foundation of the city, and in the city they laid a church in the name of the Nativity of the Most Pure (Theotokos) and of the wonderworker Sergius; for from the icon of the wonderworker Sergius great miracles occurred.

And the city that had been brought from above was set on one half of that hill, and the other half the commanders and boyar sons with their men built at once, since that place was spacious, and they completed the building of the city in four weeks.

And the hill people, seeing that the city of the Orthodox tsar had been founded in their land, began to come to the tsar and the commanders and to petition that their sovereign show them favor, set aside his anger, and permit them to live by the Sviyazhsk town and not order them to be conquered.
