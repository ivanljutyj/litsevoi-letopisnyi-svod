On the Dispatch from Dyak Rzhevsky

And on the same day Dyak Rzhevsky sent his Cossacks from the Dnieper, and wrote to the sovereign that on the Dnieper Lithuanian men, Cherkassian atamans, one called Mlynsky and Mikhail Eskovich, had joined him, and with them 300 Kanev Cherkassians. And Dyak, together with the Cossacks and the Lithuanian atamans, went against Islam-Kermen, and at Islam-Kermen they learned of them, and the people took precautions; and there they drove off horses and much livestock and went against Ochakov, and at Ochakov they seized the fort, beat Turks and Tatars, and took tongues; and when they were going away, the Ochakov and Tyagin sanjak-beys with many men rushed after them, and Dyak set an ambush for them in the reeds by the Dnieper and killed many men with arquebuses, and he himself withdrew safe with all his men.

And when he came to Islam-Kermen, a tsarevich, the Crimean kalga, came to Islam-Kermen, and with him all the Crimea, princes and murzas; and Dyak took position opposite him on an island and fought him with arquebuses for six days, and at night Dyak drove off the Crimean horse herds and ferried them to his island; and he went upstream along the Trans-Dnieper, on the Lithuanian side; and Dyak parted from the tsarevich, God be thanked, unharmed, while he wounded and killed many of the tsarevich’s men with arquebuses. And he sent his Cossacks Grisha and Kostya with this news, and wrote the Crimean tidings, that both the Crimean and Turkish tongues and the captives said that the Tsar had wanted to march against the borderlands of the Tsar and Grand Prince, but word came to him that the Tsar and Grand Prince was waiting for him, and so he did not go, and also because many of his men had died of illness, and there was no way for him to muster into the lands of the Tsar and Grand Prince.

On the Arrival of Captives from the Crimea

And at the same time captives came from the Crimea, Afanasy, Prince Zvenigorodsky, and Veriga Kleshchin with his companions, and they told the same news, that the Crimean Tsar would not come, and that the Crimean Tsar himself was wary of the coming of the Tsar and Grand Prince. And in accordance with that news the Tsar and Grand Prince did not go to the Field and sent to the okolnichy, while he himself went to Ryazan to pray to St Nicholas of Zaraysk, and from there to Kolomna; and the sovereign came to Moscow in June, and on all the borderlands he left voivodes to repel small detachments.

On the Sending of Tongues by Mishka

In the month of June Mishka Cherkashenin sent two tongues, one of them a Crimean and the other a Turk, and they had been taken, he said, near the small town of Kerts, beyond the ulus of the Shirin princes.
