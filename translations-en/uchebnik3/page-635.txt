On the arrival of service Tatars from Siberia

In the same month there arrived from Siberia the service Tatars of the tsar and grand prince, Devlet-Khozya and Sobanya Ryazanov, and with them the envoys of Ediger, prince of Siberia, Ivtemir and his companions, and they brought the tribute of the Siberian land in full: 1,000 sables and the road toll, 160 sables for squirrels, and they also brought a sworn charter with the prince’s seal, that the prince gives himself into servitude and has laid tribute upon all his land, and promises henceforth annually to give that tribute to the tsar and grand prince from all the Siberian land.

And the tsar and grand prince released his envoy Bayanda, granted him an audience and showed him favor, and released him, and with him he sent service Tatars for the tribute of the past year.

The envoy of the tsar and grand prince, Joseph Nepeya, arrived

In the same month the envoy of the tsar and grand prince, Joseph Nepeya, returned from Philip, King of Spain and England, and from Queen Mary. And Nepeya related that on the way to the English land their ships were wrecked at sea. The king’s envoy Ryzert and his companions drowned in the sea, while they were cast ashore in the Scottish kingdom, and from there he was released to the English land, and King Philip received the gifts of the tsar and grand prince with great love and honor and sent him back to the tsar and grand prince. And King Philip and Queen Mary wrote with him with great loving honor, and as a gift they sent to the tsar and grand prince a live lion and lioness, and the king sent his full armor, scarlets and many satins, and they presented Nepeya with a great gift in his favor, and to the merchants of the tsar and grand prince they opened a free path and gave them a court in their great city of London, and permitted them to trade without any duties. And the king released with Nepeya many craftsmen, doctors and seekers of gold and silver, and makers, and many other craftsmen who came together with Nepeya.
