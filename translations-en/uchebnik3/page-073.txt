The Epistle of Photius, Patriarch of Constantinople, to the Romans concerning the Holy Spirit and the unleavened bread they offer

The divine Gregory, Pope of Rome, called the Dialogist, who took part in the sixth council, declared in the language of the Romans that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone. Zacharias, however, 165 years after the statement of the Dialogist, gives an interpretation in the Greek language: the Comforter Spirit, he says, proceeds from the Father and rests upon the Son; he learned this from the Forerunner, for he saw the Spirit descending in the form of a dove and remaining upon Him. Leo and Benedict, great Roman popes, established that during the sacred divine service in Rome the Greek Creed should be proclaimed, as also in all the churches subject to Rome, and this was made law. This same Pope Leo opened the vessel repository of the great apostolic Roman church and brought out two shields from among the sacred vessels, on which in Greek letters and words the foundations of the pious faith were set forth, and therefore he was deemed worthy to be numbered among the Roman multitude. And even until the times of the pious Patriarch Sergius of Constantinople, the Roman popes, upon their accession to the patriarchal throne, composed a statement of their faith and sent it to all the patriarchal thrones, and the Creed remained unchanged. During the sacred service the Latins perform the service not on bread, as we do, but on Jewish unleavened bread, saying that the bread which Christ handed to the disciples was unleavened. Moreover, there are many testimonies from the text of the holy Gospels that Christ gave the disciples bread, and not unleavened bread. To this are added testimonies from the Acts of the Apostles: James, the brother of God, appointed bishop in Jerusalem, after the Ascension, while performing the divine service, accomplished the awesome sacred rite on bread; also the divine evangelist and apostle Mark, appointed bishop in great Alexandria by the chief apostle Peter, likewise offered leavened bread at the divine service according to the tradition received from the chief apostle Peter. And their successors do the same to this day, both in Antioch and in the other churches.
