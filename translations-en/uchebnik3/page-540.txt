To the pious tsar and to all Christians. The sovereign, chosen by God, came to the church, uttered many prayers with tears in secret, and openly proclaims: “What shall I render to Thee, O Master, for Thy blessings, save tears and a contrite heart? Merciful Master Christ! Grant complete deliverance to poor Christendom; not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to Thy name give glory.”

On the Moving of the Siege Towers to the Ditch

He lovingly embraces his commanders and permits them to kiss his hand, and exhorts them with great gracious words, honoring their bravery and courage; likewise the sovereign rewards all his soldiers according to rank.

On the Tower

In that same month the tsar commanded his clerk Ivan Vyrodkov to set up a tower by the siege towers of Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky, opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And they set up the tower six sazhens high, and placed many cannons on it, one-and-a-half pishchals and wall guns; and many streltsy with pishchals stood there and shot into the city along the streets and along the city walls; and they killed many people.

The impious hid in pits and ditches, dug under the city gates and under the wall, and dug burrows under the tarasy, for at all their gates beyond the ditch they had great tarasy filled with earth.

And from behind the tarasy they fought every day, and from the burrows, like serpents, they crawled out and fought ceaselessly day and night.

On the Moving of the Siege Towers to the Ditch

The pious sovereign tsar and grand prince commanded Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky to move the siege towers to the ditch opposite the Arskaya Tower and the Arsk Gates, to the tarasy opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And Prince Mikhail for more than one day moved the siege towers and drew near the ditch, asking God’s mercy, with great wisdom and courage.

But the Tatars fought fiercely, not wishing to let the Orthodox draw near; the tsar’s commanders, trusting in God, at the sovereign’s command drew near the ditch.

On Sunday the sovereign commanded Prince Mikhail to take position with the siege towers along the ditch and at the tarasy opposite the Tsar’s Arsk Gates; and by the sovereign’s command came streltsy and Cossacks, and commanders with boyar retainers, and they stood along the ditch and fought; and men fell on both sides.
