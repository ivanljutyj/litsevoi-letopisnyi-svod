It did not begin for a long time, but they only discussed various questions concerning the books, awaiting the Metropolitan of Great Rus', Isidore, to take part in the council. Then Tsar Sir Ivan Manuilovich informed Pope Eugene of Rome and all those gathered there that “in the eastern Russian lands, White Rus' professes Orthodoxy and the highest Christianity more than all others; its great sovereign is my brother Vasily Vasilyevich, whom the eastern tsars obey and whom the great princes with their lands serve; but only because of his humility, piety, greatness of mind, and good faith is he not called tsar, but Great Russian Prince of his Orthodox lands.” And therefore they awaited his metropolitan, not beginning the council, for six months. When Metropolitan Isidore arrived to them, a multitude of people met him with great honors for the glory of the name of the great tsar Vasily Vasilyevich of all Rus'.

After several days the council began. They assembled for the council for the first time in the month of September. Pope Eugene entered the kostel, that is, the church; with him Latin archbishops and bishops stood on the left side, while the tsar and the patriarch were on the right side near the throne.

And then they brought in and set up silver-gilt cast [statues] of St Peter and Paul, and before them they placed three lit candles and tables for the philosophers, and before the tsar they also placed a table for the theologians.

And at the table for the philosophers were seated the sages: Julian, Cardinal of Rome, and with him the Greeks John of Bologna and Adrian of Crete; and at the table for the theologians before the tsar — Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, in place of the Patriarch of Alexandria, and Metropolitan Isidore in place of the Patriarch of Antioch, and Bessarion of Nicaea in place of the Patriarch of Jerusalem. The beginning of that council was as follows. When the pope entered the church, he knelt according to the Latin custom and, having passed through, sat in his place.

And then one bishop began to pronounce loudly in Latin, and their clerks performed the chants. After this they began to deliver speeches, and their interpreters spoke in three languages: Greek, Fryazhian, and philosophical; and they brought many books and laid them before them, and, having finished the discussion, they dispersed. And thus three days passed, but nothing serious was decided among them, until the Fryazhi began to speak; the Greeks then rejoiced.

When the feast of the great John the Theologian arrived, they began to sit for the fourth time according to the rule established earlier; the Fryazhi began to speak; and they brought many books; and then disputes began among them, but they did not come to agreement.
