On December 30, a Saturday, there was a feast in honour of the Grand Prince at Posadnik Fefilat’s; gifts: 30 gold korabelniks, three bolts of Flemish cloth, and a walrus tusk; and from his son Kuzma, ten korabelniks and two walrus tusks.

On January 1 there was a feast in honour of the Grand Prince at Yakov Fedorov’s; gifts: 40 gold korabelniks, three bolts of Flemish cloth, three walrus tusks; and from his grandson Ivan Dmitriev, son of Isakov, twenty gold korabelniks and two barrels of wine.

On January 2 there was a feast at Afanasy Gruz’s; gifts: 30 gold korabelniks, three bolts of Flemish cloth.

On January 3 there was a feast at Luka’s and Vasily’s, the sons of Isak Polinaryin; gifts: 110 gold korabelniks, three bolts of Flemish cloth, and four walrus tusks.

On January 4 there was a feast at Alexander Samsonov’s; gifts: forty gold korabelniks, five bolts of Flemish cloth, and a grey mule.

On January 6, a Saturday, there was a feast at Posadnik Foma’s; gifts: three bolts of Flemish cloth, a piebald mule, and two barrels of white wine.

On January 11, a Thursday, the stepenny posadnik Foma and the tysyatsky Vasily, son of Esip, on behalf of all Great Novgorod petitioned the Grand Prince, presenting 1000 rubles.

And also, when the Grand Prince was in Novgorod, a German envoy came to him from the Swedish king Gerstur, his nephew, his sister’s son, named Orban, on January 12; and he brought from the king as a gift a bay stallion; and he petitioned the Grand Prince on the king’s behalf concerning that he had had a truce with Novgorod for several years, and those appointed years had passed, and that the Grand Prince would graciously order his patrimony Great Novgorod to renew the truce with him.

And the Grand Prince graciously granted this, and ordered his pilgrim Archbishop Feofil and his patrimony Novgorod to take a truce with them according to ancient custom; and having honoured that envoy, he dismissed him.

On January 14 there was a feast at Kuzma Grigoriev’s; gifts: 20 gold korabelniks, 3 bolts of Flemish cloth, two walrus tusks; and from his son, ten gold korabelniks, a bolt of Flemish cloth, and two walrus tusks.

On January 19 there was a third feast at Archbishop Feofil’s in honour of the Grand Prince; gifts: 1000 Hungarian and Venetian gold coins, 300 gold korabelniks, a gold ladle with pearls, three grivenkas, two aurochs horns bound in gold, a silver cup weighing 12 grivenkas, 18 bolts of Flemish cloth of various colours, and ten forties of sables.
