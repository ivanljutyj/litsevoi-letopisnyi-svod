The Patriarch of Constantinople consulted with the whole sacred council, with princes and boyars, about convening an eighth council together with the pope and with all the Romans, concerning the division and schism between the Greek and Roman Churches, and among all lands and kingdoms, over the dispute about the transubstantiation of the Most Pure Body and Precious Blood of Christ in leavened or unleavened bread, and concerning the Holy Spirit; and the champion of this council strove thus to persuade the prince. The grand prince answered him: “In the time of our forefathers and parents there was no union of our faith with the Romans, and I do not want us to be united in faith with them.” Metropolitan Isidore did not obey him, but with all his soul he strove to go to the eighth council, to Pope Eugene, Patriarch Joseph, and the Greek tsar Kir Kaluyan, in the Roman land. And Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich said to him: “Father Isidore, we do not permit you to go to the Latin land; but you, not listening to us, want to go there; but let it be known to you: when you return from there to us, bring back to us our Christian faith of the Greek law, which our forefathers received from the Greeks.” He promised to do so and swore that he would bring nothing incorrect or foreign from the Latins into the Russian land from their eighth council, but would preserve the true Orthodoxy of the Greek law, considering himself wiser than the wisest, and agreeing with the foolish in agreement.

In the year 6946 from Adam (the year 1446 from Christ)

On the 15th day of September, Princess Evpraksia reposed, and Isidore, Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus, buried her with psalms and funeral hymns together with the whole sacred council.

In the same autumn a son, Yuri the Elder, was born to Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich.

About Tsar Makhamet

In the same autumn Tsar Ulu-Makhamet of the Great Horde came to the town of Belev and remained in Belev, having fled from his brother Kichi-Akhmet, tsar of the Great Horde.

In the same autumn, in the month of November, Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich sent against Tsar Ulu-Makhamet two princes—Prince Dmitry Yuryevich Shemyaka and his brother Prince Dmitry Yuryevich Krasny—and many other princes, and with them numerous regiments; the tsar then had only a small number of warriors.

When they came to Belev, they plundered their own Orthodox Christian people, and tortured people for booty, and slaughtered cattle and sent them back to their own, and did unlawful and vile things.
