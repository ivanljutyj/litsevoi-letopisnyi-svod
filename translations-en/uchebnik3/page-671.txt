The Germans fled and abandoned eleven towns. And the voevodas burned the towns, carried away the cannon and bells and all other goods, and abandoned the towns empty, because they were not on the frontier. And the voevodas came out with all their men, by God’s grace, in good health; and the sovereign sent to the voevodas with his gracious bounty.

On the ambassadors of the Danish King Frederick

In the same month came the ambassadors of the Danish King Frederick: Klius Vernem Biltemverskoi, and Vodislav Vobigerov, and Petr Bilden, and Doctor Yeronim Tener. And the ambassadors bowed to the ground before the sovereign from the king, presented gifts, and petitioned that the sovereign order their embassy to be heard.

And the tsar and grand prince sent to them his okolnichy Aleksei Fedorovich Adashev, the clerk Ivan Mikhailov, and Kazarin Dubrovsky to hear their embassy and give them an answer, and ordered them to conclude an agreement on everything. And the ambassadors from King Frederick of Denmark petitioned the tsar and sovereign that the tsar and grand prince make peace with King Frederick and order his people to trade with his people in both directions without hindrance. And that the tsar and sovereign would show favor, and at the king’s petition set aside his anger against the Master of Livonia and all the Livonian land, and grant their land peace, even if not for long. And during that time the Master himself would come to the sovereign or send ambassadors, or acknowledge his guilt before the sovereign, and the sovereign would show him favor as befits his sovereign majesty. They also petitioned concerning Kolyvan (and called Kolyvan subject to the Danish kings), and that the sovereign would show favor and not order war against Kolyvan.

And Aleksei and the clerks recounted the king’s petition to the sovereign, and the tsar and sovereign showed favor to King Frederick: he did not refuse him his friendship and ordered his people to trade with his own people in all his towns, Russian and German, in Yuryev and Rugodiv, and in Novgorod and Pskov. And at the petition of the Danish king he did not order war against the Master, the Archbishop of Riga, and the Bishop of Kolyvan for six months, from May to November. And during this time the Master himself must come or send his ambassadors, his best men, and plead concerning his guilt on whatever terms the sovereign may grant him favor. And he gave a safe-conduct charter to the ambassadors of the Danish king, to the Livonian Master and his ambassadors, for unimpeded passage and return. And concerning Kolyvan the sovereign ordered it said that the king calls Kolyvan and the Virland land his in vain: those are age-old tributaries of the tsar; and now the sovereign wishes to keep them under his dominion. And the king should not encroach on Kolyvan and Virland, and should not seek a quarrel with the sovereign over this. And the ambassadors petitioned concerning all the sovereign’s favor, saying that all the sovereign’s favor was pleasing to their king; and the tsar and sovereign sent the ambassadors back to the king, having granted them favor and honors.
