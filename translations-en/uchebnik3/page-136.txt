The grand prince’s commanders campaigned against the Cheremis along the Vyatka River.

And they went from Vyatka down the Kama, and they ravaged as far as Tamgluga, and they killed many merchants and took much goods from them.

They went as far as the Tatar ferry, and then turned back upstream, ravaging Kazan lands as well; and they went to ravage Belaya Volozhka.

At that time two hundred Kazan Tatars also set out to raid, and having reached that same Volozhka on horseback, they left their horses there with the Cheremis and went from Volozhka upstream along the Kama in boats.

Then the grand prince’s army came and ravaged those Cheremis, cut down people, and took horses and all livestock; and they killed the horses of those Tatars who had gone upstream along the Kama, and set off after those Tatars along the Kama. And when they heard that they had already come close to those Tatars, they halted; the commanders, choosing seven nasads from their men, sent the commander Ivan Runa with them; and they caught up with the Tatars.

Seeing them, the Tatars leaped out onto the bank.

Runa ordered his men to go out onto the bank after them as well; but the Tatars ran behind a small river and began fighting from behind it.

By God’s mercy the Christians began to prevail, and they crossed that small river against the Tatars, and so killed them. And they took their commander Tulazii, son of Prince Tarkhanov, and another bardiche-bearer, and killed all the rest. And two Russians were killed in that battle, and about sixty were wounded, but by God’s mercy all are alive.

And they went from there to Great Perm and to Ustyug.

And so they came to Moscow all in good health, and brought the captive Tatars to the grand prince.

In the same year, on the 23rd day of the month of May, at the second hour of the night, the posad in Moscow caught fire near the Church of St Nicholas Mokry, and many courtyards burned. It burned upward along the ditch past Epiphany Street, past Vesyakov’s courtyards, from the Church of the Epiphany along the street to the Church of St John the Holy over five streets, and from St John down to the lowland along Vasilyevsky Meadow, and to Bolshaya Street, to Ostry Konec, and right to the river, and to the Church of Cosmas and Damian at Ostry Konec. It was hard then in the city, for the wind was strong and there were whirlwinds; but God preserved the city. In the same year the grand prince founded the votive Church of Simeon Divnogorets.

In the same year, on June 4, from Nizhny Novgorod the grand prince’s outpost and Prince Fyodor Khripun with Muscovites went to the Volga and killed many noble Tatars of the tsar’s Kazan court; then they killed Kolupa; and Prince Khozyumberdei they captured and brought to the grand prince in Moscow.
