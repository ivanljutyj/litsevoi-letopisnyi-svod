On the Arrival of Two Tsareviches to Serve the Grand Prince

In that same year there came to the Grand Prince to serve two tsareviches from Astrakhan, Isup-Saltan, son of Yazub-Saltan, and Shigavliyar-Saltan, son of Bakhtiyar-Saltan, nephews of Akhmat, tsar of the Great Horde.

In the Year 7011 from Adam (1511 from Christ)

On the 23rd day of October, a Sunday, the Grand Prince’s son Prince Dmitry Ivanovich came to Moscow, having conquered and taken captives in the Lithuanian land beyond Orsha, but he did not take the city of Smolensk, because the city was fortified.

That same winter, in January, Tikhon of Rostov left the archbishopric because of his infirmity and went to the Rostov Borisoglebsky Monastery on the Ustye.

That same winter, on the first day of January, an ambassador came to Moscow to the Grand Prince from the Hungarian King Vladislaus, by name Sigismund.

That same winter, in March, ambassadors came to Moscow from the King of Poland and Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander, son-in-law of the Grand Prince: Pan Petr Mishkovsky, voivode of Lanchinsky; Pan Jan Butchatsky, starosta of Mezhigozhsky and cupbearer of the Kingdom of Poland; and Petr Dratsynovsky, stolnik of Krakow and canon of Poznan, royal secretary; and Prince Stanislav Gorodetsky. And the Lithuanian ambassadors were: Pan Stanislav Glebovich, namestnik of Polotsk; and Vojtech Yanovich, namestnik of Koden; and the scribe Ivashko Sopegich. And they concluded with Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of All Rus a truce for six years, from the Day of the Annunciation to the Day of the Annunciation, and they wrote letters of truce.

On the Death of the Grand Princess Sophia

That same spring, on the 7th day of April, a Friday, at the ninth hour of the day, the right-believing Grand Princess Sophia, wife of Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, reposed, and she was laid in the Church of the Ascension in the city of Moscow.

And that same spring, on the 7th day of May, a Sunday, the Grand Prince sent his ambassadors to Lithuania to his son-in-law King Alexander: his boyar and okolnichy Pyotr Mikhailovich Pleshcheyev, and Konstantin Grigoryevich Zabolotsky, and Mikhail Klyapik, and the clerk Guba Moklokov, concerning the truce; and they came to Moscow in September.

On the Conciliar Assembly, on Widows, on Priests and Deacons

In that same year Archbishop Gennady of Novgorod was in Moscow, and he conferred with Simon, Metropolitan of All Rus, and with the bishops, and they ordered widowed priests and deacons not to sing and not to touch the sacred rites.
