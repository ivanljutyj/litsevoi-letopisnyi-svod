Then the divine service was performed, and the royal infant was partaken of the divine mysteries of the life-giving body and blood of Christ our God.

And in that same hour, by the command of the autocrat, the noble infant was taken to the reigning city of Moscow to his beloved mother, for a child-loving mother cannot endure long separation from her child.

Tsar Vasily himself gave alms in the monastery and fed the brethren, and with them he deigned to partake of food himself.

And after a short stay, he set out on his journey and came to Moscow, thanking God that He had fulfilled his petition and turned the sorrow of his whole kingdom into joy.

Thanksgiving

All the people, rejoicing, exulted, saying: “We no longer grieve over this, we no longer vex God’s glory, we no longer torment ourselves with the thought: who hereafter will uphold the banners of the Russian kingdom? Who will preserve the fulfillment of the Orthodox? Who will overcome the foolish? Who will abolish the pagan onslaught? Who will put to shame the corrupt speech of heretics? Who will calm in its ancestral homeland the love of disputes and the proud, seditious wavering over lineage and rank? For God has granted to our sovereign Tsar Vasily, by Elena, a son, Ivan, heir to his kingdom. Now we shall rest from our constant sorrows, now let no stranger leap into Christ’s flock, now no one from another tribe will sit on the throne of the Russian kingdom or change the borders established by our former Orthodox autocrats. Now the son and heir of this wise and merciful sovereign, the autocratic Tsar Vasily, growing and rejoicing for many years, will manage all that is useful and necessary in the name of God, destroying all injustice and strengthening piety. Now we shall rejoice, seeing the tsar’s son in his father’s bosom, nursed by his mother, growing day by day, guarded by God’s grace, strengthened by joy-bringing tears, warmed by the diligent prayers of all the saints, who pray to God with hope, for a fruit of prayer has come forth from the maternal womb of the royal line, for God wills and is able to grant him eternal life and health.” His birth was not ordinary, not as happens with others: while the child was still growing in his mother’s womb, sorrow had already departed from the hearts of all people; while the child was still stirring in his mother’s womb, the onslaught of foreign barbarians and hostile warriors against the kingdom was already diminishing, and, crossing the Russian borders like ravenous wolves, they stretched out their necks, and with their tails their trail
