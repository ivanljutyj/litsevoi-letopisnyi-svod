Envoy from the Lithuanian King

In January, too, an envoy from the Lithuanian King, Martyn Volotkov, arrived. He presented a letter to the tsar and grand prince and made a speech about the Livonian Germans, asking that the sovereign not order war against them: from old times, he said, there had been an order from the emperor to protect them. And now the master petitions him concerning all those affairs of theirs, and asks that envoys be exchanged for Christian peace. And after he had been at the sovereign’s, the next day he asked leave to go to the okolnichy Alexei Adashev and to the clerk Ivan Mikhailov, in order to tell them of an instruction from the lords. And the sovereign ordered that he be received by them in the clerks’ office.

And Martin spoke to Alexei on his own behalf, and not on that of the lords, about what he had said to the tsar and sovereign from his sovereign, and he presented a letter about the Germans, who from the beginning have kept devotion to their sovereigns, and now the king has installed his officials in some towns; and their sovereign wishes to exchange embassies with the tsar and grand prince about this and make an agreement. And by the sovereign’s command Alexei and Ivan told him that the Livonian land had from the beginning been tributary to our sovereigns, from the great Yaroslav Vladimirovich to the present time. And if they withdraw, our sovereigns will punish them. And until now none of them has interfered in the affairs of our sovereigns. And in the letters it is written that they should not join with anyone, and their king is specifically named, that they should not join or adhere to him. And they showed Martin the truce letters.

And the tsar and grand prince released Martin to the king, and the sovereign himself charged them with his royal word (to convey that) in that matter about which he had brought a letter from the king and had made a speech, the sovereign had heard his speeches and read the letter, and concerning those matters his envoy would inform his brother; and he sent a bow to the king, but he did not send a letter to the king or other speeches to the king with them.
