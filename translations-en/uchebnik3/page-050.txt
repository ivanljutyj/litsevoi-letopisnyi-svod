And so he partook with fear in his grave illness, and took the antidoron, and the Theotokos prosphora, and the service water, and lay down on his bed. After that he ate some fish soup, and meat, and fish, drank a cup of wine and said to those close to him: “Go out, leave me in peace, I want to sleep.”

They rejoiced, taking the blood that had come out for sweat, and went to eat at Dionisy Fomin’s, and drank at his house.

And when evening had already come, one of those remaining with the prince came to his spiritual father Iosia—the prince’s deacon Demetrius was there too—and said to them: “Come quickly, the prince is dying.”

And when they came, they found the prince already at his last breath, and when they had read the canon for the departure of the soul and the prayer, he gave up the spirit.

Iosia closed his eyes and covered him [with a covering]. There was much shouting and wailing then.

And afterwards, having drunk a little mead, many lay down in the same chamber around the prince and fell asleep; only his deacon did not drink then, but lay opposite the prince’s body on another bench, watching him, and he did not want to sleep.

And when midnight came, while he was still watching, the prince with his own hands threw the covering back from his head and said in a loud voice: “Peter recognized Him, who is the Lord.” The deacon Demetrius, seeing this, was numb with fear, and the prince again uttered the same words. The deacon, coming to himself a little, gathered his strength and began to wake the people sleeping there, while the prince kept saying the same words.

Then he began to sing in the demestvenny chant “Sing to the Lord and exalt Him forever,” and also “Alleluia,” and after that the Theotokos verse of the fourth tone, “The Living One has His dwelling in the highest,” and also other Theotokia; the prince sang without opening his eyes, and his body was as that of a living man. Toward morning the prince began to grow calm.

After Matins his spiritual father Iosia brought him the reserved communion, but the prince did not open his eyes; Iosia passed the spoon over his lips, then the prince looked and saw him, received communion and said from the depths of his heart: “Rejoice, womb of the Divine incarnation,” and so he partook of the Divine mysteries.

And then on Monday and Tuesday he uttered passages from the Scriptures, sang verses and recognized people, and said to them what he wanted, but incoherently; and if someone addressed him, he did not hear.

On Wednesday he stopped speaking, but recognized people; on Thursday during the liturgy, when they began to read the holy Gospel, the prince finally gave up the spirit on the twenty-second day of the month of September.
