And having set up camp, the sovereign dismounted and went to the Wonderworker Sergius, and having performed a prayer service, he entered his tent and ordered the commanders to guard vigilantly what had been entrusted to them by the sovereign; and all the regiments took their positions according to the tsar’s command.

The next day, Wednesday, the sovereign ordered the commanders of the guard regiment, boyar Prince Vasily Semenovich Serebryany and commander Semen Vasilyevich Sheremetev, and the commanders of the left-hand regiment, Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Mikulinsky and Dmitry Mikhailovich Pleshcheev, to place the turs along the Bulak upstream from the Kazan River; and the commanders came on the night before Friday and, God willing, set up the turs without a battle, (and remained) unharmed.

And the streltsy, by command of the commanders, dug in in a ditch on the other side of the Bulak and did not let the Tatars climb out of the city. And under the city wall there was a stone bathhouse called Dairova, and Cossacks sent by the commanders took position in the bathhouse. And the cannons for the turs, which were to be at those turs, were brought.

Battle of the Yertouls with the Kazanians

On the 25th of the same month, Thursday, the sovereign ordered the advance guard to march on Kazan from the upper part of Kazan, from the Arsk Field, and the right-hand regiment to go lower than Kazan, beyond the Kazan River, and take position opposite the city.

And many Kazan people made sorties from the city of Kazan against the advance guard, and both sides fought with arrows for a long time, and they joined battle.

And another commander, Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Khilkov, with boyar sons, came to the aid of the yertouls, and God aided the Orthodox; they drove the Kazanians back to the very city; some they killed and many they wounded, while the Orthodox were preserved by God; the commander Prince Yuri Shemyakin Pronskoy was also wounded.

And on Wednesday evening there was a great storm, and the sovereign’s tent fell, and tents in many regiments, and many vessels were wrecked on an island on the Volga, along with the tsar’s supplies and those of the whole army. And there was sorrow among the people over this.

The sovereign sent and ordered many supplies to be brought from the town of Sviyazhsk, and ordered what was lacking to be distributed; and the sovereign sent to Moscow and ordered men to hasten to him with a great treasury and with many supplies, and he intended to winter there.

The Tsar-Sovereign Inspects the City

And the sovereign himself rode with many people around the city day and night, examining places where fortifications should be made.
