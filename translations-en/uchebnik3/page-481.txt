In the same year, in the month of August, the town of Mikhailov was established on the Pronya River, and the voivodes who established it were Prince Alexander Ivanovich Vorotynsky and Mikhail Petrov, son of Golovin.

And when they were establishing the town and began to dig the place on which they had decided to build the cathedral church of Archangel Michael, they found, at the place where the altar was to be situated, an image of Archangel Michael in ancient style, in no way damaged, silver-clad.

And the Tsar and Grand Prince, and Metropolitan Macarius, sent priests for that icon and met it with honor.

And after serving prayer services, they sent it to that same place, to the church where the icon had appeared, and gave praise to God for this.

On the thirteenth day of August, Tsar Shigalei and all the voivodes of the Tsar and Grand Prince arrived at the Kazan estuary and stood from the Volga to Bezhbolda, and two versts upstream, and downstream beyond Kazan to Tsarev Meadow; and into the city of Kazan Tsar Shigalei sent his palace steward Prince Shabas and his equerry Prince Batikei with all his baggage train, and ordered his court to be set up.

And on the next day, the fourteenth day of August, there came to the Tsar and the boyars Kul-Sherif-molla and Maamet-Seit, son of Maisyr-Seit, and with him all the shikhs and shikhzadas, imams and mollazadas, azis and dervishes, and Kudaigul-ulan, and with him all the ulans, and Prince Muralei, and with him many princes and murzas; and the boyars ordered the oath charter to be read to them, [stating] in what the sovereign had favored them and how they were to live henceforth. And they all began to speak about the Mountain Side, saying that they could not do this—divide the land; and the boyars answered them according to the sovereign’s instruction: “God taught the sovereign to act thus, and in this his cause is just: the sovereign has set a city on the Sviyaga, and the Mountain people conquered you; it cannot be otherwise, it will be as God has decided.” And many contentious words were spoken about that, but by God’s mercy, according to the sovereign’s instruction, his boyars did as he commanded them.

On the Installation of Shigalei as Tsar

And Tsar Shigalei and all the land of Kazan gave the sovereign an oath on this, that they would not enter the Mountain Side nor the half of the Volga; and that the fishers should fish in their own halves; and the Tsar sealed the oath charters with his seals, and many noble Kazan people put their hands to them.

And the Kazan princes brought the Russian captives that they had, and gave oath concerning the rest, that they would free them all; and if the sovereign learned of Christian captives held in infidel captivity, then whoever was found with them would be put to death; and if the Kazanians did not free all the captives, the sovereign was free to search for them as much as the Merciful God would help him.
