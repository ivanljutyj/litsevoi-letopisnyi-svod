Kurbsky, who, having violated his oath sworn on the cross and abandoning his high birth and many estates, fled from the Tsar and Grand Prince to the King in Lithuania, and roused the King and incited him against the churches of God, against Orthodoxy, and against the borderlands of the Tsar and Grand Prince, desiring that Christian blood be shed.

But the Tsar and Grand Prince, not knowing the evil design of the Crimean Tsar and his collusion with the Lithuanian King, granted the Tsar’s envoy Karasha-knyaz his favor and sent him back to the Tsar; and with him he sent his own courier Andrei Nikitin, son of Myasnoy. And with him he wrote to the Tsar, as in the former correspondence, of firm friendship and brotherhood, as he had previously written with the Tsar’s courier Ashchibash; and he kept no great voivodes with their men in the border towns on the Crimean side.

But the faithless Tsar Devlet-Girey, transgressing his oath, ordered the ambassador of the Tsar and Grand Prince, Afanasy Nagy, and the envoy Fyodor Pisemsky to be held under guard, so that there should be no news of the Tsar’s campaign.

And by correspondence with the King he agreed with him on Christian bloodshed, and they conspired to come at one time: the Polish King with all his men to Polotsk, to take Polotsk, and Tsar Devlet-Girey, after crossing the Oka River, to ravage the Kolomna region and march on Moscow.

And Tsar Devlet-Girey came with his sons, with Kalga Tsarevich Magmet-Girey and Tsarevich Aldy-Girey, and with all his men to the borderland of the Tsar and Grand Prince, to the Ryazan land, secretly, and intended to go to the Oka River and cross the river.

And they captured Cossacks in the Field and local people along the borderlands. These told the Tsar about the Tsar and Grand Prince, that the Tsar and Grand Prince had not himself gone against the Lithuanian King, but had ridden from Moscow to pray at nearby places. And the Tsar, learning that the Tsar and Grand Prince had not gone against the King, then himself abandoned the march to the Oka River, halted at the city of Ryazan, and sent his men out to war.

And they ravaged many districts and villages between Pronsk and Ryazan along the Vozha River, and beyond the city as far as the Oka River and as far as the village of Kuzminskoye.

And the Tsar stood by the city of Ryazan with cannon for four days and burned the suburbs, but by God’s intercession and that of the most pure Mother of God he could do the city no harm.
