To his son, Tsarevich Dmitry; he showed favor to his tsaritsa and exhorted her with words of thanksgiving. Though somewhat ailing after the birth of her son, she wished the sovereign health and bowed her head in gratitude for the miracle that had come to pass.

In November, on the 8th day, on St Michael’s Day, there was a feast at Tsar and Grand Prince of all Rus Ivan Vasilievich in the great hall of the Faceted Palace, which faces the square by the Dormition Cathedral. And Metropolitan Macarius dined with him, together with archbishops and bishops, archimandrites and abbots; and there dined at the sovereign’s table his brother Prince Yuri Vasilievich, and Prince Vladimir Andreevich, and many boyars and voivodes who had fought manfully with him in battles.

On the Gifts

And the Tsar and Sovereign presented gifts to Metropolitan Macarius and to all the hierarchs then in Moscow, because through their holy prayers and the prayer of all the people God had granted him His ineffable mercy. And Prince Vladimir Andreevich the sovereign favored with coats and great Frankish goblets and golden ladles. Likewise the sovereign favored his boyars and voivodes, and all the sons of boyars, and all the warriors, each according to his worth: costly coats from his own shoulders, velvet on gold and on sables, and goblets. To some, coats and ladles; to some, coats; to some, horses and armor; to others, money and clothing from the treasury. This same celebration at the sovereign’s in that hall lasted three days. And in those three days he distributed from his treasury, according to the estimate of the treasurers, with all the money, clothing and vessels, armor and horses and money, apart from estates and manors and maintenance grants—forty-eight thousand rubles. And in the way of maintenance the sovereign bestowed the whole land.

That same month there came to the Tsar and Sovereign and Grand Prince the Circassian sovereign princes: Prince Maashuk, and Prince Ivan Ezboslukov, and Tanashuk, to petition that the sovereign favor them, intercede for them, and take them with their lands into his service as his servants, and defend them from the Crimean Tsar.

And in the sixty-first year and in the sixty-second year there was God’s punishment in Pskov and in Veliky Novgorod—a great pestilence; and by approximate count, in Novgorod and in its districts five hundred thousand people died of the pestilence: punishing us, God brings us to salvation. And Vladyka Serapion reposed; because of the pestilence the palace official Vasily Mashutkin also died, and many of the priestly order and of the administrative people.

In that same year, in the month of November, Pimin Cherny, an elder of the Andreyanov Hermitage, was appointed by Metropolitan Macarius as Archbishop to Veliky Novgorod and Pskov; and Tsar and Grand Prince of all Rus Ivan Vasilievich and Metropolitan Macarius brought in the relics of all the holy fathers, and having prayed to God and consecrated water, they sent the pilgrim Vladyka Pimin to Novgorod. And Vladyka arrived in Novgorod on St Nicholas’s Day.
