On the letter from Prince Ismail, with a messenger

In the same month Prince Ismail wrote with his messenger that the sons of Shikh-Mamai were disloyal to him, and what would become of him was unknown, and that the tsar and grand prince should grant him refuge in Kazan or Astrakhan. And the tsar and grand prince sent Ismail’s messenger back, and to Ismail himself, together with his messenger, he sent his serving Tatars, and wrote to him that refuge was ready for him in Astrakhan. At his request the sovereign would send his men to Astrakhan, and if he wished to come to Kazan to rest with a few men, then the sovereign would order him to be received.

On the messenger to the sovereign from the Swedish King

In that same summer, in the month of July, a messenger named Knut Ivanov came to the tsar and sovereign from the Swedish King Gustav, magister of the city of Abava, in Russian as it were an archimandrite, to petition with letters; and he sets out his petition in the letters thus: that much bloodshed had occurred through sin and through the provocation of border people on both sides, and if the tsar and sovereign would show him favor, assuage his anger and restrain his army, and recall his former wrong, that he should not conduct negotiations with the Novgorod namestniki, but his son should conduct them.

And the tsar and sovereign showed favor to his messenger, sent him back to him, and ordered a reply to be written to him: that the sovereign had shown him favor for his submissive petition and had ordered the Novgorod namestniki to confirm the old truce with him; and as for his son conducting negotiations with the Novgorod namestniki, he ordered it to be said that he himself should negotiate in the old manner, as had been from the beginning; and if he set aside his pride and wished to act in the old manner, then he should send his noble ambassadors to the sovereign to petition and to confirm the truce in the old manner. And the messenger was sent back in the same month.

On the dispatch of ambassadors to Lithuania

In the month of May the sovereign sent his ambassadors to Lithuania: boyar Ivan Mikhailovich Vorontsov and treasurer Fyodor Ivanovich Sukin, and clerk Boris Shchekin, so that they might conclude a truce and bring the king to oath.
