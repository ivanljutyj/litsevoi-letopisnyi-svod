Great Constantine was greatly indignant at this, but nevertheless he ordered their words to be written down. And he divided the masters and city craftsmen into two groups: to one he gave orders to measure out the city walls and towers and begin building them; and to the other he gave orders to lay out the streets and squares according to Roman custom.

And so they began to build churches of God, and the royal palace, and other houses for illustrious nobles and megistans and all dignitaries; and to bring in sweet waters.

In the seventh year, the king, seeing that few people lived in the city because it was very large, did thus: having gathered illustrious nobles, megistans, and dignitaries from Rome and from other lands, and with a multitude of people he brought them there, built great houses for them, and allowed them to live in the city with great comfort, and honored them with royal ranks, so that they might forget their own homes and fatherland. The king also created a great palace and a wondrous hippodrome, and laid out two market squares, that is, covered streets for commerce, and named the city New Rome.

Then he built most glorious churches: of Holy Sophia, of the Holy Apostles, of Saint Irene, of Saint Mocius, of Saint Michael the Archangel, and many others.

He also set up the wondrous purple column, which had been brought by sea from Rome to Tsargrad three years earlier, very huge and heavy: it was moved from the sea to the marketplace over the course of a year.

And the king came here often, and gave much gold to people to guard it, and placed in its foundation the twelve baskets that Christ blessed, and a portion of the precious wood and of holy relics for the strengthening and preservation of this most wondrous and single-stone column. And he set upon it an idol, which he had brought from the Sun City of Phrygia, with seven rays on its head.

He also brought here other most wondrous and praiseworthy things from many lands and cities, and, having adorned and renewed the city, rendered it great honor with festivals and great celebrations over many days. And so he established that that city should be called Tsargrad. And there was great joy among the people.
