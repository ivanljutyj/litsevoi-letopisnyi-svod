And he went to the tomb of Peter the Wonderworker, together with his brother Prince Yuri, and began to shed tears and to call the Wonderworker to his aid, and said: “O Wonderworker Peter, look upon us orphans! We were left without our father and mother while still small; we have no consolation on earth from anywhere, and now great sorrow has come upon us from the infidels; and it is fitting for you to pray for us, for God lit you for us like a bright candle and set you on the candlestick; God gave you to our family and to all Orthodox Christendom as a firm guardian; do not leave us in the time of our sorrow, pray to God for us and for all the Christian race, that He may deliver us from the ungodly!” And he went into the church and began to pray with tears and to venerate the holy icons.

And having taken a blessing from his father Metropolitan Joasaph, he went to his own court, and he ordered his father Metropolitan Joasaph to go with him.

And he came to the hall where he had been sitting with the boyars, and said to his father Metropolitan Joasaph: “Know, father, the misfortune that has come upon us: the Crimean Tsar has come to our land, to the bank of the Oka River, and has brought many hordes with him; and do you consult with the boyars about us, whether we should stay here in the city or withdraw somewhere?” And the boyars began to say: “Even before, for our sins, God permitted the infidels to come against Christendom; tsars stood before the city of Moscow, and the grand princes did not sit out in the city.” And other boyars said: “If, for our sins, tsars stood before Moscow, then our sovereigns were not little children; they could bear great hardship and take thought for themselves and help the land. But when Edigey came and stood before Moscow, Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich then left in the city Prince Vladimir Andreevich and his own brothers, Prince Andrei and Prince Peter Dmitrievich, while the Grand Prince himself departed to Kostroma; and Edigey sent his son in pursuit of him with many men, and God barely spared the Grand Prince, so that he did not fall into the hands of the Tatars. But now our sovereign the Grand Prince is small, and his brother is even smaller: they cannot endure fast riding and fatigue; and how can one ride fast with small children?” The Metropolitan said: “But the cities to which our sovereigns withdrew in times of Tatar incursions—Kostroma and other cities—those cities, for our sins, are now not at peace with Kazan; and our sovereigns did not withdraw to Novgorod and Pskov because of the proximity of the Lithuanian and German border. But to whom should we leave the Wonderworkers and Moscow? Grand princes have departed from Moscow, but they left their brothers in the city to guard it; Grand Prince Dmitry departed from Moscow and did not leave his brother and strong commanders, and what became of Moscow? Lord, protect and have mercy from such misfortune!
