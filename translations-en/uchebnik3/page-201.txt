And after this the posadnik Feofilat said: “That the sovereign Grand Prince would show favor to the Novgorod boyars who are with him, release them, and accept the petition of the archbishop and of all Veliky Novgorod.”

And the posadnik Luka Fedorov said: “That the sovereign Grand Prince would grant his patrimony: that he would come to Veliky Novgorod every fourth year and take four thousand rubles; and that he would order the viceroy and the posadnik to judge in the city, and whatever the viceroy and posadnik cannot settle, the sovereign Grand Prince himself would give judgment in, having come in the fourth year; and that the sovereign Grand Prince would grant that summonses be abolished, so that there should be no summonses to Moscow.”

And Yakov Fedorov said: “That the Grand Prince would grant that he not order his viceroys to judge in the archbishop’s courts, nor in the posadniks’ courts.” And the wealthy people spoke with their companions about how the Grand Prince’s men call the Novgorodians “Mukobryans,” and how from the viceroys and posadniks in the city they exact payment from the Novgorodians in the city before the viceroy and the posadnik; but whatever the Novgorodians exact from them, they do not answer in the city, but answer at Gorodishche; and the sovereign Grand Prince would grant that he order his viceroy together with the posadnik to judge them in the city.

After all these speeches Yakov Korob said: “What, sir, his prayerful intercessor the archbishop and the posadniks and wealthy townsmen of Veliky Novgorod petitioned the sovereign Grand Prince—that petition is before our sovereign Grand Prince; and then his patrimony petitions him, that the sovereign would grant and instruct his patrimony as God lays it on his heart to favor his patrimony; and his patrimony petitions its sovereign as to how they may be independent.”

And on that same day, Monday, the Grand Prince sent his commanders to Novgorod to seize Gorodishche and the monasteries: from Bronnich he ordered his commanders Prince Daniil Dmitrievich, Fedor Davydovich, Prince Ivan Vasilievich Striza, and Grigory and Ivan Nikitichi with their regiments to go straight to the city; and on the other side of the city, to Yuryev Monastery and Arkadyevsky Monastery, he ordered the commanders Prince Semyon Ryapolovsky, Prince Alexander Obolensky, Prince Boris Obolensky, and Vasily Saburov with regiments to go, and Semyon Saburov with the men of his mother, Grand Princess Maria, and of his brother Prince Andrei the Younger, and Elizar Gusev with the prince’s own regiment.
