It was impossible. Vasily answered: “By God’s will a city is founded, and without God’s will who can take a city? The tsar should wait a little for the grand prince’s commanders who are coming after him.” The Tatars withdrew to their camps.

The tsar ordered all the men to build siege towers and wall-breaking engines for the assault, and he intended to assault the city with all his men from all sides;

Vasily and Alexander, with all the people, including the women, strengthened the city; and they ordered stakes, stones, and water to be carried onto the wall.

At that time boyar sons came from the commanders, from Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and his companions—Andrei, son of Vasily Ovtsyn, and Ivan, son of Semyon Nashchekin Vetreny, with their companions, seven men in all—with news that they should hold the city firmly: “And we are coming to the city in haste with many men and intend to do battle with the tsar, as much as God helps us.” And there was great joy in the city.

At that time a townsman fell into the hands of the tsar’s guards and told the tsar that there was joy in the city: boyar sons had come from the commanders with news that the commanders were coming in haste with all their men and intended to do battle with the tsar, as much as God would help them. And the tsar’s guards said that they themselves had seen those men when they rode into the city. And the tsar postponed the assault.

He ordered the siege towers and artillery to be burned, and he himself went away from the city on Saturday, on the Day of the Transfiguration.

The grand prince’s commanders came to the city, but the tsar, because of that news, hastened away from the city with all his men.

And the commanders immediately set out after the tsar, came to the Don, but the tsar had already crossed the Don.

And the commanders sent a few men after the tsar, while the commanders themselves returned from Kolomna to Moscow to the tsar, the grand prince, all in good health. At that time the sovereign had been preparing a large army for Kazan, but the men who were in Vladimir and Murom did not arrive in time.

And then there was great joy in Moscow, and the sovereign rewarded the soldiers and commanders with his great bounty, with fur coats and goblets.

In the year 7050 from Adam (in the year 1550 from Christ)

On the twelfth day of September, Nogai envoys came to the grand prince in Moscow from Koshum-Murza—Asan-Sufa the bogatyr with his companions—and from Uraz-Murza, Baiterek with his companions, and they petitioned the grand prince that the grand prince should be in firm friendship with them. And the grand prince ordered them to be lodged beyond the Moscow River at the Nogai Court, and ordered them to trade.
