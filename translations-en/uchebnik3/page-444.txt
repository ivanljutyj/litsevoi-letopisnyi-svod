But not all of the Grand Prince’s men had yet come to the bank against the tsar: from the Ugra the commanders Prince Roman Ivanovich Odoevsky and Ivan Petrovich with many men had not yet come to the bank. But the Lord God, through the prayer of His Mother, the Most Pure Theotokos, and of the great wonderworkers Peter and Alexis, sent His mercy upon the Grand Prince’s army, took fear from their hearts, and armed them with courage: as if invited to a feast, they wished to fight the Tatars. And from all the regiments a few men came to the bank to help the advanced regiment, and they beat the Tatars back from the bank.

And they began to fight the Tatars and to demand the bank. The tsar saw and marveled at the courage of the Russian sons; terror came upon him, fear fell into his heart, and he wished to flee at once. But his princes restrained him.

The accursed Tsar Saip-Giray withdrew to his camps in great perplexity and took counsel with his princes.

And that night the Grand Prince’s great artillery train arrived; and the commanders ordered the great cannons and guns to be let through and made ready by morning. And the tsar heard that great cannons were being brought through, though by day they had not been there, and great fear came upon him.

And at that same hour he fled from the bank. He had come to the bank on Saturday at the third hour of the day, but he fled early on Sunday, on the commemoration of the righteous Saint Eudokimos, on the eve of the Saviour’s Day. He had come to the Russian land with great boasting, like a lion seeking to plunder Christ’s flock of rational sheep; he exalted himself in pride, likening himself to the former Greek kings who called themselves gods but descended into hell. That same accursed Tsar Saip-Giray had sent to the Grand Prince with great arrogance: “I will come against you, and I will stand under Moscow in your village of Vorobyovo, and I will scatter my army and take your land captive” — but he did not know that the Lord’s hand is high. O great mercy of God! He came with such great boasting, yet fled with great fear, unable even to sit on a horse, and they carried him in a cart; many carts they cut up, and other carts with supplies they abandoned.

The Grand Prince’s commanders, seeing that the tsar had fled from the bank, sent Prince Ivan, son of Prince Alexander Kashin, to the Grand Prince with that news; and after the tsar the commanders sent Ilya Levin with his companions to follow the tsar.

Ilya sent word to the commanders that the tsar had gone by the same road by which he had come to the Oka; and he himself went after the tsar.

The commanders began to take counsel whether to cross the river with all their men in order to go after the tsar: for it is the custom in war that one does not go in pursuit with all one’s men.
