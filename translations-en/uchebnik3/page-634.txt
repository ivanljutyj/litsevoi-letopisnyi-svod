Ivan also wrote that many merchants had come from Shemakha, Derbent, Shevkal, Tyumen, Urgench, and Zaraychik with all kinds of goods; and Ivan and Mikhail allowed them to trade and collected customs duties from them for the sovereign; and from Astrakhan too came envoys from Crimean Shevkal and from all the Shevkal land, and from the Tyumen prince with gifts, to petition that the sovereign grant them favor and order them to be in his subjection and take them into his servitude, and command the Astrakhan voivodes to protect them from all lands, and grant the merchants safe passage and order it made unobstructed; and whatever the sovereign likes from them, and whatever he orders sent to him, they will send all of it to the sovereign annually.

The envoy of the tsar and grand prince came from the Nogai Horde

In the same month there came from the Nogai Horde the envoy of the tsar and grand prince, Pyotr Sovin, and from Prince Ismail his envoy Tauzyar; and Pyotr brought the oath charter: Prince Ismail and all the Nogai murzas swore allegiance to the sovereign in everything about which the sovereign had written to them; that they would everywhere be obedient to the tsar and grand prince and would stand against the Crimean tsar together with the tsar and grand prince. And Prince Ismail showed Pyotr Sovin an honor such as had never before been shown to any Moscow envoy in the Nogai Horde. And the oath with Ismail was sworn by Nuradin Yunus Mirza, son of Yusuf, and Alei Mirza, and all Yusuf’s children, and Tikhovat Araspan-Murza, and many murzas who agreed with Prince Ismail; and all gave the oath according to the sovereign’s will in all things.

Concerning the Embassy from the Germans

In August the Master, the Archbishop, and the Bishop sent from the Livonian Germans with a letter to petition that the sovereign grant favor and give a safe-conduct for their envoys; and the sovereign gave the safe-conduct and permitted their envoys to arrive.

The envoys of the tsar and grand prince came from the Nogai Horde

In the year 7066 from Adam (1566 from Christ), in September, there returned from the Nogai Horde the envoys of the tsar and grand prince: from Yunus, Yelka Maltsov; from Eroslan, Ivan Tveritinov; and from Alei, Mokey Lachinov; and with them the people of Yunus and of those murzas. And Yelka said that Yunus with his brothers had driven Ismail from the principality, and Yunus himself had become prince and had sworn allegiance to the tsar and sovereign: if he established himself in the principality, he wished to serve the tsar and sovereign; but he had been driven out by the sovereign’s own slave, and there was no hope in anyone; and the sovereign should not hold grief against him for having quarreled with Ismail—
