Then all the multitude of Orthodox Christians went to the tomb of the saint, desiring only to touch the tomb of the saint.

Certain devout priests, during the bringing of the honorable relics of the saint, saw a white dove above his tomb, soaring very high, until the relics of the saint were covered, and then it became invisible.

When the time for the liturgy came, the metropolitan himself served in the chamber, in the Church of the Deposition of the Robe at his court, and he ordered the bishops and all the priests to serve in the Archangel Cathedral.

And then the Grand Prince ordered sufficient alms to be distributed to everyone, also to all the cathedral clergy, and throughout the city—to priests, monasteries, and beggars.

And the bishops and princes, and the brothers of the Grand Prince, and the archimandrites, and the senior priests, and many boyars ate at the Grand Prince’s table; and all the cathedral clergy ate and drank at his court as well. And from that day they established this feast of the translation of the relics of our holy father, the most reverend Metropolitan Peter the Wonderworker.

Concerning Fryazin, How He Went with the Tsarevna from Rome

In the same year, on the 23rd day of the month of May, Ivan Fryazin came to Rome to Pope Sixtus and to Cardinal Bessarion; and Ivan Fryazin and those with him were shown great honor by the pope and by the tsareviches, the sons of Thomas, Andrew and Manuel, and received great gifts; and they were there 32 days.

On the 24th day of June they sent Tsarevna Sophia from Rome to the Grand Prince; and with her they sent the legate Anthony as ambassador from the pope, and with him many Romans; and from the tsareviches an ambassador went with her—Dmitry Manuilovich with many Greeks; many other Greeks also went with her to serve her.

And they went not by the route by which Fryazin had gone, but through the papal domain on their way.

And the pope sent his letters to all cities, and also to the places through which they were to pass, even as far as the patrimony of the Grand Prince, to Pskov; and he wrote to them that all the princes of those lands, and honorable lords, and bishops, and all the lands wherever the Tsarevna might come, should meet her and show her honor, and give provisions, carts, and guides, and the same to all who went with her, as far as the patrimony of the Grand Prince.

And according to those papal letters all those lands rendered great honor to Tsarevna Sophia and to all who were with her.

In the same year, on the 26th day of June, news came to the Grand Prince from Perm that the commander Prince Fyodor Pestroy had taken the Perm land.
