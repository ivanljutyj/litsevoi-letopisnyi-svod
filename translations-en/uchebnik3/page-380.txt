The great prince rode out and stood at Kolomenskoye, waiting for Prince Andrei Ivanovich, his brother, and for the voivodes with many men.

And on that same day word came to the great prince at Kolomenskoye from the voivodes at Ryazan that Safa-Girey Tsar and Islam Tsar, and other tsareviches with many men, had come against Ryazan and burned the suburbs.

And the great prince immediately sent to the voivodes, ordering them to send across the Oka River to take tongues.

And the voivodes, according to the command of the great sovereign, sent across the Oka River the voivode Prince Ivan Fedorovich Obolensky Ovchina; and to another place the voivodes sent Prince Dmitry, son of Prince Fedor Paletsky; and to a third place the voivodes sent Prince Dmitry, son of Prince Yuri Drutsky, with few men.

And the voivode, Prince Ivan Fedorovich, by the mercy of God and of His most pure Mother and by the prayers of the holy wonderworkers of Russia, Peter and Alexius, intercessors of the Russian land, overtook the Tatars, killed many, and took others alive.

And Prince Dmitry Paletsky and Prince Dmitry Drutsky likewise killed many Tatars, and took others alive and sent them to the voivodes, and the voivodes sent those Tatars to the great prince at Kolomenskoye.

Seeing this, the enemies of God, the traitors and perjurers Saihp-Girey Tsar and Islam Tsar, by God’s help, which exalted the arm of the great sovereign Vasily Ivanovich, quickly fled away from the guarded places of the great prince.

And the voivodes sent word to the great prince that the tsareviches had fled to their own lands.

And the great prince, hearing this, returned to the reigning city of Moscow on the twenty-first day of August, and ordered his voivodes, Prince Dmitry and others, to be with him in Moscow.

On the Great Bell

In that same year, by the command of the great sovereign Vasily Ivanovich, by God’s mercy autocrat of all Rus, a great bell, the Blagovestnik, was cast, and in it were 1000 poods, and Nikolai Nemchin cast it; and it was placed on the wooden bell tower in the forty-second year, in December, on the ninth day.
