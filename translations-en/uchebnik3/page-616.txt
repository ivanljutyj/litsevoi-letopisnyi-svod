The sovereign ordered him to stand at Tarusa; and at Tarusa he also ordered the forward regiment and the right-hand regiment to take position, and he ordered the great regiment to stand at the mouth of the Protva, and the left-hand regiment at the Senkin ferry, and the guard regiment at the mouth of the Lopasnya.

The tsar and grand prince ordered the boyar children to be checked against the lists

In the month of June the tsar and grand prince ordered the commanders to check the boyar children against the lists, whether all had come. And the commanders told the sovereign and wrote from all places that the boyar children had assembled according to the lists.

And the sovereign himself reviewed his regiment, the boyars and princes, and the boyar children, and all their people, so that the sovereign might learn about his host, who and how served him, and (granted them) the sovereign’s pay according to their worth.

On the sovereign’s order to Prince Vladimir Andreyevich

And on that same day the sovereign gave an order to Prince Vladimir Andreyevich. On that same day the sovereign ordered the boyar children and their people to be reviewed in all places.

And lists were brought to the sovereign from all places, and the sovereign checked his host, something that had not happened before, since many hid, avoiding service. The tsar and grand prince also stirred up even the lazy to strive for piety, and therefore a multitude came to service.

On the tsar’s decree

And in that same month the sovereign decided to go beyond the river, and he sent his okolnichy Nikita Vasilyevich Sheremetev and ordered places to be occupied beyond the Shivoron River in the Field.

On the report sent to the sovereign by Daniil Chulkov from the Field

Daniilko Chulkov sent nine Crimean Tatars from the Field to the tsar and sovereign and said that he had met two hundred Crimean Tatars on the Don near Azov and had defeated them utterly. And the captives said that the Crimean tsar had wanted to go against the borders of the tsar and grand prince and had sent Senka Zhekoulov to obtain captives, and he had seized a peasant in the Siverian estates; and they told the tsar that the tsar and grand prince had learned about him and was ready to fight him, that he had come out and was waiting for him; and then the Crimean tsar did not go against the borders of the tsar and grand prince, but went against the Circassians. And as soon as he came to the Mius, they immediately sent after him from the Crimea, because they had seen many Russian people on the Dnieper, and they were going to Islam-Kirmen. And the tsar, according to those tidings, returned to the Crimea. Moreover, the same captives said that many of the tsar’s people had died of disease, and that his campaign could not possibly take place.
