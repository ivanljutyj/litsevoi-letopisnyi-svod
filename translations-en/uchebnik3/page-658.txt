To the brave champion and defender. And so strive always against the ungodly assemblies and scatter their storms and seditions that occur everywhere, and in God set your good deeds against ungodliness by breaking it down, and do good, conquering the ungodly with God’s help. And hearing of your bravery, let us clap spiritually, rejoicing and making merry for the strong and meekest and autocratic tsar, who has appeared to us as a second sun; and as the purest sky, so your piety shines, and as the air is fair and announces good tidings to us with hope, so through your help may we be delivered and graciously saved from the hand of the ungodly rising against us. And with God’s help may your royal victories grow every day, and may the opposing forces be conquered and thrown into confusion and be subdued; for as from the great cold of fierce winter, when summer comes, every living creature, even those flying in the air, cries aloud in the cities, so we, from the great insolence and fierce winter’s cold of the unbelieving and ungodly, await the sweetest summer, and look for and cry out for the humble and meekest peace of your kingdom, and proclaim comfort; our hearts vividly portray to us your grace-given bravery, and you are truly the foundation and beginning and helper of every army, and exceedingly our eyes look to you and we hope in you wherever you may abide. Just as the shoots of earthly branches are warmed by the sun’s radiance, so we rejoice when we hear your royal name in piety, multiplied in glory and worthy of praise, autocratic in fortitude and power; and who, having heard, is worthy to express the beauty of your comeliness? Having consulted therefore from so many things, I wished in word and discourse to set forth the fair form of your praises, worthy of your beneficent bravery and God-crowning, but I am not sufficient for this to match your grace-endowed army and struggle; so may it be possible for me to utter your worthy praises, to speak to you of worthy praise and to tell of the adornments of your royal victories. But I think, though I wish to speak, I do not have fitting strength; for the ineffably most high grace has been multiplied in your kingdom, and it is impossible for us to utter words equal to your likeness. But you, being humane, holy tsar, forgive my folly and do not compel me to a further answer or recompense, and receive this lovingly, for we understand: God does not require praises from unworthy lips, but truly accepts the praises of those who please God; and now, O tsar, do not heed the adulation in these woven and shapeless words, which I have written to you as the best of my understanding, but heed our labors and the word spoken with great love, gathering praises worthy of you. And we pray you, open to us your divine and royal ears, and hear the petition of our humility, asking from you and keeping, like the eyes of a maidservant on the hand of her mistress. And now our common royal monastery of Sinai, for we have nothing of our own, who will help and have mercy on us? And we cry out and wail and, weeping, cry aloud…
