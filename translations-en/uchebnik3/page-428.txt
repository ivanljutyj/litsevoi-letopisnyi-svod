On the commemoration of the holy righteous Eudocimus, on the eve of the Savior’s Day. He came to the Russian land with great boasting and with a multitude of his host, roaring like a lion, intending to seize Christ’s flock of rational sheep, the God-protected realm of Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, and he wished to overcome the unconquerable and to lay waste the inviolable patrimony of the Mother of God; for, puffed up with pride, the wretched Tsar Sahib-Giray sent to the Grand Prince with great arrogance: “I will come against you and stand beneath Moscow in your village of Vorobyovo, and I will scatter my host and plunder your land”; but he did not know that the hand of the Lord is exalted. O the great mercy of God! He came with such great boasting, yet fled with great fear, unable even to sit on a horse, and they carried him in a cart: many carts they cut up, and other carts with supplies they abandoned.

The Grand Prince’s commanders saw that the Tsar had fled from the bank, and with that news they sent Prince Ivan Alexandrov, son of Kashin, to the Grand Prince.

And after the Tsar the commanders sent Ilya Levin with his companions to find out about the Tsar.

Ilya reported to the commanders that the Tsar had gone by the same road by which he had come to the Oka; and he himself went after the Tsar, following the traces of his camps.

The commanders began to consult whether to cross the river with all their men and go after the Tsar; for it is not customary in warfare to send all one’s men in pursuit.

And they sent after the Tsar the commanders Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and Prince Vasily Semyonovich Obolensky Serebryany, and with them many men, chosen from all the regiments, both court and town.

And the Ryazan commanders, Prince Mikhail Andreyevich Trubetskoy with his companions, with all the men who had come with him, they sent to Ryazan.

The Grand Prince’s commanders, going after the Tsar, killed many Tatars who had fallen behind.

And having captured other Tatars alive, they sent them to the senior commanders, to Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky and his companions.

And those captives told the commanders that the Tsar had said to his princes that he had received great disgrace, having brought with him men from many hordes, yet had done nothing to the Russian land.

Then the princes began to recall Temir-Aksak, that he too had come against Rus with many forces and had not obtained what he desired; and the old Tatars told him that on that campaign Temir-Aksak had taken the town of Yelets.

And the Tsar said to the princes: “The Grand Prince has a town in the Field called Pronsk; it lies near our path, and we will take it and do...”
