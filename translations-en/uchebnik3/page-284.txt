In the same month of April, Grand Prince Alexander sent to Grand Prince Ivan his envoy, Pan Stanislav Kishka, namestnik of Smolensk, and the secretary Fedka Tolstoy, concerning Prince Semyon Belsky and other matters.

And Grand Prince Ivan answered the Lithuanian envoys that he had taken Prince Semyon with his patrimony because of coercion, since Grand Prince Alexander was forcing them to convert to the Roman faith. And he ordered them to convey through those envoys, Stanislav and Fedko, that Alexander should not force Ivan’s daughter Elena, Alexander’s grand princess, to abandon the Greek faith for the Roman, nor force all the Russians who served him to convert to the Roman faith, and that he should do everything according to the treaty and oath on the cross: “But if you begin to force them, then because of that coercion they will come to us, and we will receive them with their patrimonies and will stand for them as much as God helps us.”

Concerning Starodubsky and Shemyachich

And after that, also in April, Prince Semyon, son of Prince Ivan Andreyevich Mozhaisky, and Prince Vasily, son of Prince Ivan Dmitrievich Shemyaka, sent word to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, sovereign of all Rus, that they were being forcibly compelled to abandon the Greek faith, and therefore they asked that the sovereign show them favor and take them to himself with their patrimonies; and Grand Prince Alexander of Lithuania had also violated the treaty in other matters.

And Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus, because of that need of Prince Semyon and Prince Vasily, took them into service with their patrimonies, and concerning them sent Ivan Teleshov with a refusal, and sent Afanasy Sheenok Vyazmyatin to Grand Prince Alexander with a letter of rupture; and to the princes he sent his voivode, boyar Yakov Zakharyich, and his other voivodes with many men.

And Yakov Zakharyich left Moscow on the third day of May, a Sunday, and, coming into the Lithuanian land, he seized the city of Bryansk, and captured the voivode and namestnik of Bryansk, Pan Stanislav Bartashev, and the Bishop of Bryansk, and sent them to the Grand Prince in Moscow.

And from there Yakov Zakharyich went to the princes and brought them to oath on the cross to serve the sovereign Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus with their patrimonies.

And going from there, Yakov with the princes seized the city of Putivl on the sixth day of August, on the Feast of the Savior, and captured Prince Bogdan Glinsky together with his wife.
