I swear by God, I have seen with my sinful eyes great sovereigns who fled from the Turks with property and wander like strangers, and ask death from God as a reward. From such a victory may the Lord spare the Orthodox Christians, and us sinners, through the prayers of the Mother of God and all the saints. Amen. The tsar fled on the 11th day of November; there were great frosts then as well.

Then there were many deliberations among many people: some strove to fight the impious unto blood and death, while others thought of flight, sparing their own life, wishing to prove traitors to the Russian land and helpers to the infidels.

Archbishop Vassian of Rostov heard of this and, as if inspired by God, wrote a wondrous and instructive epistle to his spiritual son, Grand Prince Ivan, strengthening him for the feat, with many testimonies from the Divine Scriptures, and sent it to him on the Ugra.

The Epistle of Vassian, Archbishop of Rostov, to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, Autocrat of All Rus, to Yugra, concerning the brave deed against the godless Tatars for Orthodox Christianity and for the God-given power of the Russian tsardom

And in the letter he wrote thus: “To the faithful and Christ-loving, noble and God-crowned, and God-established, who has shone forth in piety in all ends of the universe, most radiant and most glorious among all kings, our sovereign Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of All Rus, your pilgrim, Archbishop Vassian of Rostov, sends his blessing and bows his head. I beseech your majesty, O God-loving sovereign, do not be angry with me, humble one, that I was the first to dare speak openly with your majesty for the sake of your salvation. We ought, great lord, to remember your deeds, and you, sovereigns, to listen to us. Now I have dared to write to your nobility: I want to remind you of something from Holy Scripture, as much as God will enlighten me, for the strengthening and establishment of your power.
