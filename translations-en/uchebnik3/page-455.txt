In the same year, in May, the Grand Prince showed favor to Prince Ivan and released him from exile.

In the same month, December, on the 30th day, the Crimean tsarevich Imin-Girey kalga, son of Tsar Saip-Girey, came with many Crimean people, secretly to the border places of Belev and Odoev. /And through our sins, because of negligence, they took many people captive./

At that time the commanders were there: Prince Pyotr and Prince Konstantin Kurlyatev, and Prince Mikhailo Vorotynsky; and they quarreled over posts, and therefore did not go to help those places; and the Tatars, having taken many people captive, withdrew.

In the same winter, on March 22, King Sigismund Augustus sent to the Grand Prince his envoy Jan Komaevsky, saying that his father had invested him with all his realm (and his father, King Sigismund, had passed away), and that he had now become sovereign in his father’s realm, and had declared himself in the realm, and wished to rule according to the truce charters, as his father had ruled.

Beginning of the Kazan War of Tsar
and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich

In the same spring, in April, the Grand Prince sent his commanders—Prince Semyon Ivanovich Punkov with his companions in the large regiment, Ivan Vasilyevich Sheremetev in the forward regiment, and Prince David Paletsky in the guard regiment—to Kazan on a small raid in boats.

And from Vyatka he sent his commanders to Kazan as well, Prince Vasily Semyonovich Serebryany with his companions.

And the Vyatka commanders met with Prince Semyon Punkov at the Kazan mouth on Sunday, on the Descent of the Holy Spirit, at the same hour, as if from one courtyard; and Prince Vasily, sailing along the Vyatka and Kama, killed many Kazan people.

And the Grand Prince’s commanders, Prince Semyon and his companions, having come to the city of Kazan, killed many Kazan people and burned the tsar’s taverns.

And into the Sviyaga River the commanders sent boyar sons from among themselves, and there, by God’s mercy, they likewise killed many Kazan people, and took Tevekel’s son, Prince Murtaza-Murza, with his son, while Murtaza’s wife and his other children they killed.
