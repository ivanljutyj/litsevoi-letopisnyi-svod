For the summer, in the frontier towns, when the time came to send them home, he ordered them released. But small detachments were left then to obstruct the enemy’s approach—light commanders with small forces in the frontier towns.

And when news came to the tsar and grand prince that Tsar Devlet-Girey had come against the Ryazan land, he quickly returned from Suzdal to Moscow, wishing to march against Tsar Devlet-Girey and himself defend his patrimony from Moscow. And in Moscow at that time was the son of the tsar and grand prince, Tsarevich Feodor.

To the bank were then sent from Moscow boyars and commanders Ivan Petrovich Fedorov and Ivan Petrovich Yakovlev, and with them the court of the tsar and grand prince, those few who were at that time in Moscow, because it was not the time then to gather many people: all had been sent home.

They, according to the sovereign’s command, came to the bank of the Oka River and sent their commanders across the river against the enemy to various places.

And those commanders joined with the Mikhailov and Dedilov commanders and with others, defeated the Crimean people in many places near the tsar’s camps, and took Crimean captives, many notable men. And the Crimean people saw the men of the tsar and grand prince ready for battle, and therefore the Crimean people did not go beyond the Vozha.

But the tsar, hearing of the commanders of the tsar and grand prince and that with them there were newly arrived people, soon departed from Ryazan and did not even wait for his men from the raids within the allotted days for war.

At the same time in Ryazan, holding an estate by the sovereign’s grant, were boyar Aleksei Danilovich Basmanov-Pleshcheev and his son Fedor, and, hearing of the arrival of many Crimean people on the Ryazan frontier, they with their few local people defeated the Crimean people and took captives before reaching the city. Those captives said that Tsar Devlet-Girey had come, and with him his sons Kalga Magmet-Girey Tsarevich and Aldy-Girey Tsarevich with all the Crimean people. That was the first news of the tsar, for he had come secretly.

Those same captives Aleksei Danilovich Basmanov and his son Fedor sent to the sovereign tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilievich.

But Aleksei himself and his son Fedor remained in the city of Ryazan with Vladyka Philotheus and reassured the people who were in the city; for there were then no service people at all except the town people living there and the villagers who had managed to run into the city. There were then no city fortifications on the wall, because the wall was very old and had collapsed in places; and the tsar’s unexpected arrival was dangerous, but that did not come to pass: they had then barely managed, with difficulty, to make fortifications at the city and strengthen the wall
