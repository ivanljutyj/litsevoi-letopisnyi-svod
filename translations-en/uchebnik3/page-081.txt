And morning came, and the sun had already risen; the grand prince arose and ordered Matins to be served on the seventh day of July, a Wednesday.

After Matins the grand prince wished to sleep a little longer, but at that hour news came to him that the Tatars were crossing the river Nerl.

He began sending messengers to all the camps, and at once began to put on his armor, and having raised the banners, set out against the Tatars. And his cousins, Prince Ivan Andreevich of Mozhaisk and Prince Mikhail Andreevich of Vereya, and Prince Vasily Yaroslavich, brother-in-law of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilievich, went after him; and all the princes, boyars, and voivodes, and all the regiments went after him, having put on their armor, and all zealously set out against the Tatars. And they went out onto the field, but their host was small, only about one and a half thousand men, because not all the regiments had yet managed to join, and Tsarevich Berdedat had not managed to come to the grand prince, since that night he had lodged in Yuryev. But Prince Dmitry Shemyaka did not come and did not send his regiments. They came out onto the field near Efimyev Monastery on the left side and there met the accursed Hagarites; their huge host appeared before the Christian regiments. They began the battle, and at first the grand prince's regiments began to prevail, and the Tatars fled; our men drove some Tatars, while others fled of themselves, and yet other of our warriors began to plunder the already slain Tatars; but the Tatars returned and fell upon the Christians, and overcame them. They captured the grand prince himself, as well as Prince Mikhail Andreevich, and many other princes, boyars, boyars' sons, and other warriors. Prince Ivan Andreevich was wounded several times and thrown from his horse, but when another horse was given him, he escaped. The grand prince had many wounds on his head and hands, and his whole body was battered, for he fought valiantly. This calamity befell the Christians on the seventh day of July, a Wednesday. Many Tatars were also killed then, more than five hundred, and their total number was three and a half thousand.

The Tatars rushed in pursuit and killed and plundered many Russian warriors, burned villages, cut down people, and led many away into captivity. The tsareviches settled in Evfimyev Monastery, and there, having taken from the grand prince his pectoral crosses, they sent them to Moscow to his mother, Grand Princess Sophia, and to his wife, Grand Princess Maria, accompanied by the Tatar Achisan.

And when he came to Moscow, loud weeping and wailing arose everywhere, not only of the grand princesses but of all Christendom.
