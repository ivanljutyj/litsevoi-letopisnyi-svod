And after nine days the vile Magmet ordered the whole army to assault the city and fight every day; and he ordered the great cannon to be remade and made even more powerful.

Upon learning of this, the nobles and Zustuney gathered together with the patriarch and began to urge the tsar, saying: “We see, tsar, that this faithless one does not weaken the siege, but is preparing for an even greater undertaking; and what can we do, expecting help from nowhere? But it is fitting for you, tsar, to leave the city for a suitable place; and when your people and your brothers learn of this, they will come to your aid, and the Albanians too, having taken fright, will come; besides, then the godless Magmet also, having been terrified, will withdraw from the city.” Having said this and much else to the tsar, they gave him ships and Zustuney’s galleys. The tsar was silent for a long time, shedding tears, and then said to them: “I praise and thank you for your counsel, and I know that all this is for my benefit, because it may indeed be so; but how shall I do this and abandon the priesthood and the churches of God, and the kingdom, and all the people in such misfortune? And what will the world say to me? I beg you, tell me.” No, my Lord, no, let me die here with you!” And falling down, he bowed to them, weeping bitterly. But the patriarch and all the people there wept aloud and ceased their persuading, so that there would be no talk among the people.

Again they sent to Morea, and to all the islands, and to the Franks for help.

The townspeople fought with the Turks by day, and by night they climbed into the ditches and broke through the walls of the ditches on the field side, and penetrated into the ground along the wall, and in many places planted many vessels of gunpowder.

Also on the walls they prepared many vessels filled with pitch and combustible sulfur, and cloth with pitch and gunpowder. Twenty-five days had already passed since they had fought every day.

The godless one ordered the great cannon to be rolled up, which was bound with iron hoops, hoping to strengthen it.

And when they fired it for the first time, it fell apart into many pieces. The infidel considered himself shamed by this and soon ordered the siege towers to be rolled up to the city with all their forces, very secretly.

And when they had set the towers along the whole edge of the ditch, they intended, after filling the ditches with trees, brushwood, and earth, to move and roll the towers up to the city, and also, having undermined the wall in many places, to bring it down to the ground.

And as soon as a multitude of people set about filling the ditches, the townspeople immediately set fire to the powder vessels that had been planted beneath the ditch, and suddenly the earth thundered like great thunder and rose with the towers and the people like a mighty storm up to the clouds.

And there was heard the crashing and creaking of the towers, and a terrible wailing, and human groaning, and both sides fled: the townspeople from the walls into the city, and the Turks farther away from it.
