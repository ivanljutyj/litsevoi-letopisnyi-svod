Previously no one had fought in those places, but they came upon them secretly and took many people captive, and seized much property and slaughtered much livestock.

The boyar Mikhailo Yakovlevich transported the cannon across the lake to the same place where the town stands, to an island in the middle of the great lake. And the voivodes took their stand around the town, stationing all the streltsy at the town, building siege towers, and rolling up the cannon; and they began to fire from morning until noon, and they breached the wall to its foundation, swept the Germans from the wall, and the Germans surrendered the town.

By God’s mercy the voivodes took the town that day and installed in it the sovereign’s voivodes, Prince Nikita Priimkov and Andrei Pleshcheyev; and they left the streltsy head Grigory Kaftyrev with the streltsy, while they themselves went to Pskov, God granting, in good health with all their people. And the sovereign gave thanks to God for this, and sent Prince Fyodor Paletsky to the voivodes with his golden bounty.

The Death of Prince Vasily

In the same month, on the 20th day, during Maslenitsa, on the night from Wednesday to Thursday, in the tenth hour of the night, three hours before dawn, Prince Vasily Yuryevich died, son of Prince Yury Vasilyevich, grandson of Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of All Rus; and he was one year old lacking two weeks; and the sovereign felt no little sorrow over this.

That same night from Wednesday to Thursday, at the same time, in the tenth hour of the night, three hours before dawn, a daughter, Evdokia, was born to Prince Vladimir Andreyevich by his princess Evdokia. And the tsar and grand prince, and with him his son Tsarevich Ivan, and with them Tsar Alexander of Kazan and many boyars, on the next day, Friday, visited Prince Vladimir Andreyevich for his joy, and rejoiced with him, and partook of fruits.

Dispatch of the Voivodes to the German Land

In the same month the sovereign dispatched voivodes to the German Land: in the great regiment, the boyar and voivode Prince Andrei Mikhailovich Kurbsky, and the okolnichy and voivode Daniil Fyodorovich Adashev; in the advanced regiment, the voivode Prince Pyotr Ivanovich Gorensky, and Ivan Sharapov, son of Zamytsky; in the guard regiment, the voivodes Prince Fyodor Ivanovich Troekurov and Vasily Vasilyevich Rozladin Kvashnin; and with them boyar scions and zhiltsy. And for the summer he ordered Kazan Tatars to be with them, and ordered them to guard his cause as God would help them.
