The legate, having stood his ground a little, did according to the will of the Grand Prince; but our Frank, Ivan the Moneyer, insisted more on this: that thereby honor be shown to the pope and to that ambassador of theirs, and to all their land, as they had shown honor to him there; and he had renounced the Christian faith, called himself a Frank of their faith, concealed our baptism, and did everything there as they do.

The Wedding of Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich

And then they entered the city, on the twelfth day of November, Thursday.

The Metropolitan himself entered the church, put the vestments on himself, and signed the tsarevna with the cross, and the other Christians with her.

And he dismissed her from the church and went with her to Grand Princess Maria.

Soon after, Grand Prince Ivan also came to his mother; then they betrothed that tsarevna according to custom, as is proper in their realm.

And they went to the church for the liturgy. Metropolitan Philip served the Mass that day in the wooden Church of the Dormition of the Most Pure, which had been set up inside the newly begun construction of the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God.

And after serving the Mass, he wedded the right-believing Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich of all Rus’ to the Orthodox Tsarevna Sophia, daughter of Thomas, Despot of Ammorea; and that Thomas was a son of Emperor Manuel of Constantinople, and brother of Emperor John Kalouyan, and of Demetrius and Constantine. At their wedding were: the Grand Prince’s mother, Grand Princess Maria; his son Ivan; his brothers, the right-believing princes Andrew, Boris, and Andrew, with all their other princes and boyars; a multitude of people; that Roman ambassador, Legate Anthony, with his Romans; Demetrius the Greek, ambassador from the tsareviches, the brothers of the tsarevna, from Andrew and Manuel; and other Greeks with him, and many Greeks who had come to serve the tsarevna.

In the morning the legate performed the embassy from the pope and presented gifts to the Grand Prince; Demetrius the Greek did likewise from the Grand Prince’s brothers-in-law, Andrew and Manuel.
