On the Dispatch of Leonty Mansurov to Astrakhan

On May 24 the sovereign dispatched the envoy Leonty Mansurov to Astrakhan to Tsar Derbysh with his sovereign's favor, and at Derbysh's petition he sent to him the Astrakhan tsarinas: Tsarina Tevkel with her daughter, and another tsarina, Gandaza, daughter of Tsar Shavkal, and Derbysh's envoys Prince Kara-Iklesh and Kurmlen, and the old envoys of Emgurchey, Prince Tonotarak and his companions; and the sovereign sent the tsarinas and envoys from Moscow in boats.

On the Sending Forth of the Icons

In the same month of May, on the 26th day, the tsar and grand prince and Metropolitan Makary sent forth the icons and Vladyka Gury to Kazan and to the town of Sviyazhsk and to all the Kazan regions, and also archimandrites and hegumens. And the tsar and the metropolitan escorted the icons and the vladyka with crosses beyond the Frolov Gate, and to the boats they sent the Vladyka of Krutitsy to escort him, and to Simonov he was escorted by archimandrites, hegumens, and protopopes, and by boyars of the tsar and grand prince.

On the Arrival of the Cherkassian Princes

In August there arrived from the Cherkassians the Cherkassian princes: Prince Sibok and his brother Prince Atsymguk, sovereigns of the Zhazhenskie Cherkassians, and Prince Tutaryk, son of Prince Yezbuluy; and with Prince Sibok came his son Kudanek, and with them one hundred and fifty of their people; and the envoy of the tsar and grand prince, Andrey Shchepotyev, also came. And the Cherkassian princes petitioned on behalf of all the Cherkassian land that the sovereign grant them aid against the towns of the Turkish Tsar and against Azov and other towns, and against the Crimean Tsar, and that they would be slaves of the tsar and grand prince, with their wives and children, forever. And Andrey Shchepotyev told the tsar and grand prince the same: that they had sworn by all the land to be inseparable from the tsar and grand prince and to serve them forever, as the sovereign commands.

And the tsar and grand prince granted them his great favor, but concerning the towns of the Turkish Tsar he ordered them to be refused, because the Turkish Sultan is at peace with the tsar and grand prince, and from the Crimean Tsar he wishes to protect them as far as possible; and in his land he granted them free departure and arrival, and satisfied them with provisions and with salary from the treasury. And Prince Sibok petitioned the tsar and grand prince that the sovereign grant him and order his son Kudanek to be baptized; and Prince Tutaryk petitioned for himself, that the sovereign grant him and order him to be baptized.
