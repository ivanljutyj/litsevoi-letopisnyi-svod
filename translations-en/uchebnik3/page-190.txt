At the farewell, the archbishop bowed low before the Grand Prince: three barrels of white wine, two barrels of red wine, and two barrels of old wine.

On the twentieth day of that same month of January, the senior posadnik Foma and the tysyatsky Vasily Esipov bowed low before the Grand Prince on behalf of all Novgorod: those posadniks and tysyatskies who had not managed to arrange feasts in honor of the Grand Prince presented one thousand rubles as a gift.

All of them came with gifts to bow before the Grand Prince—gifts that they had intended to present to him at the feasts—as did the merchants and all the best of the prosperous townspeople. No one remained who did not come with gifts; even young people were before him with many gifts and petitions.

And the Grand Prince rewarded all the posadniks and boyars, the tysyatskies, the posadniks’ sons, the merchants, and the prosperous townspeople from his own bounty with every kind of favor: costly garments, silks, goblets and silver ladles, bundles of forty sables, and horses, to each according to his dignity.

The Grand Prince’s Departure from Novgorod

On the twenty-sixth day of January, a Tuesday, the Grand Prince departed Novgorod for Moscow early in the morning, and his first camp was at St Nicholas on the Volochek.

And the archbishop came after him to that camp to bow low before him, and with him came Prince Vasily Shuysky, and posadnik Vasily Kazimir with his brother Yakov, and Zakharia Ovin Grigoriev with his brother Kuzma, and Luka and Yakov Fedorov, and other posadniks, and many boyars, and prosperous townspeople. And the Vladyka, having bowed low before the Grand Prince, presented two barrels of wine, white and red; and Prince Vasily and the posadniks and all who came with them presented a wineskin of wine each.

And the Grand Prince invited them to eat with him. And they ate and drank at his place, and the Grand Prince gave gifts to the Vladyka and Prince Vasily.

On the eighth day of February, a Thursday, the Grand Prince entered Moscow in the morning before Mass, and he ate bread and drank at his mother’s.

On the Eclipse of the Sun, That Is, on the Darkness

In that same month, on the twenty-fifth day, on the Sunday of Cheese Week, when the first hour of the day was ending, the sky was covered with clouds, and the sun had not yet shone, and it began to grow dark, and it became as dark as on a gloomy day at the second hour of the night; but it stayed so only a little while, bright clouds began to appear from the south, and then it became light as before.

And in that same week many saw in the night a shining light and circles in the heavens.
