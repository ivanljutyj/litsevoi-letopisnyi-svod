On the Sending from the Crimea

In the year 7064 from Adam (the year 1564 from Christ), in the month of October, Tsar Devlet-Girei sent from the Crimea to the tsar and Grand Prince a courier with his letter, Suleisha's man Kuchelek, and Baibiri, a Tatar of the tsar and Grand Prince; and in the letter he wrote that he had set out to go against the Circassians, but news came to him that the tsar and Grand Prince was sending his host against the Crimea, and he had gone to meet them and fought with them for two days, and had killed and taken captive as many of them as he could; and the tsar and Grand Prince should exchange ambassadors with him; and whether he wished to do so or not, the tsar and Grand Prince should send a courier to him. And Prince Suleish wrote to the tsar and Grand Prince that the tsar and Grand Prince should desire peace with the Crimean tsar and not recall the past; and the courier Kuchelek petitioned Ivan Mikhailov, dyak of the tsar and Grand Prince, on behalf of Prince Suleish, that he would desire good between the sovereigns and petition the tsar and Grand Prince that a good work be accomplished and that blood between the sovereigns on both sides be stopped. And Princes Suleish and Obreim sent two sons of boyars with the same Tatars for ransom, Ivan Trofimov and Bogdan Shelonin; and Ivan and Bogdan and the Tatar Baibiri reported to the tsar and Grand Prince that in battle with the Crimean tsar the commanders of the tsar and Grand Prince, boyar Ivan Vasilievich Sheremetev and his companions, had beaten many of his best people—princes and murzas and close men—and had inflicted dishonor and losses on the tsar, he says, in that he took his baggage train and led those horses off to the border; and in the battle few Russian people fought with him and they beat many of his people, although the tsar routed them, and those who sat in the oak grove he could not take, and he went back in haste, fearing the coming of the tsar and Grand Prince against him.

On the Envoys of the English King Philip

In the same month there came to the tsar and Grand Prince from the son of Emperor Charles, the English King Philip, and his Queen Mary, envoys, Rytsert and Yuri, with letters; and in the letters he wrote to the tsar and Grand Prince with great loving praise, and called him a pious tsar over many realms, and with petition and request for every good. And they brought three letters: one in the Italian language, another in Polish, and the third in Greek; and in all of them the same thing was written. And the sovereign granted them great favor.

On the Release of the Crimean Courier

In the same year, in November, the tsar and Grand Prince released the Crimean courier to the Crimea, and sent his own to the tsar, the Tatar Yushko Mokshov, with a letter; and he wrote to the tsar, recalling all his wrongs, and concerning the envoys and the exchange.
