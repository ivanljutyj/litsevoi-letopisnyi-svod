RUS (1452–1467 from the Birth of Christ)
Book 14

In the year 6952 from Adam (1452 from Christ)

On the fourteenth day of the month of September, Prince Ivan Vladimirovich, grandson of Olgerd, came from Lithuania to Veliky Novgorod, to the suburbs in which Prince Semyon Olgerdovich Lugven and his son Prince Yuri, grandson of Olgerd, ruled, at the request of Novgorod.

But Prince Yuri Semyonovich, grandson of Olgerd, went to the Germans, but the Germans did not let him pass, and he departed for Moscow.

In that same autumn, at the town of Yam, the Germans burned the suburbs and seized the bank, and they sent word to Novgorod: “It is not we who are conquering you, but Prince Grigory Klevsky from Overseas is conquering you for his guide and interpreter, a man of Rugodiv”; but in saying this, the Germans lied.

In that same autumn the Tverites conquered many volosts from the patrimony of the Grand Prince of Tver and villages of the Novgorod land; they conquered, seized, and burned all of Bezhetsky Verkh and the Novotorzhskie volosts, and returned home with great wealth.

In that same autumn Casimir, Grand Prince of Lithuania, son of Jagiello, sent to Novgorod, saying: “Take my governors at Gorodishche, and I will protect you; it is because of you that I have not concluded peace with the Grand Prince of Moscow”; but the Novgorodians did not agree to this.

In that same year they killed Tsarevich Mustafa near Ryazan, on the small river Listan. And the winter then was fierce and very harsh; hay was expensive, and horses died off.

Tale of Tsarevich Mustafa

Tsarevich Mustafa came against Ryazan with a large Tatar army and conquered Ryazan volosts and villages, and did much harm to Ryazan; and he withdrew with a great number of captives, encamped in the Field, and began sending to Ryazan to sell them captives.
