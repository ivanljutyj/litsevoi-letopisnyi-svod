A multitude of people gathered who saw how, at the Great Church of Hagia Sophia, from the windows above, a huge fiery flame burst forth and encircled the whole neck of the church for a long time, and, gathering together, the flame changed and became like ineffable light, and at once rose into the sky. Some of those who saw this began to weep, crying bitterly: “Lord, have mercy!” When that light reached the heavens, the doors of heaven opened and, having received the light, closed.

In the morning they went and told the patriarch. Patriarch Anastasius gathered the boyars and all the counselors, went to the tsar, and began to persuade him to leave the city with the tsaritsa. And since the tsar did not listen to them, the patriarch said to him: “You know, O tsar, all that was said before about this city; and now again another terrible sign has occurred: the ineffable light that was seen in the Great Church of Hagia Sophia together with the lamps that were there, the ecumenical hierarchs and pious tsars, and also the angel of God whom God appointed under Tsar Justinian to preserve the holy and Great Church and this city—this night it departed to heaven; and this signifies that the mercy of God and His bounties have departed from us, and the Lord God wishes to deliver this city to our enemies for our sins.” And then he presented to him those men who had seen the miracle.

And when the tsar heard their words, he fell to the ground like a dead man and was speechless for a long time; barely did they bring him round with aromatic waters.

When he rose, the patriarch began all the more firmly to exhort the tsar to leave the city. All the boyars also said to him: “To you, lord tsar, if you leave the city with whom you wish, God will help; there will be help for you and for the city, and other cities and all the land have this hope, that they will not soon be handed over to the infidels.” He did not incline to this, but answered them: “If our Lord God has so willed, where shall we flee from His wrath? And how many tsars before me, great and glorious, who suffered for their fatherland and died—shall I, the last, not do likewise? But I will die here with you.” And he did not listen to them.

On the second day after the people heard of the departure of the Holy Spirit, they all at once began to take leave of one another, and fear and trembling fell upon them. The patriarch strengthened them and taught them not to lose hope: “Take courage, children, take courage,” he said, “and let us place our salvation in the Lord God, and to Him let us raise our hands and eyes from all our soul, and He will deliver us from our enemies and destroy all the enemies’ designs against us.” With such words and many others he strengthened the people.
