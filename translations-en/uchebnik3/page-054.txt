In the year 6950 from Adam (the year 1450 from Christ)

Tatars of the Great Horde came to the Ryazan borderlands, caused much harm, and left with captives.

In the same autumn Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich grew angry with Prince Dmitry Yuryevich Shemyaka and marched against him to Uglich, but he fled to the Novgorod Bezhetsky Verkh, and the Grand Prince turned back to Kiasovo and came to Moscow.

And then Koludar Irezhsky was beaten with knouts. And then Prince Dmitry Yuryevich Shemyaka, together with Prince Alexander Chertorizhsky, approached Moscow almost at that same hour; and Zinovy, abbot of the Trinity, reconciled them and concluded a peace treaty between them.

In the same autumn there was a fire in Moscow.

In the same winter Prince Dmitry Yuryevich Shemyaka, fearing Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, sent to Veliky Novgorod asking that he be accepted on their terms; and the Novgorodians answered him: “If you wish, prince, to come to us, come, and we will be glad to see you.”

That winter was fierce with unbearable frosts and caused much harm to animals and people.

In the same spring there were violent thunders and terrible lightning, strong winds and whirlwinds, and all people were terrified.

In the same spring a prolonged frost with snow set in after a thaw; much snow fell, and scarcely had it melted when winds rose and severe frosts and winds began, and sorrow seized the people. In that year grain was dear.

In the same year in Novgorod Vladyka Evfimy built a stone church of the Holy Saviour of the Transfiguration in Rusa on the old foundation and consecrated it himself, and established a monastery, and endowed it abundantly with icons, books, church vessels, and all necessities.

In the same year in Novgorod Vladyka Evfimy erected a stone church of St Nicholas in his own courtyard.

In the same year in Novgorod there was a great fire in Podolye; 12 stone churches burned, and the whole End burned as far as St George’s, and a multitude of Christians burned, and it stopped at Lubyanitsa.

And then in the same month there was a fire in Novgorod; the Zapolye of Mikitina Street burned, and misfortune befell many people: those who had escaped the first fire with their property now all burned with their property; and great sorrow seized Christendom.
