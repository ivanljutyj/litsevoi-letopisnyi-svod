After nine days the foul Mehmed ordered the entire army to assault the city and fight every day; and he ordered the great cannon to be recast and made still more powerful.

Learning of this, the nobles and Giustiniani gathered with the patriarch and began to urge the emperor, saying: “We see, emperor, that this infidel does not slacken the siege but prepares for an even greater undertaking; and what can we do, expecting help from nowhere? But it is fitting for you, emperor, to leave the city for a suitable place; and, learning of this, your people and your brothers will come to your aid, and the Albanians too, taking courage, will come; besides, then the godless Mehmed, taking fright, will withdraw from the city.” Having said this and much else to the emperor, they gave him the ships and galleys of Giustiniani. The emperor was silent for a long time, shedding tears, and then said to them: “I praise and thank you for your counsel, and I know that all this is for my benefit, for it may indeed be so; but how can I do this and abandon the priesthood and the churches of God, and the kingdom, and all the people in such distress? And what will the whole world say to me? I beg you, tell me. No, my lord, no, let me die here with you!” And falling down, he bowed to them, weeping bitterly. The patriarch and all the people there wept aloud and ceased their persuasion, so that there should be no talk among the people.

Again they sent to the Morea, and to all the islands, and to the Franks for help.

The townspeople fought the Turks by day, and by night they climbed into the ditches and breached the walls of the ditches on the field side, and penetrated the earth along the wall, and in many places set many vessels of gunpowder.

Also on the walls they prepared many vessels filled with pitch and burning sulfur, and cloth with pitch and gunpowder. The twenty-fifth day had already passed since they had fought every day.

The godless one ordered the great cannon to be rolled up, which was bound with iron hoops, hoping to strengthen it.

And when they fired it for the first time, it fell apart into many pieces. The infidel considered himself disgraced by this and soon ordered the siege towers to be rolled up to the city with all their strength, very secretly.

And when they had set the towers along the whole edge of the ditch, they wanted, after filling the ditches with trees, brushwood, and earth, to move and roll the towers up to the city, and also, having undermined the wall in many places, to bring it down to the ground.

And as soon as a multitude of people set about filling the ditches, the townspeople immediately set fire to the powder vessels that had been planted beneath the ditch, and suddenly the earth thundered like great thunder, and rose with the towers and the people like a mighty storm up to the clouds.
