And he called him his son. But the tsar wishes to be in the will of the sovereign, and the princes and all the people of the Kazan land have hope in you, the Great Sovereign, that you, sovereign, will show them favor, and they wish to serve your sovereign will and to be steadfast and not depart from you with all the Kazan land until their death and their children’s. And the Grand Prince, protecting the Kazan lands, should have pity on the Kazan people, so that there be no bloodshed, and for this the tsar should set aside his anger, and should lift his disfavor from the Kazan princes and from all the people of the Kazan land, show them favor, and conclude an agreement between the tsar and himself concerning brotherhood and friendship. And the Grand Prince ordered sworn charters to be written setting out the terms on which the tsar, the princes, and all the people of the Kazan land were to sign the agreement.

And the Kazan envoys, Prince Tabai and his companions, signed the agreement on the sworn charter in Moscow. And on that charter, according to which the tsar and all the people of the land were to take the oath, Prince Tabai and Prince Tivikel affixed their seals, and Obreim-bakshi set his hand to it, stating that on that sworn charter the tsar in Kazan would give his word before the Grand Prince’s boyar sons and set his seal to the charter; and the princes likewise would set their hands to it, those who knew letters, while those who did not know letters would affix their seals; and throughout all the land the Kazan people would take the oath before that same boyar’s son. And the Grand Prince’s people who had fallen into the hands of the Kazan people, and the guns, were to be returned to the tsar through that same boyar’s son, and sent with him to the Grand Prince in Moscow.

And the Grand Prince sent with those charters to Kazan to the tsar his boyar’s son Ivan Vasilyev, son of Polev, in January, and with him he released the tsar’s man Baush.

And that same winter, on March 26, the Grand Prince’s boyar’s son Sura Nekhaev came to the Grand Prince from Kazan from Ivan Polev with a letter.

And Ivan wrote in the letter: the tsar had not kept the words of the sworn charter and had not given him the guns. And together with him the tsar sent to the Grand Prince his man Merden with a letter. And the tsar wrote to the Grand Prince that the Grand Prince should release his envoys and send his own great envoy to him together with his envoys. And the cannons and guns which the Grand Prince’s commanders had taken from them near Kazan, and the Kazan people whom they had captured, and the messengers whom they had detained, the Grand Prince should show favor and release with Tabai to him. And when Prince Tabai and his companions arrive with the Grand Prince’s great envoy, and bring the cannons and guns and all the Kazan people, only then did he wish to take the oath on the charter and release Ivan Polev to the Grand Prince.

And the Grand Prince ordered Fedor Ivanov, son of Karpov, and his clerk Menshoi Putyatin to reply to the Kazan envoys Tabai and his companions.
