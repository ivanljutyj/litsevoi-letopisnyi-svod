The Ryapolovsky princes—Prince Ivan, his brother Prince Semyon, and Prince Dmitry—seeing that Prince Dmitry Shemyaka had broken his word in everything and had lied to the Vladyka, began to consider how to rescue the Grand Prince. At that time those of like mind with them were Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Striga, Ivan Oshchera with his brother Bobr, Yushko Dranitsa, and many other boyar sons, and the court of the Grand Prince. At one with them also was Semyon Filimonov with all his sons; and Rusalka, and Runo, and many other boyar sons. And they agreed among themselves on a time when all should be at Uglich—on Peter’s Day after noon.

And Semyon Filimonov with all his men came at that time, but Prince Dmitry learned about the Ryapolovskys, and he did not dare go to Uglich at that time, but went beyond the Volga to White Lake. And Prince Dmitry sent from Uglich an army after them with Vasily Veprev, and he sent Fyodor Mikhailovich after them with many regiments; their time to join together was at the mouth of the Sheksna at the Church of All Saints, but Fyodor did not reach Vasily in time.

And the Ryapolovskys, having turned against Vasily, defeated him at the mouth of the Mologa.

And Fyodor at that time crossed the Volga at the mouth of the Sheksna with all his regiments.

And the Ryapolovskys had news of him, and they turned against him too. Fyodor, seeing them, fled again beyond the Volga.

And the Ryapolovskys went through the Novgorod land toward Lithuania and came to Prince Vasily Yaroslavich in Mstislavl.

And Semyon Filimonov with all his men went from Uglich to Moscow, since he knew nothing of this; Runo alone left him to follow the Ryapolovskys.

And when the Ryapolovsky princes came, and Prince Ivan Striga and many other boyar sons, named above and unnamed, they began to advise Prince Vasily Yaroslavich how to free the Grand Prince.

And Prince Dmitry Shemyaka, seeing that many people were leaving him for the Grand Prince, sent for the Vladykas and began to consider with Prince Ivan, the Vladykas, and the boyars whether to release him or not. And Vladyka Iona every day, without ceasing, said: “You have done wrong and brought me into sin and shame: you ought to have released the Grand Prince, but you even imprisoned his children with him. You gave me your solemn word, and they listened to me, and now I am in utter falsehood. Release him, take the sin from my soul and from your own. And what can you accomplish without time? His children are small. And further, strengthen him with the precious cross and the word of our brethren, the Vladykas.” And he said much else. Prince Dmitry, pondering this for a long time, decided to release the Grand Prince and give him his patrimony, so that he might live. In the same year a son, Andrei, was born to the Grand Prince in Uglich on August 13.
