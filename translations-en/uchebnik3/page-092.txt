Having learned of this, Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich sent his host against them.

That same winter the Grand Prince went against Prince Dmitry Shemyaka to Galich, and, coming with a great force, took position in Kostroma.

And from there they began to send envoys. Prince Dmitry, becoming afraid, began to ask for peace, kissed the cross, and gave sworn charters upon himself that from that time he wished no harm to the Grand Prince and his children, and to all his grand principality and his patrimony: “And if I violate this charter of mine, what is written in it, then may there be no mercy upon me from God and His most pure Mother, and from the power of the honorable and life-giving Cross, and from the prayers of all the saints and great wonderworkers of our land, the most reverend metropolitans Peter and Alexei, and Leonty, Bishop of Rostov, wonderworker, and Sergius, abbot and wonderworker, and others. Likewise may there be no blessing upon me from all the bishops of the Russian land who now sit in their bishoprics, and from all the priestly ranks under them.”

The Grand Prince, having heard his plea, made peace with him and on Maundy Thursday returned from Kostroma, and at Easter he was in Rostov. On the next day he celebrated the Annunciation in Rostov and feasted at Bishop Ephrem’s. On that same day he set out for Moscow.

And he came to Moscow in Thomas Week; and his son Prince Ivan was in Vladimir.

That same spring after Easter the Lithuanian envoy Pan Semyon Edigoldov was with the Grand Prince.

And in summer there was pestilence among horses and all livestock, and among people too there was pestilence, but not much. In that same year Prince Vasily Kosoy died.

On the Installation of Jonah, Bishop of Ryazan, as Metropolitan of Rus. In the year 6957 from Adam (1457 from Christ)

On the 15th day of December a metropolitan was installed to the metropolitanate of all Rus by the Russian bishops: Ephrem of Rostov, Abraham of Suzdal, Varlaam of Kolomna, Pitirim of Perm. And the archbishop of Novgorod, Euthymius, and the bishop of Tver sent their letters that they were of one mind with them in the installation of Jonah, Bishop of Ryazan, as metropolitan. And before this, when he was in Constantinople on the affairs of the metropolitanate, he had been blessed by the most holy patriarch and the entire sacred council to the metropolitanate after Isidore. And then, at his installation, he transferred the archbishopric to Ephrem of Rostov, since formerly there had also been Archbishop Theodore there, and he had received it in Constantinople.
