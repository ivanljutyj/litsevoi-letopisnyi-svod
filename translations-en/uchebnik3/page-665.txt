On the Envoys to the Sovereign from Shevkal

In the same month envoys came from Shevkal with gifts and a petition that the tsar and grand prince defend them from his slaves, the Circassian princes.

On the Arrival of a Tatar from Siberia to the Sovereign

In the month of November there arrived from Siberia a serving Tatar, Sobana Rizanov, and an envoy from the Siberian prince Ediger with tribute, with sables and squirrels, and he said that they would come in winter with the longstanding tribute.

On the Report from the Commanders in the German Land to the Sovereign

In the same month the commanders, Prince Mikhail and his companions, wrote that the master had sent his brother Johann Ketler for forage and had sent many German people with them as a guard.

And the commanders came to the outpost and utterly routed the German outpost, and took the master’s brother Johann Ketler, and the master’s armorer and equerry, and captured many petty princes and manor holders; and in that battle they took one hundred and six alive, and in many campaigns they beat the master’s men and took two hundred and sixty men in all the campaigns, and killed about three thousand, and all the captives said that the master was breaking down the city wall, and that no gunpowder for cannon remained in the city.

On the Report to the Sovereign from Prince Dmitry Kurlyatev from Yuryev

In the same month Prince Dmitry Kurlyatev wrote from Yuryev that the master had taken the city of Ryngol and had stood before it for six weeks. And Prince Mikhail Repnin also wrote that the master had taken Ryngol and had come against the commanders, and had trampled down the commanders, Prince Mikhail and his companions, killed boyar sons and two boyar servants, and captured Ivan Likov and Fyodor Pisessky alive; and the master went to Kes, leaving Ryngol; and the common people again joined the tsar and grand prince.
