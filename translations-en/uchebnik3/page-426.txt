Having united in love, all unanimously decided to suffer for the sovereign and for Christianity against the Tatars.

And Prince Dmitry and the other commanders began to speak the order to the princes and boyar sons, to the court of the Grand Prince, and to all the Grand Prince’s army, that they should serve the Grand Prince and firmly suffer for Christianity: “The sovereign wishes to reward you and your children with great reward.” The princes and boyar sons, the court of the Grand Prince, and all the army said as if with one voice: “We have heard, lords, your good counsel, that you deliberated to serve the sovereign together and to lay down your heads for Christianity; and you have instilled in us, lords, a great desire by your union: we are glad to serve the sovereign and to lay down our heads for Christianity; and we are ready, armed, and wish to drink the mortal cup with the Tatars.” The commanders, hearing from the Grand Prince’s army that they wished to serve the sovereign firmly and to lay down their heads for the Orthodox faith, rejoiced with great joy and received the words from their mouths as a treasure.

And the commanders set out, each with his regiment: in the main regiment were the boyars and commanders Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky, Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Shuisky, and Prince Mikhailo Ivanovich Kubensky; and from the Pakhra came Tsarevich Shigalei and boyar and commander Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov; and the Grand Prince ordered Tsarevich Shigalei and Prince Yuri Mikhailovich to be in the main regiment as well; in the forward regiment were Prince Ivan Ivanovich Turuntai Pronsky and Prince Vasily Fyodorovich Okhlyabin; in the right-hand regiment were Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shemyaka Pronsky and Semyon Semyonovich Bezzubtsev; in the left-hand regiment were Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Troekurov and Prince Vasily Semyonovich Mezetsky; in the guard regiment were Prince Yuri Ivanovich Temkin Rostovsky and Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Ushaty.

Tsar Saip-Girey came to the Oka River bank on July 30, Saturday, at the third hour of the day, and stood on a hill in a high place; and many Tatars came to the bank with rafts and wanted to cross the river. The Grand Prince’s commanders hurried to meet the Tsar.

And first to come to the bank with the forward regiment were Prince Ivan Ivanovich Turuntai Pronsky and Prince Vasily Okhlyabin; and they began to exchange fire with the Tatars.

The Tatars, seeing the forward regiment, thought that these were all the men, and came recklessly to the bank with many people, and waded into the river, and began to board the rafts; and they began to shoot at the forward regiment with many arrows, and the arrows flew like rain.
