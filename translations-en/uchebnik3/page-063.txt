Rule 5 of the Holy Apostles

A bishop or presbyter or deacon shall not expel his wife under the pretext of piety.

If he does expel her, he shall be excommunicated; and if he remains inflexible, he shall be deposed. Interpretation. If any bishop, priest, or deacon drives away his wife under the pretext of piety alone, apart from any other fault of hers, he shall be excommunicated; but if even after excommunication he does not obey and remains in the same state, not taking back his wife, he shall be deprived of his rank. Concerning this see the 13th rule of the sixth council, which was held at Trullo in the royal palace, concerning presbyters, deacons, and subdeacons, which decreed thus: bishops abstain from their wives after ordination to the episcopate. The same holy sixth council at Trullo in its 12th rule established that bishops must be unmarried, but this was decided not for the rejection or perversion of the apostolic rules, but for salvation and better care for people.

Rule 12 of the Holy Sixth Council of Trullo in the Palace

It has come to our knowledge that in Africa, Libya, and other countries the bishops there, after their ordination as bishops, do not cease living with their wives, seeing in this a hindrance and scandal to people. We have labored much to arrange all things for the benefit of the flock entrusted to us, so that such a thing may no longer occur. We say this not for the rejection or violation of the apostolic rules, but for salvation and for aiding people, so as not to allow reproach upon the priestly rank. The divine apostle says: “Do all to the glory of God; be without offense to Jews and Gentiles and to the Church of God, even as I also please all in all things, not seeking my own profit, but that of many, that they may be saved; be imitators of me, as I also am of Christ.” If anyone is found doing such a thing, he shall be deposed. Interpretation. The 5th apostolic rule does not command a bishop, priest, or deacon to drive away his wife under the pretext of piety, but on the contrary forbids the one who does this, that is, who sends away his wife; and if even after the prohibition he does not correct himself and does not wish to take back his wife, then they shall be deprived of rank.
