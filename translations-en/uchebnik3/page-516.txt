The pious sovereign also went to Kolomna, and Vladyka Feodosius met him with the consecrated council, with crosses and all the people, and gave thanks to God for our deliverance from the impious.

And to Moscow the sovereign sent Ivan Petrov, son of Yakovlev, to his tsaritsa and the metropolitan and to Prince Yuri, his brother, to proclaim God’s greatness; and the captives with the camels and the artillery taken from the Crimean Khan the sovereign sent to Moscow with Semyon Vasilyev Yakovlev.

And the sovereign sends a message to Metropolitan Macarius, and calls him to prayer, and asks his blessing to go to Kazan.

And to Sviyaga the sovereign sent Fedka Cheremisinov to proclaim to his voivodes and all the people God’s mercy, the arrival of the Crimean Tsar, and his own route to Kazan.

On the first day of July all the voivodes came to the sovereign at Kolomna from the Tula campaign and told the sovereign that the Tsar had gone by the way of no return, and many scouts had ridden after him, and those scouts who were returning said that the Tsar was going hastily, making sixty or seventy versts a day, and abandoning many horses. And for their service the sovereign rewarded the voivodes.

On the Tsar’s Council and the Campaign against Kazan

The sovereign began to deliberate with Prince Vladimir Andreevich and the boyars and all the voivodes about how and by what routes to go to Kazan; and the sovereign decided to go in two parties, for the deployment of the troops, while the sovereign himself should go to Vladimir and Murom, and send the voivodes to Ryazan and Meshchera, and assemble in the Field beyond Alatyr.

There was much discord among the people: the boyar sons, especially those from Novgorod, were displeased with the sovereign and petitioned that it was impossible for them to remain so long at Kolomna in service since spring, while others had gone after the Tsar and been in battles, and now they must go on such a long journey and stand there a long time.
