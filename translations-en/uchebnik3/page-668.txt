In the same month Daniil Chulkov sent four Crimean captives from the Field, having defeated them in the Field; they were those left behind by the tsarevich; and those captives said the same as the Asans who had come from the tsarevich.

In the same month they brought Crimean captives from the Field from Kapusta Yakovlev, the Putivl ataman; he defeated the tsarevich's stragglers beyond the Donets over two days and drove off many horses. And those captives said that the tsarevich had gone to the Crimea, greatly exhausted, and his men were dying of hardship, and many horses had died throughout the whole host.

In the same month the Cherkassian atamans Vaska Rozhen and Rykhlyk brought captives from near the Crimea, having taken them on the Irchik two days' journey before Perekop; and Vaska and Rykhlyk had raided the Nogai and Crimean uluses at the time when the tsarevich was marching against Rus, and took about 15,000 horses, and together with other atamans killed many ulus people.

On the 27th day of January, the Danish king Christian sent the courier Vlas to the tsar and grand prince; he petitions for a safe-conduct for ambassadors, so that their sovereign would graciously permit the ambassadors to come to him and submit a petition to the great lords concerning the Livonian land. And the tsar and grand prince sent that courier away, allowed the ambassadors to come to him, and gave a safe-conduct for them.

On the Dispatch of Vishnevetsky to the Donets

In the month of February the tsar and grand prince sent forth his voivode Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky to the Donets, and ordered him to attack the Crimean uluses, having fitted out boats, from Azov against Kerch and against other uluses.

On the Dispatch of Daniil Adashev to the Dnieper

In the same month the tsar and grand prince sent forth his voivodes to the Dnieper: his okolnichy and voivode Daniil Fedorovich Adashev, and voivode Ignaty Grigorievich Zabolotsky, and Shiryay Vasilievich Kobyakov, and streltsy heads with streltsy, and heads with boyar sons, and atamans with Cossacks; and the sovereign ordered them to guard his cause on the Dnieper and to wage war against the Crimean uluses. They fought divided into three regiments: in the large regiment were voivodes Daniil and Shiryay Kobyakov, and head Yakov Bundov; in the forward regiment were voivode Ignaty Zabolotsky and Dyak Rzhevsky; in the guard regiment were head Timka Ignatiev and Vasily Pivov.
