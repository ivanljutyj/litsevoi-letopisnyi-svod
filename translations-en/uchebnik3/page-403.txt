On the Campaign of the Grand Prince’s Commanders into the Lithuanian Land

Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich of all Rus and his mother, Grand Princess Elena, consulted with the boyars about sending men to wage war on the Lithuanian land because of the king’s injustice.

And the Grand Prince ordered his father Daniil, Metropolitan of all Rus, to come to him, and he told his father Metropolitan Daniil about the king’s many injustices—that the king himself sends his commanders against Christians and brings in the Tatars, and much Christian blood is shed because of him; and the Grand Prince also told the Metropolitan that he wished to send his commanders with men to wage war on the king’s land for his injustices. The Metropolitan said to the Grand Prince: “You, Orthodox sovereigns, are pastors of Christendom: it is fitting for you, sovereign, to defend Christendom from violence, and for us and the whole consecrated council to pray to God for you, sovereign, and for your army; for he who begins a war perishes, but in righteousness God is the helper.”

And Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich of all Rus and his mother, Grand Princess Elena, sent forth their commanders into the Lithuanian land and ordered them to set out from Moscow on October 28: in the Great Regiment, his boyar and commander Prince Mikhail Vasilievich Gorbaty and Prince Nikita Vasilievich Obolensky; in the Advanced Regiment, his boyar and equerry Prince Ivan Fedorovich Telepnev and Prince Nikita Borisovich Turenin; in the Regiment of the Right Hand, Prince Pyotr Ivanovich Repnin and Prince Pyotr Fedorovich Okhlyabin; in the Regiment of the Left Hand, Prince Vasily Ivanovich Repnin and Prince Ivan Semyonovich Mezetsky; in the Guard Regiment, Prince Ivan Ivanovich Belevsky and Vasily Petrovich Borisov.

And from Novgorod the Great and from Pskov, Grand Prince Ivan and his mother, Grand Princess Elena, also sent men into the Lithuanian land and ordered them to set out from Opochka: in the Great Regiment, boyar and viceroy of Novgorod Prince Boris Ivanovich Gorbaty and Vasily Andreevich Sheremetev; in the Advanced Regiment, Prince Mikhail Mikhailovich Kurbsky and Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Paletsky; in the Regiment of the Right Hand, Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Kubensky and Ivan Semyonovich Vorontsov; in the Regiment of the Left Hand, the viceroy of Pskov Dmitry Semyonovich Vorontsov and Fyodor Semyonovich Kolychev; in the Guard Regiment, Prince Fyodor Mikhailovich Kurbsky and Prince Ivan, son of Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Zasekin.

And the commanders of the great sovereign, Prince Mikhail Vasilievich Gorbaty and his companions, entered the Lithuanian land, while King Sigismund was then in Vilna and achieved nothing.
