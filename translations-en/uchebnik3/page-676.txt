On the Sending of Voivodes to Mozhaisk

In the month of November the voivode Zakhar Pleshcheyev and his comrades sent Mikhail Burtsov to the tsar and sovereign in Mozhaisk with news that they had marched against the Germans, had beaten the Germans in more than one place, and had taken captives three times; and the captives said that the Tregorod princelet Moshkalko had come, and with him German and overseas troops, to await the Master and the Archbishop in those manors. And the Master, having gathered the remaining men, would be with them with much equipment, so that they might go to Yuryev, stand before Yuryev all winter, and not withdraw from Yuryev without taking it.

But the Master had broken faith, and having received news from the men of Yuryev, he began to make war; and from Yuryev they sent several captives who had been taken from the Germans, and those captives said the same.

The Grand Prince Ordered the Voivodes to March to Yuryev

And the tsar and grand prince ordered the boyars Prince Yuri and Alexei to march to Yuryev, and ordered Zakhar and his comrades to join them as well, and sent many streltsy to Yuryev.

In the same month Zakhar and his comrades wrote that the Germans had come against them, Moshkalko with many men, but he had routed them; they killed the voivode Alexei Ivanovich Skryabin, and killed twenty boyar sons and thirty boyar servants.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered the boyars and voivodes Prince Ivan Fedorovich Mstislavsky and Prince Pyotr Ivanovich Shuisky, and many other boyars and voivodes, and many boyar sons of the Moscow and Novgorod lands, to go hastily from Moscow to Pskov. And because of our sins the thaw came greater than usual, and great difficulties arose for the army, so that it was impossible to march quickly.

In the same month two Crimean Tatars came to the sovereign in Mozhaisk and said that Divey-Murza the Nogai had come from Crimea, and with him the Shirin murzas, and with them three thousand men, and they had come to make war on the borderlands.

In the same month the voivode Prince Fyodor Ivanovich Tatev wrote that Divey-Murza and his comrades had come against the Rostov volost, and had come secretly; and Prince Fyodor marched against them and took captives from them, but did not pursue them, because his men had not managed to gather quickly.
