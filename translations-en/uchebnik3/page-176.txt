The Grand Prince showed him favor and gave him Novy Gorodok on the Oka with many volosts.

That same winter an envoy came to the Grand Prince from the Crimean Tsar Menli-Girey, son of Achi-Girey, named Azibaba, sent to the Grand Prince with love and brotherhood; and the Grand Prince, having honored that envoy, sent him back to his land with love to his sovereign. And together with him the Grand Prince sent his own envoy to the Crimea to Tsar Menli-Girey, Nikita Beklemishev, likewise with love and brotherhood, on March 31.

That same spring, on April 18, at the seventh hour of the night, a daughter, Elena, was born to the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich by Tsarevna Sophia Fominichna, daughter of the Despot of Morea.

In the same month of April, on the 25th day, Anton Fryazin came from Venice to the Grand Prince from Doge Nicolò Tron to petition the Grand Prince on behalf of that doge, that the Grand Prince would show favor, release his envoy Ivan Trevisan from confinement, and grant their first petition, help him in all things, and send him to Tsar Akhmat of the Great Horde.

And from that day the Grand Prince showed favor to Ivan Trevisan: he ordered him released from iron shackles and ordered him to come before his eyes.

In the same year, on May 20, at the first hour of the night, the Church of the Most Holy Mother of God collapsed, which Philip, Metropolitan of All Rus, had begun to build; it was very marvelous in workmanship, very tall, and had already been built up to the vaults, which were being turned so that a great top might be placed upon them. The north wall had been built over the tombs of the most reverend metropolitans Jonah and Philip, from within, stone by stone, and in the middle a stairway was constructed; and they raised it along that wall to a great height, and raised it also on the front wall, and because of that great height and the heavy vaults that pressed upon the thin wall, that wall could not hold and broke.

And great destruction befell that church, for half of the front wall fell, and the entire north wall up to the altar, and the chambers, and the pillars, and all the vaults. The tomb of Wonderworker Peter was covered with debris, but he was not harmed in any way; the tomb of Metropolitan Jonah was split but not broken; and the tombstone of Metropolitan Philip was slightly pierced. And in the wooden church that stood within the stone one, the top was broken, but the icons and sacred vessels, books, candles, chandeliers, and everything else that was in it were not moved.

What remained intact of that church was half of the front wall and the front doors; the entire south wall was intact, with its pillars and vaults, and the whole altar; but from that great fall all the pillars and walls shifted, and some were broken, but did not fall.
