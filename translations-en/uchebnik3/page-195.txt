He pitied them greatly and shed tears. And he comes to his father, Metropolitan Gerontius, informing him of the Novgorod transgression of the kiss of the cross: “And that for which they themselves sent—which I did not even want, namely the sovereignty over them—they have renounced, and they have accused us of falsehood.”

He announced the same to his mother, to his brothers, to his boyars, and to his commanders; and from that time he was wrathful against Novgorod.

By the blessing of the most reverend Metropolitan of all Rus, Gerontius, and of the consecrated council assembled concerning this, of archbishops and bishops and of all the priestly order, and also by counsel with his mother and by her prayer, and after deliberation with his brothers, his boyars, his princes, and commanders, he arms himself against his patrimony, against the apostates who had violated the kiss of the cross, against the Novgorodians.

He placed all his hope in the Lord God and in His most pure Mother, and in the power of the precious and life-giving Cross, which they had kissed and then betrayed.

And he had a prayer service sung in all the churches of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ, of His most pure Mother, of the Resurrection, of the apostles and martyrs, and at the tombs of the wonderworkers, the most reverend metropolitans of Russia, Peter, Alexius, and Jonah, and other hierarchs, and at the surrounding monasteries of the city of Moscow, and at the Life-giving Trinity, at the tomb of Sergius the Wonderworker; and throughout all his patrimony, the grand principality, he sent alms to cathedral churches and monasteries and to all churches.

He also sends to Tver, to Grand Prince Michael, asking help against Novgorod; and he, in no way disobeying, sends Prince Michael Feodorovich Mikulinsky with many of his soldiers.

After that he also sent to his brothers, who were then already in their patrimonies, ordering each to march from his own place to Novgorod.

The Novgorodians heard of this and sent to the Grand Prince to petition for a safe-conduct for the journey of Fedor Kalitin, elder of Danslavskaya Street, that the Grand Prince would show favor and permit his pilgrim, the archbishop, and the Novgorod envoys to come to him and petition.

And the Grand Prince ordered his lieutenant Vasily Ivanovich Kitai to detain in Torzhok that messenger sent for the safe-conduct until his own arrival.

In the year 6986 from Adam (the year 1486 from Christ)

On the 30th day of the month of September, the Grand Prince sends to Novgorod a declaration of war with the undersecretary Rodion Bogomolov.
