In the same month the boyars and commanders Prince Yuri and Alexei wrote that they had come to Vyboresk in Yuryev district, but Zakhar and his companions had not managed to join them; and the Master and the Archbishop of Riga with many German troops and with mercenaries attacked Zakhar and his companions secretly. And they killed many people: they killed seventy boyar sons and about a thousand boyar retainers, and wounded many; and the Germans took their entire baggage train. But the commanders stood carelessly; they had no mounted scouts or sentries; and the Germans went around them at all their camps, and so because of their sins they were beaten.

And the tsar and sovereign wished to set out soon for Moscow, but it was impossible either on horseback or in a sledge; the thaw had made the roads worse than usual. And through our sins the tsaritsa was unwell.

In the same month the boyar Prince Andrei Ivanovich and his companions wrote from Yuryev that the Master and the Archbishop with all the German troops and with many cannons had come to Yuryev.

On the Grand Prince's Journey from Mozhaisk

In the same month the tsar and grand prince set out from Mozhaisk for Moscow and arrived in Moscow with the tsaritsa and his children, the tsareviches, in December on the ___ day. In the month of December they wrote from Yuryev that the Master had gone away from Yuryev; and he had stood by Yuryev for ten days, firing at the town from a distance with the ordnance, because the regiments stood about a verst from Yuryev and did not approach close to the town, and from the cannons in the town they killed men in the Master's regiment.

And when the Master with his regiments approached the town, mounted boyar sons and streltsy came out against him, and the streltsy and boyar sons killed about a hundred of the Master's men with arquebuses; and they killed about a hundred and fifty of the sovereign's streltsy, while the other boyar sons and boyar retainers and streltsy, thank God, were all unharmed. And the Germans went away without having managed to do any harm to the town or to the sovereign's people.
