The king's host, with a great multitude of men, surrounded the city of Opochka and began to fire from great cannons and handguns and to launch assaults from all sides.

And the commander and governor of Opochka, Vasily Mikhailovich Saltykov, with all the city's people, with God's help fought firmly against the king's army, and during the assault from the city he killed a great multitude of the king's men with cannons and handguns, with the help of large rollers and screens, so that on all sides of the city the Velikaya River was dammed with the corpses of men. And the river was filled with human blood in swift streams, and they killed Sokol, commander of the great Polish host, and took his banner.

And the forward commanders of the Grand Prince Sovereign Vasily, Prince Fyodor Vasilyevich Obolensky, and Ivan Vasilyevich Lyatsky, and other commanders, coming to the Lithuanian army, struck them from three sides, and killed many men of the Lithuanian army, and took many alive and sent them to the chief commanders.

At the same time word came to Ivan Vasilyevich Lyatsky that many Poles were coming to the aid of the king's army.

And Ivan Vasilyevich with his companions went against them, fought with them, and with God's help they killed the Polish commanders and four thousand troops. And they took other commanders of theirs captive: Cherkas Khreptov and his brother Misyur, and Ivan Zelepugin, and took many men alive, and took their cannons and handguns, and sent them to the chief commanders.

But the enemy of God and traitor to the sovereign, the king's grand hetman Konstantin Ostrozhsky, with all the king's hetmans and commanders, seeing the defeat of his army and the victory over it, and learning of the arrival of the chief commanders of the Grand Prince Vasily, abandoned all his host, which had been sent to ravage the city of Opochka, and retreated with great shame.

And the forward detachment of the commanders of the great sovereign Vasily pursued them and killed many men of the Lithuanian host, and captured all who were there.

King Sigismund devised this unrighteous deed through his malice, having sent his envoys to the Great Sovereign Vasily Ivanovich, and after them sent an army to wage war on the borderlands of the great sovereign, of which they had spoken before, relying on the multitude of his host; and his deceit did not help him. Merciful God, by His mercy and righteous judgment, did as He willed.

And the Scripture was fulfilled in them: He dug a pit and hollowed it out, and fell into the pit that he made.
