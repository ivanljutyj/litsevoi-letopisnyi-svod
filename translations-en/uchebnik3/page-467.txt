On the Great Fire

In the same month, on the 21st, on Tuesday, at the tenth hour of the day, in the third week of Peter’s Fast, the Church of the Exaltation of the Honorable Cross beyond the Neglinnaya River on Arbat Street on the Island caught fire. And there was a strong storm, and the fire spread like lightning, and a mighty conflagration swept in a single hour from Zaneglimenye to Vspolie by the Neglinnaya, and Chertorye burned as far as Semchinskoye village, near the Moscow River, and as far as the Church of St Theodore on Arbat Street.

And a strong storm arose in the city, and the top of the cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God in the city caught fire, and the roof on the palaces of the tsar and grand prince, and the wooden huts and palaces adorned with gold, and the Treasury Court with the tsar’s treasury; and on the tsar’s court, by the tsar’s treasury, the gold-topped Church of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Mother of God, where was the Deesis painted by Andrei Rublev and covered with gold, and many precious icons adorned with gold and pearls, of Greek painting, gathered by his forefathers over many years; and the treasury of the great tsar burned, and the whole Armory Chamber burned with its military weapons, and the whole Bedchamber Office with its treasury burned out; and in the cellars on the tsar’s court beneath the chambers everything wooden burned, and the tsar’s stable. And in many stone churches icons and church vessels burned, and much people’s property, and the metropolitan’s court.

By God’s intercession and the prayers of the holy wonderworkers Peter and Jonah, in the cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God of Her Honorable and Glorious Dormition, the Deesis and all the church vessels were preserved.

And Metropolitan Macarius was barely rescued from the church, almost suffocated by smoke; and the metropolitan went out of the church, carrying on himself the icon of the Most Pure Mother of God painted by the wonderworker Peter, while the protopope Gury carried the Rules behind him; and with him went Keksa Tatishchev, the stablemaster of Prince Vladimir, and the cathedral priest of the Most Pure, Ivan Zhizhelev, and those two burned on the square, while the metropolitan went to the city wall into the secret passage toward the Moscow River, and the protopope with him. And there the smoky air was heavy for him and the heat strong, and he grew faint from the heat and the smoky air; they began to lower him from the secret passage, tying him with a rope, onto the timbered wall by the Moscow River; and the rope broke, and the metropolitan fell and was badly hurt, and barely recovered his breath, while the protopope Gury remained there in the secret passage.
