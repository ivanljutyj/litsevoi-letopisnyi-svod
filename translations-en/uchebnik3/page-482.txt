In the month of April the tsar and grand sovereign sent by boats, in the grand regiment, Tsar Shigalei, who had formerly been tsar of Kazan; and with the tsar the sovereign sent commanders: in the grand regiment, commander and boyar Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov and boyar and palace steward Daniil Romanovich Yuryev; in the forward regiment, Prince Peter Andreevich Bulgakov and okolnichy Ivan Fedorovich Karpov; in the right hand, commanders boyar and master of the horse Ivan Petrovich Fedorov and Prince Davyd Fedorovich Paletsky; in the left hand, commanders boyar Grigory Vasilievich Morozov and Prince Andrei Vasilievich Nagaev; and in the guard regiment, commanders boyar Ivan Ivanovich Khabarov and okolnichy Dalmat Fedorovich Karpov; and with them many people, nobles of the tsar and grand prince, boyar sons from various towns, and Kazan princes and murzas, Kostrov-knyaz and Chapkun and Burnash with companions, 500 men, since many of them were then with the sovereign, to build the city.

And ahead the sovereign sent his commanders to the city: boyar and commander Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and okolnichy and commander Vladimir Vasilievich Morozov, and okolnichy and commander Fedor Grigorievich Adashev, and commander Prince Peter Semyonovich Serebryany, and likewise commander Prince Fedor Borisovich Romodanovsky. And the tsar and sovereign instructed them, ordering them to establish the city at the Sviyaga mouth on Round Mountain, twenty versts from Kazan; and as soon as, God willing, they establish the city, boyar and commander Prince Semyon Ivanovich shall divide the gates among the commanders. And by the field route the sovereign ordered Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Khilkov to go from Meshchera.

And from Nizhny Novgorod the sovereign ordered Prince Peter Serebryany to go swiftly against the Kazan posad, and with him went boyar sons, streltsy, and Cossacks.

And from Vyatka he ordered Bakhteyar Zyuzin with the Vyatchans to come to the Kama; and from upstream along the Volga the sovereign sent many Cossacks and ordered them to station themselves at all the crossings on the Kama, the Volga, and the Vyatka River, so that military men should not travel from Kazan or to Kazan.

And from Meshchera the sovereign sent by the field route foot Cossacks to the Volga, the Severga, and the Yelka, and with them 2,500 Cossacks; and he ordered them, on reaching the Volga, to build boats and go up the Volga to subdue the Kazan environs; and he ordered them to come to the same commanders.

And Prince Peter Serebryany set out from Nizhny Novgorod on the 16th day of May, on Saturday before Trinity Day, and on Sunday, at Trinity Vespers, Prince Peter came to the mouth of the Sviyaga, to Round Mountain, and they sang Trinity Vespers and prayers on that hill where now, by God's grace, the city stands.
