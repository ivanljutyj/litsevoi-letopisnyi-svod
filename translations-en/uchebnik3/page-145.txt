The Epistle of Metropolitan Philip to Novgorod

The Most Reverend Philip, Metropolitan of All Rus, also more than once sent from himself to them in Veliky Novgorod instructions from the holy writings, for example: "I hear, my sons, that some among you incite [people] toward striving for disorder of the land and sedition, wishing to begin a great rebellion and schism of God's holy church, falling away from Orthodoxy and from the ancient sovereignty, wishing to join Latinism. And you yourselves rid yourselves of such foolish people among you and of such an evil undertaking of theirs, because, my sons, this is a God-abominable deed—to abandon the light of piety and join the darkness of Latin falsehood, not acknowledging God's righteous judgment, not fearing to receive from Him many eternal torments. And fear God's wrath and His terrible sickle, descending from heaven upon disobedient sons, seen by the great prophet Zechariah. And therefore punish the disorderly who sow discord and temptations among you, and guide them to good, to walk in the ancient path of their fathers and live in former piety and tranquility. Much unquenchable calamity lies ahead in such an undertaking, if you abandon the law of piety, the commandments of the living God, and adhere to Latinism; for for all the deceived souls of the faithful the Lord God will require [account] from those deceitful apostates. And you must repel such wickedness from among you, as it is said: 'Flee from sin as from a warrior, flee from deceit as from the face of a serpent, lest it wound your soul with the sting of endless perdition.' You yourselves know, my sons, how in former times how many kingdoms and great lands, many cities and places, through violation of the law and disobedience to the prophets, disobedience to the apostles and the teaching of the holy fathers, fell into great misfortune and desolation; disobedient cities and lands entrusted by God to their sovereign were wiped from the face of the earth and laid waste. Did not the reigning great Constantinople, which formerly shone with piety more than other kingdoms, perish because of those same Latin deceits, and was it not extinguished from piety and conquered by the impious Turks even to this day? And you, my sons, fear the fear of God: for let neither one nor two of you be tempted, wishing to fall away from the truth and depart from the right path, forgetting the great ancient sovereignty and the law of your fathers, grandfathers, for..."
