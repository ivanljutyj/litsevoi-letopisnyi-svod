The sovereign, having heard of God's mercy toward him, the Orthodox tsar, and toward all Orthodoxy, and of the victory over the impious, went to the church of Sergius the Wonderworker with tears of gratitude and gave praise for this to the merciful God, and asked for mercy and help in the future.

The commanders came to the sovereign that same day with all the boyar sons, and the sovereign rewarded them with great favor and exhorted them with loving words, and praised their bravery, and promised to reward them in the future.

On the Message to the City of Kazan

The pious tsar sent to the city one of the brought Tatars with letters and wrote in them that they should submit to the sovereign tsar and grand prince, and the sovereign would pardon them all; but if they did not submit, the sovereign would order those who had been captured alive to be killed. The townspeople gave no answer, and the tsar, having brought them to the city, ordered them all to be slain.

On the Tunnel

On the 31st day of August, a Wednesday, the sovereign summoned to him a German named Razmysl, skilled and experienced in destroying city walls, and ordered him to dig a tunnel under the city.

And they began to dig the tunnel near the towers by the Bulak River, at the okolnichy Pyotr Morozov's, between the Atalykov Gates and the Tyumen Gates.

The Tsar's Question About Water

And the sovereign summoned to him Kamai Murza, who had left Kazan, and asked him where they got water in the city, since the Kazan River had already been taken from them; and he also questioned the prisoners who in those days had fled to the sovereign. And they said that there was a secret passage from the Kazan River, near the Muraleev Gates, a spring on the bank, and they went to it through an underground passage.

The sovereign then ordered the commanders of the guard regiment, boyar Prince Vasily Semenovich and Semyon Vasilyevich, to destroy their path to the water.

They tried repeatedly but could not, for their path to the water was fortified by solid earth.

And the commanders reported to the sovereign that there was a close path from that stone bathhouse in which the sovereign's Cossacks stood, and from that bathhouse one could dig a tunnel under the secret passage.
