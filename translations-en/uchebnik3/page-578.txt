On the Flight to Lithuania of Prince Nikita Lobanov, Son of Rostovsky

In the same year, in July, Prince Nikita, son of Prince Semyon Lobanov-Rostovsky, wished to flee to Lithuania, and the boyar scions caught him in Toropets.

And they brought him to the tsar and grand prince. And the tsar and grand prince ordered him to be interrogated as to why he had fled; and Prince Nikita said that the boyar Prince Semyon Rostovsky had sent him to Lithuania to the king to say concerning himself that he was coming to the king, and with him his brothers and nephews; and before that Prince Semyon had sent his man Bakshay to the king to ask for a safe-conduct.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered Prince Semyon to be taken and interrogated; and Prince Semyon said that he had wished to flee from poverty and feeble-mindedness, because he had a scantiness of reason and for every good deed, heedlessly and in vain, squandered the tsar’s favor and his household’s substance. And from that, he said, he began to be vexed, as though the sovereign had sent him and his kin not according to their ancestry, together with many who were lesser than they. But he said all this wishing to betray the sovereign, whereas the sovereign had sent him according to his worth, and his kin with those with whom it was fitting for them to be, but they raged with malice.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered his people whom he had named in his own council to be seized. And his man Semeyka testified against him that he had corresponded with the Lithuanian envoys, with Dovoyna and his companions, through his man Bakshay, and had himself met with them twice, and had pledged himself to them by oath, and had conveyed the counsels of the tsar and grand prince to the envoys, saying that they should not make peace with the tsar and grand prince, that the realm had grown impoverished, and that the tsar and grand prince could not hold Kazan and would soon abandon it, and therefore the envoys had not concluded a perpetual peace; and he had spoken many insulting words to them about the tsar and sovereign grand prince, because the sovereign did not favor them all, dishonored great families, and brought young men near to himself, and oppressed us with them, and by this too he has oppressed us, that he married and took the daughter of one of his boyars, took his own slave, and how are we to serve our own sister, and he spoke other insulting words. Thus did Satan, the hater of good, wound him; and he sent his man Bakshay to the king, and wrote with him blasphemy and reproach against the sovereign and the whole land.

And Prince Semyon himself said that he had said all that out of madness, and had betrayed the counsel of the tsar and grand prince to the king through his envoys, and Prince Andrey Katyrev Rostovsky and others, such madmen, the Rostovsky princes, the Lobanovs and Priimkovs, and other perjurers, had wished to go with him.
