Arrival to the Sovereign from Syrensk of the Commanders with News

In that same month of June, Timofei Teterin and Barkhat Pleshcheev came to the tsar and sovereign from the commanders at Syrensk, from Prince Fyodor Troekurov and Daniil Adashev and their comrades, with the joyful news that by God’s mercy they had taken the city of Syrensk.

When Daniil and his comrades came to Skamya, five versts from Syrensk, and from there sent the commander Pavel Zabolotsky and the commander Zhdan Veshnyakov, Daniil ordered the roads from Koluvan and Riga to be blocked in view of the possible arrival of the master. And Pavel and Zhdan blocked the roads and placed commanders with boyar sons and the Kazan princes Kostrov and Burnash and their comrades along the abatis.

And Daniil came to the city of Syrensk, and brought the equipment out of the vessels, and set up siege towers.

And from the city they began to fire from many cannon, and the commanders fired upon the city on the fifth day of June, a Sunday.

Daniil sent the commander Ivan Zamytsky and the commanders Grigory Papin Saburov, the streltsy commander Timofei Teterin, the tsar and grand prince’s clerk Shestak Voronin, and the commanders Afanasy Novokreshchenov and Bogdan Rzhannikov to set up towers on the other side.

And they set up towers around the city, and placed guns on all the towers, and stationed streltsy with arquebuses in trenches before the towers, and began to fire upon the city from all the cannon and arquebuses at the windows. And on that same day the commander Prince Fyodor Ivanovich Troekurov came from Novgorod, and with them a few people arrived on the sixth day of June.

The princelet of Syrensk addressed the commanders from the city and asked leave with a few people.

And the commanders took the property, armor, and all the equipment, and after searching the princelet, released him without any property; and those Germans who had fallen behind all petitioned and gave themselves into servitude to the tsar and grand prince.

And the common people—Latvians, Baty, and Chukhontsy—from the entire Syrensk district joined the sovereign and took an oath to be inseparable from the sovereign forever. And the Syrensk district is sixty versts long, and in breadth in places fifty and in places forty; and God cleared the whole of Lake Peipus, and the Narva River from its source to the sea came to belong to the tsar and sovereign grand prince.
