To the Spirit, and to the most pure Mother of God. And they handed over the city and every order of human society into the hands of the holy Lady Theotokos Hodegetria, that is, the Guide, saying: “You, all-immaculate Lady Theotokos, lover of mankind, by nature do not abandon the city that is your inheritance, but as a mother of the Christian race intercede, preserve and have mercy on it, guiding and teaching it at all times, as a humane mother, so that the name of your magnificence may be glorified and magnified in it throughout the ages.” And all the people said “Amen” and thanked the tsar, and praised his good understanding and his striving toward God.

The tsar urged the strategoi and city administrators to build holy churches and secular houses for the prosperity of the city. To the nobles and the megistanoi, and to all the eminent people, he commanded: if anyone is deemed worthy of a degree of the imperial rank, let him create for himself a worthy memorial and raise a house or a glorious monastery, or some other wondrous building, which will be filled with most glorious deeds.

Likewise after him, reigning tsars and tsaritsas, each in his own time, strove to do something worthy: some by seeking out and gathering the instruments of the Passion of the Lord, the robes and belt of the most pure Mother of God, the relics of saints, and the divine icon of that very God-manlike image not made by hands from Edessa. Others in the expansion of the city, building great structures; others, such as Justinian the Great, Tsar Theodosius the Great, and Tsaritsa Eudokia, wife of Tsar Heraclius, and many others, erected holy monasteries and divine churches. And thus they filled the city with most glorious and wondrous things, at which sometimes even blessed Andrew of Crete marveled, saying: “Truly this city is beyond word and understanding.” Moreover, the all-immaculate Lady, the Mother of Christ our God, in former times preserved and covered the reigning city from troubles, and saved it from incurable calamities. And by such great and ineffable benefactions and gifts of the most holy Theotokos was this city deemed worthy, that, I think, not even the whole world could be compared to it. But our nature is hard-hearted, negligent, and unruly, if we turn away from the mercy of God and His bounties and turn to evildoing and lawlessness, by which we anger God and His most pure Mother and fall away from our glory and honor. As it is written: “Evildoings and lawlessness will destroy the throne of the mighty,” and again: “He scattered the proud thoughts of their hearts and brought down the mighty from their thrones.” Likewise this reigning city, by countless sins and lawless deeds, fell away from the bounties and benefactions of the most pure Mother of God, suffering innumerable troubles and various calamities for many years. Likewise now in these last times, because of our sins: sometimes by the invasion of unbelievers, sometimes by famine and frequent pestilences, sometimes by internecine wars, because of which the mighty have become depleted, the people have grown poor, and the city has been greatly diminished and brought very low.
