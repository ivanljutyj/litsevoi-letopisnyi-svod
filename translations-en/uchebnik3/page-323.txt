Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, having learned from the princes and boyars of Smolensk and from the townspeople, according to their investigation, about the treason of Bishop Varsonofy—that he was bringing Latinism upon the patrimony of the Orthodox sovereign—gave them great praise, and sent Bishop Varsonofy from Smolensk to the sovereign grand prince in Moscow.

About Ostrogsky

Some time after the bishop was sent away, Lithuanian commanders, Prince Constantine Ostrogsky and other commanders, came to the city of Smolensk with many men, and they sent many letters and charters into the city to the Smolensk people, and made frequent assaults upon the city.

But by God’s help and that of His most pure Mother, the princes and boyars of Smolensk and all the townspeople, together with the grand prince’s commanders and all the people, stood unanimously and firmly against the Lithuanian people, often went out from the city, and fought them stoutly. But the godless Prince Constantine Ostrogsky, traitor to the sovereign, having stood before the city of Smolensk, accomplished nothing and returned in great shame.

And immediately, having come out of the city, many people, Muscovites and Smolensk people, pursued them and killed many Lithuanian people, and took others, noblemen’s sons and hetmans, captive. And Prince Constantine fled, leaving behind many wagons and carts with supplies.

About the Treason of Prince Michael Izhoslavsky

Then Prince Michael Izhoslavsky betrayed the sovereign grand prince, transgressed the oath on the cross, and fell away from the Orthodox grand sovereign with Mstislavl to the Lithuanian King Sigismund.

About the Treason of the Krichavlyane and Dubrovlyane

The Krichavlyane and Dubrovlyane did the same: they betrayed the grand prince sovereign, violated the oath on the cross, and went over to the king.
