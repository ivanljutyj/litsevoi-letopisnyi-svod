It was very hard then within the city, but by God’s mercy and the prayers of His Most Pure Mother and the supplications of the great wonderworkers, the wind drew out of the city; and so it was protected. The grand prince himself was then in the city, and he helped much in all places, riding about with many boyars’ sons, quenching the flames and scattering them.

In the same month, on the 30th day, Thursday, on the eve of the fast, news came to the grand prince that the tsar with all the Horde was going to Aleksin.

At the second hour of that day the grand prince ordered the Divine Liturgy to be sung; and after hearing the liturgy and tasting nothing, he quickly set out for Kolomna, and he ordered his son to follow his grandmother to Rostov.

And Tsar Akhmat came with many forces to the city of Aleksin; and in it there were few people, and there were no defensive works, no cannons, no arquebuses, no crossbows; but nevertheless many Tatars were killed beneath it.

On Friday they again approached the city with many forces and set it on fire; and whatever people were in it, all burned, and those who escaped the fire were seized.

Then the Tatars again went swiftly to the bank of the Oka with great force and all rushed into the river, wishing to cross to our side, because at that place there was no army, since ours had been led off to a deserted place.

But only Pyotr Fyodorovich and Semyon Beklemishev with very few men stood there; and a great multitude of Tatars waded across the Oka toward them; and they began to shoot at the Tatars and fought long with them, until they were short of arrows and thought of fleeing.

At that time Prince Vasily Mikhailovich came up to them with his regiment, and then the regiments of Prince Yuri Vasilyevich arrived, and in that same hour Prince Yuri himself came after them.

And so the Christians began to overcome the Tatars; and the Tatars, seeing the multitude of Christian regiments, fled beyond the river. And the regiments of the grand prince and of all the princes came to the bank, and there was a great multitude of them, also those of Tsarevich Danyar, Tregub’s son. And behold, the tsar himself came to the bank; and he saw many regiments of the grand prince, like a heaving sea, and their armor was polished and shone like silver, and their armament was strong, and the Tatars began to retreat little by little from the bank.

And that night fear and trembling fell upon Tsar Akhmat, and he fled, driven by the wrath of God, though not one man from the grand prince’s regiments was across the river on their side. For God, the all-merciful lover of mankind, pitying the Christian race, sent a deadly pestilence upon the Tatars, and many in their regiments began to die; and, terrified, they set out to flee, so that in six days they reached their settlements, from which they had been marching all summer.
