On the Orsha Battle. In the year 7023 from Adam (1523 from Christ)

And he left in Smolensk his boyar and voivode Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuysky and his other voivodes with many people to guard the city of Smolensk, having established his patrimony as it should be.

And he himself went from Smolensk to his patrimony, Moscow, in the year 7023 from Adam (1523 from Christ) on September 10. And he sent to his voivodes, to Prince Mikhail Bulgakov and to his other voivodes, ordering them to follow him to Moscow according to his former command.

The voivodes of the grand prince, Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Bulgakov, having joined with Grigory Fedorovich and with Ivan Andreyevich, remained on the Dnieper, waiting for those people of the grand prince who had been sent from them to the Drutsk fields, to Borisov and to Minsk.

And at that time, after the sending away of the traitor Prince Mikhail Glinsky, the king's voivodes Prince Konstantin Ostrozhsky and other Polish and Lithuanian voivodes came upon them unexpectedly with many people, with cannon and with handguns, and attacked the grand prince's voivodes. And the grand prince's voivodes fought them stoutly, and many people fell on both sides.

With the king's voivodes there were many soldiers with handguns, and the place was narrow, and they shot from the woods at the grand prince's people, and with a cannon they killed in the advance regiment the voivode Prince Ivan Ivanovich Temka Rostovsky; and among them many people and voivodes, Polish and Lithuanian, and lords' sons and petty princes were killed.

And the king's voivodes overcame the grand prince's voivodes. And in that battle, through our sins, the grand prince's voivodes: Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Bulgakov, and his brother Prince Dmitry Bulgakov, and Prince Ivan Dmitrievich Pronsky, and Ivan Andreyevich Chelyadnin, and other voivodes fell into the hands of the people of the Lithuanian king. And other sons of boyars of the Moscow land and the Novgorod land were taken captive.

Many of the king's people fell in that battle; and other voivodes of the grand prince rode away from that battle and went to Smolensk.

On Vladyka Varsonofy of Smolensk

But Bishop Varsonofy of Smolensk, despising the Divine judgment, trampling on his hierarchal conscience and breaking his oath and kiss of the cross, betrayed the sovereign grand prince and sent to the king his nephew Vaska Khodykin with a letter, in which it was said: “If you now go yourself to the city of Smolensk, or send your voivodes with many people, you can now take the city without difficulty.”
