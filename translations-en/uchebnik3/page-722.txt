In the same month of January, a messenger from the Lithuanian king, Vasily Zagorovsky, came to the tsar and grand prince with the complaint that the tsar and grand prince’s commanders were building towns on the king’s land, and had already built the towns of Usvyato, Ula, and Sokol; and that the tsar and grand prince should not build towns in the king’s land. And according to the agreement of the king’s envoys, Yury Alexandrovich Khotkevich and his companions, with the boyars of the tsar and grand prince, Vasily Mikhailovich Yuryev and his companions, the envoys of the tsar and grand prince, Fyodor Ivanovich Kolychev Umnogo and his companions, did not come to Orsha at Christmas and Epiphany.

And the tsar and grand prince wrote to King Sigismund in his charter with the Lithuanian messenger Vasily Zagorovsky that, according to the decision of the tsar and grand prince, the envoy, boyar and namestnik of Suzdal, Fyodor Ivanovich Umnogo Kolychev and his companions, had not come to Orsha because plague had begun in Smolensk; and when the plague in Smolensk passes, then the tsar and grand prince’s envoys would be there in Orsha. But the tsar and grand prince orders towns to be built in his own hereditary domain, in the Polotsk district, which God gave him, the sovereign.

In the same year, on April 1, the tsar and grand prince released the Nogai envoys of Tynakhmat, Ishtora, Bekchura, and Topas. And together with them the tsar and grand prince sent to Prince Tynakhmat with his sovereign favor Fyodor Matveyev, son of Elizarov; to Urus-murza, Ivan Maltsov, son of Danilov; and to Tinaley-murza, son of Koshum, Stepan Shigin, son of Priklonsky; and with them a stanitsa of serving Tatars; to Asanak-murza, son of Koshum, a stanitsa of serving Tatars, Yansuba Useinov and companions. And the Nogai envoys Ishtora and companions and Fyodor Elizarov and companions set out from Moscow in spring by water in the month of April.

On the same day the tsar and grand prince released the Samarkand envoy Khozya-Khurt and companions, and Khozya-Isup, envoy of the Bukharian Tsar Yabdula, and companions, and with them sent letters to their sovereigns concerning trade.

On the same day the envoy of the Urgench king was released. But he was not received by the tsar and grand prince, because the Urgench king had plundered the sovereign’s people, the under-clerk Aksen Ivanov and companions, and had taken the sovereign’s gifts; and the tsar and grand prince ordered his officials to accept the letter from the Urgench envoy. And to the Urgench king he sent his royal letter about this: that he should search out the sovereign’s gifts and send them to the sovereign, and return all the plunder taken from the sovereign’s merchants. And then the sovereign would favor him as before, and would order the road to be open to his merchants and traders without hindrance, and envoys to come.
