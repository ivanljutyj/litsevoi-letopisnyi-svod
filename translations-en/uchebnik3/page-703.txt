And having spoken his speech, he gave the staff into the hierarch’s right hand, and the tsareviches and archbishops, bishops, and all the boyars sang the Many Years to the metropolitan. And then the metropolitan blessed the sovereign and spoke to him: “May the almighty and all-sovereign right hand of the Most High preserve, O Lord God, thy appointment to the Russian tsardom, autocratic tsar and lord; may thy realm be peaceful, long-lived, and victorious, and with all who submit to thee may it endure unto ages of ages, all the days of thy life! We, though unworthy of such greatness of rank, yet obeying God, must entreat the almighty right hand of our God glorified in the Trinity, and His most pure Mother, and the great wonderworkers Peter and Alexis and Jonah and all the saints, for the many years of thy reign, and for thy tsaritsa, and for thy children, and for all who submit to thy autocratic tsardom, for the benefit of good order and for the sake of well-being of soul and body. Hail, hail, hail, O beneficent, life-bearing lord and autocrat, many years!”

In the same month of March, on the 6th day, there came to the tsar and grand prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilievich, from the Italian land, from Caesar Ferdinand and from Master Vulf-Yank, a German messenger, Lavrin. And he brought to the tsar and grand prince a letter from the Roman Caesar Ferdinand, and likewise a letter from his sovereign Vulf-Yank to the tsar and grand prince. And the Caesar wrote in his letter that, at the entreaty of Master Vulf-Yank, he wrote that his tsarist majesty should show favor to the old Livonian master Velim Shtenberg and release him overseas, because he is already an old man and would live with his friends until his death. And when his life ends, his friends will bury him among themselves. And Master Vulf-Yank wrote in his letter to the tsar and grand prince with great entreaty concerning the release of the same master.

In the same month of March, on the 12th day, the fourth Sunday of Great Lent, by the command of the pious tsar and grand prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilievich, and by the election of the most reverend Athanasius, metropolitan of all Rus, and of the holy consecrated council, Herman was installed as archbishop of Kazan; before that he had been archimandrite in the town of Sviyazhsk, in the Church of the Nativity of the Theotokos. This same Herman was the second archbishop in Kazan after the taking of Kazan.

In the same month of March, on the 15th day, the tsar and grand prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilievich, dismissed the Crimean envoy Prince Yanboldy and his companions, and together with him the tsar and grand prince sent to the Crimea to Tsar Devlet-Girey the envoy Fyodor Ignatievich Saltykov and the clerk Melentiy Ivanov.
