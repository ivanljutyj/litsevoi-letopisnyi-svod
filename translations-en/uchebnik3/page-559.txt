The Tsar and Sovereign Was Met by His Brother, Prince Yuri Vasilyevich

And the sovereign went to Moscow and spent the night in his village of Taininskoye; and there his brother, the sovereign’s brother, Prince Yuri Vasilyevich, met him and congratulated the sovereign on the Kazan Tsardom and on the (birth) of the noble son Tsarevich Dmitry. And the sovereign rejoiced that, returning with such a victory, he saw his brother in health; Prince Yuri Vasilyevich, the boyars, and all the people likewise rejoiced, seeing the return of their tsar with great victories.

On the Tsar’s Coming to Moscow

In the same month the sovereign came to his reigning city of Moscow, and such a multitude of people came out to meet the sovereign that even the fields could not contain them: from the Yauza River to the posad and all the way to the city, on both sides of the way, countless people. Old and young cried in loud voices; and nothing else could be heard save: “Many years to the pious tsar, victor over the barbarians and deliverer of Christians!”

And at the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God, at the Meeting, the metropolitan met the pious tsar with crosses and wonder-working icons (including the very icon of the Mother of God that drove away the godless Temir-Aksak in the time of the pious Grand Prince Vasily Dimitrievich), with archbishops and bishops and all the priestly order. And the boyars—the most senior prince Mikhailo Ivanovich Golitsa Bulgakov, and Ivan Grigorievich Morozov—and the whole multitude of the people, old and young, small and great, of every age, male and female, meet him with thanksgiving and tears. And the sovereign came to the wonder-working icons, and crossed himself before the icons, and receives blessing from his father and pilgrim Makary, Metropolitan of all Rus, and from the whole consecrated council.

The Speech of the Tsar and Sovereign to the Metropolitan

The speech of the tsar and great prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilyevich, to Makary the Metropolitan and to the whole consecrated council: “O our father Makary, Metropolitan of all Rus, and archbishops, and bishops, and all Orthodox Russian councils! I petitioned you concerning your labor before Almighty God and your diligent prayers, that, my lord, you would pray to the most merciful God and the most pure Mother of God, and the great wonder-workers, and all the saints for our health and for the forgiveness of many sins, and for the ordering of the land, and for deliverance from the barbarian invasion, and deliverance from all visible and invisible enemies. And I also consulted with you about this, that the Kazan tsars and all the Kazan people for many years, in return for our favor, have been betraying us and plundering Christians; and they have captured many cities and villages of our Russian realm, granted to us by God, and in those cities they have destroyed the holy churches—”
