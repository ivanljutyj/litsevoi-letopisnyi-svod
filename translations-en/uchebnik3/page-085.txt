And they quickly went to the monastery. And when they appeared, galloping on horses down that hill toward the village of Klimentyevskoye, as if for a sweet hunt, the Grand Prince saw them and himself ran to the Horse Yard. But no horse had been prepared for him, for he had deceived himself by trusting in the oath upon the cross. And he had not ordered anything prepared for himself; and all his people were despondent and in great dismay, as if stunned.

The Grand Prince saw that there was no help for him and quickly went to the monastery, to the stone church of the Holy Trinity. The sexton, a monk named Nikifor, ran up and unlocked the church, and the Grand Prince entered the church; and he, having shut the Grand Prince in, went away and hid.

But those murderers, like savage wolves, rode into the monastery on horseback, Nikita Konstantinovich before all the others, and up the stairs on horseback to the front church doors. And there he got down from his horse and stumbled over the stones that lay on the porch before the church doors.

And the others ran up to him and lifted him up; he could barely breathe and was as if drunk, and his face was like that of a dead man.

Then Prince Ivan himself galloped into the monastery with all their host. And Prince Ivan began to ask where the Grand Prince was. The Grand Prince, inside the church, heard the voice of Prince Ivan and cried out very loudly: “Brother, have mercy on me! Do not deprive me of the sight of the image of God and His most pure Mother and all His saints, and I will not leave this monastery, but will be tonsured here.”

And going to the south doors, he unlocked them himself, took the icon that was on the tomb of Saint Sergius—the Appearance of the Most Holy Theotokos with two apostles to Saint Sergius—and met Prince Ivan in those same church doors, saying: “Brother, we kissed the life-giving cross and this icon in this church of the Life-giving Trinity, at this same tomb of the wonderworker Sergius, that none of us brothers should think or desire any evil against one another; and now I no longer know what will become of me.” Prince Ivan said to him: “My lord and sovereign, if we wish you any harm, may that harm be ours; but let us act for the sake of Christendom and for your ransom, for we have seen the Tatars who came with you; let them lighten the ransom that you must give to the Tsar.”
