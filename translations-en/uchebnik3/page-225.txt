The archbishop and their boyars came to Moscow during Holy Week.

The grand prince released their boyars and again sent to them Archbishop Vassian of Rostov, and with him his boyars—Vasily Fedorovich Obrazets and Vasily Borisovich Tuchka, and the clerk Vasily Mamyrev—and he said to them: “Come again to your patrimony, and I wish to favor you in all things, and to Prince Andrew I add to his patrimony and to his mother’s gift Kaluga and Aleksin, two towns on the Oka.”

They left Moscow on Thursday of the fourth week after Easter, on the 27th day of April, and came to Luki on the Saturday of Pentecost; for it was spring and the road was very difficult. They spoke to them, also concerning the grand prince’s favor; but they did not listen to the archbishop’s words, but behaved arrogantly. And they sent the archbishop and the boyars back to the grand prince. They did not obey the grand prince in anything and did not return.

The archbishop and the boyars returned and came to Moscow. The grand prince was justified before them and placed his hope in God and in His most pure Mother.

And at that time they heard of the invasion of the tsar and foreigners against Rus, the godless Tsar Akhmet of the Great Horde. The grand prince sent his commanders to the bank to meet the Tatars; the Tatars, having come, captured Besputa and withdrew.

And the grand prince sent to the bank on the Oka his son Grand Prince Ivan and his younger brother Prince Andrew with all his forces, and Prince Vasily Mikhailovich.

Princes Andrew and Boris sent their clerks to petition; their mother, the grand princess, interceded for them before her son the grand prince. The grand prince refused them and did not accept their petition.

That same autumn, after the grand prince’s departure for Novgorod, there came to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and to his son Grand Prince Ivan Ivanovich from the Steppe two tsars to serve: Merdoulat with his son Berdoulat, and his brother Aidar, sons of Tsar Achi-Girei of Crimea.

That same winter, on the 15th day of March, Wednesday of the fifth week of Lent, a Tatar of his own stabbed Tsarevich Berdoulat, son of Merdoulat, and Merdoulat, Berdoulat’s father, himself cut off that Tatar’s head.

A son, George, was born to the grand prince.

On the 23rd day of March, Thursday of the sixth week of Lent, at the fourth hour of the day, a son was born to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and was named George, in honor of Saint George of Mytilene.
