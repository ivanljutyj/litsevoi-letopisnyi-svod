On the Tsar's Sending of Voivodes

And the tsar and grand prince sent out his voivodes Prince Andrei Ivanovich Nogtev Suzdalsky, Zakhar Ivanovich Pleshcheev, and Petr Petrovich Golovin, and from Novgorod he ordered the steward Semyon Vasilyevich Sheremetev to go, and with him he ordered the Obonezhskaya and Vodskaya pyatinas to muster.

And the sovereign ordered and commanded them to investigate why the provocation had arisen, and whether it was on his side; and he ordered them to guard strictly against it, so that the Grand Prince's people should not go to the Germans singly, neither for war nor for plunder.

And the voivodes wrote about this to the tsar and grand prince, that on both sides there was much thievery, and because of that blood was being shed, and things were going badly, since between the King and the Novgorod lieutenants there was no agreement on justice.

The Swedish King Broke His Oath

In the year 7064 from Adam (the year 1564 from Christ), in September, the Swedish King Gustav, having transgressed his oath and violated the truce, sent his voivode Yakov Baga with many men in war against Oreshek; and Yakov came from Vyborg by land on horseback, and there were many foot soldiers with them, while in ships by sea along the Neva many men with ordnance came at the same time to Oreshek.

And they fired at the town with ordnance, and ravaged the land, and they stood before the town for three weeks; and in the town at that time was Petr Petrov.

And the voivodes, Prince Andrei and Zakhar, came against them and in sorties killed some of their men, and took one ship from them—on it were one hundred and fifty men and four cannon—and they killed and captured all the men, and none of them escaped from that ship.

And Semyon Sheremetev came against those Germans who had gone to ravage the Novgorod side, and he took prisoners from them.

And the Germans came to Oreshek by night, and Petr went out to meet them and killed about twenty of their princely court servants.

And when the Germans were leaving Oreshek, the voivodes came together—Semyon came from beyond the Neva to Prince Andrei—and joined into three regiments: in the great regiment were Prince Andrei Nogtev and Petr Petrov, in the advance regiment was Semyon Sheremetev, in the guard regiment was Zakhar Pleshcheev; but their men did not join them; and those who were with the ordnance came against the Germans, and the Germans began to shoot at the regiments with arquebuses and cannon, and the advance regiment wavered, and all rode off, since their forces in men were unequal; but the Germans killed no one there, and on both sides there were about five or six dead from arrows and arquebuses.
