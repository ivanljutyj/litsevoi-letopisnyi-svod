He went to Kostroma; and Edigey sent his son in pursuit of him with many men, and God barely had mercy on the Grand Prince, so that he did not fall into Tatar hands; but now our sovereign the Grand Prince is young, and his brother is even younger: they cannot endure fast riding and fatigue, and how can one travel swiftly with small children?” The Metropolitan said: “But the cities to which our sovereigns withdrew during Tatar invasions—Kostroma and other cities—those cities, through our sins, are now at enmity with Kazan; while our sovereigns did not withdraw to Novgorod and Pskov, since they are near the Lithuanian and German borders. And upon whom are we to leave the wonderworkers and Moscow? The great princes used to leave Moscow, but they left their brothers in the city to guard it. Grand Prince Dmitry left Moscow and did not leave his brother and strong commanders, and what happened to Moscow? Lord, protect us and have mercy from such a calamity! The great princes left Moscow in order, having gathered men, to help Moscow and other cities. And the Grand Prince now has many men in Vladimir: Tsar Shigalei, and with him all the Gorodets Horde, and the boyar and commander Prince Ivan Vasilievich Shuisky, and other commanders with many men, who are stationed for the Kazan campaign. And the Grand Prince has ordered the commanders from Kostroma and from Pakhra to join the Tsar; if there are many men with the Tsar and the commanders, then one can ask God for mercy, and there are those with whom to guard the Grand Prince’s cause and help Moscow. But with young sovereigns, without having thought out a place, how is one to manage them? Let the Grand Prince be entrusted to God and His most pure Mother of God and to the wonderworkers Peter and Alexei, for they have care for the Russian land and for our sovereigns; and his father, Grand Prince Vasily, gave his son, Grand Prince Ivan, into the hands of those wonderworkers Peter and Alexei.” And the boyars all agreed on one thing, that it is bad to act hastily with young sovereigns: the Grand Prince should be in the city. And the Grand Prince, having heard the words of his father Ioasaph the Metropolitan and of the boyars, summoned the city stewards and ordered them to lay in city supplies, to place cannons and arquebuses in their positions, to assign people to the gates, to the towers, and along the walls, and to set up barricades along the streets of the settlement.

And the city people, with great desire, began diligently to do everything, and among themselves they promised to stand firm for the holy churches, for the sovereign Grand Prince, and for their own homes, and to lay down their heads.

And messengers report to the Grand Prince that the Tsar is preparing siege towers and wants to cross the river.

And the Grand Prince informed his father Ioasaph the Metropolitan of this, that the Tsar wished to cross the river: “And you, father, would pray to God and to His most holy Mother, the Mother of God, that God not allow him onto the Russian land and not let the godless Basurmans do harm to His possession.”
