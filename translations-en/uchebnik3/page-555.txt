The sovereign entered the city; before him rode the commanders and many noblemen, and behind the sovereign rode Prince Vladimir Andreevich and Tsar Shigalei. And the sovereign came to the Tsar’s Court.

The commanders and all Orthodox people greeted the sovereign, and the people cried out in a loud voice: “Many years to the pious Tsar Ivan Vasilievich, conqueror of the barbarians! Be well, sovereign, in the patrimony God has given you forever!”

And the Orthodox people saw the life-giving Cross and the pious Tsar in the desolate abomination of Kazan. Formerly impious tsars had dwelt in that court, and much Christian blood had been shed for many years, and Christians had endured many misfortunes. Now the sun of righteousness shone forth there, the very life-giving tree, the life-giving Cross, and the image of our Lord Christ and of His most pure Mother of God and of the great wonderworkers.

And the Orthodox pious Tsar, the good sufferer, with Prince Vladimir Andreevich and all the Christian army, and all the people gave thanks to God, saying: “We thank You, Lord Christ, who in this generation has wrought a miracle, so that in a dark place, in desolate abomination, Your great true light has shone; instead of foul Muhammad and his deceivers, You have shown to us sinners Your life-giving Cross and Your wondrous image, and in one hour destroyed the foreign race with their tsars. Glory to You, King, our Lord, Christ, glorified in the Trinity, who has given us Christians such a sovereign in these last times, like the former pious, brave, and courageous tsars, one who lives in Your commandments and is prudent, merciful, and long-suffering toward those who sin, and who delivers us from enemies.” The faithful Tsar, having given praise to God, ordered the commanders to put out the fires in the city and ordered all his army to take the many treasures of Kazan; for himself the sovereign ordered not one copper coin to be taken, nor any captives, only Tsar Ediger-Magmet and the royal standards, and the city cannons.

All the treasures of Kazan and their wives and children he ordered all his army to take. And the sovereign himself went to his court outside the city, where he had previously stood.

And he came to the church of Sergius the Wonderworker, shedding many tears and offering thanks to God with all his army.

And the sovereign went to the table, and having comforted all his soldiers with grateful words, he promises to reward all, and rewards the wounded commanders and all the soldiers with all kinds of gifts.
