And Prince Andrey, on those reports, sent to Laius the streltsy head Andrey Koshkarov, and with him one hundred of his streltsy, and in Laius were the commanders Prince Andrey Babichev and Andrey Solovtsov, and with them one hundred boyar sons and two hundred streltsy.

A dispatch from Astrakhan

Also in December Ivan Vyrodkov sent his son Vaska from Astrakhan, and wrote to the Tsar and Sovereign that the Tyumen Mamai-murza Agishov had come to the Tsar and Sovereign to petition that the Sovereign grant him favor, give him an army against his uncle, the Tyumen prince, and establish him in Tyumen, and he would be the Sovereign's unceasing slave forever. And from the Shevkals they ask the Sovereign to send an army against Krym-Shevkal and give them another, and they with all their land would be the Sovereign's unceasing slaves.

And Ivan also wrote that Ismail had sent against the Crimea his son Tinbai-murza and his nephews, and Ivan sent with them two Nogai murzas, the Kutumov sons, who do not live in Astrakhan and serve the Tsar and Sovereign, and with him Astrakhan people together with Tebai-murza, son of Ismail.

A messenger arrived from the Lithuanian King

In that same month a messenger from the Lithuanian King, Andrey Ivanov, arrived with a letter to the Tsar and Grand Prince (to speak) about matters of grievance, and having been with the Sovereign, he asked to be received also on the next day, saying that he had a commission from the lords concerning the Sovereign's affairs to okolnichy Alexei Fedorovich Adashev, and to treasurer Fedor Ivanovich Sukin, and to clerk Ivan Mikhailov. The Tsar and Sovereign ordered him to be in the clerks' office with Alexei and his companions.

And the messenger spoke to Alexei and his companions from the King's court marshal, Ostap Volovich, asking that they work for Christendom and speak with their uncles, brothers, and boyars, so that they remind the Sovereign to desire peace and love and good accord with their Sovereign the King. And by the Sovereign's command Alexei and his companions answered him: "We always know of our Sovereign that he seeks peace for all Christians, and has many times reminded your Sovereign of goodness and peace for Christians. But your Sovereign does not want that; and now you speak to us from Ostap orally, but there is no letter to us from the King's council of lords. And we have nothing to believe, and nothing to say to our brethren and uncles; when there is a letter from the Polish council about that, then we will gladly help in all that is good for Christians; so say this from us to Ostap."
