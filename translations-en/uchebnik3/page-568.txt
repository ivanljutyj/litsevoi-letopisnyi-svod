Concerning the report sent from Kazan

That same winter, on March 10, Prince Alexander Borisovich Gorbaty sent word from Kazan that the Meadow Side people of Kazan had betrayed us, had not paid the yasak, and had beaten the yasak collectors who were collecting the yasak on the Meadow Side, Misyur Likhorev and Ivan Skuratov; they had come to Arsk and joined together, and had taken position on High Mountain by the abatis.

And the commanders sent against them Vaska Elizarov, and with him Cossacks, and Ivan Ershov with the streltsy; and they came to the abatis.

But, to our misfortune, the streltsy and Cossacks separated along different roads, and the Arsk people and the Meadow people attacked them and routed them utterly, killing three hundred and fifty streltsy and four hundred and fifty Cossacks.

Concerning the establishment of a town on the Mesha

And after that the Arsk people, the riverside people, and the Meadow people built themselves a town on the Mesha, seventy versts from Kazan, and threw up an earthen wall, hoping to hold out there.

That same winter, on March 24, the boyar Prince Peter Ivanovich Shuysky wrote from the town of Sviyazhsk that the Arsk people and the Meadow people had come against the Mountain Side, Zenseit and Sary Bogatyr with their companions, and Prince Peter had sent against them the commander Boris Ivanovich Saltykov, and with him boyar sons and all the Mountain Side people. And Boris went against them. At that time the snow was deep, and the Arsk people and the Meadow people came on skis; and for our sins they defeated Boris, and took Boris himself alive, and killed 36 boyar sons, and 50 boyar retainers, and killed 170 Mountain Side people, and took 200 alive. And this happened because of our sins (and because of our lack of unanimity and our discord, and also arrogance and proud words were revealed: since God had shown His mercy over Kazan), and proud words appeared among us, and not words of gratitude; and we began to reason foolishly, forgetting the Gospel word: whoever wants to be wise in this world, let him become a fool.

And for our great ingratitude, and at that time for our sins, infirmity visited our Orthodox tsar; a great fire overcame him, that is, a burning sickness; and the Gospel word was fulfilled in us: Smite the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered. The sovereign, this good shepherd, while he could, prayed to God for us and kept us in good, and by his prudent strengthening we were preserved; but when for a time he grew silent in extending prayer to God for us and in strengthening us for good, then we suffered from every evil and sorrowful thing.
