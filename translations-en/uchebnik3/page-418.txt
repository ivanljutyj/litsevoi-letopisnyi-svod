And he began to communicate with Prince Ivan and to ask Prince Ivan for a pledge that the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess would not seize him or proclaim great disgrace upon him. But Prince Ivan had no instruction from the Grand Prince or from his mother the Grand Princess to give a pledge to Prince Andrei.

And Prince Ivan, without consulting the Grand Prince and the Grand Princess, gave Prince Andrei his pledge, and together with Prince Andrei set off for Moscow.

And the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess pronounced great verbal disgrace upon Prince Ivan Ovchina for this, that without their command he had given a pledge to Prince Andrei.

And Prince Andrei the Grand Prince Ivan and his mother the Grand Princess ordered to be seized and put in a chamber, and fetters to be placed upon him.

And Prince Andrei's princess, and also his son, the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered to be placed in the city under guard at the Bersenev Court.

And upon his boyars — upon Prince Fyodor Dmitriyevich Pronsky, and upon Prince Ivan Andreyevich Peninsky-Obolensky, and upon his steward Prince Yuri Andreyevich Menshoy Peninsky-Obolensky, and upon his equerry Prince Boris Ivanovich Paletsky, and upon the princes and boyar sons, upon those who had been in his room and knew his counsel — upon Prince Yuri Andreyevich Bolshoy Peninsky-Obolensky, and upon Ivan Umnoy, son of Ivan Loban Kolychev, and upon Prince Andrei's brother-in-law, Prince Ivan Andreyevich Khovansky — the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess laid their disgrace, and ordered them to be tortured and also subjected to public punishment in the market.

And having put them in fetters, the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered them to be placed in the Corner Tower. And his boyar Prince Fyodor died in that prison.

And those boyar sons, the Grand Prince's Novgorod landowners, who came at that time to Prince Andrei and had set off with Prince Andrei for Novgorod — those boyar sons, Andrei Ivanov, son of Pulkov, and Gavrila Vladimirov, son of Kolychev, with their companions, thirty men — the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered to be beaten with the knout in Moscow.

And after beating them with the knout, the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered those boyar sons to be put to death, to be hanged along the Novgorod road, not together, but at intervals as far as Novgorod.

And Prince Andrei Ivanovich remained in that prison for about half a year and died.
