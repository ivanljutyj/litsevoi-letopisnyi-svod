They sent the commanders after the tsar, Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and Prince Vasily Semyonovich Obolensky Serebryany, and with them many men, chosen from all the regiments, both court and town.

And the Ryazan commanders, Prince Mikhail Andreevich Trubetskoy and his companions, with all the men who had come with him, they sent to Ryazan.

The grand prince’s commanders, going after the tsar, killed the lagging Tatars, and captured others alive and sent them to the senior commanders, Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky and his companions.

And those captives told the commanders that the tsar had said to his princes that he had suffered great disgrace: he had brought men from many hordes, but had done nothing to the Russian land.

The princes began to recall Temir-Aksak: he too had come against Rus with many forces, but had not obtained what he desired. And the old Tatars told him that in that campaign Temir-Aksak had taken the town of Yelets.

And the tsar said to the princes: “The grand prince has a town in the Field called Pronsk; it lies near our path, and we shall go and take it and do to it as we did to Yelets: let people not say that the tsar came to the Russian land and did nothing to Rus.” And the tsar set out with all his forces and with his siege train to Pronsk, and he was determined to assault Pronsk.

Prince Dmitry and all the commanders sent more commanders after the tsar, Prince Yury Andreevich Obolensky Peninsky and Prince Vasily Semyonovich Mezetsky, and with them they sent many men; and he ordered them to join all together with the Ryazan commanders and go after the tsar together: to guard the grand prince’s cause and to help Pronsk.

And Tsar Saip-Girey came to Pronsk on the third day of August, and himself encamped beyond the river Pronya, near the town.

And he ordered the army to assault the town with cannons, arquebuses, and wall-smashing siege train. And in the town at that time was the grand prince’s commander with few men, Vasily Zhulebin, great-grandson of Ostey of the Sviblov family, and another, Alexander Kobakov, one of the Ryazan boyars.

The Tatars attacked the town with all their regiments and began to fire at the town from cannons and arquebuses, and their arrows flew like rain. And they approached the walls of the town; from the town they began to fire at the Tatars with cannons and arquebuses, and the Tatars who came up to the wall they beat back from the walls with stakes and stones.

The Tatars assaulted the town all day long and fought with the townspeople; and many Tatars were killed from the town with cannons and arquebuses.

The princes and murzas, riding up to the town, told Vasily to surrender the town, and the tsar would show them mercy; but if the town were not taken, the tsar would have to go away.
