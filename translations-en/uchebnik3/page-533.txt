Be bold, O Tsar: you have come against them, and may the word of Christ be fulfilled in you, our sovereign: “Everyone who asks receives, and to him who knocks it shall be opened.” The pious sovereign hears that they do not answer with differing minds, but with one mind strive with him to suffer for piety, and he recalls that the Lord settles the like-minded in His house. And looking upon the image of the Creator, so that all might hear, he said: “O Master! In Thy name let us too arise; do Thou, O Master, assist us by the power of the Cross and strengthen us by the power of the precious Cross and the grace of the Holy Spirit!” For a long time all the regiments were moved to tears, seeing the image of Christ, and asked for help.

And he comes to his spiritual father, the archpriest Andrei, and the archpriest blesses him with the life-giving Cross and says to him: “Be bold, servant of God, for He will overcome the enemies, as the Lover of mankind, for our Master Christ is the Lover of mankind!” He fences him with the Cross, and the Tsar takes blessing with this Cross and arrays himself against the enemies.

And the sovereign commands all the regiments to fence themselves with the Cross; he mounts an argamak, and all his warriors rejoice with the sovereign with spiritual joy, having chosen death rather than corruptible life, desiring to suffer for Christ, and all were emboldened by the grace of the Holy Spirit.

And having arrayed the regiments, he rode straight to the city, and the sovereign’s advance guards came to Kazan at the upper reaches of the Bulak River by Lake Kaban, and the Kazanians came out of the city and came to meet the sovereign’s regiments; but the sovereign’s command was that without an order no one should dare to join battle.

The Kazanians, having ridden up, began to shoot at the regiment, and our sovereign’s streltsy began to shoot at them from their arquebuses; and the Kazanian horsemen charged the foot streltsy, and Prince Yuri Ivanovich Shemyakin and Prince Fyodor were ordered to send the boyar sons of their regiment to help the streltsy.

And both sides fought: the Russians and the Tatars, and God helped the Orthodox to drive the Kazanians right up to the city gates, and some Tatars they killed and others they wounded; and the commanders rode together to the city and sent all the boyar sons back to the regiments. But all the sovereign’s people were preserved by God; few of them were wounded; and from the other regiments not a single man went forth, for there was no command. And all watched and marvelled at the Tsar’s dispositions and commands.

And Tsar Shigalei and the commanders, coming to the Bulak, stood on the Arsk Field, where the sovereign commanded.
