If there should be no Lithuanian people against them, Prince Vasily and his comrades were to go with the ordnance—with cannons and handguns—to Mstislavl and besiege Mstislavl.

And from Novgorod the Great, the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered their lieutenants to go and wage war on the Lithuanian land—their boyar Prince Boris Ivanovich Gorbaty and Mikhail Semyonovich Vorontsov—with Novgorod and Pskov people, and ordered them to stand at Opochka.

And to their Novgorod steward Ivan Nikitich Buturlin they ordered him to go with the Pskovians into the Lithuanian land, with many people and with ordnance, with cannons and handguns; and the Grand Prince and his mother the Grand Princess ordered Ivan Buturlin to set up a town in the Lithuanian land on Lake Sebezh.

King Sigismund, hearing that the Grand Prince’s commanders were before Mstislavl and that other men of the Grand Prince were setting up a town on his land on Lake Sebezh, sent for his commanders and ordered them to go with all their people and ordnance, with cannons and handguns, to the Grand Prince’s town of Starodub.

The King’s commanders—Pan Yuri Nikolaevich Radivilovich, Andrei Nemirov, the Polish hetman Jan Tarnowski, and the Grand Prince’s traitor Prince Semyon Belsky—going to Starodub, took Gomel; the Grand Prince’s commander Prince Dmitry, son of Prince Dmitry Shchepin, surrendered it to them and met them outside the town of Gomel, because at that time the Grand Prince’s newly arrived troops had not reached the town of Gomel, and the local people were few—the Gomelians.

And the King’s commanders went from the town of Gomel to Starodub.

And they came to Starodub in the month of July with all the King’s ordnance, with cannons and handguns, and many newly arrived people with them, and from other lands the King hired soldiers and gunners and handgunners, and with them miners. And the Lithuanian people began to assault the town from all sides, and began to fire from cannons and handguns; and from the town the commander Prince Fyodor Ovchina ordered them to shoot at them from cannons and handguns and to fight them from the town stoutly; but he did not know that ruse—the undermining under the town—since previously in our lands there had never been undermining under a town.

The Lithuanian commanders, having surrounded the town, took position behind gabions close to the town, and undermined it, and set the town on fire, and took it, and carried off the commander Prince Fyodor Ovchina with them, and carried many people away into captivity, and cut down others; and they went away.
