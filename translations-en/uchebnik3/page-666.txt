A Dispatch from the Sovereign to Yuryev

In the same month the sovereign wrote to Yuryev to his boyar and voivode Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Kurlyatev and his companions, and ordered them to send in his name to the master and say that he should petition the sovereign tsar and amend himself in all things, so that innocent Christian blood might not be shed. And in December there was no answer from the master, and then the tsar and grand prince ordered his voivodes, Prince Semyon Ivanovich and his companions, to go to war against Riga in January; and the voivodes crossed the border on January 17.

In the same month, in the town of Dedilov, the sexton of Nicholas the Wonderworker saw a vision of Nicholas the Wonderworker. Before matins a bright light appeared outside the town, before the church, and the town gates opened by themselves; the locks were intact, and the gates opened from their bolts and hinges, and a man radiant in appearance rode in; and the sexton could not look at him and fell to the ground. And the radiant man rode up to him and said to him twice: “Rise, do not fear, look at me.” But the sexton could not look from fear; he saw only the horse under him. And the radiant man said to him: “I am Nicholas, whom you call the Wonderworker; the impious have become enraged, and many impious Basurmans have gone against your city and many Christian places, but do not fear, for I myself am here, and there will be no destruction from the impious for your city and all Christendom.” And he rode into the church, and a bright light appeared in the church.

In the same month, on the 21st day, on the memory of Peter, Wonderworker of Kiev and All Rus, there came to the tsar and grand prince two Cherkassians, Asan and Alida, and two Crimeans, Sar and Kolmak, and they said to the tsar and sovereign that news had come to the Crimean tsar that the tsar and grand prince had gone against the Livonian Germans with all his people; and he, having devised evil against Christianity, wished to enslave the Orthodox realm of our sovereign and suddenly seize Christianity, like roaring wild beasts thinking to obtain what they desire by cunning and robbery and to destroy Christianity without hindrance, as also happened in Lithuania. He sent his son Tsarevich Magmet-Girey on the campaign, and with him he sent Ak-Magmet-ulan and Prince Spat, and Prince Chegilik, and his yataylyk, and two sons of Prince Sulesh, Murza-murza with his brother, and all the Shirin princes, and with him all the Crimeans.
