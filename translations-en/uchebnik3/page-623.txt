On the Consecration of the Church

In the month of November, on the first day, the Church of John Climacus and the side-chapel of the Venerable Martyr Evdokia in the Chudov Monastery, at the rear gates of the tsar's building, were consecrated; and at the consecration were present the tsar and grand prince, and his tsarina Anastasia, and his son Tsarevich Ivan Ivanovich, and his brother Prince Yuri Vasilyevich, and Joasaph, Metropolitan of Cyzicus from Constantinople, and elders of the Holy Mountain; and the consecration was performed by Macarius, Metropolitan of All Rus', with the whole synod.

On the Arrival of Mitka Kurov from Siberia

In the same month Mitka Kurov, envoy of the tsar and grand prince, came from Siberia; and with him came from Edigirey, Prince of Siberia, the envoy Boyanda, and he brought to the tsar and grand prince tribute: 700 sables. And concerning the other part of the tribute, Prince Edigirey and all the Siberian land wrote thus: that the Shiban tsarevich had wished to conquer them and had taken many people captive; and Mitka Kurov said that it had been possible for them to send the tribute in full, but they had not wished to.

On the Tsar's Disgrace upon the Siberian Envoy

And the tsar and sovereign placed disgrace upon the Siberian envoy, ordered his property to be seized and him himself to be placed under guard; and to Siberia he sent a serving Tatar with a letter, so that he should make amends in all things before him, the sovereign.

On the Founding of a City

In that same autumn a town was founded at Galich, and Ivan Vyrodkov founded it.

On the Envoys from the Nogai Horde

In the month of December, on the first day, envoys came to the tsar and sovereign from the Nogai Horde: Temir from Prince Ismail, and Aykula from the children of Yusuf. And Prince Ismail wrote that he had made peace with Yusuf's children, since they had acknowledged their guilt before him, and Tsar Derbysh had fled from them to Mecca; and Ismail ordered them to petition the tsar and grand prince on this, so that by his favor and fear his fellow tribesmen might acknowledge their dependence before the tsar.
