After the feast of the Transfiguration of Christ, an archpriest came from Nizhny Novgorod to the tsar and grand prince with holy water and told him that in those days a sexton had come to the Church of the Conception of Saint Anna in Nizhny Novgorod before Matins and saw a light in the church. He was terrified and, quickly entering the church, saw a man of saintly appearance, radiant with gray hair, commanding him to ring the bells.

The sexton said, “I dare not, master, ring without the priest’s command.” And the one who appeared said, “Fear not, but ring quickly; I cannot delay here, for I am hastening to Kazan to help the tsar and grand prince Ivan.” The sexton quickly went, rang the bells, and returned to the church, where there was no longer anyone. The priest came and questioned the sexton as to why he had rung without his command; and he told the priest what he had seen and heard, and together they thanked God.

The pious sovereign rejoiced in hope, and those with him likewise; in many wondrous signs all understood that such a great undertaking of the pious tsar was God’s will and not human self-will.

On the Meeting of the Sovereign with the Sviyazhsk Voivodes

And when the sovereign set out from the camp, his voivodes from Sviyaga met him. The voivodes came in three regiments: the voivodes and boyars who had come to Sviyaga that spring—boyar and voivode Prince Alexander Borisovich Gorbaty, boyar and court steward Danilo Romanovich Yuryev, and voivode Boris Ivanovich Saltykov; and the annual voivodes from Sviyaga—boyar and voivode Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky, boyar Pyotr Semyonovich Serebryany, okolnichy Fyodor Grigoryevich Adashev, and voivode Prince Fyodor Borisovich Romodanovsky, and with them all the boyar scions who were with those voivodes; and in the third regiment were many mountain people, princes and murzas, and Cossacks, and Cheremis, and Chuvash.

And the sovereign showed favor to the boyars, called them to his hand and asked after their health, and rewarded them for service with many words, and likewise all the boyar scions; and there was inexpressible joy throughout the sovereign’s whole army. The sovereign, seeing many men laboring for piety, calmed them with many gracious words and commanded them henceforth to strive for piety. And all the people rejoiced and glorified God, because He had granted them such a sovereign—pious and wise, long-suffering toward sinners, brave in battle, and a firm defender of Christianity, who has undertaken such a labor.
