On the morrow they came to the Monastery of Joseph to pray to the Most Pure and to bow before the grave of Igumen Joseph; and the igumen with the brethren and the priests and all the church clergy met the Grand Prince at the monastery gates with icons and lamps.

The Grand Prince, when he came in a covered sledge from Kolp to Volok and from Volok to the Monastery of Joseph, did not leave his bed at all; he remained in bed, and they turned him from side to side, because he was exhausted by a severe illness and ate little.

When the Grand Prince set out from Volok for the Monastery of Joseph, Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Shkurlyatev and Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Paletsky were with him in the covered sledge because they turned him during the journey.

When he arrived at the Monastery of Joseph, after the igumen with the brethren had met him, those two, Prince Dmitry Shkurlyatev and Prince Dmitry Paletsky, took the Grand Prince by the arms and went to the church of the Most Pure.

And when in the church the deacon began to say the litany for the sovereign Grand Prince, he could not utter it for tears, while the igumen and the brethren, weeping bitterly, begged mercy from the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother; the Grand Princess with her children stood right there and wept bitterly before the Most Pure Theotokos for the sovereign’s health; the boyars and all the people wept and prayed to God for the sovereign.

The Grand Prince came out of the church and lay down on the bed, for he could not sit, being exhausted by the severe illness.

And they began the divine liturgy, while the Grand Prince lay on a bed in the church porch.

After the completion of the divine liturgy, the Grand Prince was carried to a cell; the igumen entreated the sovereign to take some food.

Then the Grand Prince sent his brother, Prince Andrey Ivanovich, with his boyars to sit down at the table.

And the Grand Prince rested at the Monastery of Joseph; and his brother Prince Andrey Ivanovich came to him, while the great sovereign suffered greatly from his illness.

On the morrow the Grand Prince set out for Moscow, and he released his brother Prince Andrey to his appanage. The Grand Prince was carried in a covered sledge, and in the Grand Prince’s sledge sat Prince Dmitry Shkurlyatev and Prince Dmitry Paletsky. The Grand Prince’s stops for rest were frequent.

And as he traveled he began to consider with his boyars how he might enter the city of Moscow secretly, because at that time there were many foreigners and ambassadors in Moscow.
