And the tsar and grand prince ordered his boyars who had gone with the reply to answer the envoys that the truce was being taken for half a year, because the tsar and grand prince’s winter army was gathered in the Lithuanian land, and they should live through the winter; and he also ordered the boyars to tell the envoys that if they wished to make a truce, they should make it for ten years or more, and should assign the Polotsk povet and all the Livonian land to the sovereign. And the envoys did not wish to do so, and said that they had not been ordered to do so.

And they petitioned the tsar and grand prince that the sovereign would show them favor and give them a letter of safe conduct to Borisov, so that the military men would let them pass and make no hindrances.

And the sovereign showed them favor and ordered a letter of safe conduct to Borisov under his great seal to be given to them. To his military men, princes and boyar sons of the Moscow and Novgorod lands, and to the Nogai, Kazan, and Astrakhan Tatars, and to the Circassians, and to the Germans, and to all military men he ordered that no hindrances be made; and on January 9 the sovereign released them from Moscow.

And the tsar and grand prince’s commanders were then assembling at Vyazma, Dorogobuzh, and Smolensk, and many men were gathered at Polotsk.

And from Polotsk the sovereign ordered the boyar and commander, the viceroy of Polotsk, Prince Pyotr Ivanovich Shuysky, to go to Orsha.

And he was to join with him five versts short of Orsha, at Boran; and from Boran the Vyazma and Smolensk commanders were to go to Minsk and to other places where it would be appropriate.

In the same month, December, on the 9th day, there were great rains and a great flood; the frozen river was broken up, and the ice passed, and the flood lasted two weeks: people traveled on the rivers in boats until the Nativity of Christ.

In the same month, December, on the 22nd day, Prince Tinekhmat, son of Prince Ismail, wrote to the tsar and grand prince from the Nogai lands by his man Temir that his father, Prince Ismail, had died, and that he, Tinekhmat, had been made prince over the Nogais, and that his brother Urus-murza, son of Ismail, was in the nuradyn office. And all his father’s uluses were with him, and before his death Prince Ismail had instructed them, his children, ordering them to petition the sovereign tsar and grand prince that the sovereign protect them from enemies. And that they should be inseparable from the tsar and grand prince until their death, just as he had been inseparable from the tsar and grand prince until his death; just as he had been inseparable from the tsar and grand prince until his death.
