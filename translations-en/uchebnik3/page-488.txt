They said in answer to the command that this matter could not be otherwise: what God has given to the sovereign, he will not renounce.

And the tsar and voivodes, according to the sovereign’s command, sent to Kazan to say that they should come to the tsar and voivodes: the sovereign had ordered them to be shown favor, and they, having come, should take the oath.

On August 9, Mullah Kasym and Prince Bibars, and other princes, murzas, and shikhs came from Kazan to Tsar Shigalei and the voivodes; and the tsar spoke to them a gracious speech on behalf of the sovereign, setting out on what terms they were to take the oath; and they refused in many points, for deceit was natural to them.

And the tsar ordered them to go with the boyars into the boyars’ tents and act according to the sovereign’s command; for the sovereign’s command was this: if they did not act according to the sovereign’s will, then the sovereign had ordered nothing to be done, but intended that same autumn to march with an army and all his people.

And the Kazanians talked much with the boyars about how to do something deceitful; but the boyars, by God’s mercy, firmly carried out the sovereign’s command, did not permit any of their deceit, and exposed their innate cunning.

And they agreed that the Kazanians should bring Tsar Utyamysh-Girey with his mother and the Crimean children to the town of Sviyazhsk, and release all Russian captives, and the princes should bring to the Kazan estuary all those captives who remained with them; and if the sovereign later learned that captives were held in captivity among the infidels, then the sovereign would stand as long as the Merciful God helps; and the tsar and boyars should go to the Kazan estuary and there confirm the whole agreement, and there the whole land of Kazan should meet Tsar Shigalei.

In the same month, on the 11th day, Kudugul-ulan and Prince Muralei with their companions sent Angildei-Abyz from Kazan to the tsar and boyars with news that Tsar Utyamysh-Girey and Tsaritsa Syuyunbek were being brought to the sovereign, and that Tsar Shigalei and the boyars should order them to be met.

And the tsar and voivodes sent Voivode Prince Pyotr Semyonovich Serebryany, and with him boyars’ sons, to meet Tsar Utyamysh-Girey and the tsaritsa; and the prince met them at the Kazan estuary.

On the same day in the evening they brought the tsar and tsaritsa to the town of Sviyazhsk, and with the tsar came Prince Bibars and Limerden-aziy, and they brought two Crimean children: Koshchak-ulan’s child and Akmagmet-ulan’s son.

And Tsar Shigalei and the sovereign’s boyars the very next day sent down the Volga to Moscow to the sovereign Kazan Tsar Utyamysh-Girey with his mother, Tsaritsa Syuyunbek, and the children of the fugitive Crimeans; and with the tsar they sent to the sovereign Voivode Prince Pyotr Serebryany, and with him boyars’ sons and many streltsy, Prince Kostrov and Alimerden-Azey.

And they brought the Kazan tsar and tsaritsa to the sovereign in Moscow on September 5, on Saturday, God granting, in good health.
