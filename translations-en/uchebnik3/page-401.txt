And the Grand Princess, protecting her son and the land, ordered the boyars: “Yesterday you kissed the cross to my son, the Grand Prince Ivan, that you would serve him and wish him well in all things, and you act accordingly—and if evil arises, it must not spread.”

And the Grand Princess ordered Prince Yuri to be seized and, having bound him in fetters, to be placed under guard in the chamber where Prince Dmitry the Grandson had previously been confined. And Prince Yuri was seized on the 11th day of December, on the seventh day after the death of the Grand Prince, on Wednesday, the commemoration of our venerable father Daniel the Stylite.

That same winter, in January, the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus sent to Crimea to Tsar Sahib-Giray and to Tsarevich Islam his boyar’s son Ivan Ilyin, son of Chelishev, to announce the death of his father and to declare himself ruler.

And to Kazan the Grand Prince sent to Tsar Yanalei with the same message Vasily Berechinsky, to announce the death of his father and to declare himself ruler.

In that same month of January, the Grand Prince sent to Lithuania to King Sigismund his boyar’s son Timofei Vasilyev, son of Zabolotsky Brazhnikov, to announce the death of his father and to declare himself ruler; and he ordered him to say to the king that the king should be in friendship and brotherhood with him, as he had been with his father.

On the Kazan Envoys

In that same year, in April, envoys came to the Grand Prince from Kazan from Tsar Yanalei—Kudai-ulan and Burnak, and Kozya-Akhmat the bakshi—concerning peace and agreement, and they wrote a charter as it had been with his father, Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich.

And the Grand Prince dismissed the Kazan envoys, and to Kazan he sent his envoy Fyodor Ivanovich Bezzubtsev on the 14th day of July; and Fyodor died in Kazan.

On the 22nd day of July, Timofei Vasilyevich Brazhnikov arrived from the king; and the king gave him a haughty answer.

That same spring a wooden fortress was built in Novgorod the Great on the Sophia Side. That same spring, on the 20th day of May, by the command of Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus and of his mother, Grand Princess Elena, an earthen fortress was built in Moscow on the site where his father, Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich, had intended to build it.

In that same year, in August, Prince Semyon Fyodorovich Belsky and the okolnichy Ivan Vasilyev, son of Lyatsky, with his son, fled from service at Serpukhov to Lithuania.
