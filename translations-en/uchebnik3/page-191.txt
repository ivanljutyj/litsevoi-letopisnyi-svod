He pitied them greatly and shed tears. And he came to his father Metropolitan Gerontius, informing him of the Novgorodians’ crime against the kiss of the cross: “And concerning what they themselves sent about — that which I did not even desire, the sovereignty over them — they have renounced it, and they have accused us of falsehood.”

The same he announced to his mother, and to his brothers, and to his boyars and commanders; and from that time he was angry with Novgorod.

By the blessing of the most reverend Metropolitan of All Rus’ Gerontius, and of the consecrated council assembled concerning this — archbishops and bishops and the whole priestly order — and also by counsel with his mother and by her prayer, and after deliberation with his brothers, his boyars, princes, and commanders, he takes up arms against his patrimony, against the apostates who had violated the cross-kissed oath, against the Novgorodians.

He placed all his hope in the Lord God and in His most pure Mother, and in the power of the precious and life-giving Cross, which they had kissed and then betrayed.

And he had a service of supplication performed in all the churches of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ and of His most pure Mother, and in the Resurrection, apostolic, and martyrs’ churches, and at the tombs of the wonderworkers, the most reverend metropolitans of Russia Peter, Alexis, and Jonah, and other hierarchs, and throughout the surrounding monasteries of the city of Moscow, and at the Life-Giving Trinity, at the tomb of Sergius the Wonderworker, and throughout all his patrimony, the grand principality, he sent alms to cathedral churches and monasteries and to all churches.

He also sends to Tver to Grand Prince Michael, asking help against Novgorod; he, not disobeying in the least, sends Prince Michael Fedorovich Mikulinsky with many of his soldiers.

After that he also sent to his brothers, who were then already in their patrimonies, ordering each to go from his own place to Novgorod.

The Novgorodians heard of this and sent to the Grand Prince to petition for a safe-conduct for the journey of Fedor Kalitin, elder of Danslavskaia Street, so that the Grand Prince might show favor and allow his pilgrim the Vladyka and the Novgorod envoys to come to him to petition.

And the Grand Prince ordered his governor in Torzhok, Vasily Ivanovich Kitai, to hold that envoy sent for the safe-conduct until his own arrival.

In the year 6986 from Adam (the year 1486 from Christ)

On the 30th day of the month of September, the Grand Prince sends to Novgorod a letter of rupture with the junior clerk Rodion Bogomolov.
