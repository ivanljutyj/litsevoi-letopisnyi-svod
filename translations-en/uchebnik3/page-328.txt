And Ivashko Tutykhin with his companions, having come, began to assault the Tatars from all sides and did not let them fight, and killed many Tatars.

And the Tatars, learning of the great prince’s commanders, quickly turned back.

And many frontier foot soldiers had entered the forests even before them, blocked the roads, and killed many Tatars.

And the men who came first from the commanders, horsemen, began to kill the Tatars: they struck them at the fords and on the roads, and the frontier foot soldiers struck them in the forests.

And with God’s help they killed many people, the Tatars; and many other Tatars drowned in the rivers, and others they took alive. Such then was God’s help in the victory over the Tatars, of which we heard reliably, even from the Tatars themselves who came afterwards from Crimea, that of twenty thousand few came to Crimea, and those on foot, barefoot, and naked.

On the Lithuanian Envoys and the War.
In the 7026th year from Adam (the 1526th year from Christ)

In September the Polish King Sigismund, having thought it over in his treacherous mind, sent to the great sovereign Vasily his envoys, his marshal and governor of Mogilev, Pan Jan Shchit, and also his marshal and scribe Bogush.

And King Sigismund himself went to Polotsk with all his Polish and Lithuanian people, and from there, having prepared for war his commanders—the grand hetman Prince Konstantin Ostrozhsky and all his hetmen and Polish and Lithuanian commanders—and having obtained many mercenaries, he sent them with great cannon and firearm equipment against the great prince’s frontier, to Pskov and its subsidiary town of Opochka.

And hearing of this, the commanders of the great sovereign, Prince Alexander Vladimirovich Rostovsky and many other commanders who were at Velikiye Luki in the outpost, sent news of it to the great sovereign Prince Vasily.

And against the king’s commanders they sent ahead of themselves the light commanders Prince Fyodor Vasilyevich Obolensky Lapata, and Ivan Vasilyevich Lyatsky, and other commanders, boyar sons with few men, and ordered them to help the subsidiary town of Opochka and to hinder the Lithuanian army from all sides.

And the commanders themselves went against the king’s commanders with many men. And the great prince ordered Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuisky to go from Vyazma with many men and many commanders against the king’s commanders.
