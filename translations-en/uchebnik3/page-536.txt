Coming out of the city, they fought until midnight, approached the siege towers, and fought hand to hand. And with God’s help the Orthodox defeated the impious and drove them back into the city.

Thus all night they fought without cease, coming to the towers and fighting hand to hand, without sleep; and by God’s help they defeated the pagans.

The pious sovereign continually ordered prayer services to be held in the churches; and the sovereign sent his boyars hourly to encourage them to stand firm and not weary of fighting. And in that battle, with God’s help, they defeated many Kazanians, killed Prince Islam Narykov, Bashkanda Bruntsev, and Syuncheley the Bogatyr, and many princes and murzas were killed. Of the Orthodox, by God’s grace, few fell: among the elected commanders with the boyar men, the boyar’s son Leonty Borisov Shusherin; and many were wounded, but by God’s grace recovered; and for this the sovereign gave thanks to God.

On the Vision of the Holy Apostles and the Other Saints

And then a certain God-fearing man named Tikhon, who was a servant of Ivan Petrov, son of Golovin, having been wounded in battle and lying in great pain, in his illness saw as in a dream a wondrous miracle: the chief apostles Peter and Paul and many other saints, and with them the great wonderworker Nicholas, bishop among the saints, walking through the air in the radiance of ineffable light. And on earth he saw regiments of Kazan people galloping and looking upward, crying to Saint Nicholas: “O Nicholas! Help us in battle against the Russian host!” And Saint Nicholas, turning to the holy apostles and the other saints with them, said: “The All-good God, who desires to save all people and bring them to true understanding, has willed that Orthodoxy be here also; do not delay, but quickly bless and sanctify this place, as the Lord commanded you.” And as soon as they had blessed the city of Kazan, they ascended in light to the height and became invisible. Tikhon awoke from the vision, and he received relief from his illness, and he told everything to his master and to those with him.

On the Vision of Saint Nicholas

A certain warrior, a Nizhny Novgorod man, was resting from his service and from much labor and vigil, lying in his tent, wishing to sleep, and he heard someone commanding him to rise. He answered: “Do not disturb me; let me sleep, for I am weary from my watch.” And suddenly he clearly saw Saint Nicholas saying to him: “Go, tell Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan to order his army to be overshadowed with the precious cross and sprinkled with holy water, for God is delivering the city of Kazan into his hands.” He quickly ran and told this to Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan. And it came to pass as Saint Nicholas commanded.
