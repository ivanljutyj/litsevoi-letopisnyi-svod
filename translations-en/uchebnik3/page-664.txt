He entrenched himself with a great ditch and fenced himself all around with a wagon train; and the voivodes come up to his camps and kill men, while the Master fires from the camps with cannons and handguns and does not ride out to the voivodes, but presses on toward Ryngol. Yet with the voivodes there are few men, and even those are very weary; and prisoners report that the Yuryev ratmans and posadniks are in communication with the Master. It was at their summons that the Master came; the prisoners testified that the fighting men had all been sent away with the voivodes and princes, with Peter and his companions, and that those who remain are greatly exhausted.

In the same month there came from Cherkasy to the tsar and grand prince the sons of the senior prince Temryuk of Kabarda, Bulgeruk-murza and Salnuk-murza, to petition that the sovereign show favor to them, his slaves, and defend them from the Shevkal sovereign; and with them came the sovereign's envoy, the serving Tatar Baybera.

Ivan Vyrodkov wrote from Astrakhan that the sons of Prince Ismayil, Tanbay-murza and Inbay-murza, had withdrawn from him with their uluses and gone to the tsar in Crimea, and that many murzas had gone to Crimea to serve, taking their uluses with them.

And Prince Ismayil also wrote to the tsar and grand prince that his sons and many murzas with their people had gone to Crimea. And the tsar and grand prince ordered that his own cause and Ismayil's person be guarded from the Crimean tsar.

On the Baptism of the Cherkasy Princes

And the tsar and grand prince, in accordance with their petition, ordered Saltan-murza to live at his court and ordered him to be baptized, and he was named Prince Mikhail; and the sovereign ordered that he be taught letters. To Bulgar-murza the sovereign ordered it to be said that he would arrange defense for them from Shevkal as soon as there was opportunity.

On the Envoys from Yurchinch

In the same month there came to the tsar and grand prince from Yurchinch, from Tsar Khadchim, the envoy Tinisha-Zey with his companions, with gifts and a great petition for love, asking that the sovereign grant merchants the road and defend him.

On the Envoys from Bukhara

In the same month there came envoys from Bukhara, from Shamarkhan, from the Bukharan and Samarkand tsar, Azam-Azi and Shikh, with gifts and a loving petition, asking for the road for merchants and for protection.

On the Envoys to the Sovereign from Tyumen from the Tyumen Prince

In the same month there came envoys from Tyumen from the Tyumen prince with gifts and petitioned that the tsar and grand prince hold them in his subjection.
