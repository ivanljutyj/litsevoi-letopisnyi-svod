On the Tsar’s Command

The sovereign ordered that in towns and rural districts elders and hundredmen, fiftymen, and tenmen be appointed, and that a statute be established with a terrible and dread prohibition, so that they should judge robberies and plunder and all matters, so that no enmity should arise, nor any unjust bribe, nor false witness; and if they should find such among themselves, he ordered that such men be given over to punishment. And on towns and districts he imposed dues according to their trades and lands, and he ordered his clerks to collect those dues into the tsar’s treasury. The boyars, nobles, and all warriors he provided for by maintenance and just assignments, to each according to his origin and worthiness, and to the town servicemen in the fourth year he granted monetary salary.

On the Sovereign’s Review

Then the sovereign also ordered the following: for those nobles and all warriors who had become impoverished in their many lands during service, so that their service should not be disproportionate to the sovereign’s salary and their own patrimonies, the sovereign made an equalization for them: he instituted measurement of land in their estates, as was fitting for each, and arranged it so, and the surplus he divided among the needy. And from patrimonial and service estates, for proper service, he ordered as follows: from one hundred chetverts of good land, one man on horseback and in armor; and for a distant campaign, with two horses. And those who serve according to the land, the sovereign will grant them his salary and maintenance, and for the established men he gives monetary salary; but whoever holds land and does not render service from it, from those themselves money is to be taken for the men; and whoever supplies extra men, to them is greater salary from the sovereign, and to their men, compared with those established, money is to be given two and a half times more. And the sovereign arranged everything so that the organization of the army and the tsar’s service should be without falsehood and sin; and the authentic registers thereof are held by the tsar’s commanders and chancery officials.

On the Bringing in of Crimean Prisoners

In the month of March, Crimean prisoners were brought to the tsar and grand prince from Rylsk: Ataman Mikhalko Groshev had gone into the Field and defeated the Crimeans. And those prisoners said that the Crimean tsar had not released a messenger to the tsar and grand prince in exchange for his messenger Yushko Mokshev, but had committed violence against the ambassador and messenger, and was himself preparing with all his people and intended to come in early spring against the borderlands of the tsar and grand prince.
