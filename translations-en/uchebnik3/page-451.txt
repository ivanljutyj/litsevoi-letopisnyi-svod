And the Grand Prince’s commanders, by the sovereign’s command (many from many places had gone: from Ryazan, Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky Punkov; from Zaraysk, Prince Pyotr Pronskoy; from Tula, Prince Pyotr Kurakin; from Senkin Ferry, Prince Mikhailo Glinsky; from Odoev, Prince Mikhailo and Prince Alexander Vorotynsky; from Serpukhov, Prince Yuri Temkin; and many other commanders), followed them as far as the Don. And they reached the Tatar watchmen on Kulikovo Field, and the Grand Prince’s watchmen beat many Tatar watchmen, and took some captive, while others fled. And news reached the Tatars from those fugitives.

And the Crimean Tatars went swiftly, and the Grand Prince’s commanders reached the Mecha, but did not overtake them, and from there returned, and came to the Grand Prince all safe and sound, by God’s grace.

In the year 7051 from Adam (1551 from Christ)

And in September the Grand Prince sent to the Crimea the tsar’s man Tamach, and with him he sent to the tsar in the Crimea his own man Fyodor Vokshevinov with a letter. In the same month, on the 21st, Thursday, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus’ set out from Moscow to the Life-Giving Trinity in the Sergius Monastery to pray at the commemoration of the Wonderworker, and with him his brother Prince Yuri Vasilyevich; and from there he was in his villages, in Sloboda and Oleshnya and Ozeretskoye, and he came to Moscow on Tuesday, October 17.

That same autumn, on October 15, the envoys of the great sovereign Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus’, Vasily Grigoryevich Morozov and Fyodor Semyonovich Vorontsov, and the clerk Postnik Gubin, came from Sigismund, King of Poland, with a truce charter under the royal seal.

On the Wallachian Envoys

In the same month, on the 8th, Wallachian envoys came to the Grand Prince in Moscow from the voivode Ivan Stepanovich of the Krestovladovichi: Perkolag and Pyotr Korkovich Logofet, and the scribe Ivan Stepanov. The envoys petitioned the great sovereign on behalf of the voivode, asking that the great sovereign grant him aid—to ransom himself from the Turkish Sultan, who had driven him out of his Moldavian land. And the voivode had wished to come to the great sovereign in Moscow, but he could not pass: the Turkish Sultan and the Crimean Khan had ravaged his land, and he was also at enmity with the king; so he went to the Hungarian land.
