On the eighth day after the prince’s death his brother, Prince Dmitry Shemyaka, arrived; and then, having served the funeral service over the body, they placed the dead man in a hollowed log, tarred it, wrapped it in a felt blanket, and carried him to Moscow on a litter; and on the way they dropped him from the litter twice. They brought him to Moscow on the fourteenth day of October, the twenty-third day after his death, and carried him to the Church of the Archangel Michael on the square, and served the burial service over him while he still lay in the log.

After the funeral service they split the log, thinking to find only bones, but when they opened it they saw a whole body, in no way corrupted; and his burial vestments were also in the same condition as before, when they had been placed on him. His face was white, like that of a sleeping man, and showed no trace of blackness or lividness. And so, giving thanks to God, they laid the prince in a tomb beside his father, Prince Yuri Dmitrievich.

That same winter the Greek Isidore, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, returned from the pope, from the eighth council in Rome, to Rus, to Kiev, and began to be called a legate from the side of the apostle.

That same winter Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich went with his army to Novgorod, and the lord of Novgorod, Euthymius, came out to him from Novgorod, with boyars and men of property; they met the prince at Dereva near the town of Demon and petitioned the prince, concluded peace with him according to the old custom, and gave him eight thousand Novgorod rubles.

And the Pskovians, helping the grand prince, conquered much of the Novgorod land and caused many troubles and misfortunes.

At that same time the Novgorod commanders, together with the Zavolochians, reconquered the patrimony of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich and caused much harm.

That same winter Prince Yuri Vasilyevich the Elder died, and another Prince Yuri Vasilyevich was born on the twenty-second day of January; and a year before that, on the same day, the memory of the holy apostle Timothy, on the twenty-second day of January, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich was born, and Zinovy, abbot of the Trinity-Sergius Monastery, baptized him.

About Isidore

That same spring Isidore returned to the Russian land, and making his way to Moscow, he said that at this great council the Eastern churches had united with the Western; and he wished to unite Orthodoxy, the great Muscovite power of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, with Latinism, but the Lord God did not permit it.
