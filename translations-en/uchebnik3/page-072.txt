And morning came, and the sun had already risen; the Grand Prince rose and ordered Matins to be served on the 7th day of the month of July, a Wednesday.

And after Matins the Grand Prince wished to sleep a little longer, but at that hour news came to him that the Tatars were crossing the Nerl River. He began sending messengers to all the camps, and he himself at once began putting on his armor and, raising the banners, set out against the Tatars. And his cousins, Prince Ivan Andreevich Mozhaisky and Prince Mikhail Andreevich Vereisky, and Prince Vasily Yaroslavich, brother-in-law of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilievich, went after him; and all the princes, boyars, and commanders, and all the regiments went after him, having put on their armor, and all zealously set out against the Tatars. And they came out onto the field, but their host was not large, only about one and a half thousand men, because not all the regiments had yet managed to join, and Tsarevich Berdedat had not managed to come to the Grand Prince, since he had spent that night in Yuryev. And Prince Dmitry Shemyaka did not come and did not send his regiments. They came out onto the field near Efimyev Monastery on the left side, and there they met the accursed Hagarenes; their vast host appeared before the Christian regiments. They began the battle, and at first the Grand Prince's regiments began to prevail, and the Tatars fled; some Tatars our men drove off, while others fled of themselves, and other of our warriors began plundering the already slain Tatars; but the Tatars returned and fell upon the Christians, and overcame them. They captured the Grand Prince himself, as well as Prince Mikhail Andreevich, and many other princes, boyars, boyar sons, and other warriors. And Prince Ivan Andreevich was wounded several times and thrown from his horse; and when another horse was given him, he escaped. And the Grand Prince had many wounds on his head and on his arms, and his whole body was battered, for he had fought courageously. This calamity befell the Christians on the 7th day of the month of July, a Wednesday. Many Tatars were killed then as well, more than five hundred, while their total number was three and a half thousand.

The Tatars rushed in pursuit and killed and plundered many Russian warriors, burned villages, cut down people, and led many away into captivity. And the tsareviches encamped at Evfimyev Monastery, and there, having taken the Grand Prince's pectoral crosses from him, they sent them to Moscow to his mother, Grand Princess Sophia, and to his wife, Grand Princess Maria, accompanied by the Tatar Achisan.

And when he came to Moscow, everywhere arose loud weeping and wailing, not only of the grand princesses but of all Christendom.
