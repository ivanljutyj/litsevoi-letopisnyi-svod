But there occurred a most glorious miracle of the Most Holy Mother of God and through the prayers of the great wonderworkers: all that day those stonecutters worked on the church, some laying the vaults and others closing them, carrying stone, lime, and timber; many, climbing up, watched their work. An hour before sunset all the builders came down, but some were still climbing up to look. Then all had already come down in the first hour of the night, and others had come down only a fifth of an hour before the first hour; and before the first hour had struck, it fell. And one youth, the son of Prince Fyodor Pestroy, was still walking on those vaults, and when he heard the cracking and the falling of stones, he was frightened, ran in panic onto the southern wall, and after that collapse came out of the church unharmed.

After that the Grand Prince ordered the remaining vaults and pillars on the southern side, the front wall, what remained of the galleries, and the southern wall to be dismantled, because it was terrifying to enter and venerate the image of the Most Pure Mother of God and the tomb of the wonderworker Peter and the other hierarchs.

And he sends to the Roman land for master stonecutters; and he ordered others to be brought to him from his patrimony, Pskov, because those too had come from the Germans and were trained in this craft—the skill of stonecutting.

In the same year, in the month of June, envoys came from Pskov to the Grand Prince—the posadniks Ivan Agafonov, Kozma Sysoev Ledov, and Zinovy Sidorov—to petition Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, their sovereign, that he defend them from the Germans.

The Grand Prince was angry with them and did not permit them into his presence, because they had delayed coming with their petition and had, as it were, forgotten the favor of their sovereign toward them; and they brought meager gifts, not in keeping with how their sovereign favors his patrimony. And they returned to Pskov in disgrace.
