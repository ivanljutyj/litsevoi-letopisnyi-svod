Prince Vasily Feodorovich Shuisky, voivode of Pskov, together with the other posadniks and the best men, came after his envoys to the Grand Prince at the same place, at the mouth of the Shelon.

And after their arrival the Grand Prince stood there in one place for eleven days, correcting the Novgorodians. And he showed them favor, concluding peace with them according to his will, as he himself desired.

And he confirmed the agreement of the Pskovians with the Novgorodians as it had been before, as the Pskovians wished. Then the Grand Prince concluded with them peace, love, and mercy, and, having honored Theophilus, who had been named for the archbishopric, and their posadniks, tysyatskys, and others who had come with him, he sent them back to their city.

And after them he sent to Novgorod his boyar Fyodor Davydovich to bring all Great Novgorod to the kissing of the cross, from the least to the greatest, and to collect silver from them. And they, going to Novgorod, did as they had been commanded.

On the Return of the Grand Prince from Novgorod to Moscow

The God-wise zealot of piety, the praiseworthy conqueror of adversaries and gatherer of the fatherland granted to him by God, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, sovereign of Vladimir, Moscow, Novgorod, and all Rus, returned from there to Moscow with great victory on the thirteenth day of the month of August, and with him all his brothers, the princes, boyars, and all the voivodes, and all their host with great plunder.

And in that same year Grand Prince Ivan, going to Novgorod, sent Mikita Beklemishev into the Field to seek Tsarevich Murtaza, son of Mustafa, and to summon him to serve him. And Nikita met him in the Field and called him over to the Grand Prince.

And he came with him to the Grand Prince’s son in Moscow before the Grand Prince’s return from Novgorod.

On the Vyatchans

In that same year the Vyatchans, going in boats down the Volga to the lower reaches, took Sarai, took much goods, and also took a great number of captives.

When the Tatars of the Great Horde, who were encamped a day’s journey away, heard of this, a great multitude of them went out to intercept the Vyatchans, and, seizing boats, they blocked the Volga with their vessels, intending to kill them.

The Vyatchans, however, broke through them and escaped entirely.

And near Kazan they also wished to intercept them; but there too they passed by the Kazanians with everything and went away to their own land.
