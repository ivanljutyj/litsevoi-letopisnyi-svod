They had managed to leave the Lithuanian land — Prince Vasily Yaroslavich was in Mstislavl, and with him three of the Ryapolovskys, and Prince Ivan Striga, and Oshchera, and many other sons of boyars with them; and in Bryansk were Prince Semyon Obolensky, and Basenok, and many sons of boyars with them — when at that time news came to Prince Vasily in Mstislavl that the grand prince had been released and given Vologda; and Danilo Bashmak galloped in with this news. And to Bryansk there galloped to Prince Semyon a certain man from Kiev called Poltinka; and he had sat in Moscow with Princess Olelkova, keeping watch for news of the grand prince, and from Bryansk had set out for Kiev; and he told them the same news with which Bashmak had galloped in.

And Prince Vasily Yaroslavich with all the boyars and all the people, with wives and children, set out from Mstislavl; and from Bryansk Prince Semyon and Basenok likewise with all their people; and they all gathered at Patsyn. And there Dmitry, son of Andrey, brought them word that the grand prince had already gone from Vologda to Beloozero, and from there to Tver.

They set out from there all together with many people and came to Yelnya, and there Tatars met them, and they began shooting at one another.

Then the Tatars began to shout to the Russians: “Who are you?” They answered: “We are Muscovites, and we go with Prince Vasily Yaroslavich to seek our sovereign, Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich. They say he has already been released; and who are you?” The Tatars answered: “And we have come from Cherkasy with two tsareviches, the sons of Magmet, with Kaisym and with Yagup, for they heard concerning the grand prince that his brothers had committed treason against him, and they set out to seek the grand prince for his former kindness and for his bread; for much good from him had been shown to us.” And so, having joined together and made firm alliance with one another, they went together to seek the grand prince, to help him.

And Prince Dmitry Shemyaka and Prince Ivan Mozhaisky were then standing at Volok. And at that time the grand prince secretly sent his boyar Mikhail Borisovich Pleshcheev with a few people to Moscow, so that they might pass unnoticed by Prince Dmitry’s army.

And so, passing through all that army, noticed by no one, they came to Moscow on the night of the Nativity of Christ, right at Matins, and came to the Nikolsky Gate. And at that time Ulyana, the princess of Prince Vasily Vladimirovich, was riding into the city for the festival Matins, and the gates were open.

They immediately entered the city. And Prince Dmitry’s governor Fedor Galitsky fled from the Church of the Most Pure, from Matins; and Prince Ivan’s governor Vasily Cheshikha, fleeing from the city on horseback, was seized by a menial servant of the grand princess named Rastopcha, and he brought him to the commanders, and they put him in chains.
