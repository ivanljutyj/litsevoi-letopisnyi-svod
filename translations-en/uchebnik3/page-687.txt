In the same year, in August, on the second day, a Friday, Crimean people came to the borderland of the tsar and grand prince, to Potega, and at their head was Devey-Murza with Crimean people, and all of them were three thousand men; and the next day they went away.

And the voevodes of the tsar and grand prince went after them from Tula: boyar Ivan Dmitrievich Belsky, and Prince Vasily Mikhailovich Glinsky, and other voevodes. They caught up with them on the third day in the Field, on the Don, at early dawn.

And the Tatars, thinking that many regiments had come against them, cut down and abandoned the captives, and themselves fled at a run.

On the Repose of the Great Tsaritsa Anastasia

In the same year, in August, on the seventh day, a Wednesday, on the commemoration of the holy martyr Diomedes, at the fifth hour of the day, there reposed the tsaritsa and grand princess Anastasia, wife of the pious tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus.

And she was buried in the Convent of the Ascension of Christ in the Kremlin, at the Frolov Gate. She was the first Russian tsaritsa of the Muscovite state, and she lived with the tsar and grand prince for thirteen and a half years, and there remained to the tsar and grand prince from her two sons: Tsarevich Ivan, seven years old, and Tsarevich Feodor, four years old. At her burial were Macarius, Metropolitan of all Rus, and Matthew, Bishop of Krutitsy, and archimandrites, and hegumens, and the whole consecrated council; with the tsar and grand prince were his brother Prince Yuri Vasilyevich and Prince Vladimir Andreyevich, and Tsar Alexander Safa-Kireyevich, boyars and nobles. And not only a multitude of people, but all the poor and destitute from the whole city came to the burial, not for alms, but with weeping and great lamentation they accompanied her; and because of the multitude of people in the streets they could scarcely carry her body to the convent. The tsar and grand prince, from great groaning and heartfelt pity, were scarcely led by the arms.

And alms were distributed for her in churches and monasteries in the metropolitanate and archbishoprics, and in all bishoprics, not only in city churches but also throughout all districts, many thousands of rubles; and to Constantinople, and to Jerusalem, and to the Holy Mountain, and to other countries there, and to many monasteries he sent much alms. And there was great weeping for her, for she was merciful and guileless toward all.

In the same month of August, on the sixth day, the tsar and grand prince ordered a separate court to be made for his children, Tsarevich Ivan and Tsarevich Feodor, on Vzrub, behind the Great Palace on the Embankment. He also ordered a great church to be built in the tsareviches' court in the name of the Meeting of our Lord Jesus Christ, and a side chapel—a warm church of the holy venerable martyr Nikita the Stylite, Wonderworker of Pereslavl.
