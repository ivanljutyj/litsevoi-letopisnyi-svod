And all the Kazan people came to take the oath, in groups of 100, 200, and 300, and not all at once; they came to swear over three days, and they took the oath on the same terms as their notables had sworn.

On the 16th day of August the tsar set out for Kazan to take the throne, and with him, by the sovereign’s command, went his boyars: Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Golitsyn, Ivan Ivanovich Khabarov, and the clerk Ivan Grigoriev Vyrodkov. They enthroned him, and the boyars congratulated him that same day upon the sovereign’s grant of the Kazan tsardom. Together with the tsar there went to Kazan 300 Gorodets princes, murzas, and Cossacks, and 200 streltsy with two hundred-commanders of the tsar and grand prince; and they all lived at the tsar’s court.

On the following day, the 17th, the boyars Prince Yuri Golitsyn, Ivan Khabarov, and the clerk Ivan Vyrodkov came to the tsar and said: “The Kazan people swore to the sovereign that they would release the captives; therefore, sovereign, command that the captives who are in the city be gathered and released.”

And the tsar sent bailiffs and ordered all the captives to be gathered at his court; and many captives were gathered.

And that same day Tsar Shigalei handed them over to the boyars—2,700 people—and promised to release them the next day.

And the boyars sent word to the sovereign by messenger that, thank God, his task had been accomplished according to his sovereign instruction, and they sent Daniil Fedorov, son of Adashev, and the streltsy commander Ivan Cheremisinov; and they came to the sovereign on the 28th day of the same month.

And there was great joy for the sovereign at the Christian deliverance, that God had freed the Christian race from captivity and had dealt with Kazan as had not been done before under other sovereigns. And there came out 60,000 Christian captives from the Mountain Side, from the city of Kazan, and from the Kazan side; this was recorded in the town of Sviyazhsk, where they were given the sovereign’s provisions.

And up the Volga they escorted many captives to the Russian towns—Vyatka and Perm to their own places, Murom and Meshchera, Galich and Kostroma—all to their own places; each went wherever was nearer to him. Ancient grace was fulfilled: as in ancient times the Creator led the Israelite race out of Egypt by Moses, so now Christ through our Orthodox tsar led a multitude of Christian souls out of Kazan slavery.
