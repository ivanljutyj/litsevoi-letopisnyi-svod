And the emperor assigned him two thousand of his men; and they fought the Turks so bravely and manfully that all the Turks withdrew from that place and came to it no more.

Zustunei not only set his own place in order but went around all the city walls, strengthening and instructing the people not to lose hope, to trust in God, and not to grow faint in action, but to fight the infidels with all their soul and all their heart. “The Lord God will help us”—with these and many such words he taught and instructed the people, for he himself was well trained in the art of war. And all the people loved him and listened to him in all things.

The Turks, meanwhile, pressed the assault at every point, keeping the citizens sleepless by relieving one another, for they were many, as was said before.

On the thirtieth day after the first assault they rolled up cannons, guns, and many other wall-breaking devices; they had also two great cannons, which had just been cast, the ball of one as large as a knee and of the other as large as a waist. Advancing with all their forces, they began to batter the city ceaselessly from every side.

Against Zustunei they brought up a great cannon, because at that place the city wall was old and low; and when they struck that place, the wall shook; they struck a second time and knocked down some five fathoms of the wall’s top; but before a third shot night fell.

Zustunei repaired that place by night and reinforced it from within with another wall, of wood and earth; but what can one do against such force, and still more against the wrath of God for our sins?

In the morning they again began to strike the same place with many cannons and guns; and when they had battered the wall, firing from the great cannon and hoping now to destroy it, by God’s protection the ball went over the wall, catching only seven merlons, and struck the church wall and shattered to dust. And the people who were there and saw it thanked God.

Already after noon they pressed on a second time; Zustunei, having loaded his cannon, fired at the cannon, and its powder chamber burst. Seeing this, the faithless Mehmed was greatly enraged and shouted in a terrible voice: “Yagma, yagma!”—that is, “To the plundering of the city!” At once the whole army likewise shouted and advanced upon the city with all its forces by land and by sea, with all its skill, to take the city.

All the city people went up onto the walls and, resisting them, fought stoutly; only the patriarch and the hierarchs with the priestly order remained in the churches of God and prayed to the Lord God with tears and weeping.
