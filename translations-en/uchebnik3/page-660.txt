On the Envoys from Shevkal to the Sovereign

In the same month envoys came from Shevkal with gifts and a petition, asking that the tsar and grand prince protect them from his own bondsmen, the Circassian princes.

On the Arrival of a Tatar from Siberia to the Sovereign

In the month of November, a serving Tatar, Sobana Rizanov, and an envoy from the Siberian prince Ediger arrived from Siberia with tribute, with sables and squirrels, and said that they would come in the winter with the long-standing tribute.

On the Dispatch to the Sovereign from the Commanders in the German Land

In the same month the commanders, Prince Mikhail and his companions, wrote that the master had sent his brother Johann Kettler for provisions and had sent many German men with them as a guard.

And the commanders came to the outpost and routed the German outpost, and captured the master’s brother Johann Kettler, and the master’s armourer, and the master of the horse, and took many petty princes and manor lords prisoner; and in that battle they took one hundred and six men alive, and in many campaigns they beat the master’s men and took two hundred sixty men in all the campaigns and killed about three thousand, and all the prisoners said that the master was battering down the city wall, and that no gunpowder for the cannon remained in the city.

On the Dispatch to the Sovereign from Yuryev from Prince Dmitry Kurlyatev

In the same month Prince Dmitry Kurlyatev wrote from Yuryev that the master had taken the city of Ryngol, having stood before it for six weeks. And Prince Mikhail Repnin also wrote that the master had taken Ryngol and had come against the commanders, and had trampled the commanders, Prince Mikhail and his companions, and killed boyar sons and two of the boyars’ men, and captured Ivan Likov and Fedor Pisessky alive; and the master went to Kes, leaving Ryngol; and the common people again joined the tsar and grand prince.
