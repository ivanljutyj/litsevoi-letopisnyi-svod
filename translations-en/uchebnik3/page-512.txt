He inspects the places where the warriors are to assemble and gather a host against the godless; and the sovereign orders the voivodes in the regiments, in the great regiment and in the others, also to prepare for battle and to review the warriors. The sovereign favors the voivodes and boyars' sons and strengthens them with a word, so that they should not be put to shame before the Hagarenes, and says: "The Hagarenes have no God and expect no recompense; but we have our Master, the Lord God: if we suffer for His name, we shall be crowned with martyrs' crowns; the time is at hand for us to stand firm manfully in the name of the Holy Trinity and for our own brothers, the Orthodox Christians." The voivodes and boyars' sons, strengthened in mind and rejoicing in hope, seeing their pure sovereign, who favors them, preparing for battle and strengthened by grace, all with one mouth answer the sovereign: "We are ready, sovereign, to suffer for the Christian faith and for you, the sovereign, even unto death."

The sovereign arrived at Kolomna; the voivodes of his regiment meet the tsar and tell the sovereign that in his regiment all the men are ready to march against the adversaries; the sovereign tells them where he has chosen a place for the muster against the Crimean Tsar, and also concerning the voivodes and boyars' sons, that they answered the sovereign wisely.

The sovereign sends to Moscow to his tsaritsa and to his father Macarius, Metropolitan of All Rus, and writes to him thus:

Letter to the Metropolitan

"To the Most Reverend Father Macarius, Metropolitan of All Rus, Tsar and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich of All Rus beats his brow. We have taken counsel with you that the Crimean Tsar is marching against our borderlands, and that we, having called God to our help, should ourselves go to deliver the Christians, and that you should bless us. And now we have come to Kolomna, and our scouts have come to us and say: they have seen many men coming to conquer our land. And we, placing our hope in God and in His Most Pure Mother of God and the great wonderworkers, and trusting in your holy prayers, wish to stand against them. And do you, our father, pray to God and to His Most Pure Mother of God and the great wonderworkers, both in assembly and privately, for our sin and for all Orthodoxy, that the Merciful God, through your holy prayers, may not remember our sin before Himself, may not deliver us to foreigners, and may God grant us courage and bravery, and chastity, and concord to all Orthodox, since Christ settles like-minded people in His house. And may you be pleased also in all holy places, in monasteries and cathedrals, to order them to strive (rise up) in prayer and fasting for this present time."
