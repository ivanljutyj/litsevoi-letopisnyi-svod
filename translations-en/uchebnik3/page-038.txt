...to judge truly and express a good thought, since it was you who summoned the pious Tsar Ivan, son of Manuel, and the most holy ecumenical Patriarch Joseph, and all the Eastern metropolitans and bishops, and from everywhere a multitude of Orthodox Christians, honorable and ecumenical councils, and honorable archimandrites, abbots, priests, a multitude of monks, refined and wise philosophers, and virtuous monks, and assembled a many-membered council. Establish then the right and truth concerning God; let not the labor of our assembly be in vain; let not the prophet David speak of us: “Why do the nations rage and the people imagine vain things; the kings of the earth set themselves together against the Lord and against His Christ.” Beware of this; let never the peoples faithful to Orthodoxy hear that you have abandoned the truth and accepted what is flattering and false, and then they will laugh at your false assembly. But if you do not wish to establish the truth, and do not walk in the way of God’s truth, then you yourselves know how the prophet speaks of such: “If you are unwilling and will not listen to me, you shall receive the sword, for the mouth of the Lord has spoken this.” With such gracious words Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, humbly addressed those assembled. And then Julian, Cardinal of Rome, rose from his place and went to Pope Eugene, angered at this, being unable to hear such words from Metropolitan Mark; and he said to Pope Eugene: “Do you hear, honored pope, holy teacher, what blasphemy Mark brings against you? He calls your books false, and does not wish to take part in your council, and does not wish to commemorate you, the pope, first, and does not wish to recognize the eighth council, but follows the seven councils and teaches about them.”

Pope Eugene, hearing such words from him, was greatly troubled and began to speak thus to the Greek Tsar Ivan and to the ecumenical Patriarch Joseph: “Do you hear, Tsar Ivan, and you, Patriarch Joseph, what blasphemy Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, brings against me, and how he reviles my books?” Then the tsar answered him: “We heard no blasphemy from Mark, but Mark spoke everything rightly before us.”

And then Pope Eugene of Rome, vexing Mark, ordered many books to be brought.

But the most blessed Metropolitan of Ephesus, Mark, being moved in heart within himself and inflamed in soul by the thought of their Latin falsehood, seeing God’s people confused and drawing near to the temptation of their heresy, began to speak harshly to them: “O Latins! O Latins! If you do not renounce your madness, speaking unrighteously about the seven councils of the holy fathers, the ecumenical teachers, and the holy seven most blessed popes, let it be known to you that from this hour you will soon cease uttering your foolish false speeches.” And in that same hour Mark answered a certain man who was speaking maliciously: “Get out of here, do not stay here!” And then a certain monk quickly went out.
