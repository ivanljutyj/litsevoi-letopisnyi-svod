No princely authority had been over them, and therefore the crafty had grown accustomed to betray first, and to deceive, and they lived from autumn through winter and on into spring. In summer they remained without fear because their land was flooded. But now, by God’s providence, for their punishment and correction, their land has dried up so much that not a drop of rain fell from the heavens onto their land throughout this whole summer, from the month of May to the month of September. And from the sun’s heat all their land and marshes dried up: such grace did God from above bestow upon his pious servant, the great prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilyevich, humbling his enemies under his strong hand.

Everywhere and in all places his regiments, restrained by nothing, ranged throughout the whole land of Novgorod, and warred without hindrance, and drove cattle from everywhere through formerly impassable places and marshes, but now dry land.

Victory over the Novgorodians at Korostyn, in Rusa, and on the Shelon

When the people of Novgorod heard that the great prince himself was coming against them with a great force, having occupied their whole Novgorod land from end to end, with the great and dreadful menace of his sword and fire, as in ancient times it was said by Peremniy concerning Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon: “At the thunder of his ranging chariots and at the neighing of his horses the earth shook,” so here too God showed the fury and terror of the great prince against the crafty Novgorodians. But they deceitfully sent to him with supplication, asking for assurances of safety, while doing everything with lies and injustice.

And at the same time they sent their host in boats across Lake Ilmen against the forward regiments, against the great prince’s commanders, who had come to Korostyn and were standing on the shore by Lake Ilmen.

And suddenly, again secretly, the host sent from Novgorod in boats came across the lake against the great prince’s commanders.

And having come out of the boats, they approached their camps stealthily. And at that time the careless guard of the great prince’s commanders, seeing them, informed the commanders.

They, arming themselves at once, went against them and fought with them. And God helped the great prince’s commanders, and many Novgorodians were killed, and others were taken captive.

And those captured were ordered to cut off their own noses and lips, and they were released back to Novgorod; and their armor was stripped off and thrown into the water, and some consigned to fire, for they had no need of it, since they had armor enough of their own.

And from there they returned again to Rusa; on that same day another host, larger than the first, came to Rusa; they came by river as well, in boats, along the Pola and the Ilmen.
