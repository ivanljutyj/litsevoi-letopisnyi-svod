Calling God to help (and the priests serving in the altar were performing the liturgy with tears).

Then one of those close to the tsar came and said to him: “Sovereign, it is time for you to go, so that those fighting the infidels may see you; many regiments await you.” The tsar answered him: “If we wait until the end of the singing, may we receive perfect mercy from Christ.”

Soon a second message came from the city: it was time for the tsar to go, so that the soldiers might be strengthened by seeing the tsar. The tsar, sighing from the depth of his heart and shedding many tears, said: “Do not forsake me, O Lord my God, and do not depart from me; heed my plea for help.”

And he came up to the icon of the wonderworker Sergius, and venerated it, and lovingly kissed the icon, and said: “O servant of Christ, help, help us by your prayers!” And he partook of holy water, having tasted the gifts and the bread of the Mother of God, and the liturgy ended.

And his spiritual father, the distinguished Protopope Andrew, blessed him with the life-giving cross.

The Tsar Goes to His Regiment

The tsar comes out of the church, armed with prayer, and, turning to his pilgrims, said: “Bless me (to suffer for Orthodoxy) and forgive me, and you pray to God without ceasing, and help us by prayer.”

And the sovereign sets his foot into the battle stirrup, mounts his horse, and quickly rode to his regiment, toward the city. And the sovereign sees: the Christian banners are already on the city walls.

And the autocrat arrived at his regiment and sends messengers in all directions, strengthening the soldiers.

And all the people saw that the sovereign had drawn near to them and was fighting manfully and bravely with them, and at once from all sides, as if on wings, they flew up onto the city walls, and also from the walls into the city, fighting fiercely in every place.

Battle in the City of Kazan

Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky reports to the sovereign that, by God’s mercy, the tsar’s men are fighting the infidels in the city, and the sovereign’s help is needed.

The tsar sends the commanders of his royal regiment and orders them to dismount and help their own, for it is impossible on horseback: the crowding in the city is great between the houses, and also from the multitude of people.
