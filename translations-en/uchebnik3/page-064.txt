Rule 13 of the same council

Since we know that in the canon of the Roman Church it is said that those who wish to be ordained to the rank of deacon or priest are obliged no longer to have intercourse with their wives, we, according to the ancient canon of the apostolic institution and order established by the holy fathers, say that lawful cohabitation should remain inviolate, neither dissolving their union with their wives nor depriving them of shared life at the proper time. If anyone worthy of ordination to the rank of subdeacon, deacon, or priest is found, cohabitation with lawful spouses shall not be an obstacle to his elevation to such a degree, and at the time of ordination he must not give an obligation that he will renounce his lawful wife, so that in this way marriage, established by God and blessed by His coming, may not be insulted. The voice of the Divine Gospel says: “What God has joined together, let no man put asunder”; and the apostle teaches: “Marriage is honorable and the bed undefiled”; also: “He who is joined to a wife, let him not seek separation.” We know also that those who assembled in council at Carthage, caring for the purity of the life of the clergy, decided that subdeacons who touch the sacred mysteries, and deacons, and priests should abstain from marital intercourse at the necessary time. And the apostolic tradition, handed down also from ancient customs, we likewise observe, knowing the time for every thing, but especially for fasting and prayer. For it is fitting for those who stand before the divine altar, at the time when they approach the sanctuary, to abstain in all things, so that they may obtain what they ask of God.

If anyone dares, acting contrary to the church and apostolic rules, to deprive any priest, deacon, or subdeacon of union and communion with his lawful wife, he shall be deposed. Likewise, if a priest or deacon drives away his wife under the pretext of reverence, he shall be excommunicated. Interpretation. If in the Roman Church anyone who wishes to become a priest or deacon, according to the established rules, must before his ordination renounce married life with his wife, this contradicts the Gospel and apostolic testament, and it also contradicts the present rule, since this rule permits priests, deacons, or subdeacons to live together with their wives, but they must abstain from intercourse with their wives at the necessary time, that is, during fasting, when they touch the holy things, when they perform the divine service, so that they may obtain from God what they ask.
