And the Tatars stayed in Suzdal three days and went to Vladimir, and, having crossed the Klyazma, stood opposite the city, but they were not allowed into the city, and they went to Murom, and from there to Nizhny Novgorod.

On the Moscow Fire

In the same month, on the 14th day of July, on a Wednesday, at night Moscow caught fire from within and burned entirely, so that not a single tree remained in the city, and the stone churches collapsed, and the city’s stone walls were destroyed in many places. And a vast number of people burned: hieromonks and priests, monks and nuns, men, women, and children, because within the city the fire spread everywhere, and to go beyond the city walls was to fear the Tatars. Much of the treasury burned, and a vast quantity of all kinds of goods, since at that time many people from other cities were under siege in Moscow. And when the city had burned, Grand Princess Sophia and Grand Princess Maria with their children and their boyars went to the city of Rostov, while the townspeople were in great grief and turmoil; those who were able to flee abandoned the city and wished to flee; but the common people, gathering, began to restore the city gates, and those wishing to flee from the city they began to seize, beat, and put in chains. And so the turmoil subsided, and all together began to fortify the city and to build household additions for themselves. In the same year, after the Dormition, on the 25th day of August, Tsar Ulu-Makhmet with his children and with all his Horde went from Novgorod to Kurmysh, and they took the Grand Prince with them, as well as Prince Mikhail; and to Prince Dmitry Shemyaka he sent his envoy Bigich. He was glad of this and showed him many honors, because he wished to obtain the grand principality.

He sent him back with evil to the Grand Prince; and with him he sent his envoy, the clerk Fyodor Dubensky, so that the Grand Prince would not come into the grand principality. But before they reached the Tatars, while they were still in Murom, the Almighty, Merciful, and Good Lover of Mankind God, seeing the cruelty of Dmitry Shemyaka, that he had conceived evil thoughts and fratricide against his lord, inclined the hearts of the godless and lawless Christian-killing Tatars to meekness: they considered their envoy killed by Shemyaka.

On the Release of the Grand Prince from Kurmysh

In the year 6954 from Adam (1454 from Christ), Tsar Ulu-Makhmet and his son Mamutyak showed favor to the Grand Prince: they made him swear on the cross that he would pay for himself a ransom as much as he could, and they released him from Kurmysh on the Protection of the Most Holy Mother of God, on the first day of October, and with him Prince Mikhail and all the rest who were with them. And they sent with them their envoys—many princes with many people: Prince Seit-Asan and Utesh, and Kuraish, and Dylkhozya, and Adar, and many others.
