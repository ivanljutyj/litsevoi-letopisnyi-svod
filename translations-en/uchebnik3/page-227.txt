The Grand Prince went to Kolomna, and he himself stood at Kolomna, while he placed his son, Grand Prince Ivan, at Serpukhov; and Prince Andrei Vasilievich the Younger at Tarusa, while the other princes and voivodes took their places at other points along the bank of the Oka.

Tsar Akhmat heard that the Grand Prince stood on the bank with all his forces, and he went toward the Lithuanian land, bypassing the Oka River and awaiting the king and his forces to come to his aid; and guides led him to the fords of the Ugra River.

The Grand Prince sent his son and his brother and his voivodes to the Ugra with all his forces; and having come, they stood on the Ugra and occupied the fords and ferries.

And the Grand Prince himself rode from Kolomna to Moscow to the All-Merciful Savior and the Most Pure Mother of God and to all the wonderworkers, asking help and intercession for Orthodox Christianity, and for counsel and deliberation to his father Metropolitan Gerontius, and to his mother Grand Princess Marfa, and to his uncle Mikhail Andreevich, and to his spiritual father Archbishop Vassian of Rostov, and to all his boyars: for all were then under siege in Moscow.

And they implored him earnestly to stand firmly for Orthodox Christianity against the infidel.

The Grand Prince heeded their entreaty, and having taken blessing, he went to the Ugra; and having come, he stood at Kremenets with small forces, and sent all the people to the Ugra.

At that time in Moscow his mother the Grand Princess, and Metropolitan Gerontius, and Archbishop Vassian, and Paisius the abbot of the Trinity entreated the Grand Prince on behalf of his brothers; and the Grand Prince accepted their entreaty and commanded his mother the Grand Princess to send for them, promising to show them favor.

And the Grand Princess sent to them, bidding them go straight to the Grand Prince to his aid as quickly as possible.

And the Tsar with all his Tatars went through the Lithuanian land past Mtsensk, Lyubutsk, and Odoev; and having come, he stood near Vorotynsk, awaiting help from the king.

But the king did not go to him and did not send an army, for he had his own internal strife; at that time the Perekop Tsar Mengli-Girei was ravaging the king's Podolian land, serving the Grand Prince.

Akhmat came to the Ugra with all his forces, intending to cross the river; and the Tatars began shooting at our men, and ours at them; and some came against Prince Andrei, others against the Grand Prince, and others suddenly advanced against the voivodes.
