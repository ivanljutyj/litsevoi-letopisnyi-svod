And the tsar postponed the assault, ordered the siege towers and artillery to be burned, and himself departed from the city on Saturday, the Feast of the Transfiguration.

The Grand Prince’s voivodes came to the city, but the tsar, as became known, had hastened away from the city with all his men.

And the voivodes set out after the tsar at once, and came to the Don, but the tsar had already crossed the Don.

And the voivodes sent a few men after the tsar, while the voivodes themselves returned to Moscow to the tsar, the Grand Prince, from Kolomna, all safe and sound.

Meanwhile the sovereign had equipped a large army for Kazan — the men who were in Vladimir and Murom — but they did not arrive in time.

And there was great joy in Moscow then, and the sovereign rewarded the boyars and voivodes with his great largesse, with fur coats and goblets.
