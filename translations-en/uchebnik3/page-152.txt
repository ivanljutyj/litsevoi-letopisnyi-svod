The commanders of the grand prince attacked those men too, beat them, and sent Timofei Zamytsky to the grand prince with this news; and he raced to the grand prince at Lake Kolma on the ninth day of July.

And the commanders themselves went from Rusa to the town of Demon.

The grand prince sent to them from Lake Kolma, ordering them to go beyond the Shelon River to join the Pskovians; and under Demon he ordered Prince Mikhail Andreevich with his son Prince Vasily and all his warriors to stand.

And the commanders of the grand prince went to the Shelon; and when they came to the bank of that river where they were to cross it, at that time against them came a great multitude of the Novgorod army from the other side, from their city, to that same Shelon River, so that the regiments of the grand prince were terrified, because they were few, for all the warriors who were with them did not see this, fighting in their places around Novgorod.

And the accursed traitors, the Novgorod posadniks, tysyatskys, boyars, merchants, and all kinds of craftsmen, simply speaking, carpenters and potters, and all the city people joined the army; and the rest, who had never in their lives been on a horse, who had never even thought of raising a hand against the grand prince, all those the traitors drove out by force; and those who did not want to go to that battle, they themselves robbed and beat, and others they threw into the Volkhov River. They themselves said that forty thousand of them were beaten in that battle. And they went swiftly with great force against those same commanders of the grand prince, against the forward regiment, against Prince Mikhail Kholmsky and Fyodor Davydovich. And the commanders of the grand prince, though there were few of them—as those who were there say, there were only five thousand of them—although they saw the great multitude of the enemy, were not afraid, but hoping in the Lord God and His most pure Mother and in the justice of their sovereign, they went swiftly against them, roaring like lions, across that great river; across it, the Novgorodians themselves say, there had never even been a ford, but these men, not knowing the ford, all crossed it whole and healthy.

Seeing this, the Novgorodians were stirred up and wavered like drunk men; and the prince’s men hastened to attack them, having begun shooting before them, and their horses were frightened under them, and they began to throw them off.

And soon they fled, driven by the wrath of God for their injustice and for their apostasy not only from their sovereign but from the Lord God Himself. And the regiments of the grand prince pursued them, stabbing and cutting them down; and they themselves fled, each as he could, striking and trampling one another.

And there were beaten then, as they said, a great multitude. They themselves said that twelve thousand of them perished in those battles, and more than two thousand were taken alive.
