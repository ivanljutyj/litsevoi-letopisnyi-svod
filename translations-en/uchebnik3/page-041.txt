All the Lithuanian land and the Russian cities of the Lithuanian realm gathered and elected as their great prince the king’s son Casimir, son of Jagiello, grandson of Olgerd, great-grandson of Gedimin, and quietly and peacefully seated him in Vilna upon the great principality of Lithuania.

In that same autumn the tsar of the Great Horde, Makhmet, killed his senior Horde prince Mansup, and many Tatars were killed then in the Horde; not only there, but in other hordes as well, rebellion and internal strife began, internecine wars and battles.

The Tsar, the Patriarch, and Metropolitan Isidore Set Off for Florence

In that same winter there arrived in the city of Florence the Greek tsar kyr Ivan Kaluyan Manuilovich of Constantinople, the ecumenical patriarch Joseph of Constantinople, and many other metropolitans and bishops, and also Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, who was of one mind with them in committing their evil heresies, as they dreamed of deceiving Mark and drawing him to their side.

Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, said to the tsar and patriarch: “Do not go to the Latin city of Florence, for you will not be able to fulfill the desire you dream of; but as God wills, so shall it be.” They did not listen to him and went, and on that forced journey Pope Eugene imposed his will upon them. Mark said to them: “Did I not tell you that the Latins are deceitful, and that whatever they say is all lies?” Then Tsar Ivan himself, together with Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, began often to go to the pope and bring the matter to completion; they began to gather by themselves, hoping to accomplish their will over Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus. But he, in conversation with them, uttered harsh words, calling them all Latins—the tsar, the patriarch, and all the metropolitans, the Greek bishops and their philosophers, and also Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia, who was of one mind with them—and boldly, without any restraint, rebuked them all and called them Latins.

The tsar and patriarch turned to him, saying: “O Mark! If you spoke harshly there, speak humbly here.” Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, answered them: “Even now, tsar and patriarch, I will not listen to you, but what God wills, that will He do.” Many times they gathered without him, but were unable to accomplish anything.
