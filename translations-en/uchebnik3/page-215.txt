Prince Ivan Ivanovich Slykh with that news that he had brought his patrimony, Novgorod the Great, fully under his will and had established himself there as sovereign, as in Moscow. And he arrived in Moscow on the 27th day of the same month.

On the 21st day of the same month all the Novgorod boyars, the prosperous townspeople, and the merchants presented many gifts to the Grand Prince.

On the 22nd day of the same month the Grand Prince sent to Novgorod his lieutenants, his boyar Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Striga and his brother Yaroslav, and ordered them to take up residence at his court of Grand Prince Yaroslav; but the Grand Prince himself did not go to Novgorod, because there was a plague among them then.

On January 29, the commemoration of the Hieromartyr Ignatius the God-bearer, on Thursday of Cheese-fare week, the Grand Prince entered Novgorod the Great, and with him his brothers Prince Andrei the Elder and Prince Boris, and Prince Andrei the Younger, and Prince Vasily Vereisky.

They bowed to Holy Sophia, the Wisdom of God, and he heard Mass at Holy Sophia with his brothers; and after the liturgy he went out of the city to his estate at Paozerie,

and dined at his place, and all his brothers were with him; also the Archbishop, many Novgorod boyars, and prosperous townspeople; and he drank with them. And before the meal the Archbishop presented gifts to the Grand Prince: a Sizov panagia set in gold with pearls, a goblet, an ostrich egg bound in silver, a carnelian cup bound in silver, a crystal flagon bound in silver, a silver bowl weighing 12 grivnas, and 200 gold korabelniks.

On February 1, Sunday, the Cheese-fare leave-taking, the Grand Prince ordered the merchant elder Mark Pamfilyev to be taken; and he was seized in the city.

On February 2, the Meeting of the Lord, on the night from Sunday to Monday, Grand Princess Maria, wife of Vasily Vasilyevich, took monastic vows as a nun that night at her court in Moscow; and the abbot of Kirillov Monastery tonsured her; and she was given the name Martha.

On the same day, Monday, in Novgorod, in the first week of Lent, the Grand Prince ordered the Novgorod boyar woman Marfa Isakova and her grandson Vasily Fedorov, son of Isak, to be seized.

On February 3, Tuesday of the same first week of Lent, the Grand Prince ordered his lieutenant Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Striga to take from the Novgorodians their treaty charters, so that they should have no treaties with the Lithuanian grand princes and the king; and Prince Ivan took those treaty charters from them and brought them to the Grand Prince.

On February 5, Thursday, the Grand Prince installed two other lieutenants in Novgorod, Vasily Kitay and Ivan Zinovyevich.
