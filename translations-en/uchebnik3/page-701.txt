Simeon, Bishop of Smolensk and Bryansk, Philotheus, Bishop of Ryazan and Murom, Barlaam, Bishop of Kolomna and Kashira, Matthew, Bishop of Sarsk and Podonsk, Joasaph, Bishop of Perm and Vologda, set their hands to this charter. But the Archbishop of Kazan had died at that time, and the hand of Acacius, Bishop of Tver and Kashin, is absent because, due to great old age and illness, he did not come to the council, but he wrote that he was of one mind with the archbishops and bishops and with the whole consecrated council. And this charter of our royal dignity was proclaimed and written in the city of Moscow in the year 7072 from Adam (the year 1572 from Christ) in the month of February on the 7th day, in the thirtieth year of our reign, in the eighteenth year of our Russian realm, the thirteenth of Kazan, the tenth of Astrakhan.

In the same month of February, on the 17th day, there arrived to the tsar and grand prince from Crimea, from Tsar Devlet-Girey, the sovereign’s messenger Elizar Devyatov, son of Rzhevsky; the sovereign had sent him to Tsar Devlet-Girey with a letter and to his ambassador Afanasy Nagoy with an instruction. And he ordered Afanasy to negotiate with the tsar about friendship and brotherhood and to wage war together against his enemy the Lithuanian king. And Tsar Devlet-Girey together with Elizar sent his courier Ashchibash Frukh with a letter. And he wrote that he, Tsar Devlet-Girey, and his children, Kalga Magmet-Girey Tsarevich and Aldy-Girey Tsarevich, had given an oath to the tsar and grand prince before his ambassador Afanasy Fedorovich Nagoy and before his messenger Elizar Rzhevsky, that they would be with the tsar and grand prince in friendship and brotherhood; and that the tsar and grand prince should administer an oath to him before the metropolitan and before his loyal man Ashchibash Frukh. And to accomplish the great good deed, the tsar sends to the tsar and grand prince his great ambassador Murat-Murza, son of Prince Sulesh.

And the tsar and grand prince sent to meet the Crimean ambassador Yanboldy and his companions in Kaluga Ivan Semenov, son of Cheremisinov Karaulov, and clerk Ivan Reutov, and ordered them to come to him in Moscow. And Yanboldy with his companions stood in Kaluga, waiting from Crimea from Tsar Devlet-Girey for an exchange for other ambassadors. And the ambassador of the tsar and grand prince, Fedor Ignatiev, son of Saltykov, also stood with the Crimean ambassador Yanboldy in Kaluga with the Crimean message.

In the same month of February, on the 23rd day, the Swedish King Erik sent his messenger Sven Andreev to the tsar and grand prince with a letter. And he wrote to the tsar and grand prince that, according to the safe-conduct letter of the tsar and grand prince, which the tsar and grand prince had sent to him with his courier Inrik Matveev, he had intended to send to the tsar and grand prince his chosen ambassador, but winter came and through the sea strait there was no ship route.
