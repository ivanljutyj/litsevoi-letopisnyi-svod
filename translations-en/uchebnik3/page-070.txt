They burned the faces of many martyrs in the following way. They prepared an iron plate and inscribed on it the following verses:

Verses of Emperor Theophilus

“To everyone wishing to come to this city, that is, Jerusalem, where the most pure feet of God’s Word stood for the resurrection of the world, there appeared in this honorable place wicked vessels of evil deceit.” Interpretation. “Wicked vessels of evil deceit” he calls Saints Michael, Theophanes, Theodore, and Job. The following: “By the same impiety, with impious reasoning, they committed foul and evil deeds, and as apostates they were driven far away from the city of Jerusalem. Having come to this sovereign city of Constantine, they did not abandon their evil pride; therefore their faces were sealed like the faces of criminals, and they are condemned and driven away from here.”

And so with an iron inscription he burned the faces of Saints Theodore and Theophanes and sent them into confinement. The saints bore their wounds on their faces like a shining crown with pearls and precious stones. And setting out into exile, they met a certain man fishing, and they wrote to the great Methodius, Patriarch of Constantinople, who was in prison, since he too was being held in prison in exile, a short letter in the Amorian cipher, in which it was said: “To the living dead man and the life-bearing dead man, who lives on earth and wishes to reach heaven, the bound write to the bound, the imprisoned to the imprisoned.”

Saint Methodius, having received the letter, read it, wept, and wrote to them, saying: “What is this, if in the heavenly books, by the name written on their faces, they are chastely wounded, and the living is buried as an ally with the living buried?” And while in confinement, the great Theodore departed to the Lord; at the hour of his death angelic singing was heard, and together with the angels’ singing his voice was also heard. But blessed Methodius, Patriarch of Constantinople, suffered bitterly in confinement, in a dark cave; his teeth were knocked out and his jaws broken; he endured many other torments, with which the impious inhumanly tortured him, as though hard iron were being struck with smiths’ hammers. Theophanes remained in exile until the death of Emperor Theophilus. Through their own fault Saint Michael, protosyncellus of the holy great Church of Jerusalem, with his disciples Theophanes, Theodore, and Job, did not come to Rome. But Pope Leo of Rome, seeing God’s people perishing from the aforementioned heretics of Prince Charles, was perplexed and grieved, and therefore wrote on tablets the confession of the Orthodox faith, namely “I believe in one God,” and set it on the wall in the church, so that those entering might confess the Son, begotten of the Father, and the Holy Spirit, proceeding from the Father. He did this to strengthen the faithful and to expose the heretics of Prince Charles.
