The hirelings of those traitors began to throw stones at those who wished to side with the grand prince. And great turmoil began among them, and they fought among themselves, rising against one another.

Many of them, the old posadniks, tysyatskys, leading men, and also wealthy townspeople, said to them: “It cannot be, brothers, as you say: that we give ourselves to the king and have an archbishop appointed by his Latin metropolitan. For from the beginning we are the patrimony of those great princes, from our first great prince Rurik, whom our land by its own will took from the Varangians as prince for itself together with his two brothers. Then his great-grandson Grand Prince Vladimir was baptized and baptized all our lands, both the Russian and our Slovenian, the Merya, the Krivichian Ves, called Belozersk, Murom, and the Vyatichi, and others. And from that holy and great Prince Vladimir even to this our lord Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich we have never been under the Latins, nor have we appointed an archbishop from them for ourselves, as now you wish to appoint an archbishop from Gregory, who calls himself Metropolitan of Rus, for he is a disciple of Isidore, in essence a Latin.” Those troublemakers, like the former heretics taught by the devil, wishing to have their way with Grand Prince Ivan and with the father, and daring against piety, did not want to submit to the grand prince. Some shouted: “We want to be with the king!”; and others cried: “We want to be with Moscow, with his Orthodox Metropolitan Philip!”

These evildoers stood against Orthodoxy, did not fear God, and sent their envoy to the king with many gifts, Panfil Selifontov and Kiril Ivanov, son of Makarin, with the words: “Free people of Great Novgorod bow before you, honorable king, that you may become sovereign of our Great Novgorod and lord over us, and command your metropolitan Gregory to appoint an archbishop for us, and give us a prince from your domain.” The king received their gifts with love and was glad of their words, and after showing them many honors he sent their envoy back to them with all that they asked, and sent to them as prince Mikhail Olelkov, son of the Prince of Kiev.

The Novgorodians received him honorably, but they did not drive the grand prince’s governors from Gorodishche; and Prince Vasily Gorbaty, brother of the Suzdal princes, who was with them, they sent to Zavolochye to the outpost on the Dvina.
