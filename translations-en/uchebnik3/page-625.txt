Ivan and Mikhail wrote that Yusuf’s sons, Yunus-Murza and Alay, had acknowledged their dependence before Prince Ismail, made peace with Ivan and Mikhail, and sworn an oath to the tsar and sovereign that they would serve the tsar and grand prince as they served Prince Ismail, remain steadfast until death, nomadize near Astrakhan, and plot no evil.

And Ivan and Mikhail gave them vessels so that they would have something to sail to Ismail and to sustain themselves on the Volga; and Yunus-Murza with his brothers came against Tsar Derbysh and drove him out, and the cannons that the Crimean tsar had sent to Derbysh Yunus seized and sent to the town to Ivan and Mikhail. And Tsar Derbysh fled to Azov, and from there to Mecca.

And the Astrakhan commoners came to Ivan and Mikhail, petitioned, and swore to the tsar and sovereign that the sovereign would show them favor, order them to live as of old by the city of Astrakhan and pay tribute, and forgive their guilt and not order them executed: for they were common people, and the tsar and princes had compelled them by force; and many other Astrakhan people had been scattered by the Nogais at the time when they fled from the tsar and grand prince.

And Ivan with Mikhail also wrote that messages had come to them from the tsars of Shamakhi, Shevkal, and Tyumen concerning peace and trade, and they sent service Tatars to them according to the instruction of the sovereign tsar and grand prince.

On the Letter from Vyborg to Glinsky

In the same month the ambassadors of the Swedish king wrote from Vyborg to Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich Glinsky: Prince Sten Erisoyevich of Grentsnasha, ruler of the Smolensk land and governor of Enikupenga, and Lavrenty Petrovich, Archbishop of Uppsala, and Master Mikhail Agricola, Bishop of Abo, and Panteley Gulenti of Potorpna, and Knut Knutovich Nabykny, and the scribe Olovey Lavrentyevich. They came to Vyborg on December 1 and are heading to the tsar and sovereign grand prince from King Gustav, and Prince Mikhail should send them good boyar sons as hostages in exchange.

Earlier Ivan, the king’s son, had written from Vyborg about the same. Then the tsar and grand prince ordered Prince Mikhail to write to the king that between sovereigns ambassadors first travel by safe-conduct letters, and a safe conduct for his ambassadors had been sent from the tsar and sovereign to the king, according to which he should send them with a petition, and hostages are not customary in embassies.

On the Release of the Crimean Courier

In January the tsar and grand prince released the courier of the Crimean tsar Devlet-Girey, Karadzhan with his companions, and sent his own courier to the tsar: the service Tatars Syuyunduk Tusupov with his companions; and the Crimean tsar wrote to the tsar that he wished to be in firm friendship with the tsar and grand prince.
