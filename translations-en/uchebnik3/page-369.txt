The Grand Prince set out and halted at Kolomenskoye, waiting for Prince Andrey Ivanovich, his brother, and for the voivodes with many men.

And on that same day news came to the Grand Prince at Kolomenskoye from the voivodes at Ryazan that Tsar Safa-Girey and Tsar Islam, and other tsareviches with many men, had come to Ryazan and burned the suburbs.

And the Grand Prince immediately sent to the voivodes, ordering them to send across the Oka River to obtain captives for questioning.

And the voivodes, according to the order of the great sovereign, sent across the Oka River the voivode Prince Ivan Fedorovich Obolensky Ovchina; and to another place the voivodes sent Prince Dmitry, son of Prince Fyodor Paletsky; and to a third place the voivodes sent Prince Dmitry, son of Prince Yury Drutsky, with a few men.

The voivode Prince Ivan Fedorovich, by God’s mercy and that of His most pure Mother and through the prayers of the holy wonderworkers of Russia Peter and Alexis, intercessors of the Russian land, overtook the Tatars, killed many, and took others alive.

And Prince Dmitry Paletsky and Prince Dmitry Drutsky also killed many Tatars, took others alive and sent them to the voivodes, and the voivodes sent those Tatars to the Grand Prince at Kolomenskoye.

Seeing this, the enemies of God, the traitors and oath-breakers Tsar Sahib-Girey and Tsar Islam, by God’s help, which exalts the hand of the great sovereign Vasily Ivanovich, quickly fled away from the protected places of the Grand Prince.

And the voivodes sent word to the Grand Prince that the tsareviches had fled back to their own lands.

And the Grand Prince, hearing this, returned to the reigning city of Moscow on August 21 and ordered his voivodes, Prince Dmitry and the others, to follow him to Moscow.

On the Great Bell

In the same year, by the command of the great sovereign Vasily Ivanovich, by God’s mercy autocrat of all Rus, a great bell, the Blagovestnik, was cast, weighing 1,000 poods, and Nikolai Nemchin cast it; and it was placed on a wooden bell tower in the forty-second year, on December 9.
