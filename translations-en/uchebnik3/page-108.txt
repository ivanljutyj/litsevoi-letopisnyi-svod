When the townspeople had climbed onto the wall, they saw a multitude of Turks in the moat, set fire to barrels of pitch and sent them down upon them, and they were all burned. And so by God's providence that day the city was delivered from the godless Turks.

The evil-natured Mehmed, with a multitude of his warriors, watched what had happened from afar and pondered what to do; and all his soldiers also, taking fright, withdrew from the city.

The Greeks, coming out of the city, finished off the Turks in the moats who were still alive, and gathering them into many heaps, burned them together with the remaining Turks.

The emperor with the patriarch and the whole consecrated council prayed in all the churches and thanked God, hoping for an end to the wars.

That evil-faithed Mehmed also took counsel for many days: should he retreat from the city to his own land? Moreover, the sea route had become open, and help for the city was awaited from everywhere.

But since our lawlessness had exceeded our heads and our sins had weighed down our hearts, because we did not listen to God's commandments or walk in His ways, how could His wrath be avoided? The emperor in the city with the patriarch, the whole imperial senate and all the people, having taken counsel, made this decision: "If this evil-faithed one stands many days without beginning battle, it means he is preparing against us; let us send to him about peace." And so they did.

The crafty one, learning of this, rejoiced in his heart, thinking that necessity had come upon the city, and setting aside his retreat, he began to deliberate about peace and answered the envoys thus: "If you ask for peace, I will grant it, but let the emperor leave the city for Amorea, and also the patriarch and all the people who wish, without harm, leaving the city empty to me; and I will make an eternal peace and will not enter Amorea or the islands by any guile forever. And those who do not wish to leave the city will be under my protection, without harm and without sorrow."

When they learned all this, the emperor and patriarch and all the people sighed from the heart and raised their hands to heaven, praying: "O Lord, our Defender! Look down from the height of Your glory, cast down the pride of this foul one, and deliver the city, Your possession, for the people are Your dominion and the sheep of Your pastures, living in Your court in one flock. And where shall we go, leaving our shepherd and teacher? No, emperor, no; let us all die here in Your holy court for the glory of Your majesty." Having said all this, they again prepared for battle, repenting of the embassy to the godless Mehmed, because by it they had detained him.
