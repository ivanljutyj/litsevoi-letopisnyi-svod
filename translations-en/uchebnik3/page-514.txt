The sovereign himself mounted his horse and rode to his regiment, and the regiment soon assembled.

The sovereign then set out for Kashira, whence there was a direct road to Tula. And the regiment of the great sovereign assembled in such numbers that even the fields of Kolomna could not contain it. Many people who were with the sovereign had been in many campaigns and had seen multitudes of men, but never had they seen so magnificent a regiment; and all marveled at the greatness of the regiment and the royal order, that in so short a time so many men had gathered—people do not go to hunting and fowling as they went to death for God’s grace and for the love and order of our sovereign. And not all the men were gathered here: many people were in the city of Sviyazhsk, from among nobles and boyar sons, also in Murom; to put it more simply: in Murom were gathered the hosts of all the Moscow towns, while with the sovereign at Kolomna were his boyars and resident retainers and selected boyar sons, and in the regiments were the Novgorod men.

And the sovereign came to Kashira, and crossings were prepared for the sovereign on the Oka, while Mstislavsky and his companions had already crossed the river, and Prince Ivan Fedorovich awaited the sovereign’s arrival. And on that same day, in the evening of Thursday, near Kashira, a messenger from Prince Grigory Temkin, Grisha Sukhotin, met the sovereign and reported that the Crimean tsar Devlet-Girey had come with his son, and with him all the Crimeans and many men of the Turkish Sultan, Turks and Janissaries, and his army was great, and ahead of himself he had hastily sent seven thousand to the city of Tula. On Tuesday, June 21, Temkin had sent to the sovereign with that news, thinking that few men had come.

On June 22, Wednesday, at the first hour of the day, the tsar came to the city of Tula with all his men and equipment. When day came, he fired cannon at the city, bombarded the city with fireballs and arrows, and in many places in the city courtyards caught fire, and at that time the tsar ordered many Janissaries of the Turkish Sultan to attack.

The great prince’s commanders, Prince Grigory Ivanovich Temkin and the few men with him, since he had come secretly to Tula, had already passed the Tula outskirts, and the fighting men rode to the sovereign; and in the city the Orthodox Christians began to entreat the All-Merciful God and the Most Pure Mother of God and the great wonderworkers for help against the impious, for deliverance from them, and with the help of Almighty God they extinguished the fire in the city and fought only the impious, and drove the enemies away from the city; the impious did not manage to do anything bad to the city.

On the next morning, Thursday, the impious wished to accomplish their desire, and seeing few people in the city, they ordered an attack on the city with cannon and arquebuses.
