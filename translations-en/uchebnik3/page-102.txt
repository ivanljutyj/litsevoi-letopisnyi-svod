Grant us the opening of lips to speak. But, O hymned and all-blessed Lord, do not deliver us, Your creation and handiwork, the work of Your hands, utterly to our enemies, and do not lay waste Your inheritance, and do not take Your mercy from us, and lighten this burden for us, so that we may return to You and repent before Your loving-kindness; for You Yourself, O Master, said: “I have not come to save the righteous, but to call sinners to repentance, that they may turn and live.” Yea, O Lord, King of heaven! Grant us relief now for the sake of Your most pure Mother of God and the holy patriarchs and tsars who formerly pleased Your Divinity in this city! Having said all this and much else, they also prayed to the most pure Mother of God from the heart, standing and weeping all those days.

The Tsar, however, often rode around the city, strengthening the commanders and soldiers and all the people, so that they would not lose hope or weaken their resistance against the enemy, but would trust in the Lord Almighty, saying: “He is our helper and defender,” and would turn again to prayer.

The Turks fought in all places, relieving one another, ceaselessly day and night, giving the townspeople no sleep, so that they would be worn out as they prepared for the assault; and both sides did this for thirteen days.

On the fourteenth day the Turks, having performed their foul prayer, began to play zurnas, to sound horns, and to beat drums; and, having rolled forward cannons and many arquebuses, they began to strike the city wall and also to shoot from countless hand bows. But the townspeople, because of the countless shooting, could not stand on the walls, but hid themselves and awaited the assault; and others fired back from cannons and arquebuses as best they could, and killed many Turks. Patriarch Anastasius, the hierarchs, and the whole priestly order prayed ceaselessly for God’s mercy and for the deliverance of the city.

When the godless Turks had driven all the people from the walls, the whole army cried out on every side, and at once they attacked the city from all sides, shouting and yelling: some with various fires, others with ladders, still others with wall-breaking devices and many stratagems, to take the city. And the city people fought them fiercely with shouts and cries.

The Tsar rode around the whole city, encouraged his people, giving them hope in God, and ordered the bells rung throughout the city to summon the people.

When the Turks heard the great ringing, they played zurnas, trumpets, and many drums. And there was a great and most terrible battle. And from the roar of cannons and arquebuses, from the clanging sound and from the wails and cries of people, from the clashing of weapons, and also from the weeping and lamentation of the city people, women, and children, the earth shook, and no one could hear what was said to one another; it was like great thunder. And from the multitude of fire and from the shots of cannons and arquebuses on both sides, clouds of smoke thickened, covering the city and the army.
