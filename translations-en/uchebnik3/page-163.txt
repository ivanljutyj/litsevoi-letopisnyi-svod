Then the time came for the church builders to erect the wall by the offertory, where lay the tomb of our holy father Peter the Wonderworker. Metropolitan Philip began to speak to the grand prince, asking that the relics of the hierarch be taken out and placed more honorably in the new church, but in the former place. The grand prince answered him: “You, father, yourself know what ought to be done; but send to the bishops of our land, for, father, this matter is not in our authority, but in yours and that of the other hierarchs, the priests, your children.”

Metropolitan Philip, having placed his hope in the Lord God and in His most pure Mother, and in the prayers of the great wonderworker Peter and of the other hierarchs, sent word to all the bishops; and those who were with him he then summoned to himself, together with the archimandrites and other priests who understood this matter, and told them his thought and the words of the grand prince—that “he lays this upon me and upon you, my children.” They all followed the word of Metropolitan Philip and the good will of the grand prince; and the metropolitan was filled with joy, and, taking boldness, desired to see the relics of the saint.

On the Transfer of the Relics of Metropolitan Peter the Wonderworker

On the fourteenth day of the same month, in the night from Sunday to Monday, they came to the tomb of our holy father, the Most Reverend Metropolitan Peter. And the metropolitan ordered the priests who were with him to dismantle the tomb over the relics; and he himself with them, in fear and shedding many tears—wondering whether this would be pleasing to the saint—dismantled the tomb down to the saint’s coffin.

And he saw from his relics, as it were, a shining light, and abundant fragrance issuing from them; and the coffin in which he had been laid had entirely fallen apart from fire, but nothing had touched his relics. They say that the coffin burned at the time when the impious Tsar Tokhtamysh took the city of Moscow by guile and then destroyed his tomb, hoping that something was hidden in it, and found nothing; and when he left the city, he set it on fire, and from that the saint’s coffin also burned, but not even the smell of smoke touched his relics, for they were protected by God’s grace. And the vestments that were on him were scorched by fire, but that part of them which had been under his relics remained.

Seeing this, the metropolitan marveled at the boundless love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, that He had so glorified His servant, and he offered much prayer with tears.

With great fear and joy he took his relics from that reliquary and placed them in a new stone one, and set it near the same place.
