And the apostle says, “I received from Christ,” and so on, and again, “He took bread,” but neither the Lord nor the apostle says “unleavened bread.” Concerning this Kyr Peter, Patriarch of Antioch, wrote in an epistle to the Metropolitan of Venice, and in the place where he speaks of unleavened bread he notes: “Concerning this some say that, where they found bread then, denying the law—but is it lawful to eat unleavened bread? Others also say that even the Greeks were there then and had loaves.” And concerning this, up to this point.

Let us also set forth the error of the heretics, expounded in the epistle of Kyr Michael, blessed Patriarch of Constantinople, to Kyr Peter, Patriarch of Antioch. It says there: “What the Judaizers do is this: sinning concerning unleavened bread, concerning eating the meat of a strangled animal, if they trim their beards, if they fast on Sabbaths, if they eat unclean things, if monks eat pork fat, and the subcutaneous fat that clings to the flesh; and if in the first week of holy Lent they observe meat-abstinence and cheese-abstinence together. And if, besides this, they hold to heresy also in the holy Creed, then they confess wrongly and act deceitfully.” He also writes: “And in the Holy Spirit, the True and Life-giving, proceeding from the Father and the Son”; and in the divine service they proclaim, “One is holy, one is Lord Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father through the Holy Spirit”; besides this, they forbid marriage to priests, that is, they do not receive those who have wives into the priesthood, but want priests to be celibate; and two brothers take two sisters; and during the service, at communion, one of those serving, who has eaten unleavened bread, kisses the others; and their bishops wear rings on their hands, like women who take churches, and they propose that betrothal be proclaimed; and going out to battle, they defile their hands with blood, thereby destroying souls—both those who kill and those who are killed.
