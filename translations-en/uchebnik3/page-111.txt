And some Turks, having made many bridges, rode into the city on horseback.

But all the commanders, having joined with Zustuneus, attacked the Turks fiercely and drove them back to the wall.

But the Turks who had entered the city, both horse and foot, drove the strategoi back and beat them mercilessly, and were already overpowering the Greeks; and if the emperor had not hastened to them, the city would have been finally destroyed. The emperor overtook the Turks and attacked them with his chosen men, and cut them down mercilessly and terribly: those he reached he split in two, and others he cut in half; his sword could be checked by nothing.

The Turks shouted to one another opposite his fortress, and urged one another on against him, and frightened him with every kind of weapon, and loosed countless arrows at him; but, as it is said, both military victories and an emperor’s fall come only by God’s providence, for all their weapons and arrows fell and, flying past him, did not hit him.

The emperor alone, with sword in hand, cut them down; and they fled from him out of the city to the ruined place; and there they crowded together, and many Turks were killed there, and others were driven beyond the ditches. And so with God’s help that day the emperor saved the city, and already toward evening the Turks retreated.

In the morning Eparch Nicholas ordered the townspeople to throw the killed Turks out of the city beyond the ditches, to show the godless one; and their number was sixty thousand; and the Turks took them and burned them.

The eparch also ordered the whole ruined place to be repaired with wood and a tower to be built, thinking that the accursed ones had already retreated.

But the godless Magmet took counsel for three days, having gathered his pashas and sanjak-beys, and said to them: “We see that the giaours have grown bold against us and fight us so that we cannot overcome them, because we fight in one place only, at the ruined place, where many people are cramped, and when our people are few they prevail over us; let us repeat the assault as at first, bringing towers and ladders to the city walls in many places, and the townspeople will divide among all the places to resist, but let us attack the ruined place especially strongly.”

And as the accursed one decided, by God’s permission, so he did: he ordered towers and ladders and many other assault devices to be prepared, and he ordered his soldiers to fight the townspeople every day, giving the townspeople no rest.

On the 21st day of the month of May, because of our sins, there was a terrible sign in the city.
