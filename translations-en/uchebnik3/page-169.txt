Such was the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ toward us sinners, and such was the victory over the hostile raw-eaters through the prayers and supplication of His most holy Mother, our Lady the Mother of God and ever-virgin Mary, and through the intercession of the honorable heavenly bodiless powers, and of all the holy wonderworkers of Rus, our most reverend metropolitans Peter and Alexius, and Jonah, and all the others.

The grand prince learned that the tsar had gone away, and he began to send many of his men after the Tatars along their road, so that they might overtake those lagging behind and free the Christian captives.

And when word came to the grand prince that the tsar had already reached his settlements and had gone to his winter quarters, then he gave thanks to the Lord God and to His most pure Mother, the swift helper in troubles, and to our intercessors the archangels Michael and Gabriel, and to the other heavenly bodiless powers, and to all the saints, through whose prayers the Lord delivered the Christian race from the coming of the godless Hagarenes.

And then he sent his brothers back to their own patrimonies, and likewise his princes and commanders and all his soldiers. And they dispersed each to his own place, thanking the Lord God, who had granted them a bloodless victory over the godless Hagarenes. And the grand prince himself returned to Kolomna, and with him Tsarevich Danyar, the son of Tregub.

And from there, after honoring him, he sent him back to his own little town; and he himself went to Moscow and entered the city on Sunday, the 23rd day of the month of August.

In the year 6981 from Adam (1481 from Christ)

On the first day of the month of September, the Fryazi and Greeks from Rome came with Tsarevna Sophia to the German city of Lübeck.

And they stayed there eight days, and on the ninth day of the same month they set out from there by boats to the ship, and on the tenth day they boarded the ship.

On the Repose of Prince Yuri Vasilievich

In the same month, on the twelfth day, on Saturday, at the tenth hour of the day, the right-believing and Christ-loving Prince Yuri Vasilievich reposed in Moscow, aged 31 years, 7 months, and 22 days. And at that time the grand prince was not there, nor was his mother, nor his brothers. They were all in Rostov, because their mother, the grand princess Maria, was then ill there.

And Metropolitan Philip sent to the grand prince, announcing to him the death of his brother, asking how he would command: whether to bury him without him, or not to bury him.

On Sunday, after Matins, the metropolitan came with the bishops of Sarsk and Perm and with all the consecrated council to the prince’s court, and, taking his body, they carried it to the Church of the Archangel Michael.
