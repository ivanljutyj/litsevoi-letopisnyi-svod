Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and the boyar and palace steward Danilo Romanovich; in the guard regiment, the voivode Pyotr Andreyevich Bulgakov, and Prince David Fedorovich Paletsky. And with the boyars he also ordered the heads of his tsar’s regiment to be with the boyar sons, and with them the streltsy heads, and many atamans with the Cossacks, and the Gorodets sayyid with all the Gorodets Tatars, and Prince Yenikei with the Temnikov Mordvins, and many mountain people, who also served as guides.

And the voivodes set out: ahead of the voivodes went the foot regiments, the streltsy and Cossacks, and they came to a high hill by the fort, while their fort had been built of log frames and filled with earth, and in another place was blocked with many abatis, and was made amid impassable swamps.

On the Capture of the Fort

And the voivodes began to say to the boyar sons in the tsar’s name: “Strive and serve God and the sovereign; all rush upon the pagans!” Prince Semyon Ivanovich and Danilo Romanovich approached the gates of the fort and ordered the boyar sons to go on foot to the fort; the heads of the tsar’s regiment also dismounted and went to the fort. And they began to assault the fort. The streltsy fired from a multitude of arquebuses, the Christians and Tatars from bows. And arrows flying from both sides, like a strong rain cloud with frequent drops, moistened the earth, and there was loud thunder from the arquebuses.

These were still fighting at the gates, while Prince Alexander Borisovich and Zakhary Petrovich, voivodes of the great regiment, went to the right and came to another part of the fort; they too ordered the foot to come, for impassable great fortifications had been made there for horses, and in the forest there was a great thicket. But all the Orthodox, calling God to aid, rushed together upon the enemy, and the pagans fled.

And the tsar’s Orthodox voivodes prevailed, took the fort, killed a great multitude of Tatars, and took two hundred alive. And they went, fighting and burning villages, to the Arsk fortified settlement. And they came to the Arsk settlement. And the boyars and voivodes sent off the heads of the tsar’s regiment, while they themselves stood at the settlement for two days, and the voivodes went by another road to Kazan. And they ravaged the whole Arsk side, killed many people, took their wives and children captive, and freed a great multitude of Christian captives. Their war was one hundred and fifty versts across, and in length as far as the Kama: they burned villages and killed livestock, and drove countless livestock with them to Kazan to the regiments. And all the heads from the raiding parties came together to the voivodes, God granting, in health.

On the Coming of the Voivodes to the Sovereign

And the voivodes went to the sovereign, and ahead they sent the head of the tsar’s regiment, Semyon Vasilyevich Yakovlev, to announce God’s mercy to the sovereign.
