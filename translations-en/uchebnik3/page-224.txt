That same winter, on January 19, the Grand Prince seized in Novgorod the Novgorod Archbishop Theophilus for his treason and sent him to Moscow; and he took his treasury, a great quantity of gold and silver, and his vessels, because that lord did not wish Novgorod to be under the Grand Prince, but under the King or another sovereign. For when the Grand Prince first took Novgorod, he then took from the Novgorod lord half of the volosts and the villages of all the monasteries, and for this the lord remained in anger; for those volosts had at first belonged to the Grand Princes, and so they had made them their own.

In the same month, on the 24th day, they placed him in the Monastery of the Miracle of the Archangel Michael; and he remained there six and a half years, and there he died.

On the Defection of the Grand Prince's Brothers to Luki

In that same year the brothers withdrew from the Grand Prince, Prince Andrei the Elder and Prince Boris.

At the same time news came to the Grand Prince in Novgorod from his son that his brothers wished to defect; he soon left Novgorod for Moscow and arrived in Moscow before the Great Fast.

And all the people rejoiced, for they were in great fear of his brothers: all the towns were under siege, and many, fleeing through the forests, died of cold without the Grand Prince.

That same winter, during Butter Week, Prince Boris, his brother, came from Volok to Uglich to Prince Andrei Vasilyevich, and sent his princess and children to Rzhev.

And Prince Andrei with his princess and children, and Prince Boris, went from Uglich to Rzhev through the Tver patrimony.

The Grand Prince sent his boyar Andrei Mikhailovich to them at Rzhev; but they did not return, and went from Rzhev with their princesses and children, and their boyars, and the best of the boyar sons with wives and children and their people, up the Volga to the Novgorod volosts.

The Grand Prince sent after them Vasian, Archbishop of Rostov; he caught up with them at Molvyatitsy, while they were then going to Novgorod.

And from there, after the archbishop's words, they sent with the archbishop to the Grand Prince their boyars — Prince Vasily and Prince Pyotr Nikitich Obolensky — and from there they themselves went to the Lithuanian border, and, arriving, halted at Velikiye Luki. And they sent to the King, asking him to judge their grievances with the Grand Prince and to help them; and the King replied to them, and gave their princesses the city of Vitebsk for residence.
