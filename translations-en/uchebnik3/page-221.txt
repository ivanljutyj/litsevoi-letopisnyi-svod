On the Transfer of the Relics of the Wonderworker Peter to the New Church

On the 23rd day of the same month, a Monday, the most reverend Metropolitan Gerontius, together with Archbishop Vassian of Rostov, Bishop Euthymius of Suzdal, Bishop Prokhor of Sarsk, archimandrites, archpriests, hegumens, and the whole sacred council, came to the Church of St John under the Bells, where lay the venerable relics of our holy father Peter, Metropolitan and wonderworker; his relics had already been transferred from the stone coffin into a wooden reliquary. And the metropolitan ordered the bells to be rung, and then came the pious and Christ-loving Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich, autocrat of Vladimir and Novgorod and all Rus', and his son Grand Prince Ivan; but his brother Prince Andrei the Younger was then ill; and all the boyars and nobles, and a great multitude of the people of the glorious city of Moscow; and they performed a prayer service.

Then, after the prayer service was ended, the Grand Prince crossed himself before the healing-bearing icon that lay on the shrine of the blessed hierarch Peter, and said to his father the metropolitan: “Whom do you bid to carry the relics of the great wonderworker Peter?”

The hierarch blessed and commanded the Grand Prince himself and his son.

They, filled with joy, touched the venerable shrine with awe and, lifting it with those helping them, carried the relics of the saint out of that church; the metropolitan followed them with a censer, and after him the other hierarchs and priests; and then they began to ring all the bells.

Having brought it into the new church, they placed the shrine with the venerable relics in the middle of the church, where the hierarch vests for the service.

And so they began to sing Vespers for the bringing of his relics, and after Vespers a prayer service to the Most Pure and to the wonderworker.

In that night, at the eighth hour, they began to ring for Matins; the metropolitan and the whole sacred council came, also the Grand Prince with his son, and all the others, as was said before, and they began to sing Matins as on the repose of the wonderworker Peter, which falls on December 21, with praises before the seventh canticle, and the rest according to custom, and the dismissal.

On that Tuesday, at the third hour of the day, the metropolitan, with the bishops and the aforenamed priests, came into the church and vested himself in all his sacred vestments.
