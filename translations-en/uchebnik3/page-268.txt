On the Death of Prince Boris Vasilievich

In the same month of May, on the 29th day, a Thursday, Prince Boris Vasilievich died, and he was brought to Moscow and laid in the Church of the Archangel Michael.

On the Arrival of Ambassadors with Craftsmen

In the same year the Grand Prince's ambassadors came to Moscow: Manuel Angelov the Greek and Daniil Mamyrev, whom the Grand Prince had sent for craftsmen to Venice and Milan. They brought to Moscow Aleviz, master of walls and chambers, and Peter the cannon-founder, and other craftsmen.

On the Arrival of the Grand Prince's Ambassadors from the German Land and on the Danish Ambassador

In the same year the Grand Prince's ambassadors came to Moscow from the German land, Dmitry Ralev and Dmitry Zaitsev, whom the Grand Prince had sent to the Danish King Ivan with a proposal of love and brotherhood. They brought the king to swear on the treaty charters, and exchanged treaty charters; then also an ambassador came with them to Moscow from the Danish King Ivan, named David, likewise with a proposal of brotherhood and love.

On the Seizure of the Kolivan Merchants. In the year 7003 from Adam (1503 from Christ)

The Grand Prince sent the dyak Vasily Zhuk and Daniil Mamyrev to the governors in Novgorod and ordered the German merchants of Kolivan to be seized in Novgorod, and, having inventoried their goods, to be brought to Moscow for their offenses: because in Kolivan they had inflicted many injuries and wanton insults on the Grand Prince's Novgorod merchants, and had boiled other people of the Grand Prince alive in cauldrons, without informing the Grand Prince and without investigation; also the Grand Prince's ambassadors had suffered insult from them—those ambassadors who had gone from the Grand Prince to Rome and to the Italian land and to the German land; and also many injuries and troubles had been caused by them to the Grand Prince's old Novgorod merchants, and there had been robberies at sea.

And for that Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich laid his disgrace upon them, and ordered their merchants to be imprisoned and their goods brought to Moscow, and ordered their old trading yards in Novgorod and their chapels to be taken away.
