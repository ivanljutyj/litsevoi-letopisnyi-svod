The sovereign himself mounted his horse and came to his regiment, and the regiment soon assembled.

The sovereign then set out for Kashira, from which there was a direct road to Tula. And the regiment of the great sovereign assembled so great that even the fields of Kolomna could not contain it. Many people who were with the sovereign had been in many campaigns and had seen multitudes of men, yet had never seen so majestic a regiment; and all marveled at the majesty of the regiment and the tsar’s order, that in so short a time so many people had gathered. Men do not go to hunting and the chase as they go to death for the grace of God and the love and order of our sovereign. And still not all the people were gathered there: many nobles and boyar sons were in the city of Sviyazhsk, and also in Murom. To put it more simply: in Murom were gathered the forces of all the Moscow cities, while with the sovereign at Kolomna were his boyars and resident servitors and chosen boyar sons, and in the regiments were the Novgorod people.

And the sovereign came to Kashira, and crossings were prepared for the sovereign on the Oka, while Mstislavsky and his companions had already crossed the river, and Prince Ivan Fyodorovich was awaiting the tsar’s arrival. And that same day, on Thursday evening, near Kashira, a messenger from Prince Grigory Temkin, Grisha Sukhotin, met the sovereign and reported that the Crimean Tsar Devlet-Girey had come with his son, and with him all the Crimean Tatars and many men of the Turkish Sultan, Turks and Janissaries, and his army was great; and ahead of himself he had hastily sent seven thousand to the city of Tula. On Tuesday, June 21, Temkin had sent word to the sovereign with those tidings, thinking that few men had come.

On Wednesday, June 22, at the first hour of the day, the tsar came to the city of Tula with all his men and equipment. When day broke, he fired cannon at the city and bombarded it with fiery cannonballs and arrows, and in many places in the city houses caught fire, and at that time the tsar ordered many Janissaries of the Turkish Sultan to assault.

The voevodes of the great prince, Prince Grigory Ivanovich Temkin and the few men with him, since he had come secretly to Tula, had already passed the Tula outskirts, and the fighting men rode to the sovereign; and the Orthodox Christians in the city began to pray to the All-Merciful God, the Most Pure Mother of God, and the great wonderworkers for help against the impious and for deliverance from them. And with the help of Almighty God they put out the fire in the city and fought only the impious, and drove the enemies away from the city; the impious did not manage to do any harm to the city.

On the next morning, Thursday, the impious wished to accomplish their desire; seeing few people in the city, they ordered an assault on the city with cannons and arquebuses.
