The same is the interpretation of Alexius Aristenus

This fourth rule is joined with the 25th, and also with the 27th rule of this council and with the 74th rule of the same council. And these rules in no way command bishops, priests, deacons, and subdeacons to dissolve marriage and renounce their wives, but to abstain from them at the proper time, that is, when they are engaged in prayers and fasts, bring the Holy Gifts, in other words, perform the service.

The twelfth rule of the 6th ecumenical council, held in Trullo of the royal chambers, does not forbid bishops to live with wives after elevation to the episcopate and permits intercourse with them, and this is neither a rejection nor a perversion of the apostolic rules, but is offered for the salvation of people and for better care for them, allowing no reproach upon the priestly order. The Romans in the Roman Church changed this rule, saying that if anyone wishes to receive the rank of priest or deacon, he must make a vow, saying that “from this time I will not have marital relations with my wife.” And the same 12th rule commands not to deprive priests of lawful marital communion, but that they remain together and not be separated from their wives, but only at the proper time abstain from their spouses, as the apostle Paul said, and then live together again; this ordinance must be observed by priests, deacons, and subdeacons; only bishops must not have wives, for this was established at the sixth council in the 12th rule, as said earlier, that bishops must live celibate.

Rule 66 of the Holy Apostles

If anyone among the clergy is seen fasting on Sunday or Saturday, he shall be deposed; if a layman, he shall be excommunicated.

Apostolic Rule 69

If a bishop or priest, or deacon, or subdeacon, or reader, or singer does not fast in the holy Forty Days, or does not fast on Wednesdays and Fridays throughout the year, he shall be deposed, unless he is afflicted with bodily illness; if a layman, he shall be excommunicated.
