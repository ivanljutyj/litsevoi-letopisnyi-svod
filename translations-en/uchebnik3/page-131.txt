On the Death of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich

At that same time, on Friday of the Feast of St. Theodore, the Grand Prince felt a wasting disease upon him. And he ordered himself to be cauterized, because such is the custom for those afflicted with the wasting disease: he ordered lighted tinder to be applied in many places, in great quantity; and where there was no disease, he did not feel it. When the wounds festered and his illness became severe, he wished to be tonsured as a monk, but they would not allow him.

And from that illness he departed this life on the twenty-seventh day of March, on Saturday, at the third hour of the night, toward morning, on Sunday, and was buried in the Church of the Holy Archangel Michael in Moscow, where all the grand princes, their line, lie.

On the Grand Reign of Ivan Vasilyevich.
In the year 6970 from Adam (the year 1470 from Christ)

After him, by his blessing, his eldest son, Grand Prince Ivan, took the grand princely throne.

To his other son, Prince Yuri, he gave Dmitrov, Mozhaysk, Serpukhov, and other districts and villages, and the treasury with which his grandmother Grand Princess Sophia had blessed him.

And he blessed Prince Andrei the Elder, giving him Uglich, Bezhetsky Verkh, Zvenigorod, and many other districts and villages.

To Prince Boris he gave Volok Lamsky, Rzhev, and Ruza, and all the districts and villages of his great-grandmother Maria Goltyaeva according to her will.

To the youngest Prince Andrei he gave Vologda with its environs, and Zaozerye, and other districts and many villages.

And all his treasury, and the town of Romanov, and the districts and villages throughout the whole grand principality that had been held by former grand princesses, and what he himself had taken in great abundance from traitors, and what he had purchased—all this he gave to his Grand Princess Maria.

On the Finding of the Yaroslavl Wonderworkers.
In the year 6971 from Adam (the year 1471 from Christ)

In the city of Yaroslavl, in the monastery of the Holy Savior, the relics of Prince Fyodor Rostislavich and his sons Konstantin and David were found beneath the church in the crypt, all whole and in no way damaged.

And the archimandrite of that monastery, Christopher, took them with great honor and, having transferred them to the church of the Great Savior, placed them in one coffin, and laid new monastic vestments upon them, since they had departed this life in the monastic order; and many miracles and various healings came from them to those who approached them with faith, even to this day.
