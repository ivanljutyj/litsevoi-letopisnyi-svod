And from Borovsk he sent ahead of himself his boyar and commander Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Obolensky Repnya, and his okolnichy Andrei Vasilyevich Saburov, and his other commanders with many people, to surround the city of Smolensk.

The voivode and governor of Smolensk, Pan Yuri Glebovich, the princes and boyars of Smolensk, and the hetmans with soldiers rode out to battle beyond the city, beyond the ramparts, against the grand prince’s commanders.

And the grand prince’s commanders attacked them, and by God’s mercy the grand prince’s commanders drove off the Smolensk voivode, the princes, and the pans; and they killed many people, and took many other princes, boyars, and soldiers alive and brought them to the grand prince in Borovsk.

And the grand prince’s commanders themselves surrounded the city of Smolensk and ravaged the surrounding area.

In the year 7022 from Adam (the year 1522 from Christ)

On the sixth day of September the grand prince set out from Borovsk for Smolensk and caused the city of Smolensk great distress with cannon fire and handguns over many days. But the city was strong, enclosed by steep mountains and high hills and fortified with great walls, and the grand prince, having laid waste all the land, returned to Moscow.

On the Imperial Envoy

That same winter, on the eighth day of February, there came to Moscow to Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich, sovereign of all Rus, from the elected Emperor Maximilian and supreme King of the Romans his envoy, named Yuri Snittsem Pomer, a councillor of His Imperial Majesty, come concerning love, brotherhood, and friendship.

And Grand Prince Vasily, by God’s grace tsar and sovereign of all Rus, concluded with the elected Emperor and supreme King of the Romans Maximilian an eternal treaty of brotherhood and love, and they wrote treaty charters between themselves, and the grand prince affixed his golden seal to it.

And having honored the emperor’s envoy Yuri, he dismissed him from Moscow on the seventh day of March. And together with him the grand prince sent to the Emperor Maximilian his envoy Dmitry Fedorov, son of Laskarev, a Greek, and his secretary Elizar Sukov.

On the Danish Envoy

That same spring, on the ninth day of April, the grand prince dismissed from Moscow David, envoy of the Danish king; and together with him the grand prince sent to the Danish King Christiern his envoy Ivan Mikulin, son of Yary, and his secretary Vasily Bely.
