And Prince Ivan Turuntai was taken at matins by the New City at the Neglimen Gate, as he wished to enter the city with priests; and Prince Mikhail was seized by Prince Pyotr Shuysky in the settlement, in the courtyard of the Church of the Resurrection at the Good Bell Tower beyond the Neglinnaya River on Nikitskaya Street; and they brought them both into the city on the eleventh day of that same month. And the tsar and grand prince ordered them to be placed under guard and interrogated about their flight; they petitioned that out of fear after the murder of Prince Yuri Glinsky they had gone to pray at Okovets to the Most Pure (Mother of God) and had gone astray, not knowing the road.

And after that the tsar and grand prince inquired into their guilt and, at the intercession of his father Metropolitan Macarius, pardoned them, forgave them their guilt, and ordered them to be released on surety, because they had made that flight in folly, out of fear after the murder of Prince Yuri Glinsky.

On the Tsar's Campaign against Kazan

In that same autumn the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus, with the metropolitan, his brothers, and the boyars, resolved to march against his foe the Kazan tsar Safa-Girey and the oath-breaking Kazan people for their perjury.

On the twentieth day of November, a Sunday, the tsar and grand prince sent ahead of him to Vladimir his commanders, Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky and his other commanders, and ordered them to muster with their men in Vladimir.

And he ordered Tsar Shigalei to come from Meschera, and with him the commanders Prince Vladimir Vorotynsky and others, and he ordered them to join him at the mouth of the Tsivil River.

And in that same autumn, on the eleventh day of December, a Sunday, the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus set out for Vladimir, and he ordered the cannon and handgun equipment to be conveyed after him to Vladimir as well.

And the tsar and grand prince arrived in Vladimir on the twentieth day of that same month, a Thursday; but the equipment, cannon, and handguns were sent to Vladimir after Epiphany with great difficulty, because there were heavy rains and no snow.

At that same time, on the sixth day of January, the archpriest of the Annunciation, Fyodor Barmin, fell ill; fear came upon him, and he asked the sovereign's leave to become a monk and was tonsured at the Chudov Monastery.

And the tsar and grand prince set out from Vladimir on the eighth day of January, a Sunday, and arrived in Nizhny Novgorod on the twenty-sixth day of that same month, a Thursday.

And from Novgorod he set out against his foe the Kazan tsar Safa-Girey and the oath-breaking people of the Kazan land on the second day of February.
