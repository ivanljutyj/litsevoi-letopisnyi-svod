The Sign in the City of Kazan

Many times the Tatars saw a black-robed monk walking about in the tsar’s court and in the tsar’s chambers, sometimes sitting, and they tried in every way to kill him; but he walked among them from the city to the Volga River and became invisible.

They also saw two black-robed monks on the city walls, walking quickly, and no one could catch them. The Kazanians were perplexed by this, and it greatly troubled them.

The Beginning of the Tale of How the All-Merciful Lover of Mankind, God, Wrought Most Glorious Miracles in Our Generation Through Our Orthodox Tsar, the Faithful and Great Prince Ivan Vasilievich, Sovereign and Autocrat of All Rus, for Orthodox Christianity, from Infidel Captivity and Slavery Under the Godless Kazan Tatars, and of the Founding of the New City of Sviyazhsk, Called Novgorod Sviyazhsky, in Which He Established Churches and Dwellings for Christians, in the Year 7059 from Adam (the Year 1559 from Christ), in the Nineteenth Year of His Sovereignty and the Fifth Year of His Tsardom

The great tsar in piety, sovereign and great prince Ivan Vasilievich of All Rus, seeing Christianity enslaved and much Christian blood being shed, and the desolation of many holy churches, asked himself: from whom did these intolerable calamities come? I say that all this evil came from the godless Kazan Saracens.

The pious and God-beloved soul of our pious tsar could not endure that Christianity should remain in such calamities, in captivity, and he said to himself thus: “O All-Merciful God! Through the prayers of Thy Most Pure Mother and of all the saints, and through the prayers of our Russian wonderworkers, Thou hast established me as tsar and pastor of this Orthodox land and of all Thy people, as leader and ruler, that I may rule Thy people and abide in unshakable Orthodoxy, that I may defend them from all evils that fall upon them and fulfill all their needs. O Master! Help me and deliver Thy enslaved servants from the hands of the wicked; truly Thou art the Good Shepherd, who layeth down His life for the sheep.”

And the sovereign began to deliberate with his boyars how to deal with Kazan; and he began to summon the Kazan princes who had come from Kazan to serve him, Prince Kostrov with his companions; and he began to consider founding a city at the Sviyazhsk estuary on Round Mountain, since the sovereign himself, returning from Kazan, had seen that this place was convenient and suitable for building a city.
