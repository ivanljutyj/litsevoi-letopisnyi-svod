In the same year, in May, the governors of Yuryev in Livonia, Fyodor Buturlin and his companions, and the clerks Shemet Shchelepin and Vasily Dyadin, wrote to the tsar and grand prince that from Yuryev the boyar and governor Prince Andrei Kurbsky had betrayed the sovereign tsar and grand prince: from Yuryev at night on April 30 he fled to a German city, to the sovereign’s enemy, the Lithuanian king; and 12 men fled with him, but he did not take his wife or his property. And the governors, having caught his man Vaska Shibanov, sent him to the sovereign.

The same man, Vaska Shibanov, told the sovereign tsar and grand prince about the treasonous deeds of his master Prince Andrei, that he had plotted many treasonous deeds against the sovereign tsar and grand prince. And the apostate traitor Prince Andrei, having come to the king, incited the king and all his council in every way to much Christian bloodshed, so that he would not make peace with the tsar and grand prince, but would shed more Christian blood.

In the same month of May, on the 7th day, the tsar and grand prince of all Russia, Ivan Vasilyevich, went from Moscow to Pereslavl Zalessky with his tsaritsa, Grand Princess Maria, and with his son, Tsarevich Ivan, to the Nikitsky Monastery to pray to Nikita the Wonderworker and to consecrate the stone church in the Nikitsky Monastery in the name of the Great Martyr of Christ Nikita; and Prince Vladimir Andreyevich was with the tsar and grand prince. At the consecration of the church in the Nikitsky Monastery was Afanasy, Metropolitan of all Russia. The sovereign tsar and grand prince had great faith in the venerable wonderworker Nikita, and in that monastery he built a stone church and refectory, and, having adorned the churches with icons and books and all church utensils, he gave land to the monastery and ordered a stone wall to be built around the monastery. The sovereign, having performed a prayer service in that monastery and fed the brethren, went from there to the Trinity in the Sergius Monastery. And, having prayed to the venerable wonderworker Sergius and fed the brethren, he went to Mozhaisk, and from Mozhaisk to Prince Vladimir Andreyevich’s patrimony, to the Oleshnya volost, and visited all the palace villages and Vereya, and Vyshgorod.

In the same year, in May, on the 9th day, at night, the church of the Great Martyr of Christ Paraskeva Pyatnitsa Pirogoshchaya beyond the Moscow River caught fire from a candle; then the wonderworking image of the Martyr of Christ Paraskeva also burned, and thirteen households and five cells burned.
