The sovereign sent a messenger to Lithuania

In that same month the tsar and great prince sent the messenger Nikita Sushchov to Lithuania to the king with a charter. And in the charter he wrote in reply to his letter, in which the king had written with Martin Volotkov that henceforth he should not call the Livonian Germans his own. And in whatever towns he had placed his people, he should withdraw his people from those towns: that land has from of old been tributary to them, the pious sovereign tsars of Russia, and has never belonged to the kings of Lithuania; for that he, the sovereign, has the confirmed charters of all former sovereigns. And if the king does not wish to quarrel with the sovereign over that, let him not encroach on what is not his, but send his great envoys. And the tsar and sovereign would order all the confirmed charters to be shown to them, why they are tributaries of their sovereign; the sovereign thereby showed his justice before God and men, that he did not wish innocent Christian blood to be shed, and he sent a safe-conduct to the king for the envoys.

Dispatch of the great artillery train to the German land

In that same month the sovereign sent his boyars and commanders to the German land on campaign with a great military train, to the towns, for their many wrongs, for violating the Christian faith and burning images of God and all the saints, and for all their violations of obligations to the sovereign, and for the fact that many towns had been pledged and surrendered to the king, and that he himself had gone to the king and joined with the whole land, and had hired help from overseas against the sovereign's army. And he ordered them to wage war against the Germans, if the merciful God should grant help and establish it. Also in the great regiment, with men for missions, with Prince Ivan Fedorovich, commander Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Ovchinin, and Prince Ivan, son of Prince Petr Okhlyabinin.
