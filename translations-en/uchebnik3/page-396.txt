Metropolitan Daniel came to him, and his brothers Prince Yuri and Prince Andrew, and all the boyars, and the boyars’ sons; and Metropolitan Daniel and Bishop Vassian began to say to him that the sovereign Grand Prince should send for the great image of the Most Pure Mother of God of Vladimir, the wonder-working one painted by Luke the Evangelist, and for the icon of Nicholas the Wonderworker of Gostun.

Many wonder-working icons that had been brought were set up before him. Among them was the icon of the Great Martyr Catherine, on which he gazed steadfastly with a joyful face, saying: “Lady Great Catherine, it is time for us to reign.” He said this three times and awoke as if from sleep, and took the icon of the Great Martyr Catherine, and lovingly venerated it, and touched its image with his right hand, because at that time his hand was afflicted.

Then they also brought him the relics of the Great Martyr Catherine, and he venerated the relics and touched them with his right hand.

And lying on his bed, he summoned his boyar Mikhail Semenovich Vorontsov and kissed him and took leave of him. And from that time he lay ill for a long while.

And his spiritual father, Archpriest Alexei, came to him, intending to give him the Gifts; but the Grand Prince stopped him and said: “You yourself see that I lie ill but am in my right mind; and when the soul begins to part from the body, then give me the Gifts; watch over me with discernment and keep guard.”

And after a little while he summoned his brother Prince Yuri Ivanovich and said to him: “Do you remember, brother, when our father Grand Prince Ivan departed on the day after St Demetrius’s Day, on Monday, because his infirmity tormented him day and night? And for me too, brother, the hour of death and the end are approaching.”

And after a little while he summoned his father Metropolitan Daniel and Bishop Vassian of Kolomna, and his brothers, and all the boyars, and said: “You yourselves see me exhausted and nearing the end, and my desire has long been to take the monastic habit; tonsure me.” And then he said to the Metropolitan: “Why, father, do you delay? The time has already come; tonsure me, for I have long desired it.” Then his father Metropolitan Daniel and his boyar Mikhail Yuryev praised him for this intention, that he desired what was good.

And his brother Prince Andrew Ivanovich, and Mikhail Semenovich Vorontsov, and Shigona opposed him, saying: “Vladimir, Grand Prince of Kiev, died not as a monk: was he not granted righteous rest? And other grand princes died not as monks: did they not find rest with the righteous?” And there was a great dispute among them.
