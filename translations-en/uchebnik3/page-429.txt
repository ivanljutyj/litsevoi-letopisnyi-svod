To him, as to Yelets: let people not say that the tsar came to the Russian land and did nothing to Rus. And the tsar set out with all his forces and artillery to the Pronya, and he was to attack Pronsk without fail.

Prince Dmitry and all the commanders sent more commanders after the tsar, Prince Yuri Andreevich Obolensky Peninsky and Prince Vasily Semenovich Mezetsky, and with them they sent many people; and he ordered them to join all together with the Ryazan commanders and go after the tsar together, and to guard the Grand Prince’s cause and help Pronsk.

Tsar Saip-Girey came to Pronsk on the third day of August, and he himself stood beyond the Pronya River, near the city.

He ordered the army to attack the city with cannons, handguns, and city-breaking engines. In the city at that time was the Grand Prince’s commander Vasily Zhulebin, great-grandson of the Lithuanian prince Ostey, of the Sviblov family, with a few people, and another commander, Alexander Kobyakov, from the Ryazan boyars.

The Tatars attacked the city with all their regiments, began firing at the city from cannons and handguns, and their arrows flew like rain. They approached the city walls, and from the city they began to strike the Tatars with cannons and handguns; and those Tatars who came up to the wall they beat off from the city with stakes and stones.

The Tatars attacked the city all day long, fighting the townspeople; and many Tatars were killed from the city with cannons and handguns.

The princes and murzas, riding up to the city, began to tell Vasily to surrender the city, and the tsar would show them mercy; but without taking the city the tsar would not go away. Vasily answered: “By God’s will a city is built, and without God’s will who can take a city? Let the tsar wait a little for the Grand Prince’s commanders, for the Grand Prince’s commanders are coming after him.” The Tatars withdrew to their camps.

The tsar ordered all the people to make siege towers and siege assaults, and he intended to attack the city with all his people from all sides.

Vasily and Alexander with all the people and the women began to strengthen the city, and they ordered stakes and stones and water to be carried to the city wall.

At that time from the commanders, from Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and his comrades, came boyar sons Andrei Vasiliev, son of Ovtsyn, and Ivan Semenov, son of Nashchekin Vetreny, with their comrades, seven men in all, with news: that they should hold the city firmly, “and we are coming to the city hastily, with many people, and we want to fight the tsar as much as God helps us.” And there was great joy in the city.
