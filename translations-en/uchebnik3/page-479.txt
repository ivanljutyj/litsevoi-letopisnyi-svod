On the Signs in the City of Kazan

A certain holy fool Tatar in Kazan, walking about the city, began suddenly to wail and ceaselessly say: “The Tatars shall not live here, but only Russian people.” The Tatars wanted to kill him, but the tsar did not permit them to kill him, and ordered him to be shut up in an empty house. He broke out of that house, shouting and cursing the Tatars and foretelling their destruction.

A Child from a Cow

At another time in the city of Kazan it was plainly seen by the Tatars that a child of human form was born of a cow, which many people gathered there saw.

And suddenly the child turned into a man; he seemed to be armed and said to those looking at him: “Submit without deceit to the Muscovite sovereign! If you do not, you will perish.” And the Tatars decided to kill him, but he became invisible.

A Marvel at the Fishing

Once the Kazan fishermen were fishing on the Volga; they drew out a net in which they saw an old living man lying and saying to them: “Hasten to entreat the Muscovite tsar for your wrongdoing; he is merciful and will have pity on you; if you do not do so, you will be destroyed by him.” They thought this was sorcery of the Russian people and wanted to kill him; but he, having scarcely come out of the net, dived into the river.

A Wonder in the City

In the city of Kazan at that time there were many huts dug into the earth as shelter from Russian fiery shot, and Tatars entered one earthen hut and found it warm and fragrant, as if it were a Christian hut; they saw also a stove full of wheat loaves, and in one half of that hut stood a nobleman’s bed, on which lay a gray-haired man, and he said: “Do not resist the Muscovite tsar, but rather entreat him, for he is very merciful and will harm you in nothing; if you do not submit to him, only a small number of you will escape destruction.” They said among themselves: “This is Russian sorcery; let us kill him, and nothing will happen to us.” And that man became invisible, nor was there bed, nor loaves in the stove, nor fragrance; only a stench did they feel.
