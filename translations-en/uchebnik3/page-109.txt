He ordered the whole army to go to the city and continue the battle every day.
This was God’s permission for our sins; all that had previously been said about this city in the time of Emperor Constantine the Great and Leo the Wise by Methodius, Bishop of Patara, was fulfilled.

And on the sixth day of May the faithless one ordered them to strike the city at the place where they had struck at first, with many cannons, for three days. And when they had worn down the wall, they fired from a great cannon, and many stones fell out of the wall; they fired a second time, and a large part of the wall collapsed.

And when evening came, the Turks began to fire from many cannons at that same place incessantly all night, not allowing the citizens to repair that place. That same night the Greeks prepared a great tower opposite that place.

In the morning the Turks fired from a great cannon a little lower than that place, and a large part of the wall fell out, and again a second and a third time, and when they had already destroyed a large section, shouting, a multitude of men rushed onto that place, pushing one another.

The Greeks also came out of the city against them and fought, roaring fiercely like wild beasts; and it was terrible to see the boldness and strength of both sides.

Zustunei, gathering many men, with a shout valiantly attacked the Turks and had already driven them from the wall, and filled the moat with the dead.

A certain Janissary Amurat, strong of body, mingling with the Greeks, reached Zustunei and began fiercely to slash at him.

A certain Greek, leaping down from the wall, cut off his leg with an axe and thus saved Zustunei from death.

Flaburar, the western commander Amarbey, with his regiments attacked the Greeks, and the battle was fierce.

From the city the commander Rakhkavey came to the rescue with many men and drove the Turks back to Amarbey himself.

He, seeing Rakhkavey fiercely cutting down the Turks, drew his sword, attacked him, and both fought fiercely.

Rakhkavey, stepping onto a stone, struck Amarbey on the shoulder with his sword with both hands and split him in two, for he had great strength in his arms.

The Turks, surrounding him in countless numbers, fought fiercely and cut Rakhkavey to pieces, and drove the Greeks into the city. And the Greeks had great weeping and horror for Rakhkavey, because he was a great warrior, valiant, and loved by the emperor.

And when night came, the slaughter ceased, and the adversaries withdrew.

And the Turks began to fire from cannons at the ruined place, while the townspeople began to widen and strengthen the tower along the whole breach and secretly rolled many cannons into it, because the tower was inside the city.
