He comes again to the tombs of his Orthodox forebears lying in the same church, the great princes of Vladimir and Novgorod, Grand Prince of All Rus Vladimir and his sons, the righteous passion-bearers Boris and Gleb, from Grand Prince Ivan Danilovich to his father Grand Prince Vasily and all his holy kinsmen, praying to them and saying: "O my holy parents and forebears and kinsmen, you have departed from here in body, but your souls are committed into the hands of God, and toward Him you have boldness; by your prayer help me against those who have fallen away from your Orthodox realm."

And going out from there, he goes around all the cathedral churches and monasteries, everywhere performing supplicatory services and giving sufficient alms.

After this he comes also to his father Philip, Metropolitan of All Rus, asking blessing and forgiveness; the hierarch signs him with the cross, and arms him with prayer, and blesses him and all his soldiers against the adversaries, as Samuel did David against Goliath.

And the great prince Ivan Vasilyevich, having received the blessing from his father Metropolitan Philip and from all the bishops of his land and from the whole sacred council, set out from Moscow in the same month of June on the 20th day, on Thursday, on the commemoration of the holy father Methodius, Bishop of Patara.

And with him Tsarevich Daniyar, many princes and boyars, and all the commanders, and the other soldiers of the great prince armed themselves with great force against the adversary, as previously his great-grandfather the right-believing Grand Prince Dmitry Ivanovich against the godless Mamai and his God-abhorrent Tatar host. So also this right-believing Grand Prince Ivan against these apostates, who although they called themselves Christians, their deeds were worse than those of unbelievers. For they always betrayed, transgressed the kissing of the cross, but worse than that they began to rage. As was written before, for five hundred and four years after holy baptism they were ruled by Orthodox Russian grand princes. But now, in the last times, twenty years before the end of the seventh thousand, they conceived to go over to the Latin king and wished to set up their archbishop from his metropolitan Gregory, a Latin. And the great prince sent to them many times, urging them to abandon such an undertaking; Philip the Metropolitan also sent to them, instructing them as his children with the words of God spoken in the Gospel: "If your brother sins against you, go and reprove him between you and him alone; if he hears you, you have gained your brother; but if he does not hear you, take with you two or three, for by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall be established; if he does not hear them, tell the Church; if he begins to disregard the Church, let him be to you as a Gentile and a tax collector." But these Novgorod people paid no attention to all that, but carried out their evil design. Is this not worse than unbelievers?
