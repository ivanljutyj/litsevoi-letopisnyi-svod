On the War against Lithuania and on the Capture of the Town of Masalsk

The Grand Prince sent the Vorotynsky princes, Prince Dmitry and Prince Semyon, against the Lithuanian land, against the town of Masalsk.

They set out thither, took the town, burned it, and conquered the land.

On the Arrival of the Mezetsky Princes

In the same year Prince Mikhail Romanovich Mezetsky came to serve the Grand Prince and, having captured them, brought with him two brothers, Prince Semyon and Prince Peter; and the Grand Prince sent them into confinement in Yaroslavl, while he granted Prince Mikhail his own patrimony and ordered him to serve him.

On the Arrival of Tsarevich Abdul-Letif

In the same winter, on January 11, Tsarevich Abdul-Letif, son of the Kazan Tsar Abreim, came from Crimea to serve the Grand Prince, together with the Grand Prince’s envoy Loban Kolychev.

On the Bishop of Sarsk

In the same winter the Bishop of Sarsk, Prokhor, left his bishopric and went from Krutitsy to the Epiphany Monastery.

On Prince Ivan Lukomsky

In the same winter, on January 31, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich executed Prince Ivan Lukomsky and the Latin interpreter Matias Lyakh, burning them in a cage on the Moscow River below the bridge; and he also executed two brothers from Smolensk, Bogdan and Olekhna Selevin, by public punishment in the marketplace. Bogdan died of that punishment, and Olekhna was beheaded, because they had sent their man Volyntsov with letters and news to the Grand Prince of Lithuania, Alexander.

King Casimir of Poland had sent Prince Ivan Lukomsky to serve the Grand Prince, and had made him kiss the cross on the condition that he kill the Grand Prince or poison him with poison, and he sent his poison with him; but that poison was taken from him. And Prince Ivan Lukomsky testified against Prince Fyodor Belsky, that he had wished to flee from the Grand Prince to Lithuania; and for that the Grand Prince ordered Prince Fyodor to be seized and sent him into confinement in Galich.

On the Fire in Kostroma

In the same spring, on Palm Saturday, the entire town of Kostroma burned.

On the Fire in Moscow

In the same spring, on April 16, on Radonitsa, the entire city of Moscow burned from within; only the Grand Prince’s new court behind the Cathedral of the Archangel Michael remained; and in the Chudov Monastery the treasury burned down.
