And, having considered the deceit of the Tatars and their wooden breastworks, behind which many townspeople were sheltering, the sovereign ordered them to be undermined.

On the same month of September, on the 30th day, Friday

The sovereign ordered gunpowder to be placed under the breastworks to blow them up, and ordered siege towers to be set up along the moat opposite the Tsar’s and Arsk gates, and by the sovereign’s command the commanders of those regiments that were at those towers prepared for battle; to other regiments the tsar forbade an assault on the city.

The powder was lit, and it blew the breastworks with Kazan people up to a great height, and from that height beams fell into the city, and a great many Tatars were killed; the townspeople were filled with fear and horror, so that for a long time no arrows flew from them.

The commanders stood along the moat opposite the Tsar’s gates and the Arsk, Atalyk, and also Tyumen gates, along the whole moat, and secured the towers.

The townspeople gathered from all places and came out from all the gates, and fought fiercely; and the tsar’s warriors pressed hard against the city and cut down the Tatars.

The pious tsar himself rode out to the city. And the warriors saw their tsar, and at once all rushed upon the city and fought courageously against the infidels in the city places and at the gates, and also on the walls; from the wall-breaking cannons and with fiery and stone balls from above, and from the arquebuses the streltsy fired without ceasing; the warriors fought with spears and sabers and hand to hand. And the battle was fierce and terrible, and there was a great thunder from the cannon fire and from the cries of people, and from the crack of the arquebuses, and from the multitude of fire and smoke. And the smoke thickened and covered the city and the people. God helped the Christians.

And the Christians were already on the walls of the city gates and in the tower of the city on the side of the Arsk field.

The pious tsar, seeing his warriors fighting on the walls and in the city, and Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky informs the sovereign that in the city of Kazan, God willing, the Christians have killed many Tatars, and the tsar orders an assault to begin from all sides. But the army in the other regiments was not prepared for that day.

And the tsar ordered the commanders, and also sends many yesauls, and orders the people to be called back from the city and from the walls. But the warriors did not want to retreat from the infidels, and with difficulty they were called back; the city wall, the gates, and the bridges were set on fire, and they secured themselves in the tower, on the walls of the city and at the Arsk gates.
