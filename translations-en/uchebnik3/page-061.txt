The patriarch said: “Since the pious tsar has permitted me to eat meat, who are you to forbid me to eat meat? And now I have no other consolation but this one, to enjoy the smell of meat.” And all this my spiritual father Kyr Luke, Metropolitan of Anavarza, told me; and I feared that no one else knew it, and before I die I decided to set forth what I know.”

And many others also told me about this patriarch that he ate meat in secret, not in the presence of many people; and I learned that the meaning of that message was to denounce the passion for meat and many other sins—concerning the use of blood and the meat of strangled animals, which the Franks eat. He writes to the Patriarch of Constantinople that “blood is consumed in the Greek land and in Constantinople itself, and if you cannot forbid this in the Greek land and in Constantinople itself, how can you tell a Frank to stop eating the meat of strangled animals?” And both he and you know that each person has a passionate and fleshly thought and compulsion, as it is also said in Scripture, that each is drawn away and enticed by his own passion; and the Lord also says: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, for you yourselves do not enter the Kingdom of Heaven and you forbid others,” and it is also said of those who received talents and could not find a worthy use for them: “Wicked servant, you ought to have given My silver to the money-changers, and I, having come, would have received it with interest.” And interpreting this saying, the divine Chrysostom says: “It is better for you to learn and to speak, and for me to leave the profit”; and he calls the profit the work of repentance.

Saint Maximus also says: “Many tell me that they can do little; but no one should hide the word of God because of his laziness, but one must confess one’s weakness without hiding God’s truth, so that we may not be guilty of distorting the word of God and transgressing the commandments. And therefore his message speaks passionately about those who do this and compel others to it. The first and necessary law is to turn away from things sacrificed to idols, from fornication and blood, and from eating the meat of strangled animals, since the Roman pope also condescends to such people, and all fall into the same pit. But our fathers did not do this; yet when we do not obey them, we withdraw from there, as it was written, and we will learn to hear from disobedient and foretelling people: ‘You who are being saved, save your soul.’”

As Saint Eustathius the metropolitan did, when because of disobedience he renounced his people and went into silence—he was metropolitan of Sebaste—and did not silently accept the sin of the people, as people sometimes do.
