And on that same day many Novgorodians, petitioners, and all kinds of prosperous people, and the people of Rusa, and monastic people, and others from the nearby districts of Novgorod came to petition the Grand Prince, and some to ask for bailiffs so that they would not be plundered by his soldiers; and others with complaints against their own brethren, the Novgorodians, each about his own affair; for this land had lived by its own will for many years, and had disregarded that they were the patrimony of the grand princes and had not obeyed them, and much evil had been in that land among themselves—murders and robberies, and the needless ruin of households by them, as each was able against another.

In the same month of November, on the 23rd day, a Thursday, the Grand Prince entered Novgorod. And Archbishop Theophilus with all the consecrated council in priestly vestments, and all the archimandrites and abbots, priests and deacons, and the whole priestly and monastic order of that city met the Grand Prince with crosses, as the Grand Prince himself had commanded them, not exalting himself; and after them—a great multitude of posadniks and tysyatskys, and boyars, and prosperous people, and all the sovereign Great Novgorod, and people without rights or rank. And the archbishop blessed the Grand Prince with the cross.

After that he again enters with crosses and icons, and with all the consecrated council, into the church of the Wisdom of God, Holy Sophia; and the Grand Prince after him entered the same church. And a prayer service was celebrated, and he crossed himself before the image of the Lord and of His Most Pure Mother, and of the other saints, and also at the tombs of his forebears, the former grand princes, lying there. The archbishop then celebrated the liturgy with all the consecrated council; and the Grand Prince stood at that service, praying tenderly and decorously and asking forgiveness of his sins from the Lord God and His Most Pure Mother, calling also the saints who had pleased his Lord Jesus Christ to his aid.

After the service was completed, the Grand Prince went to dine with the archbishop.

He ate with him and drank merrily; and the archbishop bestowed many gifts on the Grand Prince.

And the Grand Prince, having made merry, rode to his own place at Gorodishche; and the archbishop rode after him with great honor and with wine. And the gifts of the lord archbishop were: three bolts of Flemish cloth, one hundred gold coins, a walrus tusk; and for the farewell—a barrel of red wine and another of white.
