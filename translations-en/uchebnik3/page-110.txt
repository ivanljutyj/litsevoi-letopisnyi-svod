He kissed it and said: “God gave you to the world as tsar; why did you perish in vain?” And he sent it to the patriarch, so that he might encase it in silver and gold and keep it as he knew best.

The patriarch took it, placed it in a silver and gilded reliquary, and hid it in the Great Church beneath the altar. From some we heard that those remaining of those who had been with the tsar at the Golden Gate stole it that same night and carried it to Galata and kept it.

Concerning the tsarina, who had passed through a great trial, they told the sultan that the Grand Duke and the Grand Domestic, and the anaktos, and Andrew the son of the protostrator, and his nephew Asan Thomas Palaiologos, and Nicholas the city eparch, had sent the tsarina off by ship; and Mehmed at once, after torturing them, ordered them put to death. So it was and so it came to pass for our sins.

The lawless Mehmed sat on the tsar’s throne, the noblest of all that have been under the sun and that ruled two parts of the universe, having overcome those who once defeated proud Artaxerxes, who led warriors through the sea depths over the whole breadth of the earth and destroyed those who had destroyed wondrous Troy, defended by seventy-four kings.

But understand, O wretched one, if all that was said before by Methodius of Patara and Leo the Wise has come to pass, and the signs concerning this city have been fulfilled, then the last thing will not pass by either, but will also come to pass; for it is written: “The Russian race will defeat the ancient race of the Ishmaelites and will take the Seven-Hilled City with its former laws, and will reign in it.”

On the Death of Grand Princess Sophia

In the same year, on the 15th day of the month of June, Sophia, wife of Grand Prince Vasily Dmitrievich, died, being in the monastic rank, and was laid to rest that same day in the Ascension Monastery in Moscow, where her mother-in-law, Grand Princess Evdokia, named Euphrosyne in the monastic rank, had also been laid.

In the same month, July, on the 23rd day, On the Death of Shemyaka

News came to the Grand Prince from Novgorod at Vespers at the church of the great martyrs Boris and Gleb in Moscow on the Moat, that Prince Dmitry Shemyaka had died in Novgorod and was laid in Yuryev Monastery; and the undersecretary Vasily Beda came rushing with that news, and from that time he became a clerk.
