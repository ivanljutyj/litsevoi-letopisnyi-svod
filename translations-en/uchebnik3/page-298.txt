At the crossing, thirty versts below Kazan, in the ship army were Prince Ivan Ivanovich Shchetina Strigin and Fedor Yuryevich Shchuka, and Prince Fedor Sitsky Krivoy.

And on the Kama were Prince Semyon Fedorovich Kurbsky, Prince Alexander Alenka, Ivan Veniaminov Brekh, and Prince Ivan Golenishche Andomsky.

And in the cavalry army, in the large regiment, were Prince Alexander Vladimirovich Rostovsky; and together with Prince Alexander were the voivode of Prince Yuri, Mikhail Elizarov son Gusev, and the voivode of Prince Dmitry, Vasily Alekseev son Davidov, and the voivode of Prince Fedor Borisovich, Prince Grigory Meshchersky. And in the advanced regiment were Prince Peter Semyonovich Ryapolovsky Loban, and in the advanced regiment of the right hand was Tsarevich Zenaley. And in the right hand was Prince Vasily Semyonovich Mnikh, and in the left hand were Prince Fedor Semyonovich Striga and Andrei Ivanovich Korobov.

And Prince Dmitry Ivanovich and the voivodes of the Grand Prince and the ship army came to Kazan on the twenty-second day of May, on Friday, and came out of the vessels onto the suburban field carelessly, quickly and without looking around, and went to the city on foot; and the day was then very hot.

And the Tatars came out from the city against them, while other Tatars, hiding from the vessels, rode up on horses. And the battle began, and for our sins the Tatars killed the foot voivodes and many boyar sons who were in the field, and took others captive, and many drowned in Poganoye Lake.

And on the ninth day of June Prince Vasily Golenin came to the Grand Prince with that news, and Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of all Rus sent that same day his voivodes to Kazan with an army, Prince Vasily Danilovich Kholmsky and other voivodes; and to his brother Prince Dmitry Ivanovich and to the voivodes he sent a letter, ordering them to wait for Prince Vasily Danilovich and the other voivodes of the Grand Prince, and not to assault the city without them.

And Prince Alexander Vladimirovich and the other voivodes with the cavalry army went to Kazan on the twenty-second day of June.

And Prince Dmitry Ivanovich with the voivodes of the Grand Prince, not waiting for Prince Vasily Danilovich and the other voivodes and not obeying the command of the Grand Prince, on the twenty-fifth day of June began to assault the city carelessly; and although they killed many Tatars, they did no harm to the city, and were themselves defeated by the Tatars.

And Prince Dmitry Ivanovich with the voivodes of the Grand Prince soon fled from Kazan to Novgorod, pursued by no one, only because of our sins, and they lost many people.
