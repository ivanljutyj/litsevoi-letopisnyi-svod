The Grand Prince, having spent St Peter’s Day in Moscow and having prepared the city for a siege, left in it his mother Sophia, and his son Prince Yuri, and many boyars and boyars’ sons, and above all his father Metropolitan Jonah and Ephrem, Archbishop of Rostov, and the whole priestly and monastic order, and a great multitude of the people of Moscow; while he himself went out of the city of Moscow with his son, Grand Prince Ivan; and he sent his Grand Princess with the younger children to Uglich. And he himself spent that night at Ozeretskoye and from there went to the Volga.

And the Tatars, having come, halted at the bank, waiting for an army to oppose them, but there was none. They thought that the army had hidden itself, having left the bank free for them, and sent guards to the other side of the Oka. These searched everywhere and found nothing, and, returning, said that there was no one opposing them.

They crossed the Oka River and rushed toward Moscow. They came to it on Friday, the second day of July, on the feast of the Deposition of the Robe of the Most Holy Theotokos, at the first hour of the day.

And they set all the suburbs on fire in a single hour, and at that time they themselves began to assault the city from all sides. And then there was a great drought, and fire encompassed the city from all sides, and the churches caught fire, and because of the smoke nothing could be seen; and they attacked the city suddenly, at all the gates and where there were no stone fortifications. And the city was in great grief and sorrow and in much perplexity. Having no help from anywhere, but only with tears they prayed to the Lord God and to His most pure Mother, the strong helper and intercessor with her Son and our God; especially since her feast had then come, and also that of the great wonderworkers.

When the suburbs had burned out, those in the city received rest from the great suffering from fire and smoke, and, going out of the city, began to fight with the adversaries. And in the dusk the Tatars withdrew from the city, and the townspeople in the morning began to prepare the city’s defenses against the godless: cannons, arquebuses, crossbows, weapons, shields, bows and arrows, as is fitting for battle with the enemy. When the sun rose, the townspeople, prepared for defense, saw no one; going out of the city, they searched here and there and saw no one.

And they sent messengers to their camps; these, having come there, found no one. Then they took all the heavy things that the Tatars had abandoned, of copper and iron, and a great quantity of other goods, and extinguished the fire. For the Tatars had withdrawn from the city, seized with fear and trembling; and as if sensing some great host behind them, they fled, driven by the wrath of God and by the prayer of His most holy Mother and the supplication of the great wonderworkers and all the saints.
