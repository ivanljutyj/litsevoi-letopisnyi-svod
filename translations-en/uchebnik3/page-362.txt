And the sovereign would also graciously return the Kazan captives. And we, on coming to Vasil-gorod, will send from ourselves a letter to Kazan, and to the Mountain Cheremis, the Meadow Cheremis, and the Ar princes concerning the sovereign’s favour: that the sovereign wishes to favour them and protect them with his care, and as the sovereign’s favour was in the time of Tsar Mahomet-Amin, so now the sovereign wishes to favour them and protect the land of Kazan.”

Fedor and Menshoi Putyata recounted those words to the grand prince, and the grand prince ordered that the envoys be told: “You petition us concerning the tsar because you came to us from the tsar; but did you have an instruction about this from the princes and from the land, that we should graciously grant the land of Kazan and give them Shigalei as tsar?”

And the envoys said: “We had no instruction about that; but with what business we were sent to the sovereign, that is what we petitioned the sovereign about; and we wish that the sovereign would favour us and order us to serve him; but we do not wish to serve the Kazan tsar: we died with the tsar, but revived with the sovereign’s favour. The tsar sent us about great matters, and everything we did he disregarded, and sent us away; and if we are not needed by the tsar, then the tsar is not needed by us. And in Kazan we have relatives, brothers, and friends. And the Kazan people who are detained in Nizhny Novgorod, and those people who fell into the hands of the grand prince’s men—all those people have fathers and brothers, relatives and friends in Kazan. And as soon as we come to Vasil-gorod and send letters to them, those people will stand for us.”

And Fedor and Menshoi Putyata told this to the grand prince, and the grand prince consulted with the boyars about it, and ordered Postnik Golovin to be sent to Kazan to the tsar, and instructed him to find out from Kachigalei Murza and Prince Bulat: what do they think? And he ordered a deadline to be set—that his messenger be with him on the 25th day.

And the grand prince himself went to the Life-Giving Trinity in the St Sergius Monastery to pray at the miracle-working memory, with Grand Princess Elena and his son Prince Ivan, on the 17th day of September of the fortieth year, a Sunday, and from there to his royal hunt, to Volok and Mozhaisk. And he came to Moscow on the 19th day of November, a Sunday. And the grand prince consulted with the boyars about whether he should release Tsar Shigalei and the envoys to Nizhny Novgorod.

And that same autumn, on the 10th day of December, the grand prince released Tsar Shigalei and the envoys to Nizhny Novgorod.
