Having divided up. And, God granting, they fought and took many uluses, and killed and captured many people; and the Tatars who gathered and came against them—many of those they killed with arquebuses.

And, God granting, they withdrew safe and sound to the island of Otzyb. And the Tsar with his children, his princes, and murzas, having gathered, went after Danila; but many people did not hurry to gather to him, for by God’s providence fear and dread fell upon them before our Orthodox sovereign Tsar.

And Danila and his comrades came from the sea to Ochakov at the mouth of the Dnieper, God granting, safe with all his people and with captives—Crimean, Russian, and Lithuanian—whom he had captured and taken in the uluses. But the people of the Turkish Tsar whom they had taken on ships and at crossings he released to Ochakov and ordered them to say that the Tsar and sovereign had sent him to fight against the uluses of his enemy the Crimean Tsar and henceforth to scout the road to Crimea; but with the Turkish Tsar our sovereign is in friendship and did not order war with him. And Danila learned that among them were Turkish people whom the Ochakov rulers, agas, and sanjaks had taken on ships, and they came to Danila and his comrades and brought them much fodder and showed great honor to the sovereign’s people, according to the sovereign’s favor and justice.

And from there they went up the Dnieper, and the Crimean Tsar began to attack them in many places, but he did not manage to do them any harm, and, God granting, they beat them off everywhere with arquebuses.

And the Tsar decided to attack Danila at the crossings from both sides, but Danila and his comrades, God granting, passed the rapids, and the Tsar did not manage to do him any evil.

And Danila came to Monastyrsky Island, God granting, safe. And here Fedko Ershevsky, who had served the Tsar in Crimea, ran over to Danila from the Tsar and said that the Tsar had been following Danila for the sixth week and everywhere wanted to attack Danila, but God did not fulfill his evil desire. The Tsar was enraged and now wants to come to Monastyrsky Island, and all the Crimean and Nogai people have already been gathered by the Tsar.

And Danila sent Nechay Rtishchev with comrades to scout concerning the Tsar; and Nechay came upon the Tsar’s camps fifteen versts from Monastyrsky Island, but (he found no one there, because) when Fedko fled from him, the Tsar had hastily gone away.

The Tsar and Grand Prince, having learned of this, gave thanks to God, seeing His ineffable mercies upon the Christian race, and announced this to the Metropolitan, ordering a moleben to be served. But before this, from the time the Crimean Yurt came into being, when the impious Basurmans settled on that Korsun Island, the Russian saber had never been in the impious dwellings.
