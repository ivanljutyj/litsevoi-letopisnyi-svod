A truce with the Grand Prince for seven years from the day of the Annunciation in the year 7050 from Adam (1550 from Christ) to the day of the Annunciation in the year 7057 from Adam (1557 from Christ).

And having honored the Lithuanian envoys, the Grand Prince dismissed them on March 23.

In the same month Kovgorshad-tsarevna sent a charter to the great sovereign in Moscow with Luka Pukhov, a man of Teterin, and she wrote in the charter that the Grand Prince should desire peace with the tsar; and Prince Bulat sent a charter with the same man to the boyar Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky and to all the boyars, that they should speak to the Grand Prince about peace with Kazan.

On the Coming of the Crimean Tsarevich to the Seversk Lands in War

In the same month the Crimean tsarevich Minli-Girey came with many people to the Seversk lands, to Putivl and Starodub and Novgorod-Seversky.

And by God's mercy the Grand Prince's commanders beat many Tatars and took captives from them, and sent twenty Tatars to Moscow to the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, and killed others.

And the tsarevich, having ravaged the Seversk land, went away from the borderlands.

On the Grand Prince's Journey to the Trinity

In the same month on the 25th day, on Thursday of the seventh week after Easter, the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus set out to the Life-Giving Trinity in the Sergius Monastery to pray, and with him were his brother Prince Yury Vasilyevich and Prince Vladimir Andreyevich and many princes and boyars; and he came to Moscow in the same month on the 31st day.

On the Installation of the Archbishop

That same spring, on June 18, on the second Sunday of the Peter's Fast, Theodosius, archimandrite of the Yuryev Monastery of Great Novgorod, was installed by Metropolitan Macarius as archbishop of Great Novgorod and Pskov.

In the same month on the 26th the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus sent his envoys to Lithuania to King Sigismund: his boyar Vasily Grigoryevich Morozov and the dvoretsky of Uglich and Kaluga Fyodor Semyonovich Vorontsov, and the dyak Postnik Gubin, son of Moklokov, with truce charters, on which the king and the king's son were to kiss the cross and affix their seals to those charters. In the same month on the 26th the Grand Prince's envoy from Kazan, Istoma Martynov, arrived from Tsar Safa-Girey; and together with him the tsar sent to the Grand Prince his man Isenkildey with a charter, and he wrote in the charter about peace.
