To Semyon Vasilyevich Sheremetev, with the boyar Prince Vasily Semyonovich Serebryany to assist him. And to the Tyumen Gate, to voivode Dmitry Mikhailovich Pleshcheev, with voivode Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Mikulinsky to assist him. And the sovereign ordered everyone to prepare by the third hour of Sunday.

The Tsar Alone with His Spiritual Father; and on Arming Himself

And that night, from Saturday to Sunday, the sovereign was alone with his spiritual father, Archpriest Andrei; and when he began to arm himself and put on his chain mail, he heard many bells and said to those close to him: “I hear bells, it seems, like the ringing of Simonov Monastery.” And the tsar understood in his heart that God’s grace would be upon him, and the sovereign went to the church and began to sing Matins.

On Placing Gunpowder beneath the City

And Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky sent word to the sovereign: “The engineer has placed gunpowder beneath the city, but they saw him from the city, and it is impossible to delay until the third hour.”

The pious tsar then sends to all the regiments to announce that all should soon prepare for battle.

The sovereign himself went to the church and ordered the prayer rule to be completed quickly, while he shed many tears from his eyes, asking God for mercy; dawn was approaching.

And the tsar sent the voivodes of his royal regiment, boyar Prince Vladimir Ivanovich and Ivan Vasilyevich Sheremetev, and those of other regiments, and ordered them to take their appointed places at the city and await his royal arrival.

On the Sovereign’s Hearing of the Divine Liturgy

And the tsar himself ordered the Liturgy to begin, desiring to touch the holy things and, having completed the Liturgy, to render unto God the things that are God’s and ride to his regiment. The Liturgy began; it was fearful and worthy of compunction to see at that time the pious tsar standing in church armed, his armor covered by nothing, but like all those with him, armed and prepared for the hour of death for piety.

The Tsar’s Prayer

The pious tsar himself gazed earnestly at the image of Christ our God, and at the Mother of God who gave birth to Him, and at His great saint Sergius. And then he approaches the icon of the wonderworker, which was painted from the living lamp, secretly in his heart continually sending up prayers...
