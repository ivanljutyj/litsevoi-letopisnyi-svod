He thought: “I do not know what to do, but I will place my hope in the one Lord God.” And after pondering this for a long time, he announced to his father, Metropolitan Philip, and to his mother, Grand Princess Maria, and to the boyars who were with him, that he wished to march with an army against Novgorod. When they heard this, they advised him, having placed his hope in God, to carry out his intention against the Novgorodians because of their violation of the treaty and their defection.

At that same hour the grand prince sent for all his brothers, and for all the bishops of his land, and for his princes and boyars, and for his commanders, and for all his warriors.

And when all had gathered to him, he then announced to all his intention, that he wished to march with an army against Novgorod, for in everything they had broken faith, and no truth at all was to be found in them. “But should we go against them now, or not go? Since it is already summer time, and in their land there is much water, and great lakes, and rivers, and many impassable marshes.

And former grand princes did not go against them at that time, and whoever did go lost many people.” And they pondered this not a little, and began to put their hope in the Lord God and His most pure Mother, and in the power of the precious and life-giving cross, whose kiss the Novgorodians had broken.

And the grand prince, having received the blessing from Metropolitan Philip, and also from all the hierarchs of his land and from the whole consecrated council, began to arm himself to go against them; and so did his brothers, and all his princes, and his boyars, commanders, and all his warriors. To Novgorod he sent letters of renunciation because of their violation of the treaty; and to Tver he sent to Grand Prince Michael, asking for help against the Novgorodians.

And to Pskov he sent his clerk Yakushka Shabaltsev on the twenty-third day of May, on the feast of the Ascension of the Lord, saying to them: “My patrimony, Great Novgorod, is falling away from me to the king, and they wish to appoint their archbishop with his metropolitan Gregory, a Latin, and I, the grand prince, am going against them with an army, and I have renounced the oath with them; and you, my patrimony, the Pskovians, the posadniks and wealthy townsmen, and all the land of Pskov, should also renounce your oath to your brother Novgorod and go against them with an army under my commander, Prince Fyodor Yuryevich Shuysky, or with his son Prince Vasily.”

On the thirty-first day of the month of May, a Friday, the grand prince sent Boris Slepts to Vyatka, ordering them to march with an army against the Dvina land. And to Vasily Fyodorovich Obrazets he sent to Ustyug, so that with the Ustyuzhans he would march with an army to the Dvina, against the Novgorodian dependent towns and all their volosts and pogosts, after waiting for Boris with the Vyatchans and Vologzhans.
