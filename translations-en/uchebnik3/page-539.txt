To the pious tsar and to all Christians. The sovereign, chosen by God, came into the church, uttered many prayers with tears in secret, and declares openly: “What shall I render to Thee, O Master, for Thy blessings, save tears and a contrite heart? Merciful Master Christ! Grant complete deliverance to poor Christendom; not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to Thy name give glory.”

On the Moving of the Siege Towers to the Moat

He affectionately embraces his commanders and permits them to kiss his hand, and exhorts them with great words of favor, honoring their bravery and courage; likewise the sovereign rewards all his soldiers according to their rank.

On the Tower

In the same month the tsar ordered his secretary Ivan Vyrodkov to set up a tower by Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky’s siege towers, opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And they erected the tower six sazhens high, and placed upon it many cannon, one-and-a-half pishchals, and wall guns; and many streltsy with pishchals stood there and shot into the city along the streets and at the city walls; and they killed many people.

The ungodly hid in pits and ditches, dug under the city gates and under the wall, and dug burrows under the timber breastworks, for at every gate beyond the moat they had great earth-filled timber breastworks.

And from behind the breastworks they fought every day, and from the burrows they crawled out like snakes and fought without ceasing day and night.

On the Moving of the Siege Towers to the Moat

The pious sovereign tsar and grand prince ordered Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky to move the siege towers to the moat opposite the Arsk Tower and the Arsk Gates, to the breastworks opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And Prince Mikhail for more than one day moved the siege towers and drew near the moat, asking mercy of God, with great intelligence and courage.

The Tatars fought fiercely, unwilling to let the Orthodox draw near; but the tsar’s commanders, trusting in God, advanced toward the moat at the sovereign’s command.

On Sunday the sovereign ordered Prince Mikhail to take position with the siege towers along the moat and by the breastworks opposite the Tsar’s and Arsk Gates; and at the sovereign’s command streltsy and Cossacks came, and officers with boyar retainers, and stood along the moat and fought; and many fell on both sides.
