In the year from Adam (1571 from Christ), on November 3, Prince Temryuk was at that time in Astrakhan with his son Domanuk; and Prince Temryuk and his son Domanuk-murza came to the Circassians on December 6, and Grigory came to the Circassians with them, and with him was the strelets head Grigory Vrazhsky, and with him five hundred streltsy, and five Cossack atamans with Cossacks; and with them were five hundred Cossacks.

And Temryuk, together with the sovereign's people, ceased hostilities with his foe and brought them under his will, and he warred against Shepshuk's uluses, and warred against the Tatar lands near the Skinsky towns, and took three towns: the town of Mokhan, the town of Engir, the town of Kavan; they killed Murza Telishka and beat many people. And those towns belonged to Prince Shepshuk; and the people of those towns submitted to Prince Temryuk, and Prince Temryuk imposed tribute on them; and he warred against their land for eleven days. And they took one hundred and sixty-four Mshan and Son settlements, and beat many people and took captives. They took four murzas: Burnat, Ezdnour, Burnak, and Dudyl; and Temryuk sent Grigory back to the tsar and grand prince.

In the same month, October, on the 4th day, the tsar and grand prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilievich, sent to Crimea to Tsar Devlet-Girei the Crimean envoys Yanboldy and Tetmildesh, who had been detained in Moscow. And they had been in Moscow for six years. And he sent his envoy Fyodor Ignatiev, son of Saltykov, and clerk Melety Ivanov, and ordered them to stay in Kaluga and await news; and when there should be news from the tsar about his envoys, then they were to go to Putivl and exchange envoys beyond Putivl on the Seim River. But the exchange did not take place then.

In the same month, October, on the 24th day, there came to the tsar and grand prince from the Nogais the envoys of the tsar and grand prince, Mikhailo Kolupaev, son of Priklonsky, and Ivan Vasiliev, son of Bezsonov, and Mikhailo Ivanov, son of Shishelov, and Dmitry Kurov, son of Nepeitsyn. The sovereign had sent them to the Nogais on embassy: Mikhail Kolupaev to Prince Ismail; Ivan Bezsonov to Tinakhmat-murza, son of Ismail; Mikhail Shishelov to Urus-murza, also son of Ismail; Dmitry Kurov to Tinalei-murza, son of Kushum.
