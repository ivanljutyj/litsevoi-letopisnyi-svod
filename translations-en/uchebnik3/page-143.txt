After the repose of this far-seeing and most holy Archbishop Jonah, the people of Novgorod, according to the old way and their own custom, established a veche and began to choose from among the hieromonks a candidate for the archbishopric. Having chosen three, they cast lots, and the lot fell upon a certain hieromonk named Theophilus, and they brought him to the archbishop’s court.

They sent their envoy Nikita Larionov to petition Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and to ask permission to travel, so that he might grant their named monk Theophilus and command him to come to him in Moscow, and command his father Metropolitan Philip to consecrate him to the archbishopric of Great Novgorod and Pskov, as had been done before under the former grand princes. The grand prince, according to their petition and request, remembered nothing of the old grievances and was not harsh, but having granted and honored their envoy, he fulfilled all that the whole of Novgorod and Pskov had petitioned him for, giving them this answer: “My patrimony, Great Novgorod, has sent to petition me concerning that God has taken their father, our man of prayer, Archbishop Jonah, and they have chosen for themselves, according to their custom and by lot, the hieromonk Theophilus. I, the grand prince, grant them and your chosen one, Theophilus. I command him to come to Moscow to me and to my father Metropolitan Philip, so that he may be consecrated to the archbishopric of Novgorod and Pskov without any hindrances, but according to the former custom, as it was under my father Grand Prince Vasily, and under my grandfather, and my great-grandfather, and under all the former grand princes of Vladimir, of Great Novgorod, and of all Rus.”

Of the Novgorod Sedition and Their Turmoil

That same envoy of theirs, Nikita Larionov, came to them in Novgorod and told them of the grand prince’s favor. Many of the best people there, their posadniks and tysyatskys, and men of substance were glad of this, as was their named Theophilus. But some of them, the sons of posadniks, Isaac Boretsky with his mother Martha and with other traitors besides, taught by the devil, who were worse than demons, instigators to the ruin of their land and to their own destruction. They began to speak absurd and cunning words and came to the veche shouting: “We do not want the Grand Prince of Moscow, nor to be called his patrimony; we are free people, Great Novgorod, and the Grand Prince of Moscow commits many offenses and injustices against us, and we want the King of Poland and Grand Prince of Lithuania Casimir.” And so the whole city was stirred up, and they wavered like drunken men: some wanted the grand prince, according to the old way, to be with Moscow, and others wanted the king, to be with Lithuania. Those traitors began to hire poor common men who were ready for anything, according to their custom, and they came to the veche, rang all the bells, and some began to shout: “We want the king!”; while others shouted to them: “We want the Grand Prince of Moscow according to the old way, as it was before!”
