On the arrival of the Tatars from Crimea.
In the year 7062 from Adam (the year 1562 from Christ)

In September the tsar and grand prince's Tatars, Senka Tutaev and his companions, came from Crimea, and with them Romashko Lukyanov, a man of Putivl, who had been captured together with Myagky Bityagovsky; and together with them came the tsar's messenger Talyshman-Atalyk and his companions. And Senka said that the tsar had released Stupa, Myagky Bityagovsky, and the boyar's sons of Putivl, and together with them had sent his envoy Shagmansyrulan and companions to the tsar and grand prince; and they rode together with the envoys as far as Dumchy Kurgan, and from Dumchy Kurgan they rode ahead, and Stupa and the envoys would soon be at Putivl.

On the dispatch of the voivodes to Kazan

In September the sovereign sent his voivodes to Kazan against the Ar traitors: in the large regiment — the boyar and voivode Prince Semyon Ivanovich Mikulinsky and the boyar Petr Vasilyevich Morozov; in the forward regiment — the boyar Ivan Vasilyevich Sheremetev and the okolnichy and oruzhnichy Lev Andreevich Saltykov; and in the guard regiment — the voivode Prince Andrei Mikhailovich Kurbsky and Mikhail Volynsky Voronoy. And the sovereign ordered them to set out by the first winter route. And they set out from Nizhny Novgorod on St Nicholas's Day.

On the sending of envoys to the King

In the same year the tsar, sovereign, and grand prince sent his envoys to the King — the boyar Vasily Mikhailovich Yuryev, the treasurer Fedor Ivanovich Sukin, and the clerk Ishuk Bukharin — to conclude a truce.

They were with the King, concluded the truce, brought the King to swear to the truce charters, and returned in the same year.
