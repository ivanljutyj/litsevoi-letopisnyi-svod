Well then: "You petitioned us and gave an oath that the tsar, the princes, and all the Kazan princes would be obedient to us in all things; but the tsar, the princes, and the whole land of Kazan, by your own petition, did not keep the oath. And Prince Tabai said to us: 'A messenger of our sovereign told us that news came to Kazan that the sovereign Grand Prince is sending his army to Kazan, and that is why they did not fulfill the oath. But you yourselves know that in Kazan there are few good people; all are petty folk, and therefore there is no one to strengthen the land; the people are all divided. And out of great fear the people have lost confidence and do not accept our plan. The sovereign Grand Prince will himself provide for his land; in his land God and the sovereign are free. The land of Kazan is God's and yours, sovereign's; as he wills, so he will do, and we are God's slaves and yours, sovereign's. And we have been sent to the Grand Prince to petition from the tsar and from the princes and from all the people of the land of Kazan with truth, not with falsehood. And whatever we petition the sovereign about, the sovereign ought to do likewise throughout the whole land. But now the tsar has not stood by his word: what he sent us to petition the great sovereign about, he has all violated, and has forgotten us, and has not wished to fulfill that on which we swore. And we think thus: the Crimeans and Nogais and the local wicked men have emboldened him; but the land is decidedly not with him, and they dare not follow him, because it may be that the sovereign does not need that tsar in Kazan. And they await the sovereign's decision from here: whether that tsar is to be in Kazan, or the sovereign will grant another tsar and send him to Kazan.'"

And Fedor Karpov and Menshoi Putyata recounted everything to the Grand Prince. And the Grand Prince sent to them Fedor and Menshoi Putyata and ordered them to say: "Our sovereign has ordered us to tell you: if the tsar were obedient to us and had kept his word, why then would I not wish him to be in Kazan? But you see yourselves that he is not honest. The former tsar did much evil: without our knowledge and without consulting the Kazan princes, he killed them and committed many other wrongs. But our sovereign, at your petition, showed favor to the tsar, wished to make him one with himself and to call him his son. But our sovereign should not have done so. Our sovereign, protecting the land of Kazan and favoring the Kazan people, acted so that all would be good and there would be peace in the land. And the tsar does not believe the word of our sovereign and does not keep his word." And the envoys said: "We see ourselves that the tsar does not wish to be honorable toward our sovereign, the Grand Prince; he has broken his oath and scorned his obligation, and has forgotten us and what he instructed with us; and what good can be expected from him? But now God and the sovereign know how he has planned to arrange his land of Kazan: we are your slaves, sovereign's; and whichever tsar the sovereign grants us, that one is pleasing to us." And they petitioned, "that the sovereign would show favor and give us Tsar Shigalei, because Tsar Shigalei did no harm to the land of Kazan, but wicked men did such things to him.
