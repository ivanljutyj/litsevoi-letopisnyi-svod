The Instruction of Theophilus

But even before this and after this, the venerable hieromonk Theophilus, named to the archiepiscopate, also strongly forbade them these wicked designs and commanded them to abandon this evil undertaking. But they did not listen to his words; and because of that he wished to go to a monastery and live in silence in a cell, but they would not let him. But the Novgorod boyars and posadniks, tysyatskys, and many other townspeople who did not wish to change the ancient custom or break the kiss of the cross, having heard the words of the grand prince’s envoys and the metropolitan’s blessing and instruction, and the prohibitions of their Theophilus, named to the archiepiscopate, rejoiced with great joy. And they wished to return to the grand prince according to the ancient custom, according to the old tradition. But the aforementioned children of Isaac with their mother Martha and with their other supporters and hired men seemed to have gone mad or to have fallen into a bestial, inhuman mind. They not only refused to hear the speeches of the grand prince’s envoys and of the envoy of Metropolitan Philip, but also hired wicked peasants, murderers, troublemakers, and other nameless muzhiks, like cattle, having no reason, but only one cry; even an irrational beast does not roar as do the ignorant Novgorod people who call themselves Sovereign Great Novgorod. And coming to the veche, they ring the bells and shout and bark like dogs and cry out absurdly: “We want the King!” And such was their tumult as there was in ancient Jerusalem when the Lord delivered it into the hands of Titus: as those were then, so these made war among themselves.

The grand prince, hearing of this, was grieved and lamented greatly for them. Such a thing they had not done even when they were not in Orthodoxy: from Rurik to Grand Prince Vladimir they did not fall away, desiring another sovereign. And from Vladimir to this day they have known one lineage of his and in all things were governed by grand princes, first of Kiev, then of Vladimir.
