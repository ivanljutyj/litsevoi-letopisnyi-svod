The regiment had wavered, but at that time Prince Semyon Ivanovich Punkov-Mikulinsky and Prince Vasily Semyonovich Serebryany-Obolensky came up soon with their regiment and stood on the bank.

After that Prince Mikhailo Ivanovich Kubensky came with his regiment, and then Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Shuysky stood with his regiment, and Prince Dmitry Fyodorovich Belsky also stood with many men of his regiment.

And the Tsar saw that the great regiments were coming, and the regiment of the right hand and of the left; and the Tsar began to look and marvel that so many men were coming, having drawn up their regiments so that it was a beautiful sight; and the men were in multicolored armor, each commander in his own regiment; and they came to the bank opposite the Tsar.

And the commanders of the Grand Prince began to draw up and station their men opposite the Tatars.

And the Tsar beheld that the guard regiments too were coming, with many men; and the Tsar summoned Prince Semyon Belsky and his princes: “They told me that the Grand Prince’s men had gone to Kazan, and that I would not even be met, yet I have never seen so many equipped men, neither kutazniks nor argamak riders, in one place; and my old Tatars, who have been in many campaigns, also say that they have never seen anywhere so many men ready for battle in one place.” And he began to be angry with Prince Semyon and his princes. But not all the Grand Prince’s men had yet come to the bank opposite the Tsar: the commanders from the Ugra, Prince Roman Ivanovich Odoevsky and Ivan Petrovich, with many men, had not yet come to the bank.

But the Lord God, through the prayer of His Mother, the Most Pure Theotokos, and of the great wonderworkers Peter and Alexis, sent His mercy upon the army of the Grand Prince; fear departed from their hearts, and they armed themselves with courage, as if for an invited feast, desiring to fight the Tatars. And a few men from all the regiments came to the bank to help the forward regiment, and they drove the Tatars back from the bank.

And they began to revile the Tatars and to demand the bank. The Tsar saw the regiments and marveled at the courage of the Russian sons; and terror came upon him, and fear fell into his heart, and he wanted to flee that very hour, but the princes restrained him.

But the accursed Tsar Saip-Girey withdrew to his camps and was in great perplexity, and took counsel with his princes.

And that night the Grand Prince’s great artillery train arrived with cannons; and the commanders ordered that the cannons and great guns be brought through and made ready by morning. And the Tsar heard that great cannons were being brought through, though the day before there had been none, and great fear came upon him.
