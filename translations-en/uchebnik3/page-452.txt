On the journey of the Grand Prince to Borovsk

That same autumn, on Friday the 8th of December, the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of All Rus set out to pray at Borovsk, and Mozhaisk, and Volok, and with him his brother Prince Yuri Vasilyevich and Prince Vladimir Andreyevich, and many boyars; and he came to Moscow on Wednesday the 20th of the same month.

On the 23rd of the same month the Grand Prince’s Tatar, Tayut Tosubin, came from Astrakhan from the Tsar with a letter, and with him the Tsar sent his own man, Kuslubek-Murza, and from Kalga-Tsarevich, Kalkaman, with letters, and he wrote in the letters that the Grand Prince should be in friendship with them.

And the Grand Prince sent the Astrakhan envoy Prince Kaybula and his companions back to their sovereign.

That same winter the Grand Prince sent Yakov Grigoryev, son of Zakharov, to King Sigismund to ask for travel letters for Semyon Batyushkov, so that he might go through his land to the Wallachian voivode and back without hindrance, and for the Wallachian envoys.

That same winter, on the 11th of February, the Grand Prince sent the Wallachian envoys back to their voivode, and with them he sent his own man, Semyon Danilov, son of Batyushkov, with his gracious gift to the voivode, and the Grand Prince ordered Semyon to wait for Yakov in Vyazma.

And Yakov arrived from Lithuania and brought from the King travel letters through the Lithuanian land to Wallachia, and gave them to Semyon Batyushkov in Vyazma.

On the 25th of the same month, in the third week of Holy Lent, Alexy, abbot of the Trinity-Sergius Monastery, was installed by Macarius, Metropolitan of All Rus, as Archbishop of Rostov and Yaroslavl.

That same winter, on the 16th of March, the Grand Prince’s envoys from Crimea from Tsar Sahib-Girey, Fedor Voksherinov and Gavrilo Tyrtov, came to the Grand Prince; and with them the Tsar sent to the Grand Prince his own man Akseit with a letter, and the Tsar wrote in the letter about firm friendship.

And the Grand Prince sent the Tsar’s man Ak-Seit back to the Tsar; and with him the Grand Prince sent to the Tsar in Crimea his boyar’s son Fedor Ilyin, son of Chelishchev, with a letter, and he wrote in the letter that he wished to be in friendship with the Tsar.

That same winter Bishop Ferapont of Suzdal died, and was buried in Suzdal.

That same spring, on the 6th of June, the whole town of Starodub burned from lightning, but God spared the suburb.
