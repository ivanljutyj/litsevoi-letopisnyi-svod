On Kolomna

In that same year they began to build a stone fortress in Kolomna.

On the Founding of the Novodevichy Convent

In that same year they established a new convent for maidens in the city of Moscow, beyond the suburb, near the monastery of St Sava.

On the Drought and the Haze

In that same year there was a great drought from Trinity Day to the Dormition of the Holy Theotokos, and there was a great haze: for four weeks they did not see the sun or the moon.

On the Taking of Vows by Grand Princess Solomonia.
In the year 7034 from Adam (1534 from Christ)

In November Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich tonsured Grand Princess Solomonia at her wish, because of the severity of her illness and her childlessness. He had lived with her twenty years, and there were no children.

On the Second Marriage of Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich

That same winter, on January 21, Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich married a second time. He chose as his bride Princess Elena, daughter of Prince Vasily Lvovich Glinsky; Metropolitan Daniel married them.

On the Kazan Ambassadors

That same winter, in March, ambassadors came to the Grand Prince from Kazan from Tsar Safa Giray: Prince Kazy, Prince Chura, and Devel-bakshi.

On the Appointment of Two Archbishops: of Novgorod and of Rostov

That same winter, on March 4, the Most Reverend Metropolitan Daniel appointed Macarius, archimandrite from Luzhki, Archbishop of Veliky Novgorod and Pskov, and appointed Archimandrite Cyril from the Savior Monastery in Suzdal Archbishop of Rostov.

On the Ambassadors from the Emperor

That same spring there came from the Emperor the Grand Prince's ambassadors: Prince Ivan, son of Ivan Zasekin, and Semen, son of Boris Trofimov; and with them came ambassadors from the Emperor, and Leonard from the Count, and from the Emperor's brother Ferdinand, Sigismund Gibresten.

On the Arrival of Envoys from Rome

That same summer, in July, the Grand Prince's envoy Mitya Maloy, interpreter from Latin, came from Rome, and with him came to the Grand Prince from Pope Clement of Rome a bishop named Ivan Frenzhuzhkov.
