He lovingly embraces his commanders and permits them to kiss his hand, and encourages them with great gracious words, honoring their bravery and courage; likewise the sovereign graciously rewards all his soldiers according to rank.

On the Tower

In that same month the tsar ordered his clerk Ivan Vyrodkov to set up a tower by the siege towers of Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky, opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And they set up the tower six sazhens high, and placed many cannons on it, one-and-a-half-pood guns and wall guns; and many streltsy with guns stood there and fired into the city along the streets and at the city walls; and they killed many people.

But the impious hid in pits and ditches, dug under the city gates and under the wall, and dug tunnels beneath the tarasy, for at every gate beyond the ditch they had great tarasy filled with earth.

And from behind the tarasy they fought every day, and from the tunnels, like serpents, they crawled out and fought ceaselessly day and night.

On the Moving of the Siege Towers to the Ditch

The pious sovereign tsar and grand prince ordered Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky to move the siege towers to the ditch opposite the Arsk Tower and the Arsk Gates, toward the tarasy opposite the Tsar’s Gates. And Prince Mikhail for more than one day moved the siege towers and drew near the ditch, asking God for mercy, with great wisdom and courage.

The Tatars fought fiercely, unwilling to let the Orthodox draw near; but the tsar’s commanders, trusting in God, approached the ditch at the sovereign’s command.

On Sunday the sovereign ordered Prince Mikhail to take position with the siege towers along the ditch and at the tarasy opposite the Tsar’s and Arsk Gates; and by the sovereign’s command the streltsy and Cossacks, and the commanders with the boyars’ men, came and stood along the ditch and fought; and men fell on both sides.
