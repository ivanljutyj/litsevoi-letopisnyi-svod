Their posadniks were taken: Vasily Kazimir, Dmitry Isakov Boretsky, Kuzma Grigoriev, Yakov Fedorov, Matvey Selezenev, Vasily Selezenev, two nephews of Kazimir by his sister, Pavel Telyatev and Kuzma Gruzov, and many inhabitants. And the prophetic word was fulfilled in them: “Five of you shall put a hundred to flight, and a hundred of you shall put ten thousand to flight.”

They fled for a long time, until their horses were exhausted, and they began throwing themselves from their horses into water, into marshes, and into forests, for the Lord had blinded them, and they recognized neither their own land nor the road to their city from which they had come, but wandered through the forests, not knowing where to find a way out; and so the soldiers caught them. Others, wounded and wandering through the forests, died; and others drowned in the waters.

And those who did not fall from their horses were carried by their horses to the city like men drunk or asleep; and others in their fright ran past their own city, thinking that it had already been taken, for in their confusion they became like drunkards, as though they had lost their minds.

And the soldiers of the Grand Prince pursued them for twenty versts and returned, greatly exhausted.

The commanders of the Grand Prince, Prince Daniil Kholmsky and Feodor Davydovich, who had stood firm to the death, sounded the trumpets, waited for all their army, and, seeing all their soldiers safe, thanked God and His most pure Mother and all the saints.

And the commanders began to say to the Novgorodians they had captured: “Why, with so great a multitude of your soldiers, did you hold out so briefly when you saw our small army?” They answered the commanders: “Because we saw you as an innumerable multitude, coming not only to meet us, but behind us as well we saw other regiments coming with yellow banners and great standards, and a great noise of people and a terrible tramping of horses. And such terror fell upon us, and fear seized us, and trembling entered our hearts.” This was on the fourteenth day of June, a Sunday, early in the morning, on the feast of the Holy Apostle Aquila. And after that glorious victory, the same army of the Grand Prince, after that battle, laid waste the Novgorod posad and many districts, reaching the German frontier, the Narva River; and they plundered and burned the great place called Novoye Selo.

The commanders of the Grand Prince, having rested a little after that battle and waited for their men, sent Zamyatya to the Grand Prince with the news that God had helped them: the Novgorod army had been beaten. He galloped to the Grand Prince at Yazhelbitsy, announcing such a glorious victory, granted from above by God, and all the details of the above-described courage and bravery of his God-protected army. Of such men the Scripture says: if a warrior kills in battle for piety, that killing is not imputed to him by the holy fathers.
