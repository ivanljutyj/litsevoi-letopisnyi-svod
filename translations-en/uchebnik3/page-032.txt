RUS (1433–1451 from V.Kh.).
Book 13

Metropolitan Isidore came from Constantinople to Moscow

In that same spring, on Tuesday of Bright Week after the Great Day, Metropolitan Isidore, a Greek, a scholar versed in many languages and a man of books, arrived in Moscow from Constantinople from Patriarch Joseph to the metropolitan see; and Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich received him with honors.

And a prayer service was held in the holy cathedral church of the Most Pure Mother of God, and Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich arranged a great feast in honor of Metropolitan Isidore, and bestowed many precious gifts on him.

In that same spring the Tatars came to Ryazan, seized the outlying villages, and returned home.

In that same spring in Novgorod the water washed away the rampart by the city of Detinets, and the earth slid away from the walls, and a stone wall and a stone bell tower fell into the Volkhov River.

In that same spring the stone church of St. John Chrysostom over the gates at the Vladyka's court was painted. In that same spring in Novgorod the stone church of St. Nicholas on Vezhishchi collapsed.

In that same year the Novgorod Vladyka Euthymius laid the foundation of a stone church of St. Peter the Wonderworker, Metropolitan of all Rus', over the gates of his own court, and destroyed the old church.

In that same year the Novgorod Vladyka Euthymius, according to ancient custom, at the veche on Sunday blessed with a cross the posadniks, the tysyatskys, and all of Great Novgorod, and set off for Moscow to Isidore, Metropolitan of all Rus', on the 7th day of June, to receive a blessing from him and to bow low before the metropolitan.
