RUS (1562–1566 from the Incarnation of Christ). Book 22

On the Sending from Mtsensk with Captives

And in August Prince Pyotr, son of Prince Vasily Gorensky, sent from Mtsensk with captives, five Crimean Tatars: fifty Crimean men had come to the Mtsensk borderlands, and Prince Pyotr routed them utterly. And those captives said that the Crimean tsar had gone against the Circassians and had not marched against the borderland of the tsar and grand prince.

And the sovereign went to Moscow and released the tsars and tsareviches to their patrimonies; and at their request he released the Circassian princes to the Circassian land; and they kissed the cross to the sovereign that with all the Circassian land they would serve the sovereign until the end of their lives: wherever the sovereign sends them for service, there they are to go. And the sovereign sent with them to the Circassian land Andrei Shchepotev to oversee their fulfillment of the oath.

And the tsar and grand prince came from Kolomna to Moscow on the 18th day of August, a Friday.

On the Establishment of a Town at the Shatsk Gate

In the same year a town was established in Meshchera at the Shatsk Gate on the Shat River; and the voivodes were there to guard it with troops—Prince Dmitry Semyonovich Shastunov and Stefan Sidorov; and Boris, son of Ivan Sukin, built it.

On the Lithuanian Envoys

That same summer, in August, envoys came to the tsar and grand prince from Sigismund Augustus, King of Poland: Pan Stanislav Stanislavovich Dovoino, voivode of Polotsk, and Ivan Ostap Volovich, and the scribe Petr Semeshko, concerning a perpetual peace; and they did not reach agreement on a perpetual peace, but at the envoys’ request the tsar and grand prince concluded a two-year truce with the king, from the year 7062 from Adam (the year 1562 from Christ) from the Day of the Annunciation to the year 7064 from Adam (the year 1564 from Christ) to the same Day of the Annunciation. And he released the king’s envoys to the king, and appointed his own envoys to the king.
