And the Grand Prince came on the feast of the Entry of the Most Pure to his village of Vorobyovo, and was in Vorobyovo two days, suffering greatly from illness and growing weak.

Then his spiritual father Metropolitan Daniel came to the Grand Prince in the village of Vorobyovo to visit him, and with him Bishop Vassian of Kolomna and Dositheus, Bishop of Krutitsy, and archimandrites, and the Grand Prince’s boyars who were in Moscow: Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuysky and Mikhailo Semyonovich Vorontsov, and treasurer Pyotr Ivanovich Golovin, and many other sons of boyars who had not been with the Grand Prince at Volok.

There was then much weeping and lamentation among all the people, seeing the sovereign lying in such infirmity.

And the Grand Prince ordered a bridge to be built on the Moscow River below Vorobyovo opposite the New Monastery, because the river had not yet frozen solid.

And they cut through the ice, set up pillars, and laid the bridge; and the town overseers at that time were Dmitry Volynsky and Alexei Khoznikov, and others.

On the next morning, on the twenty-third day of November, a Sunday, the Grand Prince rode to the glorious city of Moscow; and when he came to the newly built bridge, four horses were then harnessed to the shafts of the Grand Prince’s carriage; and when the sledges came onto the bridge, the bridge broke, but the boyars’ sons held back the Grand Prince’s covered carriage, and they cut the harness straps of the sledges.

And from there the Grand Prince turned back and was vexed with the town overseers, but did not impose disgrace on them. And the Grand Prince entered the glorious city of Moscow through the Borovitsky Gate.

And they carried him into the bedchamber. And so by God’s will, though he suffered greatly, nevertheless his diamond-like royal soul offered up intensified thanksgiving and fervent prayers to God, which were continually on his lips. And on that same day his brother Prince Andrei Ivanovich came to the Grand Prince.

And the Grand Prince began to take counsel with his boyars, and his boyars at that time were: Princes Vasily Vasilyevich Shuysky, Mikhailo Yuryevich, and his Tver butler Ivan Yuryevich Shigona, and his clerks Menshoy Putyatin and Fedor Mishurin. And the Grand Prince began to speak to them about his son Prince Ivan and about his grand principality, and about his testamentary letter, since his son was still small, only three years old going on four, and how the realm should be governed after him.

And then the Grand Prince ordered his testamentary letter and bequest concerning the governance of the realm for his son and heir to be written by his clerk Grigory Nikitin Menshoy Putyatin, and he ordered his clerk Fedor Mishurin to be his assistant.
