The Grand Prince therefore soon sent his clerk Ivan Fedorov, son of Kuritsyn, to Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky and to all the commanders, that they suffer steadfastly for Orthodox Christianity, and that there be no dissension among them, but that they serve the Grand Prince all as one and stand firm—so as not to yield the bank to the Tsar, so that, God grant, the Tsar might not cross the river: “And if the Tsar crosses the river, then you must suffer steadfastly for the holy churches and for Orthodox Christianity, and do battle with the Tsar as much as God helps you; and I am glad to reward not only you but your children; and whichever of you God takes, I will have him written in the book of life, and I will reward his wife and children.”

When the commanders had read the Grand Prince’s letter, they began with tears to take good counsel: “Our sovereign, the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich, has written to us that there be no dissension among us, and that we serve him and suffer for Christianity; let us, brothers, strengthen ourselves in love and remember the bounty of his father, Grand Prince Vasily, and of our sovereign Grand Prince Ivan—then he was not yet born; the time has come for him himself to arm himself and stand against tsars, though still incomplete in years. Let us serve the young sovereign, and from the great one we shall receive honor, and after us our children too; let us suffer for the sovereign and for the Christian faith: and if God fulfills our desire, we shall receive glory not only here but in distant lands; for we are not immortal but mortal, and whoever it befalls to suffer unto death for the faith and for the sovereign, that will be unforgotten by God, and there will be recompense from the sovereign to our children.”

And those commanders among whom there had been division began with humility and tears to ask forgiveness and to exchange the kiss in Christ; and all were united in love—of one mind to suffer for the sovereign and for Christianity against the Tatars.

And Prince Dmitry and the commanders began to give the order to the princes and boyar sons, the court of the Grand Prince, and all the Grand Prince’s army, that they serve the Grand Prince and suffer steadfastly for Christianity: “And the sovereign wishes to reward you and your children with great bounty.” And the princes and boyar sons, the court of the Grand Prince, and all the army said as with one mouth: “We have heard, sir, of your good counsel, that you promised to serve the sovereign as one and to lay down your heads for Christianity; and you, sir, have implanted in us a great desire by your unity; we are glad to serve the sovereign and lay down our heads for Christianity; and we are ready and armed, and wish to drink the mortal cup with the Tatars.” When the commanders heard from the Grand Prince’s army that they wished to serve the sovereign steadfastly and lay down their heads for the Orthodox faith, they rejoiced with great joy and received their words from their mouths as a kind of treasure.
