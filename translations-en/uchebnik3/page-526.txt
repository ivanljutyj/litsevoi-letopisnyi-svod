On the Vision of the Venerable Daniel

There was a certain man there who held the rank of presbyter; he was there with the Orthodox Tsar, and having spiritual counsel with the Venerable Daniel of Pereyaslavl, he turned to him, calling him to the aid of the pious Tsar, saying: "I know, venerable one, that you have boldness before God; when you were in this life, you helped many in sorrows, and once by your prayers you saved me from the very hands of robbers, and unexpectedly delivered me from drawn weapons and bitter death. So now by your prayers to God stand up to help the Christ-loving Tsar and all his army, whom you yourself with your sacred hands received from the holy font in his holy baptism, and now, as a humane father, do not abandon him, but intercede before God with prayers, that God may humble His adversaries and enlighten this place with piety, so that even the impious may turn to the faith of the knowledge of God, and may the Lord destroy all who war against us, and may all who show us evil be put to shame, and may their strength be crushed, and may we be delivered and saved from every evil of soul and body, and living in quietness in a God-pleasing manner, joyfully glorify the most holy and magnificent name of the Holy Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and the most pure Mother of God and all the saints."

And after that, having slept a little, he sees the Venerable Daniel in his monastery, sitting on a certain lofty place, and around him men of holy countenance; he sees himself at the foot of the Venerable Daniel and wearing a holy mantle, and he wondered when the mantle had been placed upon him, and he did not dare ask anyone about it. Some looked at him and, sorrowful, asked the saint, saying: "Will he indeed be numbered among the brethren?" The saint said: "Not yet, but later; now he is released." And immediately he awoke, trembling at the vision, thinking that even awake the mantle was on him, and he felt himself with his hands, seeking the mantle on himself, and did not feel it, not seeing the covering of God, which always abides upon those who believe in God through the prayers of His saints; and he was joyful, having hope to receive forgiveness.

And he opened the small window of the chamber. It was still deep night, and he saw clearly, and not in a dream, over the city of Kazan an unusual light, as if a great fire were seen in the city, and the light spread over the whole city, and in the light a multitude of most radiant pillars shone.

He woke a certain man sleeping there who had been wounded by an arrow from the Tatars, and he was one of the Tsar's counsellors, and brought him to the window together with others who were there; and they all saw the ineffable radiance and marvelled, and said that a Christian sign was appearing thus, and they rejoiced with hope.

All marvelled at such a manifestation of light and related that many other signs had been seen many times; and this was told also to the pious Tsar himself.
