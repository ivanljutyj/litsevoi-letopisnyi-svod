And in both regiments, they said, all the outlanders were Lithuanian people—zemlyans, Volhynians, Podolians, and Samogitians—horse and drab foot, about fifty thousand, besides the Poles; and there were twelve thousand Poles; and their grand hetman was Stanislav Tekhavnovsky. They neither fired at the city nor made any assault, but stood two versts or more short of the city, between the Dvina River and the Polota on one side; and from the city, by shooting, they killed many of the Lithuanians in their regiments.

At Luki then was Tsar Simeon Kasaevich of Kazan, and with him the boyars and voivodes of the tsar and grand prince: Prince Ivan Ivanovich Pronsky, and Prince Vasily Semenovich Serebryany-Obolensky, and many other voivodes with many men; and from Luki they went to Polotsk to help.

The Lithuanian people stood at Polotsk from September 16 to October 4, and without waiting for the sovereign’s voivodes, on October 4 all the Lithuanian people went away from the city and crossed the Dvina River to the Lithuanian side.

On that same day the tsar and grand prince sent back to the Nogais the Nogai envoy Ishtora with his companions.

In the same month of April, on the 29th day, the tsar and grand prince rode on a tour of inspection to Kozelsk, Belev, Volkhov, and other border places on the Crimean side; and he ordered the boyars, nobles, and boyars’ sons to ride with him with all their service equipment.

And he ordered the tsaritsa and grand princess, and tsarevich Ivan and tsarevich Feodor, to go from Moscow to Alexandrovskaya Sloboda; and they set out from Moscow for Alexandrovskaya Sloboda on May 1.

In the same year Tsar and Grand Prince of all Rus Ivan Vasilievich ordered a court to be built for himself outside the city beyond the Neglinnka, between Arbat Street and Nikitskaya, on an open place where stood the Church of the Great Martyr of Christ Demetrius and the Church of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul; and he ordered a stone wall to be made around the court.

In the same year Tsar and Grand Prince of all Rus Ivan Vasilievich granted Prince Vladimir Andreevich, ordering him to build a court in the old place, beside the metropolitan’s court, apart from the Trinity court; and there the sovereign also granted Prince Vladimir Andreevich the court place of boyar Prince Ivan Fedorovich Mstislavsky.

In the same year, on May 19, Afanasy, Metropolitan of all Rus, left the metropolitan see because of great infirmity, going from the metropolitan’s court to the Chudov Monastery for his tonsure, at the second hour, on the eve of the commemoration of Wonderworker Alexei.
