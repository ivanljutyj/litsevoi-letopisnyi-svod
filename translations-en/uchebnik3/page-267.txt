The Grand Prince betrothed his daughter Elena to the Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander

In the same winter, on January 17, Lithuanian envoys came to Moscow to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich from the Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander Andreevich with peace and love: Pan Pyotr Yanovich, Pan Stanislav Yanovich, Voitko Klochko, and the scribe Fedko; and they concluded peace with Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich; and they wrote treaty charters with the Grand Prince, and ceded cities to the Grand Prince: Vyazma, Serpeysk, Meshchevsk, Vorotynsk, Odoev, and other cities along the Ugra River.

And then the Lithuanian envoys began to speak to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich about the matchmaking of the Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander Kazimirovich; and Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich accepted their matchmaking and betrothed his daughter Elena, and in place of his sovereign, the Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander Kazimirovich, Pan Stanislav Yanovich, Elder of Samogitia, betrothed Princess Elena on February 6, Thursday; and having honored his envoys, he released them from Moscow on February 12, and with him he released the Lithuanian captives, people taken at Serpeysk, Meshchevsk, and Opakov, who had been held in confinement in various cities.

In the same spring, on March 9, the Grand Prince sent his envoys to his brother and son-in-law, Grand Prince Alexander Andreevich: Prince Vasily Ivanovich Kosoy and Prince Semyon Ivanovich Ryapolovsky, and the clerk Fyodor Kuritsyn, to finalize the love and marriage agreement; they came to Vilna and brought the Grand Prince of Lithuania Alexander to kiss the treaty charters, and they exchanged treaty charters with each other.

And they took a charter confirmed at Grand Prince Alexander Kazimirovich’s in Vilna by his seal, that he would leave the Grand Prince’s daughter, his own Grand Princess Elena, to profess the Greek faith, and would neither lead nor compel her to the Roman confession, and would command a church to be built for her at his court and would keep priests of the Greek confession; and they returned with great honor.

And the Grand Prince’s envoys came to Moscow on May 25.

On Metropolitan Zosima Leaving the Metropolitan See

In the same spring, on May 17, Metropolitan Zosima left the metropolitan see not of his own will, but because he was immoderately addicted to drinking and did not care for the Church of God, and so he went to a cell in the Simonov Monastery, and from there to the Trinity-Sergius Monastery.
