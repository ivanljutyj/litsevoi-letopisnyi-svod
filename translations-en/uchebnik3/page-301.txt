The tsar together with Alexei sent to the Grand Prince his bakshey Bozek with a letter petitioning him that the Grand Prince should show him favor, conclude peace with him according to the old ways and friendship, as had been with his father, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus’: “And the envoy Mikhail Klyapik with his companions I shall release at once, and all those people who fell into our hands in battle.”

And Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of all Rus’, having taken counsel with his brothers and boyars, for the sake of those Christian souls who had fallen into Basurman hands, and for the sake of Christian order, forgave him his offense and released the bakshey Bozek; and with him he sent to the tsar his dyak Elka Sukov, and ordered him to say to the tsar that he should release the Grand Prince’s envoy Mikhail, and send his trusty man for peace negotiations.

And in that same year Tsar Magamet-Amin released the Grand Prince’s envoy Mikhail Klyapik, and released the Grand Prince’s people whom he had seized and plundered in Kazan together with Mikhail, and sent his envoy Barash-Seit to Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of all Rus’ to petition him concerning brotherhood and friendship, as had been with his father, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus’.

On the Coming of the Crimean Tatars to the Borderland

In that same year news came to Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of all Rus’ that many Tatar people were coming onto the Field, and their arrival was awaited in the borderland, at Belev and the Belev region, and the Odoev and Kozelsk regions.

And the Grand Prince sent from Moscow to Belev his commanders, Prince Ivan Kholmsky and Prince Konstantin Ushaty; and he ordered Prince Vasily Odoevsky, Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Vorotynsky, and the Kozelsk namestnik Prince Alexander Strigin to be there together with the commanders.

The Grand Prince’s commanders were in Vorotynsk, and news came to them that the Tatars, having taken many captives in the borderland, had gone away.

The commanders, placing their hope in God, set out in pursuit of them onto the Field, and caught up with them on the Oka, and by God’s mercy and the sovereign’s health and the good fortune of Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich of all Rus’ they beat many Tatars and took some alive, and returned all the captives, and pursued them as far as the little river Rybnitsa on the ninth day of the month of August, a Monday.

And the Tatars whom they took captive in battle said that the Crimean Tatars of Zyatseit-Murza, son of Yankuvatov, with his companions had come to the borderland.
