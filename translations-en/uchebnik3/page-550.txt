Sending up his prayers, while from his eyes a river of tears poured forth, and he spoke clearly thus: “O Master, most merciful Christ, have mercy on Your servants! The time of Your mercy has come! Grant us, Your servants, strength against our adversaries! Have mercy, O Merciful One, have mercy on Your fallen servants, O Lover of mankind! Arise for good and free the captive and the orphaned! Send down Your compassion and Your ancient mercy from on high, and the impious will understand that we, trusting in You, O our God, overcome. And You, O most pure Lady Theotokos, entreat Christ, the true God of ours, born of You, that He not remember my sins and great iniquities, how much I have sinned before the majesty of His glory, but have mercy on me for the sake of Your great mercy! You, O Lady, be our helper and the helper of all our army; trusting in you, we shall not be put to shame in battles, through your prayers and those of all the Russian wonderworking hierarchs, our kinsmen, intercessors and helpers against our adversaries.”

On the Explosion of the Mine

And the time came during the Liturgy to read the Holy Gospel; the sun was already rising, and when the deacon finished and proclaimed the last line of the Gospel—“And there shall be one flock and one shepherd”—at that very moment it was as if a mighty thunderclap struck, the earth trembled and shook. The pious Tsar came out of the church doors and saw the city wall torn open by the mine, and it was fearful to see how the earth, as if in darkness, rose to a great height, and many beams and pagans were scattered.
