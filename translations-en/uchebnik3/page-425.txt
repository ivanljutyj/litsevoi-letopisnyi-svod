Prince Ivan Vasilievich Shuysky and other voivodes with many people, who stand for the Kazan affair; and the Grand Prince also ordered the voivodes from Kostroma and Pakhra to join with the tsar; if with the tsar and the voivodes there are many people to ask God’s mercy, then there is someone to protect the Grand Prince’s affair and help Moscow. But with young sovereigns, without having settled on a place, how should one act against them? To place the fate of the Grand Prince on God and His most pure Mother, and on the wonderworkers Peter and Alexis, for they have care for the Russian land and for our sovereigns; and his father Grand Prince Vasily gave his son Grand Prince Ivan into the hands of those wonderworkers Peter and Alexis.” And the boyars all agreed on one thing: that with young sovereigns it is bad to act hastily; the Grand Prince should be in the city. And the Grand Prince, having heard the speeches of his father Metropolitan Ioasaph and of the boyars, summoned the city stewards and ordered city supplies to be laid in, cannons and arquebuses to be placed in positions, and people to be assigned to the gates, to the shooting towers, and along the walls, and barriers to be made along the streets by the suburb.

The city people with great eagerness began diligently to do everything, and among themselves they promised to stand firm for the holy churches, for the sovereign Grand Prince, and for their own homes, and to lay down their heads.

To the Grand Prince messengers often come, saying that the tsar is preparing towers and wants to cross the river.

“And if the tsar crosses the river, you would firmly suffer for the holy churches and for Orthodox Christianity, do battle with the tsar as much as God helps you, and I am glad to favor not only you but also your children; and whichever of you God takes, I will order that one to be written in the book of life, and his wife and children to be favored.”

The voivodes, having read the Grand Prince’s charter, began with tears to take good counsel: “Our sovereign Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich wrote to us that there should be no discord among us, and that we should serve him and suffer for Christianity; let us, brothers, strengthen ourselves among ourselves with love and remember the favor of his father Grand Prince Vasily; and the time has not yet come for our sovereign Grand Prince Ivan himself to arm and stand against tsars, for he is not yet mature in years; let us serve the young sovereign, and from the grown one we will receive honor, and after us our children too; let us suffer for the sovereign and for the Christian faith, and if God fulfills our desire, we will win glory not only here but also in distant lands; for we are not immortal but mortal, and whoever happens to suffer to death for the faith and for the sovereign, that will not be forgotten with God, and to our children there will be reward from the sovereign.”
