And Saip-Girey, the Crimean Tsar, was at that time at peace with the Grand Prince: the Grand Prince’s envoy Prince Alexander Vasilyevich Kashin was in Crimea, and Tsar Saip-Girey’s envoy Shagaldy Prince was in Moscow with the Grand Prince.

And Grand Prince Ivan knew Saip-Girey’s custom, that Tsar Saip-Girey was not a man of his word and did not stand firmly by his oath: though he was at peace with the Grand Prince, the Grand Prince was wary of him as an enemy and kept voivodes at Kolomna with many men.

And at that time two captives came running to the Grand Prince from Crimea, Yakimko Ivanov, a man of Lyubuchaninov, with a companion, and they told the Grand Prince that the Tsar’s man Aziferhat had come from Moscow to Crimea before them and had told the Tsar that the Grand Prince had sent his voivodes with many men to Kazan, and that they had already set out before him.

And the Tsar forgot his oath and friendship and began to prepare for war against Rus with his son Tsarevich Imin-Girey.

And he led the whole Horde with him, leaving the old and the young in the Horde; and with the Tsar were also Prince Semyon Belsky and men of many hordes, the Turkish Tsar’s men with cannons and handguns, and from the Nogais Baka Prince with many men, and the Kaffans and Astrakhans, the Azovians and Belgorodians; and they went against Rus with great boasting, intending to destroy Christianity.

And he ordered it to be proclaimed in the Horde that any people who did not manage to set out with him should overtake him at the town of Klamkilimine; there the Tsar would wait for his men.

And on these tidings the Grand Prince sent to Putivl to his governor Fedor Pleshcheev Ochin and ordered him to send a scout detachment into the Field across the road.

And Fedor sent Gavrila Tolmach, and Gavrila came from the Field and told the Grand Prince that he had come upon great camps in the Field: many people were marching on Rus, a hundred thousand and more.

And on these tidings the Grand Prince dispatched from Moscow his boyar and voivode Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky, and ordered Prince Dmitry and all his voivodes to leave Kolomna and stand with all their men by the Oka River along the bank, in those places where previously the voivodes had stood on the bank against the tsars.

And the Grand Prince sent from Moscow Tsarevich Shigalei Shibansky and his boyar Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov, and with them he sent many men from his court, ordering the tsarevich and Prince Yuri to stand on the Pakhra.

And to Vladimir he sent to his boyar and voivode Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuisky and his comrades, ordering them to stand in Vladimir.
