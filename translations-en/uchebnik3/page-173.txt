The Grand Prince, hearing of this, at once established that it was indeed so, but Ivan Fryazin wished to conceal it from him; and he intended—so he said—to bring that Trevisan with him to the Tsar. And enraged at them, he ordered Fryazin seized and, having bound him in irons, sent to Kolomna, and ordered his house plundered and his wife and children taken.

And Trevisan he seized and wished to execute. But that same legate and the other envoys who were with him began to petition the Grand Prince that he would show favor and mercy to him until he had exchanged messages with the Venetian Doge. The Grand Prince ordered him put in irons, and he was held at the house of Nikita Beklemishev.

That same legate Anthony and Dmitry the Greek, and the other Italians and Greeks with him, the Grand Prince kept with him for eleven weeks, showed them great honor, and gave them many gifts. He released them on January 26, and sent many gifts to the Pope, and also to his brothers-in-law; his son Grand Prince Ivan sent gifts of his own, and Grand Princess Sophia of her own.

And so they went from Moscow to the Lithuanian land, to the Polish land, and through many other lands to their great city of Rome.

On the Death of Metropolitan Philip and the Fire

That same spring, on April 4, on the fifth Sunday of Lent, which is called the Sunday of the Praises, at the fourth hour of the night, fire broke out within the city of Moscow near the Church of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos, which had a side-chapel of the Resurrection of Lazarus; and many households burned, and the metropolitan’s court burned, and the court of Prince Boris Vasilyevich burned as far as the Epiphany of the Trinity and the city granaries; and the Grand Prince’s granary palace burned, while his great court was barely saved by force, since the Grand Prince himself was then in the city; and the fire reached as far as the stone cellar that was in the court of Prince Mikhail Andreevich by the city wall. And the roof of the Church of the Nativity of the Most Pure burned, as did the roof of the city wall and all the city annexes; and all the households that were near there burned as far as the city granary court. The last hour of the night was already ending, and the fire was subsiding; Metropolitan Philip came from beyond the city to the Church of the Most Pure Theotokos, because on account of that fire he had gone out of the city to the Monastery of St Nicholas the Old.

And entering the Church of the Most Pure, he began to sing a moleben with many tears at the tomb of the wonderworker Peter. At that same time the Grand Prince himself also came there, and seeing him weeping, began to say to him: “Father, lord! Do not grieve; God has so willed it, and as for your court having burned, I will give you as much…”
