He granted favor and ordered the Novgorod viceroys to conclude a truce as had been customary in olden times, and the king ordered all the old frontiers cleared—those that people had seized without his knowledge; and the merchants whom he had detained, and all the other people, the king released with all their goods; and he petitioned that the sovereign grant favor and release those whom the boyar Prince Pyotr Mikhailovich Shchenyatev and Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Paletsky with their companions had taken captive during the war. And the tsar and sovereign ordered the reply that the king had begun all this, and not the tsar and grand prince, and that he had brought war upon his own land because of his perjury; and those captives had already been distributed among different lands, and those not yet baptized, and those whom he wished to sell to cover his losses, the king must order all of them to be ransomed. And the envoys petitioned for that sovereign favor, and according to the whole will of the tsar and grand prince the envoys acknowledged their dependence, and the tsar and grand prince granted favor to the elected King Gustav of Sweden and ordered the Novgorod viceroys, boyars Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich Glinsky and Alexei Danilovich Pleshcheev, to conclude the truce on all those conditions on which they had acknowledged their dependence before the sovereign, for forty years. And the tsar and grand prince sent the envoys from Moscow to Novgorod in March; and the boyars and Novgorod viceroys, Prince Mikhail Vasilyevich and Alexei Danilovich, brought the envoys of the Swedish king to kiss the cross, wrote the truce charters, confirmed them with seals, and sent them back to the king, and from themselves sent to the king the envoy Ivan Sharapov, son of Zamytsky.

On the Arrival of the Livonian Envoys to the Sovereign

In the same month the Livonian Master sent to the sovereign to petition through envoys Valentin and Melchior and the clerk Ganus, that they should not pay that tribute of the tsar and grand prince which the Bishop of Yuryev had assumed in his domain—one grivna from every person. And the tsar and sovereign ordered Okolnichy Alexei Fedorovich and Clerk Ivan Mikhailov to hear the embassy and refuse them, because he had imposed his tribute on them according to the former truce charters and their petition, and on this Ivan Bokostr and Vladimir with their companions had kissed the cross in Moscow. But the Master himself, the Archbishop, and the Bishop of Yuryev had kissed the cross before the Novgorod viceroys and the envoy Kelar Terpigorev, that according to these charters they would henceforth regularly pay the sovereign's tribute, which they had not paid for many years, from the Yuryev region at one German grivna from every person, except churchmen. And the term for this was the third year, to set it right in accordance with that truce.
