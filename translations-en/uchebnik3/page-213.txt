That same winter, on the 19th day of January, the Grand Prince seized Archbishop Feofil of Novgorod in Novgorod for his treason and sent him to Moscow; and he took his treasury, a multitude of gold and silver, and his vessels, for that lord did not wish Novgorod to be under the Grand Prince, but under the King or another sovereign. For when the Grand Prince first took Novgorod, he then took from the Novgorod lord half of the volosts and the villages of all the monasteries, and for that the lord remained in anger; for those volosts had at first belonged to the grand princes, and so they had made them their own.

In that same month, on the 24th day, they placed him in the Monastery of the Miracle of the Archangel Michael; and he sat there six and a half years, and there he died.

On the Defection of the Grand Prince’s Brothers to Luki

In that same year the brothers defected from the Grand Prince, Prince Andrei the Elder and Prince Boris.

At that same time news came to the Grand Prince in Novgorod from his son that his brothers intended to defect; and he soon left Novgorod for Moscow and came to Moscow before the Great Fast.

And all the people rejoiced, for they were in great fear of his brothers: all the cities were under siege, and many, fleeing through the forests, died of cold without the Grand Prince.

That same winter, in Butter Week, his brother Prince Boris came from Volok to Prince Andrei Vasilievich at Uglich, and sent his princess and children to Rzhev.

And Prince Andrei with his princess and children, and Prince Boris, went from Uglich to Rzhev through the Tver patrimony.

The Grand Prince sent his boyar Andrei Mikhailovich to them in Rzhev; but they did not return, and went from Rzhev with their princesses and children, and their boyars, and the best boyar sons with wives and children and people, up the Volga toward the Novgorodian lands.

The Grand Prince sent after them Vassian, Archbishop of Rostov; and he overtook them at Molvyatitsy, while they were at that time going to Novgorod.

And from there, after the archbishop’s words, they sent their boyars with the archbishop to the Grand Prince—Prince Vasily and Peter Nikitich Obolensky—and from there they themselves went to the Lithuanian border, and having come, halted at Velikiye Luki. And they sent to the King, asking him to judge their grievances with the Grand Prince and to help them; and the King answered them, and gave their princesses the city of Vitebsk for residence.
