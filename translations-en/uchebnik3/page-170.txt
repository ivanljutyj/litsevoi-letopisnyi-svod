And after singing the funeral service, they placed him in a stone coffin and set it in the middle of the church. On the fourth day, Wednesday, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich came from Rostov, and shed many tears and made great lamentation; likewise his other brothers, and other princes and boyars, and all Orthodox Christendom shed many tears; even one who had never in his life shed a tear wept, seeing the people's grief.

Metropolitan Philip, with the aforementioned bishops and the whole consecrated council, sang the funeral hymns and buried the body of the right-believing Prince George in the Church of the Archangel Michael on the 16th day of the same month, where all the right-believing grand princes lie, their lineage.

The Arrival of Tsarevna Sophia in Moscow

On the 21st day of the same month, the tsarevna came by ship to Kolyvan, and the sea bore them for 11 days.

And from Kolyvan they set out on October 1, and came to Yuryev on the sixth day of the same month, and came to Pskov on October 11; the people of Pskov gave great honor to the tsarevna and to all who were with her, and gave gifts to the tsarevna. And they stayed there 7 days, and entered Novgorod on October 25, and left on October 30, having received great honor and gifts from the archbishop and from all Novgorod.

And when they were already near Moscow, they told the grand prince that the envoy, Legate Anthony, was coming with the tsarevna, and that a cross was being carried before him, for the pope had given such great honor to his envoy—that he should go thus through all their lands and as far as Moscow—because of the greatness of this land and the great distance.

Hearing this, the grand prince began to deliberate about it with his mother, his brothers, and his boyars; and some began to say that he should not forbid him this, while others said: "Such a thing has never happened in our land, that honors should be shown to the Latin faith; Isidore alone attempted it, and he perished."

The grand prince sent to his father, Metropolitan Philip, informing him of this. The metropolitan answered him: "It cannot be that he should enter this city or even approach it. But if you do so, wishing to honor him, then he may come in at one gate of the city, and I, your servant in prayer, will go out by another gate; for we ought not even to hear of it, let alone see it, because whoever has loved and praised another faith has mocked his own."

Hearing this from the hierarch, the grand prince sent to that legate that they should not carry the cross before him, but ordered it to be hidden.
