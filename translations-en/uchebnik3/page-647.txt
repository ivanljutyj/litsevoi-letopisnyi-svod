On the Sovereign's Bounty to the Voivodes

And to the voivodes and to all the boyar sons he sent with his bounty, and he granted the common people and ordered them to live in their own places, and ordered the captive Rugodivians to be released, after seeking out those who had not yet been sold off to other lands.

On the Report from Vishnevetsky with Ivan Myachkov

In the same month Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky sent from the Dnieper to the Tsar and Grand Prince the sovereign's court servitor Ivan Myachkov, and wrote with him that he had come to Perekop and beaten the guards six versts from Perekop. And none of the Crimean people offered him resistance. He stood and spent the night there, and the next day by midday he had gone ten versts beyond Perekop. And he went to the Dnieper, to the Khovansky ferry, twenty-five versts below Islam-Kirmen, and stood at the ferry for three days. And the Crimeans did not come to him or show themselves. And they say the Crimean Tsar with all his people was under siege.
