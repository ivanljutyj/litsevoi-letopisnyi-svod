And from Vologda, Voivode Semyon Peshek Saburov with the Vologdians.

And having come to Ustyug, they went in boats to Vyatka, and the Ustyuzhans with them; and having come to Vyatka, they conveyed to the Vyatchans the words of the Grand Prince, that they should go with them against the Kazan Tsar. They said: “The Tsar has compelled us, and we have given him our word that we shall help neither the Tsar against the Grand Prince nor the Grand Prince against the Tsar.”

And at that time there was on Vyatka an envoy of the Kazan Tsar, and he sent word to Kazan that from Vyatka a river-borne army of the Grand Prince was coming, but not a large one.

And Konstantin Bezzubtsev joined with all the aforesaid warriors stationed in Nizhny Novgorod.

And the Grand Prince sent his charter, ordering him himself to remain in Novgorod, and whichever sons of boyars were under him and all the other warriors who wished it, those he ordered him to release to go and fight in the Kazan lands.

He, having read the charter, sent to all his subordinate princes and voivodes who had gathered to him and said to them that the Grand Prince had sent him a charter: “And he has ordered all of you who wish to go and fight in the Kazan lands on both sides of the Volga, and he has ordered me to remain here in Novgorod; so you go, but do not go to the city of Kazan.”

Hearing that, the Grand Prince’s warriors said to their voivode Konstantin: “We all wish to go against the accursed Tatars for the holy churches and for our sovereign Grand Prince Ivan, and for all Orthodox Christianity.” And they all went, while Konstantin remained in Novgorod. And they went from the Oka towards Novgorod, towards Stary; they halted below the church of St Nicholas on Becheva, and, coming out of the boats, they went into the city to the old church of the Transfiguration of the Lord, and ordered the priests there to celebrate a prayer service for the Grand Prince and for his warriors.

And, departing from there, they likewise held a prayer service at St Nicholas and gave alms, each according to his means.

After this they all gathered together and began to consider whom to appoint as voivode, so that all might obey one man. And after long deliberation they chose Ivan Run of their own will; and that same day, having sailed sixty versts from Novgorod, they spent the night.

And in the morning they dined at Roznezha and spent the night at Cheboksary; and from Cheboksary they sailed the whole day, and all that night they sailed.

And they came to Kazan at early dawn on 22 May, on the fiftieth Sunday. And, coming out of the boats, they went to the posad, while the Kazan Tatars were all still asleep.
