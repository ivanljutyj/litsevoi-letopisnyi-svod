Concerning Tsarevna Sophia. The year 6977 from Adam (the year 1477 from Christ)

That same winter, on February 11, there came from Rome, from Cardinal Bessarion, a Greek named Yuri to the Grand Prince with a letter in which it was written that “there is in Rome, at the court of Thomas Vetshoslovets, despot of the Morea, a daughter of the royal house of Constantinople named Sophia, an Orthodox Christian; if you wish to take her as your wife, I will send her to your realm. The King of France and the Grand Duke of Milan have sent matchmakers to her, but she does not wish to join the Latin faith.” There also came Italians: one named Carlo, the elder brother of Ivan Fryazin, the Moscow mintmaster, and a nephew, Anton, son of their eldest brother.

The Grand Prince heeded these words, and after taking counsel about this with his father Metropolitan Philip and with his mother and with his boyars, that same spring, on March 20, he sent Ivan Fryazin to Pope Paul and to that Cardinal Bessarion to see the tsarevna. He came to the pope, saw the tsarevna, and set forth the matter with which he had been sent to the pope and Cardinal Bessarion. The tsarevna, learning that the Grand Prince and all his land were in the Orthodox Christian faith, desired to marry him. The pope, having honored the envoy of the Grand Prince, Ivan Fryazin, released him to the Grand Prince with the intent to give the tsarevna to him, provided that he send his boyars for her. And the pope gave Ivan Fryazin his letters to the effect that the Grand Prince’s envoys might travel freely for two years through all lands that swear allegiance to his papacy, as far as Rome.

The Grand Prince Ivan sent a fleet by river against Kazan

That same spring, in the second week after the Great Day of Pascha, the Grand Prince sent an army by boats to the Kazan lands: the voivode Konstantin Alexandrovich Bezzubtsev, and with him many boyar sons, his court, and also from all the land his boyar sons, from all his cities and from all the patrimonies of his brothers. And from Moscow he sent the Surozhans, cloth merchants, trading people, and all other Muscovites who could be of service according to their ability; and over them he appointed as voivode Prince Pyotr Vasilyevich Obolensky Nagoy. And they went by the Moscow River to Nizhny Novgorod, and others by the Klyazma.

And the Kolomna men and all who live above them on the Oka went by the Oka, and the Murom men also.

And the Vladimir men and Suzdal men went by the Klyazma; the Dmitrov men, Mozhaisk men, and Uglich men, and the Yaroslavl men, Rostov men, Kostroma men, and all other Volga people went by the Volga to Nizhny Novgorod at one appointed time.

And all those assembled in one place — at Nizhny Novgorod.
