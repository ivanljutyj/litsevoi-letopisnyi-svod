Concerning the envoy from Ismail, Prince of the Nogai

In the same month Ismail, Prince of the Nogai, sent from the Nogai Horde his envoy Bekhchura, and envoys came from the murzas, and a gift from Bulat-Murza and from other murzas, and he wrote thus: those murzas, nine brothers, and Shiiadyakov’s children fell away from Ismail, and the Crimean Tsar came against them and captured Islam-Gazu Murza Shiiadyakov and other murzas with their uluses, while the nine brothers, Urazlyev’s children, Aisa-Murza with his brothers, departed from him altogether and came to Ismail, and by the command of the Tsar and Grand Prince they acknowledged their subjection to Ismail and united with him.

And from Astrakhan Ivan Cheremisinov and his companions wrote about the same thing, that the murzas had fled from the Crimean Tsar; and Ivan ordered them to be transported to Ismail with their uluses.

Concerning the report from Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky from Cherkassy and Kanev

In the same month Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Vishnevetsky wrote from Cherkassy and Kanev to the Tsar and Grand Prince that he had left the Dnieper, from Khortitsky Island, because he had run out of provisions, and the Cossacks had dispersed from him, and the Crimean Tsar had marched against his town, and numerous men of the Turkish Tsar in boats, and Vlachs, and because of the lack of provisions he had not remained in the town but had settled in Cherkassy and Kanev, and what did the sovereign command him to do?

And the Tsar and Grand Prince wrote to Prince Dmitry Ivanovich that he commanded him to come to him, and from Cherkassy and Kanev he commanded him to withdraw in favor of the King, because the Tsar and Grand Prince was in a truce with the King.

Concerning Vishnevetsky’s arrival to the sovereign

In November Prince Dmitry Ivanovich came to serve the Tsar and Grand Prince, and the Tsar and sovereign rewarded him with his great favor, gave him the town of Belev with all its volosts and villages as his patrimony, and in other towns the sovereign gave him villages and rewarded him with great favor. And Prince Dmitry kissed the life-giving cross to the sovereign that he would serve him, the Tsar and Grand Prince, forever and wish good in all things to him and his lands.

Concerning the campaign against the Germans
