The Great Sovereign Vasily, having honored that envoy of the Master, Theodoricus, dismissed him, and with him the Grand Prince sent his close man Dmitry Zagryazhsky to the Prussian Master Albrecht. With him he sent his confirmed charter, that the Great Sovereign Vasily, by the grace of God Tsar and Sovereign of All Rus, had granted the Prussian Master Albrecht and had agreed with him upon unity. And in Dmitry’s presence the Master kissed the cross that, according to that charter, he would be faithful to the Great Sovereign until the end of his life.

On the Imperial Envoy

That same spring, on April 18, there came to Moscow to the Great Sovereign Vasily, from his brother the elected Emperor and supreme King of the Romans, Maximilian, his envoy, named Sigismund Gerdbersten, knight, beseeching and asking that the Great Sovereign Vasily, for the sake of him, the Emperor, permit the envoys of the Polish King Sigismund to come to him and make peace with him, so that because of their strife Christian blood might not be shed between them, and he asked for travel charters for the King’s envoys.

And the Great Sovereign Vasily answered Maximilian’s envoy Sigismund through his boyars that, for the sake of his brother the elected Emperor Maximilian, we will conclude an agreement with Sigismund, King of Poland, as shall be fitting for us, and he ordered that travel charters for the King’s envoys be given to him. And the Emperor’s envoy, the knight Sigismund, sent safe-conduct letters to the King with his nephew Jan.

On the King’s Sending to Crimea

In that same year the Polish King Sigismund sent to Crimea to the infidel rulers, to the sons of Tsar Mengli-Girey, his lord Albrecht Martynov with a great treasure.

And he incited them against the Christians, ordering them to march against the borderlands of the Great Sovereign Vasily.

On the Arrival of the Crimean Tatars at Tula and Besputa

And that same summer, in August, by the counsel of King Sigismund, there came Crimean Tatars: Tokuzan-Murza, son of Prince Shirin-Agiz, and Kudash-Murza Bekterev, son of the Shirins, and Uidei-Murza Mingish, and Altov, brother-in-law of the tsarevich, and with them 20,000 troops. And having come to the Grand Prince’s borderland, around Tula and Besputa, they began to wage war.

And the Grand Prince’s commanders, Prince Vasily Semenovich Odoyevsky, Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Vorotynsky, and other commanders sent ahead of themselves against the Tatars the boyar sons with few men, Ivashka Tutykhin and the Volkonsky princes, and ordered them to surround from all sides...
