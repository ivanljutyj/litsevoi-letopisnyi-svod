Since it was difficult for him in the Field, and that same autumn the whole Field was burned in a fire, while the winter was fierce and very severe, much snow fell, and strong winds and whirlwinds began. And therefore the tsarevich came in peace to Ryazan and wished to winter there out of extreme necessity.

Hearing of this in Moscow, Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich sent against the tsarevich Prince Vasily Obolensky and Andrei Fedorovich Goltyaev, and his court with them, and the Mordvins on skis, because the winter was severe and snowy, and the Tatars had lost their horses owing to the strong frosts, and from the frost and great cold they were frozen and grief seized them; and besides, hay was very expensive. And Tsarevich Mustafa was then in Ryazan, in the town of Pereyaslavl; but the Ryazanians drove him out of the town.

He, having left the town, encamped right there below the town of Pereyaslavl. And from Moscow the commanders of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich came against him and met on the Listani River. The Tatars had quite weakened and were frozen; they were left without horses, and because of the strong frost and cold, and wind and whirlwind, their bows and arrows became utterly useless; there were great snows then as well.

And the Mordvins came against them on skis with javelins, boar spears, and sabres, and from the other side the Ryazanian Cossacks came on skis with javelins, boar spears, and sabres; and also the commanders of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich with their troops; and many foot soldiers gathered against them with cudgels, axes, and boar spears.

And a fierce, strong battle began on the Listani River, and the Christians began to prevail. The Tatars, however, did not yield at all and fought bravely; but many Tatars were killed, and Tsarevich Mustafa himself was killed together with many Tatar princes; and Prince Ikhmut-Murza and Azberdei, son of Misherevan, were taken captive, and many other Tatars were captured. And in the regiment of the grand prince the Tatars killed Ilya Ivanovich Lykov.

That same winter the Novgorodians of Great Novgorod went in force into the German land, beyond the Narva River, with Prince Ivan Vladimirovich, grandson of Olgerd; and they conquered, captured, and burned the lands around Rugodiv and as far as Purdoznya, and around the Narva River and as far as Lake Peipus they conquered and took captive all the lands, put them to the sword and death, burned them, and returned to their own land with great wealth. That same spring Prince Ivan Andreevich of Mozhaisk, grandson of Ivan, great-grandson of Daniel Alexandrovich of Moscow, took Andrei Dmitrievich captive and burned his wife in Mozhaisk on the Sunday of the Myrrh-bearing Women.
