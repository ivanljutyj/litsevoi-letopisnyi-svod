And he ordered the trumpets to sound, and they began to cut down the Tatars, to plunder them, and to take them captive; and all the Christian captives who were there in the suburb—Moscow, Ryazan, Lithuanian, Vyatka, Ustyug, Perm, and men of other cities—all of them they freed from captivity; and they set all their suburbs on fire from every side. Many of the Muslim infidels and Tatars, not wishing to fall into Christian hands and caring more for their great wealth, locked themselves with their goods in their temples together with their wives and children and with all that they had, and so burned.

When the suburbs had burned, the army withdrew from the city, very weary, and boarding their vessels, they withdrew to Korovnich Island and stood there seven days.

And then a captive from Kolomna ran to them from Kazan and told them that the Kazan tsar Ibrahim had gathered against them with all the land of Kamsk, Syplinsk, Kostyatsk, Belovolzhsk, Votyak, and Bashkir: “And he will be upon you at early dawn with his ship army and his horse army.”

And hearing this, the commanders of the great prince and all his warriors began to send the young men away from them with the large vessels, while they themselves remained behind on the shore to defend them. And they ordered them to go and take position on Irikhov Island on the Volga, and not to go into the narrow place. But they, not obeying, went into the narrow place in the large vessels. And then the Tatars came at them on horseback and began to shoot, wishing to kill them; but they, firing back, beat off the Tatars.

And the Tatar ship army, its best princes and men, went against the army of the great prince, also the ship army, wishing to destroy them, for few of them were left. But they, not fearing, went against the Tatars, although there were many of them, and fighting zealously, they drove the Tatars all the way to the city of Kazan, under the wall; then they returned and went to Irikhov Island and joined together there with the large vessels.

When they were standing on that island, Constantine Alexandrovich Bezzubtsev, their chief commander, came to them.

On his arrival he sent to Vyatka the command of the great prince to the Vyatchans, that they should go to Kazan with an army, and he set them a term: from that day in three and a half weeks to be before Kazan. The Vyatchans answered: “When the brothers of the great prince go to Kazan, then we too will go.” But Constantine at that time with all his force stood another three and a half weeks, and from the commanders of the great prince and from the Vyatchans no news came to them. And they were already short of provisions, for they had brought little supplies with them, because they had come on a foray.
