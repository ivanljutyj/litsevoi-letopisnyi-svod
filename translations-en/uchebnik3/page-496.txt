And the sovereign heard their petition and ordered the boyars Ivan Vasilyevich Sheremetev and the clerk Ivan Mikhailov to speak with them: why they did not love the Tsar, how the sovereign was to remove the Tsar, by what custom the viceroy was to live among them, and what in this was to be believed? And they told the boyars about the Tsar that he killed and robbed them and took their wives and daughters by force. “And if only the sovereign will grant favor to the land and remove the Tsar, then here under the sovereign there are about 300 of us—ulans and princes, murzas and Cossacks; send any of us as your envoy to the Kazan land: as soon as Tsar Shigalei leaves, all the Kazanians will swear allegiance to the sovereign and let his viceroys into the city, and surrender the whole city to the sovereign; and whomever he orders to live in the city and the posad, those will live there, and all the others will live in the villages, and the viceroys of the Tsar and Grand Prince are to administer all the Tsar’s revenues; and those princes who are childless, let the sovereign grant favor to whomever he wishes, and all people will then be under the sovereign’s authority, whomsoever of the people he favors with whatever; and if they do not act as the Kazanians, then, sovereign, order us all to be put to death here.” And in that they gave an oath that if the Tsar did not wish to leave Kazan, the sovereign would take away his streltsy, and he himself would then flee.

In the same winter, in February, the sovereign sent Alexei Adashev to Tsar Shigalei and ordered him to remove the Tsar from Kazan; and to the Kazan land he sent the Kazanian Tereulduvan, and Muraley and Kostrov with their associates sent a charter to the land together with Tereulduvan concerning what they had petitioned the sovereign about. And the sovereign ordered Alexei to speak firmly with the Tsar about this, that he should let the Grand Prince’s people into the city without great losses; and whatever he wished to ask of the sovereign, the sovereign would grant him that; and he also ordered him to say that the Kazanian princes wanted the Grand Prince’s viceroy to be in Kazan.

And Alexei came to the Tsar in Kazan and spoke to the Tsar according to the sovereign’s instruction. And the Tsar replied that he could in no way violate the Basurman customs. “But I cannot live in Kazan; the Kazanians have sent to the Nogai Horde to ask for a tsar, and I am going to the town of Sviyazhsk; otherwise I would be killed by them.”

On the Departure of Tsar Shigalei from Kazan

In the same winter, on March 6, in the first week of Holy Lent, Tsar Shigalei rode out from Kazan to a lake to fish, and with him were many princes and murzas, Kazanians and townspeople, and all 500 streltsy of the Grand Prince. And the Tsar, having ridden out of Kazan, said to the Kazanian princes and murzas: “You want to kill me and petitioned the Tsar and Grand Prince against me, that he should remove me because I treat you badly, and should give you his viceroys; and the Tsar and Grand Prince has ordered me to leave Kazan, and I am going to him, and I am taking you with me to him; there we will settle matters.”
