On the Tidings That Came to Kolomna Concerning Tula
in the month of June, on the twenty-first day

On the eve of Wednesday a courier galloped up to the sovereign from Tula and said: “Crimean men have come into the environs of Tula, to the city of Tula; and they think it is the tsarevich with a few people.”

The sovereign at once sent a host. He ordered the boyar and voivode Prince Pyotr Mikhailovich Shchenyatev and voivode Prince Andrei Mikhailovich Kurbsky of the right-hand regiment to go from Kashira to Tula; and from near Rostislavl the sovereign ordered boyar Prince Ivan Ivanovich Pronsky and Prince Dmitry Ivanovich Khilkov to go; and from near Kolychev the sovereign ordered Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Vorotynsky to go; and the great sovereign himself intended to set out in haste in the morning with many men.

And as the night hours came on and Wednesday arrived, a townsman came galloping from Tula and said that few people had come—seven thousand men—and that, after ravaging, they had turned back from the land.

The sovereign ordered the aforesaid voivodes to go in haste, and he ordered that he himself should not be kept without news for even an hour, and he ordered them to send messengers ahead of themselves to learn how many Crimeans had come and whether it was possible to reach them.

And on Thursday, when the sovereign was sitting at table, a courier from Prince Grigory Temkin came galloping from Tula and said that the Crimean Tsar had come and was attacking Tula, and that many people were ravaging, and that they had much equipment, and many Janissaries of the Turkish Sultan.

The sovereign, without delay and without finishing his meal, came to the Church of the Dormition of the Most Pure Mother of God and ordered Vladyka Feodosy to serve a moleben and to sing Vespers quickly in the side chapel: this happened on the Nativity of John the Forerunner, since the tsar had the pious custom of never sinning against the rule established by law.

And he sent the voivodes of his regiment out of the city, and ordered the regiment to be drawn up in the fields; and he ordered the voivode of the great regiment, Prince Ivan Fedorovich Mstislavsky, and the voivodes of the left hand to hurry ahead of him and cross the Oka River.

The sovereign himself, while hearing Vespers and the moleben, came to the most pure image of the Mother of God of Her Honorable Dormition, and cried out with tears, praying: “O Lady, do not give over Your flock to be seized by the savage beast that roars and suddenly devoured!” And he offered other prayers known only to God alone, received a blessing from Vladyka Feodosy, and ordered him not to leave the church until God should accomplish something.
