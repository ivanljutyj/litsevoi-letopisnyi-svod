On the eighth day after the prince’s death his brother, Prince Dmitry Shemyaka, arrived, and then, after serving a funeral service over the body, they placed the deceased in a log coffin, tarred it, wrapped it in a felt rug, and carried him to Moscow on a bier; on the way they twice dropped him from the bier. They brought him to Moscow on the 14th day of October, the 23rd day after his death, and carried him into the Church of the Archangel Michael in the Square, and served a burial service over him while he still lay in the log coffin.

After the funeral service they split the log coffin, thinking to find only bones, but when they opened it they saw a whole body, in no way damaged; his burial vestments were also in the same condition as before, when they had been placed on him; his face was white as that of a sleeping man, and showed no trace of blackness or lividness. And so, giving thanks to God, they laid the prince in a tomb beside his father, Prince Yuri Dmitrievich.

In that same winter the Greek Isidore, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus, returned to Rus, to Kiev, from the Pope and the Eighth Council in Rome, and began to call himself a legate from the side of the apostle.

In that same winter Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich went with an army against Novgorod, and Archbishop Euthymius of Novgorod came out from Novgorod to him, and with him boyars and men of property; they met the prince at Dereva near the town of Demon and petitioned the prince, made peace with him according to the old ways, and gave him eight thousand Novgorod rubles.

The Pskovites, helping the Grand Prince, conquered much Novgorod land and caused much trouble and misfortune.

At that same time the Novgorod commanders with the Zavolochians seized by war the patrimony of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich and caused much harm.

In that same winter Prince Yuri Vasilyevich the Elder died, and another Prince Yuri Vasilyevich was born on the 22nd day of January; and a year before that, on the same day, the commemoration of the Holy Apostle Timothy, on the 22nd day of January, Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich was born, and Zinovy, abbot of the Trinity-Sergius Monastery, baptized him.

Concerning Isidore

In that same spring Isidore returned to the Russian land, and on his way to Moscow he said that at this great council the Eastern churches had united with the Western; and he wished to unite Orthodoxy, the great Muscovite realm of Grand Prince Vasily Vasilyevich, with the Latin faith, but the Lord God did not permit this.
