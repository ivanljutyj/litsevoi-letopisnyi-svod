This storm shook many churches, and many stone churches in the city of Moscow were stripped and their tops swept off; and the upper part of the city walls was scattered and carried away, and in villages and volosts many churches were torn from their foundations and carried far away. Many houses, too, were ruined and scattered. And old forests and pine woods, and dense woods, and great oaks were torn up by the roots. Some had their tops broken off; others were broken to half their height, others to a third, and some to the very root, even those that were very thick. All the people were in great sorrow and despondency, and not hoping to survive, they did not know what to do for their own good, but only asked forgiveness of one another. Such was the love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ that it injured no one anywhere, neither in cities nor in villages, nor on the road, nor in the field, nor in the forests. Even those who were in those churches that were scattered or buried by those trees were all saved by His love for mankind. For this punishment was from our Lord Jesus Christ upon us sinners, that we might turn from our evils and learn to do His will. And in that same hour this storm ceased, and the thunder and the flashing of lightning, and silence came, as before.

In the same year, on the 18th day of the month of July, on Friday at the second hour of the day, the sun began to grow dark and was like the moon on the fifth day; and after the fourth hour had passed it became full as before; and in the night of that same month the moon also vanished.

In the same year the godless tsar of the Great Horde, Akhmat, came with all his forces to Pereslavl Ryazansky and stood before it for six days, during the fast of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos. Many of his warriors received wounds from the townspeople, and many were killed. And having accomplished nothing against that city, they withdrew from it in shame and went off into the Field.

In the same year a stone Church of the Epiphany was erected in Moscow by the abbot of the Trinity-Sergius Monastery.

In the year 6969 from Adam (the year 1469 from Christ)

In February, the Grand Prince Boris Alexandrovich of Tver reposed, and after him his son Michael took the throne in Tver.

On the death of Metropolitan Jonah

That same spring, on the 31st day of March, at the second hour, on Tuesday of Holy Week, the most reverend Jonah, Metropolitan of All Rus, reposed and was laid in the cathedral church of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos in Moscow, behind the left choir, opposite the most reverend metropolitans Cyprian and Photius, who had been before him. The Grand Prince was not then in Moscow; he was in Vladimir, for he had then begun a war with the Kazan tsar.
