By God’s will and His great mercy, and that of the Most Pure Theotokos, and of the great wonderworkers and all the saints who from the beginning of time have pleased God, and through the undoubted faith and diligent prayer, steadfast labor, and unwavering hope in Almighty God of our pious and Christ-loving tsar Ivan, God-loving and man-loving, and by the blessing of our humility and the prayer of the whole hierarchal and priestly order and of all Orthodox Christendom, God was pleased that this city be built, in which holy churches of God have been built to the honor, praise, and glory of His most holy and great name, glorified in the Trinity, and of His immaculate Mother, the Most Pure Theotokos, and of all the saints; and this city was built and filled with many people and with every good. And the Lord God granted to our pious tsar and to all his Christ-loving army an easy, bloodless victory over all adversaries; the Kazan kingdom submitted and surrendered wholly to the will of our pious sovereign and tsar, and the Kazan tsar and tsaritsa gave themselves into his hands, and their strong power—the Crimean princes, ulans, and murzas—was taken captive by the army of our Orthodox tsar. And our pious tsar and sovereign entrusted the city of Kazan, with all the Kazan uluses, to his tsar Shigalei, and all the Mountain Cheremis joined the new city of Sviyazhsk for our sovereign tsar and grand prince; and countless Christian captives, male and female, youths and maidens, and infants, from impious hands joyfully returned to their own lands, restrained by no one, but by God’s mercy and sovereign freedom and your courage and bravery (freed). The Crimean tsar and the Nogai princes, and many hordes, and the Lithuanian and German kings sent their envoys to our tsar and sovereign with charters of peace and honorable gifts concerning love and peace, and all the ends of the earth were terrified, and from many countries tsars and tsareviches and people of other great powers came to our tsar and sovereign of their own will to serve for his favor and great bounty; and our cities and lands were peaceful and remained without tumult, while the native Kazan princes and murzas, seits and ulans, and all people of rank come of their own will to serve our pious tsar, by God’s providence and fear of our Orthodox tsar, and were unwavering. And for all these ineffable blessings, what shall we render to the Lord our God? Or what thanksgiving and praise shall we bring Him? And heed, children, what does God require of us for all this? Nothing else, only the keeping of His commandments. His commandments are not heavy; one need only have true righteousness, immaculate and fervent faith in Almighty God, mercy, peace, and unfeigned love for all, righteous judgment, and defense of the poor and prompt redress, and liberation of captives, and purity of soul,
