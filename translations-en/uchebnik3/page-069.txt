He extended a great helping hand to Pope Leo, and again settled him in Rome, seated him on the throne, and subjected all to his authority.

Pope Leo gathered a council with the hierarchs and Roman princes, so that they might choose for themselves a pious emperor in Rome, so that the empire, he said, might begin from Rome: “For the Greek emperors always pass from heresy to heresy; therefore we shall not suffer greatly.” Speaking thus, he gave thanks to Charles, who had been his helper and conqueror of his enemies; he summons him and crowns him as emperor according to the Greek law, but introduced Jewish laws into this: it is unknown with what meaning he anointed him.

Thus the first union of cities fell apart, and quarrels began, and a sword entered between mothers, dividing and cutting with fierce anger and fury the young and the old. The new Rome, Constantinople, descended from the old and ancient Rome, clothed itself in division and hatred.

If Charles was called a Christian after his grandfather and father, honored the laws of Christ, observed piety, and showed honor to the episcopal, priestly, and monastic order, and rendered great honors to God’s holy churches, yet there came with him priests and teachers secretly bearing heresy within themselves, and so little by little they began secretly to corrupt the Lord’s people and to teach them heresies. Later this was also shown openly in their teaching; they were loved and encouraged by Emperor Charles; and they began to assert that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son as from a single principle, and that the service must be performed on unleavened bread, and many other heresies.

Pope Leo, seeing God’s people perishing, since they gladly accepted the teaching of the heretics, but he had no wise teachers in Rome to drive out this evil heretical teaching, wished to send to Tsargrad for wise men, but could not do this because he was afraid, for at that time the imperial scepter in Constantinople was held by Emperor Theophilus, son of Michael, a fierce iconoclast.

Then Pope Leo sends to Jerusalem to Patriarch Thomas, asking him to send him wise men able to expose and drive the heretics out of the Roman city.

Patriarch Thomas of Jerusalem chose Michael, synkellos of the great church, and sent him to Rome to Pope Leo together with his disciples, the brothers Theodore, Theophanes, and Job.

They agreed to go to Constantinople and expose the iconoclast Emperor Theophilus, in order to strengthen God’s people in the veneration of the honorable icons. Emperor Theophilus ordered them seized and subjected to torments.
