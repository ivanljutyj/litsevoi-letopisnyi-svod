Many of the Muscovites also agreed with them: both boyars and merchants; there were also monks who thought as they did. And the princes with their counsellors began secretly to arm themselves and to seek a suitable time to drive out the Grand Prince. They chose a fortunate occasion when, by their evil counsel, the Grand Prince decided to go and venerate the Life-Giving Trinity and the relics of the Wonderworker Sergius. And he went with his noble sons, with Prince Ivan and with Prince Yuri, and with few people, thinking of nothing else but to feed the brethren of the great Lavra who were there.

And messengers were sent every day from Moscow by the traitors to Prince Dmitry Shemyaka and Ivan of Mozhaisk.

And they, having joined together, stood in Ruza, ready like hounds on the hunt or wild beasts wanting to be sated with human blood. And when the news came to them that the Grand Prince had left the city, they at once went secretly to Moscow.

And they came on the twelfth day of February, on Saturday, at the ninth hour of the night, and on the Sunday of the Prodigal Son they took the city, where there were none to oppose them. For no one knew of it except their fellow conspirators, who opened the city to them. And they, entering the city, took the grand princesses, Sophia and Maria, and plundered the treasury of the Grand Prince and of his mother; and the boyars who were there they seized and plundered, and many other townspeople they plundered. And that same night Prince Dmitry sends Prince Ivan of Mozhaisk against Grand Prince Vasily to the Trinity, secretly, with many of his own men and the men of Prince Ivan of Mozhaisk.

He came galloping to the Grand Prince during the liturgy on the Sunday of the Prodigal Son—Bunko was his name—and told him that Prince Dmitry Shemyaka and Prince Ivan of Mozhaisk were coming against him with an army. And the Grand Prince did not believe him, because that Bunko had only shortly before left for Prince Dmitry, and he said: “They are troubling us, but I am in a cross-kissing oath with my brothers; how can such a thing be?” And he ordered him to be driven from the monastery and turned back. And when he was on the Vora, armed guards took him and beat him.

And the Grand Prince, although he did not believe him, nevertheless sent guards to Radonezh.

They came and stood on the hill above Radonezh to keep watch. And the armed guards of Prince Ivan recognized them from afar, but they did not notice those armed men and did not believe those tidings.
