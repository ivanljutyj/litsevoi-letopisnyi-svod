In the guard regiment — Prince Dimitri Semyonovich Shastunov and Pyotr Petrovich Golovin. And the sovereign released the Astrakhan tsarevich Kaibula Akhkubekovich, and with him all the Gorodets Tatars, and ordered him to march behind the forward regiment. And when the sovereign had released the voivodes and the tsarevich, he ordered them to assemble in Novgorod.

And he ordered the boyars Prince Peter and Prince Dmitry to send a letter to the king, and in the letter they were to write, reminding him of the truce that he had concluded with the Novgorod namestnik Prince Boris Gorbaty for sixty years, and how he had violated it, why discord had arisen; and if he wished to preserve the truce that he had sworn to, he should be at the border on the Nativity of Christ that same year, and should bring with him those guilty of starting the war and execute them in their presence, or send the best men who could be trusted; and the boyars would be at the border on the Nativity of Christ with a great host, and having found the guilty, they would bring them from both sides and execute them, so that they would not betray the kissing of the cross and would not violate the truce; but if the king did not come by that time and sent no one, then the tsar and grand prince ordered them to wage war for his grievance and for the king’s injustice: “And the kissing of the cross will be on you and on your officials, and the blood of old and young will be shed because of you, King Gustav, and your counsellors, and not by our righteous sovereign, not by us, his namestniks.”

And the letter arrived in Vyborg two weeks before the Nativity of Christ, and from Vyborg his officials Avraam and his companions wrote to Prince Dmitry Paletsky that Yakov Bag had gone to war without the king’s knowledge, and he wrote boastfully of how the voivodes had faltered before him, but he made no mention of the meeting.

And the tsar and grand prince ordered his voivodes to march into Swedish land, placing their hope in the merciful God and in the life-giving Cross, by which he had confirmed the truce with the namestniks: that blood had not been shed by him; and he ordered them to avenge his grievance as the merciful God would help. And after the Baptism of Christ the voivodes crossed the border.
