The Shuiskys rose against Prince Ivan Fedorovich Belsky and Mikhail Vasilyevich Tuchkov because Prince Ivan Belsky and Mikhail Tuchkov advised the Grand Prince that the Grand Prince should grant Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Golitsyn boyar rank and make Ivan Ivanovich Khabarov an okolnichy; but Prince Vasily and Prince Ivan Shuisky did not want this. And many quarrels arose among them over advantages and their kinsmen; each cared for his own, not for the sovereign’s, not for the land’s. And over this they began to hold great enmity and wrath against Metropolitan Daniel, Prince Ivan Belsky, Mikhail Tuchkov, and the clerk Fyodor Mishurin.

And in that quarrel the Shuiskys and other boyars ordered the Grand Prince’s clerk Fyodor Mishurin to be stripped in the Grand Prince’s own courtyard by the young princes, boyar sons, and courtiers, and they laid him naked on the block and ordered him executed; and his head was cut off by the prisons without the sovereign’s command, on October 21, a Sunday.

In that same year the boyar Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuisky died.

That same winter, in February, Metropolitan Daniel was removed from the metropolitanate by the boyar Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shuisky and his advisers.

On the Installation of Joasaph as Metropolitan

That same winter the great sovereign and autocrat Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus sent for all the archbishops and bishops who were under his rule.

And the archbishops and bishops assembled in the renowned and reigning city of Moscow.

And careful inquiries were made concerning the metropolitan, and by divine judgments, through the grace of the Holy Spirit, by the will of the great sovereign and autocrat, and by the counsel of the archbishops and bishops and the whole sacred council, Joasaph Skripitsyn, hegumen of the Sergius Monastery of the Life-Giving Trinity, was chosen and named to the metropolitanate.

And he was brought into the metropolitan court on the 6th day of February, Thursday of Meatfare Week.
