The Sign. In the year 6978 from Adam (1478 from Christ)

On the fifteenth day of the month of April, on Palm Sunday, after Vespers at the twelfth hour of the day, there was a sign in the sun: a circle appeared in the sky, with one edge at the middle of the sky and the other descending toward the west; the half of that circle toward the west was red within and green around it; and the half of that circle which was above, at its edges, was of the same color as a rainbow in the sky; and the other half, which was above, was white. And beneath that circle were two arcs in the same manner: green on the outside and red within. And the ends of those arcs reached all the way to the west, and in the middle the sun had already inclined closer to the west. And between the arcs, beneath the very circle, as if between two horns, one of which ended toward the south and the other toward the north, it was bright as if the sun were shining, and it stood directly above the real sun. And below that sun, between the horns on both sides, there was another sun, opposite the real sun. And so it was visible for two hours, and then the circle and the arcs disappeared. And those three unusual suns came together and became as one, and it went after the real sun, a little above it; and they set together: first the real one, and after it that unusual one. Around the circle that had its edge at the middle of the sky, on both sides of it, a little higher, it was white, but it did not rise upward; rather it descended down to the semicircle of their ends. Many people in Moscow saw this same sign. But in other cities, they say, no one saw it anywhere.

On the Wedding of Prince Andrei Vasilyevich of Uglich

That same spring, on the twenty-seventh day of May, on the Sunday of the Blind Man, Prince Andrei Vasilyevich of Uglich married in Moscow; he took Princess Elena, daughter of Prince Roman Mezetsky; and Metropolitan Philip married them in the Church of the Most Pure Mother of God.

In the same year, on the thirtieth day of August, at the end of the second hour, Moscow caught fire inside the city in the Podol, near the Church of Constantine and Helena, from Bogdan Nosov's courtyard; and by Vespers it had burned entirely. Then from midnight a strong wind arose, and across the river many courtyards burned, while others remained standing. And the firebrands and birch bark with fire were carried very far, many versts. And the Grand Prince was then in Kolomna. There remained in the city the courtyard of Prince Ivan Yuryevich, and two courtyards of his nephews, and that of Orina Alekseeva: for the wind blew from there.
