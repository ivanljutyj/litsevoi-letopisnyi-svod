And from the time of that miracle, the God-given heir to the Russian kingdom was conceived in her womb; and when the fitting time had passed, a promising son was born to Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich by Tsarevna Sophia, and he was named Vasily Gabriel in the year 6987 from Adam (the year 1487 from Christ), on the 25th day of March, on the very feast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Mother of God, at the eighth hour of the night. He was baptized at the Trinity in the Sergius Monastery, and Archbishop Vassian of Rostov and Paisius, Abbot of the Trinity, baptized him on April 4, on Palm Sunday, at the wonder-working relics of the venerable wonderworker Sergius, concerning which there should be a separate account. This account was told by Joasaph, Metropolitan of all Rus, who heard it from the lips of Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich himself, autocrat of all Rus. Afterwards other sons and daughters were also born to them.

In the same year, on July 11, the pious and Christ-loving Grand Prince Ivan laid the foundation of a stone church of John Chrysostom and dismantled the former wooden one. It had originally been a church built by Moscow merchants, but that monastery had already begun to decline.

The Grand Prince established the abbot of that church above all the cathedral priests and abbots of the city of Moscow and the suburban priests, even a year before he made his vow, because his name was given when the feast of the Translation of the Relics of John Chrysostom is celebrated, on January 27. And in the side-chapel of that church he ordered another church to be built in the name of the Apostle Timothy, on whose commemoration, on the 22nd day of the same month, he was born. And that dismantled wooden church he ordered to be set up in his monastery at the Protection in the Gardens; and this was done when the first, small one was dismantled.

On the Completion of the Cathedral Church of the Most Holy Mother of God in Moscow

In the same year the great cathedral church of the Dormition of the Mother of God in Moscow was completed under the pious Grand Prince Ivan Vasilievich, of Vladimir and Novgorod and autocrat of all Rus, and under his son Grand Prince Ivan, and under Metropolitan Gerontius. That church was wonderful in size, height, light, sonority, and spaciousness; no such church had existed in Rus before, except the church of Vladimir; and the master was Aristotle.

And the Grand Prince ordered the Novgorod masters to roof it; the masters began by roofing it with wood very well, and over the wood with German iron.
