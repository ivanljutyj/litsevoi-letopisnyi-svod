On the Discord with the Swedish Germans

In the year 7063 from Adam (the year 1563 from Christ), a dispute arose between the people of Oreshek and Korela and the Swedish Germans over the border. A truce had been made between the Tsar and Grand Prince's Novgorod governors and the Swedish King Gustav for sixty years, and twenty years had passed, and many disputes began on both sides; the Germans crossed the border in many places.

And the Novgorod governors sent to the king, but the king did not wish to have dealings with the governors, and ordered the Vyborg governors not to admit the Novgorod envoys to him, but ordered the Vyborg governors to communicate with Novgorod; and the Novgorod governors would not listen to this. And therefore many people living along the border quarreled, and on both sides many murders and robberies began; and the Germans, coming, began to burn villages and kill boyar sons, and detained many merchants in their country and did not release them, and impaled a boyar son by the throat on a stake.

And the boyar and Novgorod governor sent his envoy Nikita Kuzmin, a local man, to the Swedish King Gustav concerning all those offenses; and they held him in Vyborg for a long time and then let him through to the king, but the king did not let him go back.

And Prince Dmitry Paletsky, having communicated with the sovereign Tsar and Grand Prince, sent his own man Ivan Bibikov to recover his people, and with him local men and commoners, so that later, after communicating on the matter, an exchange might be made.

And the Germans routed Bibikov utterly and came in war upon many places.

And the Vyborg governor Magnus Nikolaev wrote to the governor in Novgorod that many skirmishes had occurred and blood had been shed along the border on both sides, and that this needed to be investigated.

And Prince Dmitry, having communicated with the sovereign, sent Andakan Tushin to the border for a meeting.

And the Germans from Vyborg, without waiting for Andakan, sent to Paletsky to ask for letters of safe conduct for their Vyborg envoy; and Prince Dmitry gave a safe-conduct letter, but the Germans did not go to the meeting and did not send their man for the letters of safe conduct. And news came to Prince Dmitry that the king had completely violated the truce and had sent his army against the Tsar and Grand Prince; and he wrote to the sovereign.
