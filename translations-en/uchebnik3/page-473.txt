On the Tsar's Journey to the Trinity

In the same month, on the 21st (day), on Thursday of the fourth week of Peter's Fast, the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus, with great desire and great faith, went on foot to pray to the Life-Giving Trinity in the Sergius Monastery, with his tsaritsa grand princess Anastasia and his brother prince Yuri Vasilyevich, to hear at the Life-Giving Trinity the all-night vigil and matins, a moleben, and the divine liturgy; and having given the abbot and the brethren alms and sufficient provisions, he came to Moscow in the same month on the 28th (day), on Thursday.

In the year 7057 from Adam (the year 1557 from Christ)

On the 14th (day) of the month of September, on Friday, on the feast of the Exaltation of the Honorable Cross, the blessed tsaritsa grand princess Anastasia went on foot to pray to the Life-Giving Trinity in the Sergius Monastery; and the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus rode from Moscow to the same Life-Giving Trinity in the same month on the 22nd (day), on Saturday; and from the Trinity the tsar and grand prince rode on a tour for his tsar's hunt with his tsaritsa and grand princess Anastasia to Sloboda and Dmitrov, and to Zvenigorod, and Mozhaisk; and he returned to Moscow on the 28th (day) of the month of October, on Sunday.

In the same month, Kazan people came to conquer the Galich lands; many people came, and their leader was Arak-bogatyr.

And the Kostroma namestnik Zakhar Petrovich Yakovlev with his companions went after them and caught up with them on the small river Ezovka, on the Gusev field; and the grand prince's commanders beat all the Kazan people; and they killed Arak-bogatyr and beat other Kazan commanders; and he sent many alive to the sovereign, and with that news Zakhar sent Belyanitsa Zuzin to the sovereign.

On the Lithuanian Ambassadors

In the same winter, on the 19th (day) of January, Lithuanian ambassadors came to the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus from the Polish king Sigismund Augustus: pan Stanislav Petrovich Kishka, voivode of Vitebsk, and pan Yan Yuryev, son of Komaevsky, holder of Ozh and Perelom, and scribe Gleb Esmanov; and they spoke on behalf of the king about eternal peace; but they did not conclude eternal peace, but concluded a truce for 5 years.
