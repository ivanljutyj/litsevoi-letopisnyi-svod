The Grand Prince then summoned his father, Metropolitan Daniel, and said to him: “I have confessed to you, father, all my secret, that I desire the monastic life; why should I lie here thus? But vouchsafe me to be clothed in the monastic order; tonsure me.” And after waiting a little, he said to him: “Must I lie like this, lord metropolitan?”

And he ordered the Holy Communion to be brought and kept near him; and he began to cross himself with his hand and say: “Alleluia, alleluia, glory to Thee, O God!” He also spoke individual words from the oikoi of the Akathist, and other words he spoke quietly to himself; and again he crossed himself with his hand, saying: “Rejoice, O womb of the divine Incarnation”; to this he added: “We bless thee, venerable father Sergius, and honor thy holy memory, teacher of monks and converser with angels.”

He grew very weak; and after that the end of his life drew near, and he could not speak with his tongue, but asked for tonsure and ordered himself to be dressed in monastic garments, and took a sheet and began to kiss it lovingly.

And then his right hand ceased to rise, and his boyar Mikhail Yuryevich lifted it; but he did not stop making the sign of the cross on his face and looked upward, to the right, at the image of the Most Pure Theotokos, which stood before him on the wall.

Then Daniel the Metropolitan sent for Elder Misail Sukin and ordered monastic clothing brought into the chamber; and the epitrachelion was with the metropolitan, and everything for the tonsure; and the renunciation had been made when the Grand Prince confessed to the metropolitan, when he received the Gifts on the Sunday before St Nicholas’s Day. And he then instructed the metropolitan: “If they do not allow me to be tonsured, then lay the monastic clothing on me as on a dead man, for this has long been my desire.”

And Elder Misail came with the clothing, while the Grand Prince was approaching his end. The metropolitan took the epitrachelion and handed it across the Grand Prince to Joasaph, abbot of the Trinity.

But Prince Andrew Ivanovich and boyar Mikhail Semyonovich Vorontsov did not want to allow the Grand Prince to be tonsured; and Daniel the Metropolitan said to Prince Andrew: “I do not bless you in this age or in the age to come! And you cannot take this from me, for a silver vessel is good, but a gilded one is better still.”

The Grand Prince was departing this life; and hastening to tonsure him, Daniel the Metropolitan laid the epitrachelion on the Trinity abbot and ordered the Trinity abbot Joasaph to begin the service of tonsure.
