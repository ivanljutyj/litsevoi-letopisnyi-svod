The canon orders that one who wishes to dismiss his wife under the pretext of piety be excommunicated; and if after this he does not correct himself and does not wish to take her back, then, according to the fifth apostolic canon, he is to be deposed from his rank—whether priest, deacon, or subdeacon. Bishops, however, must not have wives; this was decreed by the Sixth Council, as was already said earlier in the 12th canon; from that time celibacy was established for bishops.

Canon 3 of the Holy Council of Carthage

It is decreed that those ordained to these three degrees, through this very consecration joined by a bond of chastity—I mean bishops, priests, and deacons—it is fitting for holy bishops and priests of God, Levites serving at the divine sacraments, to be continent in all things, so that they may obtain all that they ask of God, and that we also keep the tradition of the apostles and what has been confirmed from antiquity.

Canon 4 of the Same Council

It is decreed that bishops, priests, deacons, and all who touch the holy things must keep chastity and abstain from relations with their wives.

Interpretation of Canons 3 and 4 of the Same Council

The canon is as follows: first of all it speaks of the continence of bishops, priests, and deacons, and especially of chastity and abstinence from relations with their wives. Guided by these canons, and especially by the one above, those living in Rome and those of like mind with them falsely assert that neither priests nor deacons ought to have intercourse with their wives, but should remain unmarried. In this they sin, as in all else, since the fifth canon of the holy apostles says: a bishop, priest, or deacon must not leave his wife under the pretext of piety; but if someone says that among us bishops have no wives, this was legislated by the holy fathers of the Sixth Council in the 12th canon, so that, not contradicting what was established by the apostles, we say this, but caring for the salvation and increase of people, and not in order to cast suspicion on the priestly order. The same Sixth Council, in its 13th canon, interpreted this canon thus: priests, deacons, and subdeacons must abstain from their wives at the time when they touch the holy things; and it says that if anyone dares to deprive a priest, deacon, or subdeacon of lawful relations with his wife, he shall be deposed.
