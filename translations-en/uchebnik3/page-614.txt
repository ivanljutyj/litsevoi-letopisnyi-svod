And in accordance with that news, the sovereign sent Dyak Rzhevsky from Putivl to the Dnieper with Cossacks, ordering him to go along the Dnieper below the Crimean uluses and take captives for interrogation, to learn the Tsar’s intentions.

And Dyak gathered with the Cossacks and came to the Psel River, had boats built, and set out according to the Tsar’s instruction.

And the sovereign sent Danilka Chulkov and Ivan Maltsev down the Don to verify the same Crimean news.

In that same month Grisha Velin and Istoma Matyukov returned and said that Yusuf’s sons had not allowed them to reach Ismail, had routed them, and had captured some Nogai envoys and seized Andrei Tishkov.

On the Letter from the Viceroy of Kazan

In the month of April the boyar and viceroy Prince Peter Ivanovich wrote from Kazan: he had sent the boyar Peter Vasilyevich Morozov, and with him boyar scions, Cossacks, streltsy, newly baptized men, and Tatars against the bank-side people and the town of Chalym; and Peter went there, conquered them and killed many, burned the town, and returned safe and sound.

Peter Morozov Went from Kazan against Arsk

In the month of May the same Peter Morozov went from Kazan against Arsk, and beyond Arsk on the Mesha River he met Peter Devlyak-murza, brother of Kulai-murza, with the Arsk people; and Peter sent against him the commanders, boyar scions Prince Peter Glebov Zasekin and Roman Pivov, and two Enaleys, and with them were boyar scions, streltsy, and newly baptized men; and they routed Devlyak with all his companions, captured him alive, and caught many of his companions.

And after that Peter fought for ten days and conquered all the Arsk environs, killed many people, and led away numerous captives.

And together with Peter went the voivode Fedor Ivanovich Buturlin, serving without precedence, and they came to Kazan, by God’s grace, safe.

On the Expedition of Peter Morozov and Fedor Saltykov

In the month of June the boyar Peter Morozov set out from Kazan, and with him the voivode Fedor Ignatievich Saltykov and boyar scions from Kazan and from the town of Sviyazhsk, and replacement troops, veteran troops, and annual servicemen, and Tatars, and newly baptized men, and Cossacks, and streltsy, beyond Arsk, beyond Oshit and Urzhum, toward Vyatka. And they stopped fifty versts short of Vyatka, and waged war there, took countless captives, women and children, and killed all the men; and they came to Kazan, by God’s grace, with all their men safe.
