In the year 6980 from Adam (the year 1480 from Christ)

On the first day of September, at the beginning of the indiction, which is the beginning of the new year, on the feast of the Venerable Simeon the Stylite, the Grand Prince came to his patrimony, to the glorious city of Moscow, having defeated his enemies, executed those who opposed him and the cruel apostate Novgorodians who did not wish to obey him, all of whom with God’s help he brought under his will, and he received much wealth and acquired great glory.

He was met with praises by countless multitudes of the Moscow people, known and unknown. His noble son Grand Prince Ivan, and his brother Prince Andrew the Younger, and other princes and boyars, nobles, guest-merchants, merchants, and leading people met him on the thirty-first day of August, where he stopped for the night.

Some people met him seven versts out, and the footfolk closer.

His Holiness Philip, Metropolitan of all Rus, met him with crosses near the great church, having come only from the Great Stone Bridge as far as the well on the square, with all the sacred council in the city square; and there was great joy for all the people of his realm in the city of Moscow.

About the Novgorodians, How They Drowned on Lake Ilmen

In the same year, in September, on the second day, a great multitude of people set out from Novgorod with wives and children across the lake in large boats, each to his own place. They say there were more than 180 of those boats, and in a boat fifty people or more. And when they were in the middle of that lake, a strong wind suddenly blew upon them and sank all those boats; not one of them survived, and all the people drowned, and their goods.

That same autumn Kirei came to the king from the Horde with the tsar’s envoy. And the king at that time was at war with the Hungarian king.

On the tenth day of the same month of September, Anton Fryazin came from Venice, and with him came an envoy to the Grand Prince from Venice, from the Venetian doge Nikola Tron, by name Ivan, surnamed Trevisan. He was sent to the Grand Prince by that doge and by all the lands under him to petition that the Grand Prince show favor and order that Trevisan be escorted to Tsar Akhmet of the Great Horde. And he had sent to him with many gifts and a petition that he come to their aid at Tsargrad against the Turkish Sultan.

The same Trevisan, having come to Moscow, first went to Ivan Fryazin, the Moscow moneyer, because that Ivan Fryazin was a native of those parts and was known there, and told him everything about why he had come to Moscow, though he had not yet been to the Grand Prince.
