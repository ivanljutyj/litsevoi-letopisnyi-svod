And the Crimean Tsar ordered his son, all the princes and murzas, telling them to go hidden from the stanitsa scouts and watchmen.

And they crossed the Donets low down and came to the Don, and went upstream along the Don; and the tsarevich was to go to Ryazan, Ak-Magomet-ulan to Tula, and the Nogai murzas and Shirin princes to Kashira, and to wage war divided.

And when they came to the river Mecha, two days' march from the frontier, they seized five fishermen there, and those fishermen said that the tsar and grand prince was in Moscow, and that the tsar had sent commanders against the Germans. And the tsarevich inquired about Prince Dmitry Vishnevetsky and Ivan Sheremetev, whether they were in the German land, and the fishermen said that Ivan was in Ryazan, and Vishnevetsky in Tula, and Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky in Kaluga. And by God's mercy and through the prayers of His Most Pure Mother of God and the great wonderworkers, fear and trembling seized the people of the Crimean Tsar, and soon, turning their troops back, they rushed to flee. O great miracle of the Lord's bounty! They had not heard our tsar near them, nor seen the tsar's regiments, but only heard his name—that he abides on his throne in the reigning city of Moscow—and deliverance from the enemies round about came, and the enemies were afraid and fled, and turned back and rushed into flight, driven by no one, but only by the wrath of God and the name of our pious tsar, and by his righteous labors, and his care for the Christians entrusted to him by God, and his warm faith in God, and earnest prayer, and fulfillment of Christian law.

And the pious tsar heard what great miracle had occurred with him himself, with the sovereign and his realm; he heard of the deliverance from the invasion of foreigners and the shaming of the enemies, and gave great thanks to God with his father Metropolitan Macarius, and with all Orthodox Christians offered prayer and sent alms to all holy places, and ordered the commanders from Kaluga to go swiftly after the tsarevich: in the main regiment, his servants Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Vorotynsky and Prince Daniil Odoyevsky; in the forward regiment, the commanders Prince Ivan Ivanovich Kashin and Vasily Ivanovich Umnogo-Kolychev; and in the guard regiment, Prince Fyodor Vasilyevich Sisiev and Prince Andrei Okhlyabinin.
