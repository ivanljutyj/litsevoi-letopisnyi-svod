To the boyars who had kissed the cross in the evening, the sovereign said: “Boyars, I cannot; I have no time for that. But as you kissed the cross to me and to my son Dmitry, so act accordingly.”

And those boyars who had kissed the cross to the sovereign and to his son Tsarevich Prince Dmitry began to meet the boyars and tell them to kiss the cross to the sovereign and to his son Tsarevich Prince Dmitry.

But the boyars who did not wish to kiss the cross to the sovereign and to his son Tsarevich Prince Dmitry began fiercely to quarrel with those boyars who had kissed the cross to the sovereign and his son. And they said to them that they wished to rule themselves, and they did not wish to serve them or submit to their authority.

And the noblemen who were in the sovereign’s council, Alexei Fedorov son of Adashev and Ignaty Veshnyakov, the sovereign also brought to the oath that same evening.

And at the same time Prince Vladimir Andreevich and his mother gathered their boyar sons and began to give them payment — money.

And the boyars began to speak to Prince Vladimir about this, that his mother and he were not acting well: the sovereign is unwell, yet he is rewarding his people. And Prince Vladimir and his mother began to be indignant and aggrieved at the boyars, and the boyars began to be on their guard against them and stopped allowing Prince Vladimir Andreevich to see the sovereign.

At that time there was in the Church of the Annunciation, which is on the gallery of the tsar’s court, a certain priest named Silvester, by birth a Novgorodian. This priest Silvester was held by the sovereign in great honor and in spiritual and deliberative counsel, and was as it were all-powerful: everyone obeyed him, and no one dared oppose him in anything because of the tsar’s favor. And he gave directions to the metropolitan, and to bishops, and to archimandrites, and to abbots, and to monks, and to priests, and to boyars, and to clerks, and to officials, and to governors, and to boyar sons, and to all sorts of people, and, one might say, managed all affairs, both ecclesiastical and royal. And no one dared do anything against his will, and he controlled both authorities, the ecclesiastical and the royal, as though he were both tsar and hierarch, except that he had neither the name, nor the image, nor the throne of a hierarch or a tsar, but only that of a priest; and he, together with his advisers, was greatly honored by all.

This Silvester was among the advisers and in great favor with Prince Vladimir Andreevich and with his mother Princess Eufrosinia, for through his efforts they had been released from exile.

This same Silvester then began to forbid the boyars, saying: “Why do you not let Prince Vladimir see the sovereign? The brother is better disposed to the sovereign than you boyars.” The boyars said to him: “They do as they swore to the sovereign and to his son Tsarevich Prince Dmitry, as if their sovereign—”
