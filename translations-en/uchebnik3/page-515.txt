God did not want the Christian people to perish, and news came to the city that the Orthodox tsar was approaching; the impious tsar also learned the same from captives. And from the city walls they saw a great dust rising to heaven from the approaching people of the grand prince, and the people in the city cried out in many voices: “Merciful God, help us, the most Orthodox tsar is approaching us!”

And all rushed against the godless, and out of the city came not only the voivodes but also all men and women who had found manly courage, and young children; they killed many Tatars beneath the city, and killed the tsar’s brother-in-law Prince Kambirdei, and the Orthodox captured the artillery equipment, cannonballs and arrows, and much gunpowder brought to ravage the city.

And at that hour the impious Crimean tsar Devlet-Girei fled from the city with great shame, and he did not manage to do the city any harm, and he ran off to the Field, because the city of Tula was near the Field.

And Grisha Sukhotin told the sovereign about the voivodes, that they had come to Tula, and soon the tsar fled from the city; and Prince Grigory Temkin sent him to the sovereign, and he met all the voivodes at the city; and the voivodes came to them: from Pronya, Prince Mikhail Repnin, and from the city of Mikhailov, Fyodor Saltykov.

And the pious sovereign stopped to spend the night at Kashira, and in the morning he intended to set out, for the road was long; and early on Friday Bebekh Glebov came to the sovereign from the voivodes and said on behalf of all the voivodes that the voivodes had come to Tula, but the tsar had fled three hours before their arrival; and many people were in raiding parties, and by God’s mercy and the sovereign’s fortune the voivodes killed many people and took many alive, and freed many captives. And they overtook the tsar on the river Shivorok, and the tsar fled.

And he left the carts, and slaughtered many camels, and abandoned some alive. And they brought to the sovereign many prisoners and camels of the Crimean tsar. The blessed tsar glorified the All-Merciful God for granting him such a victory over the impious, and ordered the prisoners to be tortured; and the prisoners said that the tsar had gone against Rus because they had told him in the Crimea that the tsar and grand prince was in Kazan with all his people; and when he came close to Ryazan, he seized the station guards. And they said that the great sovereign was in Kolomna, waiting for the tsar and wishing to fight a direct battle with him for Orthodoxy. And the tsar wanted to return from there to the Crimea, but the princes said to him: “If you wish to cover your shame, the grand prince has a city, Tula, on the Field, behind great fortresses and forests and far from Kolomna, and you will deal with it as you did in Lithuania with Bryaslavl.”
