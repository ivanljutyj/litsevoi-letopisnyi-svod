And they came to Zvenich and spent the night there from Saturday to Sunday. And on Sunday in the first half of the day they ordered the priests who were with them to serve the liturgy for them and to prepare food; and after hearing the liturgy, they wanted to sit down to dine. But at other churches they had not yet managed to serve the liturgy when at that time the Kazan Tatars came upon them—all the princes and all their land, with a river force in boats and cavalry along the bank.

Seeing this, the grand prince’s commanders and all his warriors rushed to their boats, rowed out to meet the Tatars’ river force, and began to fight them. And the Christians overcame the Tatars; they fled to the bank where their cavalry was.

The Tatar cavalry began shooting at them from the bank, and they retreated to their own bank; and the Tatar boatmen turned back after them again.

They, turning about, drove the Tatars back to their own. And so they fought all that day until night itself, and parted to spend the night, each on his own bank.

And after that Prince Fyodor Semyonovich Khripun, of the Ryapolovsky princes, defeated the Tatars on the Volga on 4 July.

On Kazan

In the same year there was a battle and fierce slaughter on the Volga, at the mouth of the Kama, between the Ustyug men and the grand prince’s retainers and the Kazan Tatars; and many were killed there on both sides. Then too they killed Nikita Konstantinovich, and captured Yurla Pleshcheyev and his companions; the rest of the Ustyug men fought their way to Novgorod.

In the same year the grand prince of all Rus, Ivan Vasilyevich, sent his brothers, Prince Georgy and Prince Andrey the Great, and Prince Vasily, son of Prince Mikhail Andreyevich, and his other commanders with many men on horseback as an army to Kazan.

In the year 6978 from Adam (1478 from Christ)

On 1 September Prince Yury Vasilyevich with all the Muscovite warriors came before Kazan, and the river forces went on foot to the city. The Tatars rode out of the city, and after fighting a little, fled into the city.

The Muscovites pursued them, stood before the city, and cut off their water.

Tsar Obriam, seeing his great misfortune, began to send his envoys to Prince Yury Vasilyevich, humbly petitioned, and accepted peace on the grand prince’s terms. And they returned to Moscow with all their army.
