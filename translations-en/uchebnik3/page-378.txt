On the Cloud and Lightning, and Thunder, and Drought, and Murk

In the same year, on the twenty-third day of July, at the eleventh hour of the day, a great and terrible cloud rose from the winter east; great lightning flashed incessantly, with thunder and whirlwind. And lightning killed nine people in the city of Moscow; and beyond the Yauza, at the church of the holy martyr Nikita, it pierced the wall and singed the gold of the Deesis, but did not damage the images; and it split the icon cases of the icons. And in other places it killed many people and livestock.

And from that time there was no rain until the month of September, and there was drought and a great murk, bitter as smoke. The forests burned up, and the watery marshes dried up, and at the third hour of the day the red sun appeared, and a ray shone from it all day, so that it was impossible to look at it. And after two hours it set and became invisible; such a great murk was there that even the birds soaring in the air could not be seen, and a person could see another person only close by, and birds fell to the ground.

On the Appearance of a Star

In the same summer, in July, at the fourth hour of the night, a star appeared above the suburbs of the glorious city of Moscow, between the churches of the holy prophet Elijah and the Epiphany, beyond the Market, small and faintly shining, but a long and broad ray shone from it toward the winter east; and it was visible for many nights.

On the Deceit of Islam

When Tsar Islam was expelled from Crimea, he went to the autocratic sovereign, Grand Prince of all Rus Vasily Ivanovich, to give himself to him as a son and for service.

The Grand Prince then sent Prince Mikhail Kubensky to him in the Field. Having come, he assured him that he would not forsake Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich or his son.

But later Islam lied, to his own ruin, and having joined with Tsar Safa-Girey, who had been expelled from Kazan, and with other tsareviches and with many people, they went toward the Russian borders.

On the Ryazan War and on the Arrival to the Grand Prince from Wallachia of His Tatar Nebolsa Kobyakov, and on Islam’s Muster against Rus

In the same year Peter, Voivode of Wallachia, sent back to Grand Prince Vasily Ivanovich his Tatar Nebolsa Kobyakov, whom the Grand Prince had sent to the Wallachian voivode through Crimea.

And from Wallachia he went to the Grand Prince, and overtook Tsar Islam and Tsar Safa-Girey and other tsareviches, who were standing in the Field and gathering people, wishing to go against the Grand Prince’s Ryazan borderland.
