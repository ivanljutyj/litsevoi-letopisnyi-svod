And the voivodes set out, each to his own regiment: in the great regiment were the boyars and voivodes Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky, Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Shuisky, and Prince Mikhailo Ivanovich Kubensky; and from Pakhra came Tsarevich Shigalei and the boyar and voivode Prince Yuri Mikhailovich Bulgakov, and the Grand Prince ordered Tsarevich Shigalei and Prince Yuri Mikhailovich to be in the great regiment as well; and in the advanced regiment were Prince Ivan Ivanovich Turuntai Pronsky and Prince Vasily Fedorovich Okhlyabinin; and in the regiment of the right hand were Prince Ivan Vasilyevich Shemyaka Pronsky and Semen Semenovich Bezzubtsev; and in the regiment of the left hand were Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Troekurov and Prince Vasily Semenovich Mezetsky; and in the guard regiment were Prince Yuri Ivanovich Temkin Rostovsky and Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Ushaty.

Then Tsar Saip-Girey came to the bank of the Oka River on July 30, on Saturday, at the third hour of the day, and halted on a hill in a high place; and many Tatars came to the bank with rafts, intending to cross the river. The Grand Prince's voivodes hastened against the tsar, and before that Prince Ivan Ivanovich Turuntai Pronsky and Prince Vasily Okhlyabinin came to the bank with the advanced regiment and began shooting at the Tatars. The Tatars saw the advanced regiment and thought that these were all the men; they came quickly to the bank in great numbers, waded into the river, and began to board the rafts; and from the advanced regiment they began to shoot many arrows, and the arrows flew like rain. The tsar ordered the cannons to fire and the arquebuses to shoot, and ordered the people to be driven back from the bank, for he wished to cross the river. And the advanced regiment wavered; but at that very time Prince Semyon Ivanovich Punkov Mikulinsky and Prince Vasily Semenovich Serebryany Obolensky came up with their regiment and stood on the bank; and after that Prince Mikhailo Ivanovich Kubensky came with his regiment, and then Prince Ivan Mikhailovich Shuisky took his place with his regiment, and then Prince Dmitry Fedorovich Belsky took his place with many men of his regiment. And the tsar saw that the great regiments were coming, and the regiments of the right hand and of the left; and the tsar began to look and marvel that many men were coming, the regiments were drawn up beautifully, the men splendidly arrayed, in armor, each voivode with his own regiment; and they came to the bank opposite the tsar. And the Grand Prince's voivodes began to deploy the regiments and to set men against the Tatars. And the tsar saw that the guard regiments also were coming, many men; and the tsar summoned Prince Semen Belsky and his princes: "I was told that the Grand Prince's men had gone to Kazan, and that I would meet no resistance; but never have I happened to see so many equipped men, neither mace-bearers nor argamak-riders, in one place. And my old Tatars, who have been in many campaigns..."
