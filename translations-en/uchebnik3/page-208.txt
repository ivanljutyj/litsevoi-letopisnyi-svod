And the boyars, having come, told those words to the grand prince; and the grand prince sent back to them those same boyars of his with an answer, and ordered them to reply thus: "You have petitioned me, the grand prince—you, our pilgrim, and our patrimony Veliky Novgorod—calling us your sovereigns, and asking that we graciously indicate to our patrimony what our state should be in our patrimony, in Veliky Novgorod; and I, the grand prince, told you that I wish the state in my patrimony in Veliky Novgorod to be such as our state in the Nizov land in Moscow; and now you yourselves are instructing me and setting conditions as to what our state should be; so what kind of state is mine?" And the archbishop, and the posadniks, and the prosperous townsmen petitioned, and said to the boyars thus: "We do not set conditions for our sovereigns, the grand princes, concerning their state; but would our sovereigns, the grand princes, show favor to their patrimony and declare to their patrimony Veliky Novgorod what their state should be in their patrimony; because, sir, their patrimony Veliky Novgorod has not known the Nizovian custom since the time when our sovereigns, the grand princes, have held their state in the Nizov land."

And the boyars, having gone, told this to the grand prince; and the grand prince sent them again with an answer and ordered them to speak thus.

Prince Ivan Yuryevich began to speak:

"The grand prince says to you, his pilgrim the archbishop, and to you, posadniks and prosperous men, and to the common people: as for your petition to me, the grand prince, that I should declare to you what our state should be in our patrimony—for our state of the grand princes is such: there is to be no veche bell in our patrimony in Novgorod, no posadnik is to be, but we are to hold our state; otherwise, on what basis are the grand princes to be in their patrimony? There are to be volosts, there are to be villages, as with us in the Nizov land; and whatever lands of ours, of the grand princes, are in your hands, let them become ours; and as for your petition to me, the grand prince, that there should be no removal from the Novgorod land, and that we, the grand princes, should not encroach upon the hereditary estates of the Novgorod boyars, in their lands, we by this grant favor our patrimony: do not fear removal, and we do not encroach upon their estates; and let the court be in our patrimony in Novgorod according to the old custom, as the court has stood in the land."

On the same day, Sunday, the Pskovians came to the grand prince outside the city: the grand prince's viceroy in Pskov, Prince Vasily Vasilyevich Shuysky, and with him the Pskov posadniks with many warriors, posadnik Aleksey Vasilyevich Kochanov, and Zinovy Sidorov, and Stefan Maksimov, and other posadniks, and boyars, and sons of boyars, and many Pskovians.
