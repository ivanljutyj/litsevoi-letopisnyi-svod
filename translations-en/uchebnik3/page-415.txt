And his mother, the Grand Princess Elena, said to Prince Andrei: "A report concerning you reaches us that you bear anger against us; you should stand firm in your truth and not listen to the words of evil men; for we have nothing bad in our heart concerning you; and you should name those men who set us at odds, so that henceforth there may be nothing bad between us." And Prince Andrei named no one by name, but said that it had been a doubt on his part.

And the Grand Prince Ivan and his mother, the Grand Princess Elena, gave Prince Andrei their sworn word that they had nothing bad in their heart against Prince Andrei, and they sent him away to his patrimony with great reassurance.

And Prince Andrei came from Moscow to his patrimony, but he did not set aside his doubts and fear, and in his heart he bore anger against the Grand Prince and against his mother, the Grand Princess, because they had not added to his patrimony.

And evil men joined in this, and began to tell the Grand Prince Ivan and his mother, the Grand Princess Elena, against Prince Andrei that Prince Andrei intended to flee. And the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess, did not believe those words, because even before that many wrongs had been spoken of between them; and at that time Kazan was not at peace with the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess.

And the Grand Prince Ivan and his mother, the Grand Princess Elena, sent for Prince Andrei on account of the Kazan affair. And Prince Andrei did not go to the Grand Prince and to his mother, the Grand Princess, but pleaded that he was ill, and asked that a physician be sent to him.

And the Grand Prince Ivan Vasilyevich and his mother, the Grand Princess Elena, sent Master Feofil to Prince Andrei.

And Feofil came and told the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess, concerning Prince Andrei that his illness was slight: he says there is a sore on his leg, and he lies in bed.

But the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess, were in great doubt about this, why Prince Andrei had not come to them in Moscow for the great Kazan affair.

But the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess, sent their envoys to Prince Andrei — to inquire about his health and other matters, and secretly to find out about Prince Andrei: whether there was any report about him and why he had not gone to Moscow.

And those envoys, on arriving, told the Grand Prince and his mother, the Grand Princess, that he had newcomers with him who did not always live with him, but they did not dare speak; and those who were on friendly terms with them told them secretly that the prince lay abed because he did not dare go to Moscow.
