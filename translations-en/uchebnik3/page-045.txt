“Also, that the holy apostolic Roman see, together with its hierarch, is accepted throughout all the ends of the universe as the first vicar of the blessed and supreme Apostle Peter, vicar of Jesus Christ, and as father and teacher, the glory of all churches and all Christians, according to the tradition of Jesus Christ, Who entrusted Saint Peter to shepherd, judge, and care for all churches, which was accepted by the holy canons of the ecumenical councils. Our most honorable brothers the patriarchs accept such a statute according to the true tradition: the first, the Patriarch of Constantinople; the second, of Rome; the third, of Alexandria; the fourth, of Antioch; the fifth, of Jerusalem; and they oblige themselves in all their places and laws to accept and fulfill it in all things. Given in Florence, in the cathedral church of the ecumenical council, in the year of the Lord’s Incarnation 1439, on the 6th day of June, in the ninth year of our priesthood. We all so believe and decree, and all, having agreed, signed in the year 6948 from Adam (1448 from Christ), on the 6th day of the month of June.” And the Greek emperor John of Constantinople signed, and with him Isidore, Metropolitan of Russia.

Joseph, Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople, died soon after, on the 10th day of the same month of June, and they laid him in the Catholic church, that is, in the church where the pope had been during the council. The most blessed Mark, Metropolitan of Ephesus, did not sign their heretical and false statements; the same did Gregory, Metropolitan of Iberia, Isaac of Nitria, and Sophronius of Gaza, and they left the city. But the emperor summoned them back from the road, while Gregory of Iberia fled by sea to his king in Venice and thus avoided being brought back. Having returned, they nevertheless did not submit to the emperor and did not fear his wrath and ban. The council ended in the city of Florence in the year 6948 from Adam (1448 from Christ), on the 6th day of the month of June.

The End of Their Accursed Council

Eugene, Pope of Rome, was in great joy with all the Romans. And he honored the emperor, all the Greek metropolitans and bishops, their monks and their philosophers, and all the Greeks present at that council, and bestowed many gifts: he gave gold and silver to the emperor, the metropolitans, the bishops, their philosophers, and all the Greeks, thereby drawing them into his heresy.
