Senzjak-bey, however, sent to the sultan, saying: “If you yourself do not enter the city, the city will not be overcome.”

He, striving with all his might to find the tsar and tsaritsa, was afraid to enter the city and was in great deliberation; and he summoned the tsar’s boyars and stratigoi whom they had taken in battles and whom the pashas had taken under their protection.

The sultan gave them his firm word and sent gifts, and sent them with pashas and sanjakbeys to proclaim to the townspeople in all the streets and to the soldiers in the towers the sultan’s word with an oath, that they should cease fighting without any fear, and without killing and taking captive: “But if not, the sword will devour all of you, and your wives, and your children.”

And the fighting ceased, and all surrendered into the hands of the boyars and stratigoi and pashas; and hearing this, the sultan rejoiced and sent into the city to clear the streets and squares.

On the eleventh day after the battle he sent sanjakbeys through all the streets with many people to combat treason, while he himself went with all his ranks from his gate to the gate of Saint Romanus, to the Great Church, into which the patriarch and all the clergy and countless people, and women, and children had been gathered.

And coming to the square by the Great Church, he dismounted from his horse and fell to the ground on his face; taking a handful of earth and sprinkling it on his head, he thanked God; and marveling at this great building, he said: “Truly these people were and have passed away, and after them there will be no others like them”; and he went into the church.

And the abomination of desolation came into the sanctuary of God; and he stood in His holy place.

And the patriarch and all the clergy and the people cried out with tears and lamentations and fell before him.

He, giving a sign with his hand that they should stop, said to them: “I say to you, Anastasius, and to all your company, and to all the people: from this day and henceforth do not fear my wrath, nor slaughter, nor captivity”; and turning, he said to the pashas and sanjakbeys: “Let them forbid all the army and every rank of my gates, let them do to all the city people, and to women, and to children neither slaughter, nor captivity, nor any other harm; and if anyone transgresses our command, let him be put to death.”

And he ordered all to be sent out of the church, let each go to his own house, for he wished to see the church’s structure and treasures.

And the people went out until the ninth hour, and still many remained in the church, waiting.

And, not waiting for them all, he went out of the church and saw that a multitude of people had come out, who were walking through all the streets, and he marveled: how many people had come out of a single church!

He went to the church courtyard, and there a certain Serb met him, bringing him the head of the tsar.
