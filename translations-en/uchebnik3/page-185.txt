On the morning of Friday, at the first hour of the day, he was buried; and no one from the city or the surrounding country was at his burial, only the priests of that monastery and his disciples, the elders tonsured by him; one secular priest was then present, the confessor of Prince Andrew the Younger, named Nikita. This same Nikita, and others of the brethren, disciples of Paphnutius, related that there was no one to sing or read the funeral canons, but only one of his disciples, named Innocent, read the funeral service with many tears; and all the rest were bathed in tears, so that many of their tears fell and poured onto the ground. The brethren then numbered 85.

By birth, they say, he was from the village of Kudinovskaya, son of Ivan; his teacher was Elder Nikita, and Archimandrite Markel tonsured him into the monastic order, and he was then 20 years old, and he spent 30 years in monastic life, in the priesthood, and as abbot. And when he went to that place where he founded the monastery of our Most Holy Lady, the Mother of God, of her honorable Nativity, he first erected a wooden church, then built a stone one, and painted it wondrously, and adorned it with icons and books and all church furnishings, so that even the autocrats of the Russian land marveled. During his lifetime people came for his blessing and prayers on their behalf, as to the ancient venerable fathers of our land; also other princes and boyars, and other Orthodox Christians, both from the Lithuanian land and from other countries, asking blessing and prayers from him. And he was in his monastery for 33 years.

On the 31st day of the month of May, from Friday to Saturday, on the eve of All Saints, there was a great frost, so that the puddles froze, and all manner of vegetables in kitchen gardens and orchards were killed, and all the produce.

And in the same month, while the ambassadors of the Grand Prince were still in Novgorod, a revolt occurred among the Novgorodians: they held a veche, and coming upon Vasily Nikiforov, they seized him and brought him to the veche, and shouted: “Traitor, you were with the Grand Prince and swore an oath to him against us.” He answered them: “I swore an oath to the Grand Prince in this, that I should serve him faithfully and wish him well, and not against my sovereign, Great Novgorod, nor against you, my lords and brothers.” They killed him without mercy, and at the denunciation of Zakhary Ovin; and later they killed that Ovin together with his brother Kuzma in the archbishop’s courtyard. And from that hour they went mad like drunkards; each spoke whatever he pleased, and they again desired to go over to the king.

The Grand Prince, having heard of this from his ambassadors, and also from the local posadniks who were well disposed to him, that all had scattered, and that such evil turmoil had arisen among them after their first crime, as also before their Shelon defection.
