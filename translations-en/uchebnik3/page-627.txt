On the Famine

In the same summer of 7065 from Adam (the year 1565 from Christ) there was famine throughout all the Muscovite towns and throughout the whole land, but most of all in the Trans-Volga: at harvest time there were heavy rains, and beyond the Volga in all places frost killed all the grain; and a multitude of people died in all the towns. And this happened because of our sins and for the failure to keep the law; for every injustice the Lord mercifully punishes, desiring to see conversion, repentance, and the cessation of human wickedness, so that, as a Benefactor, by His grace He might establish the people in His commandments and in all piety. And that winter was cold, strong frosts continued all winter, and there was not a single day of thaw; and there was more snow than usual, many villages were snowed in, and people died in the villages, and many people also perished on the roads; they died as martyrs for our sins, the poor brethren of our faith perish cruelly from hunger and frost, and in dying they are cleansed; but if we, seeing this, do not cleanse ourselves and do not repent, then a still greater condemnation awaits us for this.

On the Envoys from the Elected Swedish King Gustav

In the month of February envoys came to the tsar and grand prince Ivan Vasilyevich of all Rus from the elected Swedish king Gustav: Prince Sten Eriksson and Archbishop Lavrenty of Uppsala, and Bishop Agricola, and Michael of the town of Abo, and Bintegulent, and Knut Knutsson, and the royal seal-bearer Alivey Lavrentyev, to petition that the sovereign tsar and grand prince set aside his anger and halt his army and conclude a truce. And in their embassy they spoke about how the king should reach agreement with the tsar and grand prince, and not with the Novgorod governors: it was from this that the discord began and the war flared up. And the tsar and grand prince sent to them with an answer his okolnichy Alexei Fedorovich Adashev and dyak Ivan Mikhailov, and ordered them to say that the sovereign would not break the old custom, and if King Gustav wished to conclude a truce with the Novgorod governors according to the old custom, then let him clear the borders according to the letters of King Magnus, which he signed with Prince Yuri when he was in Novgorod, and let him release all those people whom he had unjustly detained.
