And at Ismail’s petition he sent to the Volga the streltsy head Grigory Kaftyrev with streltsy and Fedka Pavlov, and ordered them to guard the crossings on the Volga from Yusuf’s sons, and to be in communication with Tsar Derbysh in Astrakhan, whatever news there might be, and they were to row to Astrakhan to help Astrakhan.

Concerning the message sent from Astrakhan by Pyotr

In the same year, in the month of May, Pyotr Turgenev sent word from Astrakhan that Tsar Yamgurchei with his brothers and Yusuf’s sons had come to Astrakhan, and Tsar Derbysh had agreed with Kasym-murza and with Yusuf’s sons, and they had beaten Tsar Yamgurchei with his brothers, Tsarevich Nagaily and his brother Tsarevich Krym-Girei; and the murzas, Yusuf’s sons, beat them, and Derbysh ferried them across the Volga for that.

Dispatch from the Volga from Grigory Kaftyrev

In the same month of May Grigory Kaftyrev wrote from the Volga that he had met Pyotr Turgenev, who was rowing from Astrakhan, and he said that Tsar Derbysh had released him to the tsar and grand prince, but had not sent his own ambassadors, and said that he was in communication with the Crimean tsar. And Grigory turned Pyotr back, and he himself rowed to Astrakhan with the streltsy and all the Cossacks.

Concerning the couriers sent from the Crimea

In the same year, in the month of May, Tsar Devlet-Girei sent from the Crimea the courier Yan-Magmet, and wrote about friendship, and sent his ambassadors and released the grand prince’s ambassador Fyodor Zagryazhsky; and that the tsar and grand prince should send ambassadors to him, and he set a term of twenty days; but he lied, did not send the ambassadors, and went himself to war against Rus.

Concerning the arrival from the Nogais of Ignaty Zagryazhsky and Myasoed Visly

In the same year, in the month of June, Ignaty Zagryazhsky and Myasoed Visly came to the sovereign from the Nogais, and they said that Yusuf’s sons and Kazy-murza, son of Urak, had come against Ismail, and had driven Ismail out, and had killed Kasai-murza.
