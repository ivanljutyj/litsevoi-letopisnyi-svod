They came to the town of Smeltin, killed Germans in the posad, attacked the town, and fought them until midday; and the Cossacks broke the stone wall with crowbars in two places, entered the city, killed the Germans, and took the city with all its equipment, and took 20 Germans alive and 200 Latvians with all their goods. And the Tsar and Sovereign rewarded the atamans and Cossacks for their service.

On the Ambassadors from Lithuania

In the same month ambassadors came to the Tsar and Grand Prince from King Sigismund Augustus: Vasily Tishkovich, Voivode of Podlaskie and Starosta of Minsk, and Marshal Nikolai Shimkovich Poshunshensky, and Scribe Sangayko, and they spoke in the King’s name about eternal peace, asking about the city of Smolensk and others, and about the Livonian Germans, that the Tsar and Grand Prince should moderate his wrath against them and not order them conquered. And the Tsar and Grand Prince sent to them with a reply his okolnichy Alexei Fedorovich Adashev, his treasurer Fedor Ivanovich, and clerk Ivan Mikhailov, and ordered them to say that our sovereign does not wish to renounce his hereditary cities, but for Christian peace and the freedom of Christians from infidel hands he wishes to conclude eternal peace, keeping for himself all his ancient patrimonies, Kiev and others, and what his father earlier took, Gomel; and they should make eternal peace as the border is written in the truce charters. And the ambassadors said that they had no such instruction, that they must speak only of eternal peace, in order to extend the truce. And so it was said to them: the truce has been concluded for another 3 years, if the sovereigns, having learned the truth between themselves, defend Christianity from the impious. And many truces had been concluded, and in those truces the sovereign kisses the life-giving cross on the truce charters that in those years they would send great ambassadors between themselves about eternal peace and good relations for Christian peace and defense against plunder by the impious. Much Christian blood is shed unjustly by infidel hands because of their sovereigns’ troubles and discord, and our sovereign Tsar and Grand Prince all these years remembered the ambassadors and envoys of his brother King Augustus, so that his brother might conclude with him eternal peace and good accord for the deliverance of poor Christians; from his brother the King our sovereign has heard not one word for good all these years, except that he has entered into a truce. Every year the King sends tribute and great gifts to the Crimea, inciting it against Orthodoxy; and the Crimeans take many gifts, while in his royal realm they wage war every year and take cities, and shed innocent blood of poor Christians unjustly, and take captives, and scatter them over the face of the whole earth; and from whose hands will God require that Christian blood? Is it not from the pastors to whom power is given by God? And our sovereign Tsar and Grand Prince, as he came of age and mounted his horse, so against all infidel sovereigns
