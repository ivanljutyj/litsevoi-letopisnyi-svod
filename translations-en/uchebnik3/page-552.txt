The tsar’s commanders with the boyar sons courageously attacked the infidels. And the tsar’s warriors fought courageously in all places at all gates, hand to hand, with spears and sabers; in the crush they cut at one another with knives.

On many streets Christians and Tatars, both sides with many spears, long fought with spears without overcoming one another.

With God’s help the Orthodox prevailed

God helped the Orthodox; Christians went over the roofs and struck the Tatars from above.

From all places the commanders reported to the sovereign: “Your warriors fight stoutly, and God helps you, pious tsar, but many of the weak are plundering Kazan’s treasures; and you, tsar, help us.”

The tsar sent his boyars and many close men to beat those so that they would not plunder the treasures but would help their own; and he sent the commanders of his tsar’s regiment with men to all places to help.

And the Christians approached the Kul Sharif mosque, by the Tezit ravine, and here many pagans gathered with Kul Sharif the mullah and fought fiercely.

They killed Kul Sharif with his regiment

By God’s mercy the Orthodox overcame Kul Sharif and killed all who were with him. All the Tatars fled to the Tsar’s court.

The Orthodox warriors approached the Tsar’s court and mercilessly cut down pagan men and women; rivers of blood flowed through the valleys.

And the Tatars gathered at the Tsar’s court, seeing their final destruction, said to one another: “We must flee the city quickly; God helps them; already many of ours have perished.” And they all ran to the Elabugin Gate and began leaping from the wall and forcing their way through the gate, and many ran off making for the forest.

And at that same hour they told the pious tsar that many people had thrown themselves from the wall and fled from Kazan. And in that place was the commander, boyar Prince Pyotr Mikhailovich Shchenyatev; he pursued them with his regiment, and many of them were killed.

And the commander Prince Andrei Mikhailovich Kurbsky rode out of the city, and, mounting a horse, pursued them and broke in among them; they knocked him from his horse and wounded him many times, and took him for dead, but by God’s mercy he later recovered. The Tatars fled separately toward the forest.
