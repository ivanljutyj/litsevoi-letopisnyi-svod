The princes began to petition him not to grieve: “Wicked people have stirred up the land; wait until they come to an agreement.”

And the boyars sent Kudagul-ulan and Prince Burnash into the city (and asked): “Why have you betrayed us? Yesterday and today you swore, and now you have betrayed us; yet we, in accordance with what we swore, still do them no harm.” At that time people were living in the posad with all their property, and the boyars, not wishing to sin against their oath on the cross, did harm to no one.

And Kudagul-ulan and Prince Burnash rode out of the city to Prince Semyon and said: “The people fear a massacre and do not listen to us.”

There was much negotiation and talk with them, but the voivodes saw that no good would come of it, and ordered the seizure of Kudagul-ulan and Prince Liman with his son, Prince Shamsa, Prince Chura Kadyev, Prince Bogodan of Arsk, Prince Khankildey, and other princes and Cossacks; and all those whom the tsar had brought out were also to be taken, except those who had betrayed and fled into the city: Islam, Kebyak, and Alikey.

And the voivodes stood all day at the gates and in the posad and spent the night there, and in the morning they stood until noon and negotiated; then Chapkun betrayed the sovereign, and all the Kazanians with him, and the boyar sons whom the voivodes had sent into the city, together with all the voivodes’ baggage trains, were shut up in the city.

And the boyars went to the town of Sviyazhsk, but they did not burn or attack the posad, in accordance with their oath on the cross; they waited for an agreement and trusted in the life-giving cross that they had kissed, so that no harm should come to our sovereign.

And the voivodes came to the town of Sviyazhsk on the 12th day of the same month of March, on Saturday of the second week of Holy Lent, and threw into prison all those princes whom they had taken near the city and those whom the tsar had brought out.

And the Kazanians, having betrayed the sovereign, sent to the Nogai Horde to ask for a tsar, and began coming in war to the Mountain Side and drawing the Mountain people away from the sovereign. And the Mountain people killed some of their envoys, two princes, Shahchura and Shamay-murza, and brought them to the voivodes; and the voivodes rewarded the Mountain people and executed the traitors.

And the voivodes also came to an agreement with Tsar Shigaley that the tsar would remain to spend the spring in the town of Sviyazhsk with the tsaritsa, while the boyar Ivan Sheremetev rode to the sovereign, and all the envoys rode with him; and he came to the sovereign on the eve of the Annunciation of the Most Pure (the Mother of God), and they told the sovereign about the Kazanian betrayal. The sovereign was not surprised at this...

And the sovereign himself considered how to deal with them for their betrayal, and after the river opened he sent in boats to the town of Sviyazhsk his boyar and dvoretsky Daniil Romanovich, and with him boyar sons and Cossacks; and he ordered Tsar Shigaley to go with the tsaritsa to Gorodok, and instructed Daniil to send with the tsar the okolnichy Vladimir Morozov.
