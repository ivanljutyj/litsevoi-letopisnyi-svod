As he came out of the church, the Tsar said only one thing: “Whoever wishes to suffer for God’s churches and for the Orthodox Christian faith, let him come with me.”

And mounting Pharis, he went to the Golden Gate, desiring to meet the godless one; in all, three thousand soldiers gathered with him.

And at the gate he met a multitude of Turks guarding him, and he struck down many of them.

He went into the gate, but could not pass because of the many corpses; and a multitude of Turks met him, and he fought with them until night.

Thus suffered the right-believing Tsar Constantine for God’s churches and for the Orthodox Christian faith on the twenty-ninth day of May, having killed with his own hand, as those who remained said, more than six hundred godless Turks. And what was said came to pass: what Constantine founded would also end with Constantine, since sins are condemned by God’s judgment in due time, for “Crimes and lawlessness will destroy the thrones of the mighty.” O, great is the power of the sting of sin! O, how much evil does transgression do! O, woe to you, Seven-hilled city, if the impious prevail over you! For how many graces of God shone upon you, when He glorified and magnified you above other cities, when in many ways and many times He taught and instructed you by good deeds and most glorious miracles, and when He glorified you by victories over enemies; for He did not cease to teach and call to salvation, and comforted you with worldly abundance, and adorned you in every way. Likewise the most immaculate Mother of Christ our God favored and preserved you at all times with ineffable benefits and countless gifts. But you were like a madman who turns away from God’s mercy and His bounties; and now God’s wrath has been revealed against you, and He has delivered you into the hands of your enemies! And who will not weep or wail over this?

But let us continue our account. The Tsaritsa, in the hour when she received forgiveness from the Tsar, took the monastic habit. The remaining generals and boyars, taking the Tsaritsa, noble maidens, and many young wives, sent them in ships and galleys of Zustunei to the islands and to Amorea, to their kinsmen.

But the people in the streets and courtyards did not submit to the Turks, but fought with them, and many people, women and children, fell that day.

Also the soldiers in the towers did not surrender, but each fought against two Turks, both outside the city and inside the city.

And, pursued by day, they fled and hid in the rocks, and by night they crept out and attacked the Turks.

And some people threw tiles and slabs down upon them from the chambers, and threw burning wooden roofs of the chambers with fire upon them, and others did other great harm. The pashas were terrified.
