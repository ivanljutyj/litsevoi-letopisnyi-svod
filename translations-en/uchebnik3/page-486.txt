And seeing such love and bounty in him, and his constant care for the people entrusted to him by God, all began to strive to suffer for God and for him, the sovereign, and the surrounding peoples began to feel fear and dread. And it cannot be described how he called God to his aid and mounted his horse and began to draw up his army; God assisted him, seeing his unwavering faith in Him and his zeal in Christ. But let us return to what came before.

The Kazan people in the city and in the villages saw that the mountain people had firmly joined the sovereign, while they themselves were in great difficulty: war was being waged against them from all sides, and there was no passage from any state, nor did they expect help from anywhere, because the grand prince's men stood firm along the Volga from Vasilygorod and along the Kama, and up the Kama to Vyatka, and up the Vyatka at all the crossings—boyar sons, streltsy, and Cossacks—by the sovereign's command, on service and under voivodal watch. And the Kazanians began to quarrel with the Crimeans, and Chuvash from the Arsk side came to fight the Crimeans: "Why do you not petition the sovereign?" They came to the tsar's court, and the Crimeans, Koshchak-ulan and his companions, fought them and defeated the Chuvash. God punished them with internecine strife for the much shedding of Christian blood.

And many princes and murzas came to serve the sovereign, seeing the sovereign's great favor and their own exhaustion; and the sovereign, showing them favor, sent them back to Sviyazhsk and ordered them to be settled there.

And the Crimeans, seeing that the Kazanians would submit to the sovereign, all gathered and, plundering what they could, fled from Kazan—300 men, ulans and princes, and azeis, and murzas, and good Cossacks, besides their servants—and abandoned their wives and children.

And they fled to the Kama, where the grand prince's boyar sons and streltsy were stationed; and they ran up the Kama and reached Vyatka, but there they did not detect the grand prince's men, who stood secretly at the watch posts—Bakhteyar Zuzin with the Vyatchans and the sovereign's Cossacks, Fedka Pavlov and Severga.

And the Crimeans made rafts and began to cross, and Bakhteyar with the Vyatchans and Cossacks came against them and routed them utterly and drowned them; and they captured alive Koshchak-ulan, Barbolsun-ulan, Torchi the prince-bagatyr, Ishmakhamet-murza, Suleisha's brother, a Crimean, and other ulans and princes.
