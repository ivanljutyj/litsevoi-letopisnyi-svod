He especially glorified the power of the Honorable and Life-Giving Cross and bestowed many honors upon it, for he was confident in it because of the miracles that always occurred: by it he defeated and overthrew the impious Maxentius, trampled the united Byzantine enemies by the power of the Cross, and subdued the Scythians, guided by divine zeal.

After Saint Helena returned from Jerusalem to Constantinople, the Christ-loving Emperor Constantine made three great crosses according to the number of visions that appeared to him in battles: the first in Rome, when he drowned Maxentius; the second in Byzantium, when he took Byzantium; the third when he built a bridge over the Danube and subdued the Scythians. In the image of the three visions he made three honorable crosses of pure copper and called them by these holy names: “Jesus,” “Christ,” “Conquers.” The one called “Jesus,” made of cast gold, he set facing east on the arch where the Market Square now is, warmly revealing to all his piety, since by the power of the Cross he had conquered the Greek rebellions.

The all-honorable and divine cross called “Christ” he placed on top of the red Roman column in the Brotherly-Love quarter of Constantinople, which stands to this day in the same place where it was fixed. And another life-giving, wonder-working divine cross, named “Victory,” was later called “Anikitos” (“Invincible”) by Heraclius, the faithful and Christ-loving emperor; and so it is called by all who believe in the name of Christ from then until this day: the life-giving Cross of the Lord “Saint Anikitos,” by which the Power of Christ is invincible. Great Constantine raised it upon a high marble column, in a beautiful place, on Bread Street. Many powers and signs were wrought through it at the place where Christ our God was nailed to the Tree of the Cross, granting us all healing of diseases, deliverance to those suffering from cold and terrors, and health and sight to damaged, unseeing eyes.

Let no one disbelieve me, but what I want to tell will be for the faith, not for the cunning-minded and not for heretics, but for faithful souls and those wise in goodness; but the most rational are worthy of no miracles. Many who have heard of this honorable cross relate that an angel of the Lord late at night, three times a year, descended upon it from heaven with a star-like sign, as if with the light of flashing lightning: on the 7th day of May (the sign of the Cross over Jerusalem); on the honorable and holy Exaltation of the Cross by faithful hierarchs, on the 14th day of September; and also on the Adoration of Christ our God at Mid-Lent. On these three days the honorable Cross of Christ, Saint Anikitos, is carried around, and incense is offered with sweet singing, and a delicate ringing sounds around it.
