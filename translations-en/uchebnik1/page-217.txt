And they tore the leather from shields and ate it with joy. And if they found rotten hay or straw, this was food to them, as if prepared from fragrant grass.

Some people gathered small bits of hay and sold them for half a grivna, four Attic zolotniks. And why should I describe in such detail the calamity in which the city found itself? It is enough for me to cite such a case as has been described neither among the Greeks nor among wild peoples. It is a terrible thing for the narrator and unbelievable for the listeners. Nor would I have told this event to subsequent generations of people, fearing that they would consider me a liar, and I would gladly have passed over this terrible story if there had not been countless witnesses and eyewitnesses. Besides, I would render a dubious service to my fatherland if I concealed the sufferings it endured.

On the Woman Who Ate Her Child

There was one woman, from the other side of the Jordan, named Maria, daughter of Eleazar, from the village of Bethezop, which means “house of hyssop.” By birth and wealth she was among the best people. She fled with others to Jerusalem. But her property, which she had set aside and brought from beyond the Jordan, the tormentors plundered and left her nothing at all, no precious things. And all the food she obtained, the robbers took from her, coming every day. A wild ferocity entered this woman, and she cried out for pity, and cursed the robbers, and incited them to have someone kill her. Then she saw that no one undertook to kill her, neither out of anger nor out of pity. And she labored and went to other people to find something edible, and could find nothing anywhere. Hunger penetrated all her innards and her brains, and this woman took need as her counselor and set upon her own flesh. She had an infant child, and the mother said to her child: “Sweet little one! War, famine, and rebellion have surrounded us. For whom am I keeping you? If the Romans seize us, we shall fall into heavy and unbearable slavery. If the Romans do not succeed, we shall fall to hunger. The rebels are even more terrible than the Romans and than hunger. Go, child, back to where you came from, and be food for me, a curse to the rebels, and a testimony to posterity of how far the life of the Jews has come.” And with these words the woman stabbed her son, and baked him, and divided him into two halves: with one half she satisfied her belly, and the other she covered and left.
