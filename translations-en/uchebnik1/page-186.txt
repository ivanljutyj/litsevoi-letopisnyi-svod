The stone flew; they hid, and the stone passed harmlessly by. But the Romans, learning of this, began to paint the stones black; they launched them and killed many with a single throw.

And the carpenters finished the mound, and brought ropes with pieces of lead tied to their ends, and threw them against the walls to measure whether the battering engines could be set in place. They could not measure otherwise, because they were being struck at from the walls. And so the men discovered that the rams would now reach the walls, and they set them up. And Titus ordered the walls to be struck in three places. Suddenly a clamor seized the city, for those inside were shouting with all their might. And an inexpressible fear fell upon the Jewish fighters; they saw that a common calamity had come upon all, and they decided to take vengeance together, and cried to one another: “O brothers, O kinsmen! We are circumcised with one circumcision. Why do we do what so pleases our enemies? Why do we not set aside enmity? If at another time God does not grant us unanimity, now it is fitting for us to set aside our quarrels and stand against the Romans.” And immediately Simon Niger promised peace and freedom of action to all who wished to leave the temple and stand on the walls.

John, that is, Yochanan, though he did not much trust Simon’s promises, ordered his men to go out, and the rebels, forgetting the hatred and enmity that had been between them, became one body. The united rebels stood on the walls, threw fire at the rams and at the missile engines, and killed soldiers. Many brave men leaped forward, hacked apart the shelters, and attacked those hiding beneath them.

Titus helped his men by riding to the places where the fighting was heaviest. He placed horsemen and archers between the rams, and these prevented the Jews from throwing fire and shot at the fighters on the walls. The rams struck the walls but did not damage them. Only the fifteenth legion broke off a corner of the tower; the wall remained intact.

The Jews ceased making sorties. Roman soldiers saw this and dispersed: some to build fortifications, others to rest, for they thought the enemies were tired and frightened. But the Jews came out of the Horse Tower through secret gates, carried fire to the battering engines, and with a shout attacked the nearby Roman soldiers standing close and the distant ones running up. Jewish audacity outstripped Roman order. The Jews cut down the Romans who came their way and broke up those gathering again. A fierce battle raged around the engines: some sought to set them on fire, others prevented it. Both sides raised an incredible cry, sparks flew from clashing swords, they seized one another by the hands, and so they fought. Many defenders of the engines fell.
