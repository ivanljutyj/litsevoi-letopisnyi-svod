The patriarch baptized his wife and the youth, and made the wife a singer in the church, and the youth a singer and reader.

Justin the New was good and righteous; he hated injustice and evil disposition, loved good, was bright-souled, comely, did not care for wealth, hated idle talk, was meek, hated guile, and loved justice greatly. But his painful life cut off his will to do justice, and that Justin remained on his bed in his chambers as an invalid. Justin was like a noble tree, abundant with beautiful fruits and ready to bring rich nourishment to all, but from illness, as from winter, frozen. This was an obstacle that did not allow Justin’s benevolence toward all to be seen and put into action; that is, it did not allow Justin to judge and govern all the people.

From this injustice greatly multiplied, along with plunder, oppression, theft, and robbery; from this there were tears and wailing, outcry and groaning in the city and in the villages, and many murders. The autocrat heard this and was wounded in soul, and could do nothing because of the illness that had seized him. As an unfortunate beast-mother, seeing her children cruelly carried off, can help them in no other way than by uttering a terrible cry from the depths of her heart, so this king could bring no benefit to his oppressed subjects, and for this he grieved greatly and sighed.

On the Righteous Judge. And so one day someone from the rulers came on a Wednesday and boldly exclaimed: “O king, if you wish to deliver your people from the hands of such evil, entrust to me the sword of vengeance; and if I cut off the head of extortion and root out the root of injustice, praise is due to me from you and from others; if not, then cut off my head.” Hearing this, the king greatly loved his words, and appointed him mighty eparch, and entrusted to him the sword of vengeance, and commanded him to care for justice. And this ruler applied himself well to such matters.

It happened soon afterward. A poor widow came before the judge and cried out against a certain noble kinsman of the king, of the rank of magister; she cried out against his unjust deeds, that he had seized all her property and substance. The righteous judge listened to her mercifully and sent to summon the magister to court; but he despised this summons and mocked the judge, and went off to the king’s supper.

The judge became angry and went to the royal chambers, and saw the rich feasting, and saw there that man who had wronged the widow, and boldly said to King Justin: “If you have not yet repented that you gave me eparchal power to avenge the wronged, then here he sits before you, whom it is fitting to seize.
