Arsenius prayed to God unceasingly, and a Voice came to him saying: “Arsenius, flee from men and you will be saved.” Hearing this Voice, Arsenius told no one anything, but clothed himself in wretched garments, went down to the harbor, and by God’s providence found a ship, entered it, and arrived in Alexandria.

And Arsenius was tonsured a monk, and went into the desert of Scetis, and began to live there, and there he remained in various labors.

And Arsenius came to John Kolobos. The elder wished to test him, and when he was eating with another brother, he left Arsenius standing and threw him a piece of bread. Arsenius came on all fours, took the bread, and ate it like a dog; and when he was asked about this, he said: “The elder understood that I am a dog, and therefore threw me a piece as to a dog, and I, like a dog, took it and ate it.” Seeing this, the elder said: “This one wishes to be a perfect monk.”

And when he was in Scetis, Arsenius prayed again: “Lord, guide me, how shall I be saved?” And he heard a Voice saying to him: “Arsenius, flee, be silent, keep silence, for this is the root of sinlessness.” The blessed one understood that he must flee not only from the world but also from monks and remain in silence, and from that hour he allowed no one to converse with him.

Tsar Theodosius was greatly grieved at Arsenius’s departure, and wrote an edict everywhere, here and there, to seek him, and he could not find him even until his death.

When Arcadius and Honorius reigned, it was told them that Arsenius was in Egypt, living the monastic life and abiding in virtue. And the emperors wrote many letters to him, entreating him to pray for their children and disciples.

Arcadius also wrote, entreating Arsenius to forgive his transgressions, by which Arcadius had sinned against Arsenius in plotting to kill him. Arcadius wished to be assured that Arsenius would forgive him, and wrote Arsenius a letter in which he swore to take from the governor of Alexandria, named Augustalis, the Egyptian taxes and distribute them to whomever Arsenius commanded, if only he would forgive Arcadius and pray for his kingdom and for that of Honorius, his brother, and would write him even one word in reply.

But our father among the saints, Arsenius, did not wish to write to the emperors, and sent them this word: “God, my children, forgive you all and grant you to do His divine will. As for what you wrote to me about gold, I have died to the world, so let no one consider me to be still in this life.”
