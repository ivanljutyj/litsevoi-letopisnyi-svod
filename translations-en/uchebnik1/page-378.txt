Concerning the Maiden Who Killed Attila

In the reign of Theodosius and Valentinian, Attila turned against Rome and Constantinople; he was of the Hunnic tribe and had a multitude of warriors, tens of thousands.

And Attila sent a Gothic envoy to Emperor Valentinian in Rome with these words: “My lord and your lord Attila commands that you prepare for him your royal chambers.”

Also to Emperor Theodosius in Constantinople Attila sent one of the Goths as envoy. When the first counselor of Valentinian in Rome heard Attila’s words, full of immeasurable insolence, he feared to answer Attila, and went to Alaric in Gaul, who was surrounded by soldiers because of Honorius, and the Roman counselor persuaded Alaric and brought him against Attila, since he had destroyed the Roman cities. And immediately Alaric suddenly attacked Attila, who was encamped near the Danube, and cut down many thousands of his warriors. In the clash Alaric also received a wound from an arrow and died. As the writings relate elsewhere, that King Attila came from the Northern lands with a vast army and occupied the Roman cities.

And Attila besieged the city of Rome with a multitude of thousands of warriors, many times desiring to take the city of Rome. And there was then a princess, a beautiful maiden, who lived in an annex to a tower; she was thirty years old. Then King Attila, having learned of her, that she was very beautiful, asked for her for himself and said: “If you do not give her to me, I will destroy your city of Rome.”

Then all the elders and governors of Rome went to the maiden and implored her, saying: “Go out quickly to King Attila, so that he does not cruelly destroy our city of Rome, and so that our city does not perish.”

And the maiden, since they had entreated her, went to Attila. When she sat at his feet and he lay reclining, blood flowed from his nose, and from that he died. And the maiden took Attila’s cloak and went and told the townspeople that King Attila was dead.

And Attila’s warriors took his body and carried it through the lands of the Germans and Vlachs and laid it in the land of the Hungarians. And about that maiden they thought that she had killed Attila. The wise Priscus the Thracian wrote the account of that maiden. Others said that Aetius, a Roman nobleman who held the rank of patrician, persuaded Attila’s swordsman, and he stabbed him, and Aetius returned to Rome having overcome Attila.
