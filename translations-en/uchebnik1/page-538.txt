The saints bore branding on their faces as women wear a pearl crown shining with precious stones. On the way into exile they met a certain fisherman who was bringing food to Patriarch Methodius.

Then they wrote to the great Methodius, who was in prison, a brief note in Amvrian verse: “The living dead man and the life-bearing dead man, who lives on earth and walks in heaven, are greeted by the inscribed ones—captives of the captive.”

He wrote back to them: “To those inscribed by name in the heavenly books, the chaste, who bear marked faces, the one buried alive sends greeting—the holy orators, persecuted like him for the Truth.” And these golden words were like life-giving streams issuing from a Divine source.

Saint Theodore departed to the Lord in exile, and at the hour of his death the singing of angels was heard, and his voice sounded together with them. But the good ascetic, the great Methodius, imprisoned in a dark, terrifying cave, endured the crushing of his teeth, the shattering of his jaws, and all the other tortures with which the impious inhumanely tormented him, and he was like solid iron on an anvil under striking hammers.

King Theophilus, being excessively cruel and inventive in every evil against the veneration of icons and its supporters, was at the same time very wise and magnanimous in matters of state governance, and he cared for the adoption of just laws and their execution in the courts. Proof of the greatness of his reign was also the renewal of the walls of the reigning city, which had been devastated by the Hagarenes and had also suffered from severe frost in the year when the sea froze, from the heat that followed the cold, and from the constantly blowing hot winds. Just as great mountains crumble, the walls of the city began to crumble. He not only renewed them but gave them an even more majestic appearance, exhausting numerous treasures on this.

He created a wondrous organ, elaborately adorned with gold-forged trees, with birds sitting on their branches, made so skillfully that in the sounds of the wondrous breath of music one seemed to hear the sweet-voiced singing of songbirds perched on poplar branches or on pointed cypresses. Thus the wondrous creations of the hands of gifted people seem to come alive.
