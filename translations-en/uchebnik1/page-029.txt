He was 70 years old, of simple station and name, but a skilled carpenter, living in the Galilean city of Nazareth.

Mary was in Jerusalem in the Lord’s temple. She was 14 years old, when the weak nature of woman also makes itself manifest, and the priests thought Mary was one of many—for the mysteries concerning her had not yet been revealed—and they gathered in council and set themselves to prayer for her. Zechariah, the father of John the Baptist, received 12 rods from the priests and from the Maiden’s relatives, and placed them at the altar, and said: Let God show whose the Maiden is. And Zechariah prayed, and the rod of Joseph the carpenter sprouted. And then, as God judged, they entrusted the Maiden Mary to Joseph, not for marriage, but for the preservation and keeping of her immaculate virginity. Thus by this is explained the word of the Holy Virgin spoken to the archangel Gabriel. He said to her after the greeting: You will conceive a Son and call His name Jesus, and the Lord God will give Him the throne of David your father, and the rest. And the Virgin answered him: How shall this be to me, since I know not a man? For Joseph had been lawfully joined in marriage, having been called to this. The Virgin did not answer thus: From the husband betrothed to me I shall conceive all that is lawful. But we see clearly that Joseph took the Maiden not for marriage, but for preservation and keeping. The spoken word is full of all truth: How shall this be to me, since I know not a man? That is: she was not given to a man for marital union, but for the preservation of virginity in purity and immaculateness. And not for marriage did Joseph receive his grandniece Mary from the hand of the Lord. All the priests bear witness to this. Joseph brought Mary into his house and handed over to her two of his daughters, so that she might make them wise and instruct them, since Mary herself lived in Joseph’s house with all chastity and purity. And Mary observed in his house for six months the fast appointed by custom.

On the Annunciation

In the year 5499, and in the 36th year of the reign of Augustus, on the 25th day of the month Dystrus, that is March, on the day of the week (later this day of the week came to be called Sunday), at the ninth hour of the day, the Most Holy Mother of God was praying. And at that hour the archangel Gabriel was sent from God to the city of Nazareth to the Most Holy Mother of God, and he told her all the mysteries concerning the only-begotten Son of God, as is set forth in the Gospel. And none of Joseph’s household understood what had happened, and the Mother of God did not announce it to anyone, not even to Joseph himself, until she saw her Son ascending into Heaven. Therefore the evangelist Matthew says: And they did not understand her until she bore her firstborn Son; that is, they did not understand the mystery of God in her, nor the hidden depth concerning her, nor what had been accomplished.
