And the Bulgarian prince Simeon sent envoys to Alexander concerning peace, saying that he loved peace and greeted him, and honored him as in the time of Emperor Leo.

But Alexander, possessed by folly and unreason, dismissed the envoys without honor, sending threats to Simeon, thinking to frighten him.

Since the peace was broken, Simeon decided to raise arms against the Christians.

Alexander, having become drunk at dinner, went down to the fortress where the puppies were whelped to take a puppy.

But he was wounded by a weapon sent by God; because much blood flowed from his nose and mouth, he died two days later, in the month of June, on the sixth day, a Sunday, in the first indiction, leaving as guardians Nicholas the Patriarch, and John the magistros, surnamed Hellas, and John the rector, and Euthymius, and Vasilitzes, and Gavrilopulos, having handed the kingdom over to Constantine, son of Leo.

And they placed Alexander in the imperial tomb together with his father Basil.

The 48th reign, that of Constantine Porphyrogenitus, son of Leo, in the year 6400 from Adam (the 900th year from Christ)

After Alexander, Constantine Porphyrogenitus, son of Leo, reigned. When Leo died, Constantine, a boy of seven years, was left in the reign by Alexander, his uncle.

Thus he reigned under these guardians and with his mother Zoe, Leo's fourth wife, for 7 years; together with Romanus, his father-in-law, for 27 years; as autocrat for 15 years; and the total years of his reign were 54.

So, having taken authority in the palace, Patriarch Nicholas, since he himself was also a guardian together with the magistros Stephen and John Hellas the magistros, exercised general oversight and care for the daily needs of the kingdom.

During their rule it was reported to Constantine Doukas, then Domestic of the Schools, by certain nobles of the city of Constantine, who loved him as brave and wise and capable of governing the kingdom, that he should enter and take power without difficulty.

As some say, Patriarch Nicholas, knowing that by Alexander's command the power had been entrusted to him, took a false letter and sent Artabasdos, to whom the priesthood was fitting, to Constantine Doukas, commanding that as soon as he entered the kingdom he should immediately go in to the Empress. For delivering the letter he was appointed first priest of the glorious Sophia.
