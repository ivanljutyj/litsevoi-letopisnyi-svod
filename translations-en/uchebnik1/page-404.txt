In Antioch, moreover, a grievous disease fell upon the people. And a certain man saw in a dream and said that those whom Anastasius had forced to curse the Fourth Council were suffering because of the curse of the Fourth Council. From this it is clear that God's judgment not only does not assent to the curses of the wicked, but also brings torment upon those who caused this.

Concerning the Turks and Their Leader Alamundar

When the Turks had ravaged the eastern land, Anastasius made peace with them.

In the reign of this Anastasius, his general Vitalian revolted against him on the pretext that Anastasius had deposed the Orthodox bishops; and Vitalian came, ravaging all Thrace and Europe.

And so Vitalian reached Sycae, a suburb of Constantine's city, and the harbor, and planned to take Constantine's city. And he encamped at the harbor in Sosthenion, a suburb of Tsargrad, by the church of the Archangel Michael.

The emperor said to Proclus the Athenian, "What shall I do with this dog?" And he ordered Proclus the philosopher to bring hot stone and crush it fine.

And Proclus ordered that, when the sun rose, this crushed hot stone be poured onto the enemy ships, and it burned up all the ships, and the enemies fled, and Christ the Savior and the emperor's ships prevailed. For hot stone, from the heat of the sun, if crushed fine and scattered into the air, ignites, and by this one can set fire either to a ship or to a house.

In Anastasius's reign, the Turkish leader Alamundar believed in Christ and was baptized.

Then Severus the heretic, according to his former custom, sent two bishops who remained in his heresy to Alamundar, to persuade him to fall away from the faith. And these bishops came and taught this Alamundar, saying: "The Godhead, having been mixed with the flesh, became one nature, and since the flesh suffered, the Godhead also suffered."

But Alamundar, wishing to show them that they were lying, arranged that one of his men came and spoke secretly into Alamundar's ear in the presence of those bishops. And having heard those secret words, Alamundar showed himself to be grieved.

Those heretic bishops asked Alamundar: "Why are you grieved? And what did this man say to you?" Alamundar answered and said: "This man tells me that Michael the Archangel has died." And they said to him: "What you hear is a lie, for it is impossible that Michael the Archangel should die."
