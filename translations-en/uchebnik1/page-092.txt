But Archelaus did not delay to show that he was not of another stock but of Herod’s stock, and at once marked his accession with murder. A thousand citizens—such was the sacrifice he offered to God, defiling the temple and the festival with corpses. We who survived had to endure his malice and seek justice. And now we ask you, Caesar: have mercy on the remnant of the Jewish people, do not give us over to cruel rulers, but unite our land with the land of Syria, and appoint your own rulers to govern us, and we will show ourselves peaceful and obedient, not opponents and rebels as we are now represented.” With this request the Jews ended their speech. Then Nicolaus rose and in his speech cleared the king of the accusation and laid all the blame on the Jews, and said: “This people is disobedient; from ancient times it has resisted authority and does not wish to submit to either mild or harsh rulers; so it has continued from Moses to this day.” Caesar heard both sides and dismissed everyone.

After several days Caesar gave Archelaus half the kingdom. The other half he divided into two quarters and gave one to Philip and the other to Antipas. Antipas received rule over Perea and Galilee, from which 200 talents of tribute were collected. To Philip Caesar gave Batanea, Trachon, Auran, and part of the villages of Zenon, from all of which 100 talents of tribute were collected.

To Archelaus Caesar gave all Judaea, Idumaea, and Samaria, from which 400 talents of tribute were collected. But the Greek cities—Gaza, Gadara, and Hippos—Caesar took from Archelaus and attached to the province of Syria. And to Salome Caesar gave Jamnia, Azotus, Phasaelis, and the palace in Ascalon, from all of which 60 talents of tribute were collected.

The remaining relatives of Herod each received what Herod had bequeathed to them. To the king’s two unmarried daughters Caesar gave 500,000 coins of his own silver. Then Caesar divided among Herod’s heirs those 1,000 talents of gold which the king had handed over to him before his death. Of the valuable things Caesar kept a small portion for himself, to honor the deceased.

At that time a certain young man, a Jew by birth, brought up in Sidon, resembling Alexander, the son of Herod, began to lie and say that he was Alexander. He had someone among his accomplices who knew the state of affairs at that time. And people asked this young man about Aristobulus, and he said that Aristobulus too was alive, since they had been stolen away before the execution out of pity, and others had been killed in their place. And many of the Jews living in Crete believed this lie.
