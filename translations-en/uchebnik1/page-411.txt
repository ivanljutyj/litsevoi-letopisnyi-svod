It was then the feast of the Ascension of our Savior and God, and a great multitude of people gathered into the city from outside, according to custom holding a solemn assembly in the church called Crateon. That earthquake itself revealed both the multitude of citizens and the great property and wealth they had, and the city’s grandeur in length and great breadth. Concerning the beauty of the city and the loveliness of the houses, strangers who had seen the city before and came afterward said: “This great retreat from the world and refuge of the universe has become desolate. Where is the beauty of those creations?” Many of those buried were pulled out alive and died; others of the surviving citizens took from their property what they could and fled, carrying what was necessary for their remaining life.

And robbers met the citizens, who went about plundering strangers, and took from them what they were carrying. They went about robbing and plundering, so that they killed many, for people resisted giving them their own, since they had nothing else to hope in except that property, that they might preserve their lives. And then the robbers took from them what they carried when they killed them. Strangers also entered the city and plundered what was buried, and found silver caskets, and silver, and scattered gold coins lying about, and many women adorned with much gold, precious stones, and pearls. But the power of God was shown upon these robbers and strangers: as many of those as had taken anything died at once, all of them, and not one of them remained alive—some rotted, some were crushed in body; by the works of their hands they showed their sins and gave up their souls.

Among them a certain Thomas, a silentiary by rank, called Erveov, plundered much with his slaves; during the earthquake he had gone out whole from that city two or three miles away at the gate of Saint Julian, and lived there, and took everything from passersby and fugitives without fear of God; also with the robbers who plundered what was buried he took much gold. This he did for four days and took much gold, silver, and other property. Since he was destroying everything, suddenly, while healthy and in strength, he fell apart, and without writing down the number of all that he had plundered, he thus unexpectedly gave up the spirit. And all who heard of this glorified God, the righteous Judge. All his property was scattered, plundered by his elders, and perished, so that nothing remained to him except the robe he wore, in which he was buried on that spot, out of fear of the citizens who spoke against him.

Certain mysteries of God were also revealed. After twenty-one days, women who had given birth came out from under the rubble healthy. Many gave birth while under the dust on the earth, and came out with infants born a few days later without harm, and were alive with those born of them.
