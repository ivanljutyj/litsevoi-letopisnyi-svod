On the Curtain, and on Jesus, and on the Guard

The curtain had been whole until this generation, since the people were pious. But now it was pitiful to look at, for it was torn suddenly from top to bottom, when the virtuous Man, and by his deeds God, was handed over to death for payment. They tell of many other terrible signs that occurred then. The Slain Man was not found in the tomb after burial. Some said He rose again, others that His friends stole Him. I do not know which of them speaks the truth. For a dead man cannot rise by himself, but only with help and through the prayer of a righteous man, or if he is an angel, or someone else from the Heavenly Powers, or finally if God Himself appears as a man and does as He wills: and He will walk with people, and fall, and lie down, and rise, as pleases Him. Some said He could not have been stolen, since around His tomb they placed guards, a thousand Romans and a thousand Jews. This is what they tell about this curtain, and this is the reason it was torn. Those who passed inside came into the temple chamber, whose height was 60 cubits, and its length the same, and its width 20 cubits. This chamber of sixty cubits was divided in two. The first part was 40 cubits. In it were 3 wonderful objects known to all people: the lampstand, the table, and the censer. On the lampstand were 7 candles, according to the number of the seven heavenly bodies called planets; and on the table were 12 loaves, according to the number of the heavenly circles that make up the year. On the censer were 13 incenses, which were gathered from all seas, and from all the universe, and from all deserts, and these incenses signified that all is from God and to God. The inner place, 20 cubits, was invisible because of another curtain, and nothing stood or lay in it, and no one entered there or defiled it, and it was closed from the eyes of all. This place was called the Holy of Holies. And near the lower temple, on the sides, many three-tiered buildings stood closely packed. On the upper side the buildings were not like these, since it was cramped there. To look at the temple from the front was dreadful both to eyes and to souls, for it was everywhere overlaid with thick golden leaves, and the sun’s radiance was reflected, and all of it had a fiery appearance. Just as one cannot look at the bright sun, so anyone who wished to look at the temple covered his eyes. To strangers who looked at the temple, it appeared like a mountain covered with snow, for in those places where it was not overlaid with gold, the temple was very white. On top of the temple were set golden nails, both large and long and numerous, and sharp as arrows, so that no bird would perch and defile the summit of the temple.
