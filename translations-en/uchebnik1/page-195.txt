Come to your senses and realize your guilt. O, what iron hearts you have! Throw down your weapons, have mercy on our fatherland. Look around you, see what beauty, what a city, what a temple, what gifts from all nations! All this you now consign to the flames in your stubbornness and stony hardness. Spare your families. Set before your eyes your children, wives, and parents, for whom destruction is very near, either by hunger or by the sword. I know that my glory will perish, and my noble family, and my mother, and my wife, and my house. If you think that I am entreating you for the sake of all this, then kill them. Let my blood be shed for your salvation. If you have regard for me, I too will gladly die for you.” So Joseph cried out to the rebels, but they did not yield and firmly resolved that they would not surrender.

But the people inclined to the side of the Romans, and people sold their property and precious things for gold. And they swallowed the gold so that the rebels would not see it, and so fled to the Romans. There they voided the gold through the rear passage and used it for what was necessary.

Titus sent the refugees out among the lands, wherever each wanted. John, that is Johanan, and Simon Niger then stopped allowing anyone to leave the city, but killed all the refugees.

After that the famine increased every day. There was no wheat for sale, and soldiers began to go about searching for it in the houses. If they found it, they killed the owners because they had hidden it before; if they did not find it, they killed the owners all the same, claiming that they were hiding wheat now. And many shut themselves in inner rooms and did not grind the wheat but ate it as it was. Their food was pitiful and worthy of tears. Famine was worse than all their misfortunes. Wives snatched food from their husbands, children from their fathers, and mothers from their infants; they squeezed it from the throats of their loved ones and tore it from their hands. No one gave even a drop of life out of mercy. But even living thus, people could not remain hidden. Fighters walked everywhere, and if they saw a closed house, they understood that food was being taken inside, and at once broke down the doors and rushed in and squeezed the food from their throats, and beat the old men who would not let go of the food, and the women hiding bread, and took it from the children and smashed the children themselves against a stone. And they tortured those who hid food with unheard-of tortures: they pushed peas into their secret member and a sharp stake into the anus—only to make them say where a handful of flour or a piece of bread was hidden. The torturers did not go hungry but satisfied themselves with all that they obtained in this way.

Some went outside the walls and gathered wild herbs, hiding from the Roman soldiers. If the rebels met them on the way back, they took from them what they had gathered. They begged them and invoked the terrible name of God, that they might give them at least part of the herbs, since they had labored and brought them at the risk of their lives. But the rebels did not give them anything.
