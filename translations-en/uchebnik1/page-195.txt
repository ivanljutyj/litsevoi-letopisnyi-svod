Who then brought them against our people? Or why did we fall into slavery? Was it not because of the quarrels of Aristobulus and Hyrcanus, which brought Pompey against the city? And God subjected to the Romans those who were unworthy of freedom. And after three months, unable to withstand the siege of Jerusalem, they surrendered, although they had not sinned against the sanctuary as you have, nor violated the law. So God did not grant our people to conquer by arms.

It is fitting, then, for those who live by the holy place to lay judgment and vengeance for all their grievances upon the Supreme Judge. But you have rejected what the one who gave us the law blessed, and you do what he cursed. Theft, adultery, robbery, and bloodshed you do not regard as sin, but you fall into other, unheard-of sins. And you have made the temple a repository of all evil, and with your own hands you have defiled the divine place, which the Romans revered from afar, transgressing much of their own custom and inclining toward our law. Doing such things, how do you hope to have as your helper the One against whom you commit lawlessness? You are not righteous at all, nor are you men of prayer, and not with clean hands do you summon God as your helper. Yet it was with clean hands that your king called upon God for help against the Assyrians, and then the great Assyrian army fell in one night. Indeed, the Assyrians deserved punishment, so that the king could hope for God’s help. But the deeds of the Romans are not like what the Assyrians did. Our king paid tribute to the Assyrians so that they would not ravage the temple, but they broke their oath and came to ravage the city and set the temple on fire. The Romans ask only for the customary tribute, which our fathers and grandfathers already paid to their fathers and grandfathers. If you pay this tribute, they will not destroy the city or touch the holy place, but will give your families freedom, and you will possess your wealth and preserve the sacred law in purity. But if you think that God will do to the righteous the same as He did to the unrighteous, then you have lost your senses. The Lord took vengeance on the Assyrians in the very first night. If He had deemed our generation worthy of freedom, He would not have allowed Vespasian to conquer Galilee, nor Titus all the lands and, finally, this city. Do you not know that the war with us led Vespasian to become emperor, and that for Titus well-springs opened which had dried up for us before? You know that before his arrival Siloam and the other springs before the city dried up, and we bought water by measure. But now these waters flow so for our enemies that they suffice not only for them and their livestock, but gardens and fields can be watered with this water. Such signs had appeared before also, and they foretold the conquest of the city when Nebuchadnezzar came and burned the city and the temple. But I think that God has departed from the holy place.
