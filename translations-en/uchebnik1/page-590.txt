He was there... And when Leo Phokas came to the village called Goliontovo, he was seized.

Romanos sent Ivan Tuvakios and Leontes, his kinsman, to bring him to Constantinople.

Having seized him, they put out his pupils and the lights of his eyes, blinding him and darkening him. And no command concerning execution had been given them; they did this of their own will, at which the emperor’s father was indignant.

In the month of August it became known about the conspiracy of Constantine Ktimatines, David Kamoulianos, and Michael, the overseer of the Mangana palace. They were beaten, publicly disgraced, and sent through the whole city into exile.

They brought Leontes, magistros and domestikos, to Constantinople; he proceeded through a gathering of the people, sitting on a female mule.

Empress Zoe, who had planned to prepare food with poison for Romanos, was seized by Theoklitos the notary, which was part of his official duties. She was led out of the palace, brought into the monastery of Saint Euphemia, and tonsured.

Theodore, attendant of Emperor Constantine, and Simeon, his brother, were invited to dinner by Theophylact, patrician and comes.

When they were eating, Ivan, the drungary of the guard called Kourkouas, entered with many people and, seizing them, sent them into exile in Opsikion, to their villages, as those plotting against Romanos.
