I say to you plainly that the holy angels can separate us from our bodies in peace, and we can pass without grief and without hindrance through the principalities and powers of the air, if we have good guides, and if the demons do not find in us what they seek: they will see an undefiled body and be put to shame; they will see an incorrupt, pure, and radiant soul and be ashamed; they will see a tongue without blemish and guiltless and fall silent. And we shall pass by them and overcome them, saying: “Blessed is the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth. The snare is broken, and we are delivered.” Then, as they meet us, the angels would rejoice, the righteous would rejoice and kiss us, the venerable would be glad, saying: “Well have you come, friends of Christ, and heirs of eternal blessings.”

A Word of Saint Gregory the Theologian on the Departed. And Gregory, the namesake of theology, at the burial of his brother Caesarius, also said: “I submit to the wise words that every good and God-loving soul, when it is released from the body bound to it and exchanges this world for eternity, immediately receives the perception and vision of the good that will be with it. And even while that which darkens it is being destroyed or laid aside—or I do not know how it is fitting to speak of this—then already this good and God-loving soul enjoys a certain wondrous sweetness, and rejoices, and with gladness awaits entry to its Master, for it has fled as from a certain cruel prison from this life and has cast off the bonds that lay about it, from which the wing of thought is drawn forth, so that the good soul, immediately upon its departure from the body, receives in imagination the sweetness of the blessedness that surrounds it.”

A Word of Saint Athanasius on the Departed. Similarly Athanasius, in the Life of Saint Anthony, says: “At one time the God-bearing Anthony stood at prayer at the ninth hour and perceived that he was caught up in mind, and, without ceasing to stand, he saw himself as if he were beside himself, and certain beings led him through the air. And certain fierce and evil beings standing in the air wanted to prevent Anthony from passing by them. But those who were leading Anthony opposed them and struggled on his behalf. And those fierce and evil ones demanded an inquiry into whether Anthony was guilty of anything, and wanted to conduct the inquiry from Anthony’s very birth. But those who were leading Anthony forbade them, saying: ‘What was from his birth, the Lord has set aside. Only from the time when Anthony became a monk and made his vow to God is it possible to make inquiry.’ And then they made this inquiry into Anthony’s life and could not convict him, and the way became free and unobstructed for him.
