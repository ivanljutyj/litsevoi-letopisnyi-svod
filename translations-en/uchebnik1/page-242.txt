The soldiers, however, felt a special affection for Vespasian, for they more than all others knew the greatness of his deeds and the wisdom of his rule. All remembered the cowardice and ignorance of the former emperors, and so they awaited Vespasian’s accession, that he might deliver them from their former shame, save them, and adorn them with glorious victories. And all were filled with such love for Vespasian that the nobles and the most honored officials could not wait for his arrival in the city and went far out from Rome to meet Vespasian. And all the rest of the people, looking to the elders, went out together from the city to meet Vespasian; no one delayed, and one could then feel that few people remained in Rome, for those left in it were fewer than those who went out to meet the emperor. Heralds walked before Vespasian and announced his coming. People saw his gentleness, and all spoke of the beauty of his countenance. Then all the people came out with their wives and children and stood on the walls, on the towers, and on other buildings, wherever each could, to see the emperor. And all past whom he rode saw with joy his countenance, the beauty of his face, and his humility. People addressed various cries to him, thanked him, called out, glorified him, and exclaimed: “You are our benefactor! You are our savior! You are our deliverer! You alone are worthy to reign in Rome, and may your power be long-lasting, and that of your children and your grandchildren.” Others too, and yet others, and all who followed, exclaimed as their turn came. The whole city was like a temple, full of wreaths and lamps, of sweet spices and incense. Because of the multitude of people surrounding him, Vespasian barely made his way to the palace.

Entering the palace, Vespasian offered sacrifice to the household gods and thanked them for his safe and glorious return. The people began to celebrate and rejoice. People gathered, offered sacrifices to the gods, and prayed that Vespasian’s power might last for many years and his dominion remain invincible. Thus the city of Rome received Vespasian with joy, and then abundance and wealth began to grow in Rome.

In the year before this, while Vespasian was still in Alexandria and Titus was besieging Jerusalem, people in many parts of Germany rose up against Rome, and the Gauls nearest to them were of one mind with them. And they then conceived great hope of freeing themselves from Roman rule. What moved the Germans to revolt was their innate recklessness, as well as a faint and unsteady hope. For they saw that Roman power was tottering from the frequent change of emperors, and they learned that the whole world under the Romans was shaken; and they conspired, deciding that a convenient time had come for vengeance on the Romans.
