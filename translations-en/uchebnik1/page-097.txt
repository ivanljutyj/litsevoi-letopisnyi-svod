If later a brother falls into some sin, he is expelled from the community, and the expelled man dies a cruel death, since he is bound by oath and will not dare accept food from anyone, and he eats grass, and withers, and dies of hunger. Therefore some who have sinned are mercifully received back when they are already near death.

The Essenes judge thoroughly and justly. If they wish to judge, they choose 100 brothers, whose verdict cannot be changed. They honor the name of him who gave the law; if anyone blasphemes it, he is condemned to death. The Essenes obey their elders. If ten of them sit together, one may not speak unless the other nine wish it. They do not spit in their midst, nor spit to the right. The seventh day, the seventh week, the seventh month, and the seventh year they strictly observe. On the Sabbath they do not prepare food, do not kindle fire, do not move vessels, and do not relieve themselves.

On other days they dig pits a cubit deep, relieve themselves in them, and cover them again; for this they have an axe — to dig and fill in the pits — so as not to defile the sun or a divine ray; after this they wash. The Essenes are divided, according to the time of their entry into the brotherhood, into four parts; if the older touch the younger, they wash afterward, as if they had touched a foreigner. They live more than a hundred years because of simple food. They do not remember misfortunes or anything bad. They believe that a glorious death is better than life.

The strength of their souls was shown in the war with the Romans, when they were tortured, beaten, burned, and subjected to every kind of torment to force the Essenes to blaspheme him who gave them the law, or to eat what custom did not permit. But they remained unconquered and unshaken; and they did not beg their killers, did not weep, but only mocked their tormentors, and reviled them, and gladly gave up their souls. For they have such a teaching: that the body arises from corruption, and after death it is divided into four parts, of which it was composed. But the soul is immortal, and is joined to the body, and enters the body from the ether as into a prison, and as if enchanted is compelled to human nature. And when the soul is released from the bonds of the flesh, it is freed as from long slavery, rejoices, and goes up on high. And if it was a good soul, it is carried to a paradise field beyond the ocean, where there is neither rain, nor snow, nor heat of the sun, but a calm spirit from the ocean, and there a fragrant warm breath wafts over the soul of the righteous.

But the soul of the wicked is led to a dark and cold place, full of endless torment. I think the Greeks expressed the same idea when they wrote that demigods lead their brave men to the islands of the blessed, while the wicked fall into hell.
