And he gave orders, and a great fire was kindled. And lifting his hands to heaven, the archbishop said: “Christ God, may Your name be glorified!” And he placed the holy Gospel on the fire. It remained in the fire a long time, and the fire did not touch it. Seeing this, the Rus marveled and were astonished at the power of Christ. And all were baptized.

The aforementioned Nikephoros, son of Phokas, was appointed commander in the western lands and performed many valiant and brave deeds.

Prokopios, the emperor’s protovestiarios, having come to Sicily with all the strategoi of the western lands, did what he could.

The rest he entrusted to the strategos of the Peloponnese named Apostup, who killed Prokopios.

And afterward John, Domestic of the Hikanatoi, surnamed Koias (Kourkouas), having gathered up to sixty notables with him, devised the following conspiracy against the emperor.

Afterward the protovestiarios John reported this conspiracy to the emperor; and sending for them, he seized them all, and bringing them to the Hippodrome, torturing and scourging them, he had them all tonsured and burned the rest.

But on the Annunciation, while he was piously making the entry, at which they had intended to carry out their plan, he himself went in front, while they were led behind him, naked and bound, all the way to the marketplace, through the whole vast city, and he expelled them.

Once the emperor was hunting and pursuing a huge stag, when suddenly the stag sprang out, caught the emperor on its horns by his belt, dragged him from his horse, and rushed off carrying him on its horns.

And one of those accompanying him approached him, drew his sword, cut the belt, and freed the emperor. But the emperor turned to the one who had cut the belt and ordered his head cut off, as one who had dared to draw a sword against him. Although the soldier explained at length that he had done it “for your sake,” nothing helped him.

From the wound received from the stag, Basil fell ill and soon died, leaving Leo, his son, as autocrat, and Alexander, his brother. Basil had a flourishing appearance, was strong, thick-browed, with kind eyes, calm, restrained, dark-haired, of medium height, and broad-chested.

The aforementioned Nikephoros, son of Phokas, besieged Amantia and was already about to take it when he received a letter from Emperor Leo announcing Basil’s death and ordering him to return.

Nikephoros, having learned of the emperor’s death, concealed from the Saracens that he was withdrawing, persuading them to surrender the city to him and promising that he would grant them freedom to go wherever they wished.
