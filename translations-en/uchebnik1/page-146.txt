And every family was divided in two: foolish young men who wanted war joined with others like themselves, while the old and sensible kept to their own age, but the young men prevailed over them.

The young men, relying on their numbers and strength, began to plunder the local inhabitants. And their own kinfolk were worse than foreigners. The Roman guards saw these robberies and injustices but did not intervene, sparing their own strength and hating the Jews.

These same destructive young men acquired much wealth, and they all gathered and entered Jerusalem when the city had no leader. It is an ancestral custom among the townspeople not to drive out any kinsman, but to receive him without questioning. And then they thought that all these newcomers came in peace. And they ruined the city gradually, for from them came both unrest and war, and afterward famine.

And afterward other evildoers began coming into the city, and they joined the Jerusalemites and robbed even more, omitting no evil: they cut down the living and plundered those lying in tombs. And it was not at night, nor in secret, nor did they torture the worst people, but by day, openly, and they began with the best.

The first they killed was Antipas, a man of the royal lineage and the most influential of all the townspeople, to whom the royal treasury had been entrusted. Then Levi was shut in prison, and Sophas son of Raguel, and the other leading men. A great fear fell upon the people. And those of them who were put in chains were not held long, for they feared them, since their families were large and there were brave men among them who could avenge them and save them.

The common people too might have risen against the lawlessness of the evildoers. But John the robber wanted to kill these leading men shut in prison, and for this he sent one assassin named Tabitha. This Tabitha took with him ten executioners, entered the prison, and killed the nobles confined there. And a false accusation was laid upon them, that these nobles had sent to the Romans proposing to surrender Jerusalem, so that it appeared the executioners had killed not the rulers of the city but traitors to the common freedom.

And so the destroyers grew ever prouder and more exalted, and called themselves benefactors and saviors of the city. Later they themselves began appointing the high priests. They rejected the high priests of noble birth and installed men unknown to anyone, from the common people, so as to have them as helpers in their lawlessness. It is a common thing among men: if a bad and unworthy man is put in power, then whatever those who appointed him command him to do, that he must do. And if he does not obey, they will drive him out. And they began to appoint the high priest by lot and by command.
