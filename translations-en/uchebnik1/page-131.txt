Frightened by the waves and, without waiting for the storm, he scuttled the ship. Suicide is contrary to nature and is not characteristic of any living creature. You will not find a single animal that kills itself. Natural law rules over all — the desire to live. Therefore we count those who take life from us as enemies. And a man will anger God if he abuses His gift, and he will appear as a lawless one in the eyes of God. All flesh is mortal and created from corruptible nature. But the soul will never die; it belongs to the Divinity and dwells in the body. If someone loses another’s treasure given to him for safekeeping, such a one will be called treacherous, unfaithful, and wicked. If someone does not preserve God’s treasure in his body, how will he hide from the One whom he has offended? If a slave flees from his master through his own wickedness, he is worthy of torment. But we, fleeing from the beautiful Master, the all-good God, will we not be found impious? Do you not know that those who leave this life according to natural law and give back to God what they received from Him as a loan, when He wishes to take it — to them belong eternal glory, strong houses, and the memory of their kindred, and their souls, pure and spotless, dwell in the holy heavenly place and await there the time when they will again enter their bodies, “when the ages return”? On the contrary, the souls of those who have laid hands on themselves will be received by dark hell, and God the Father will torment them even to their great-grandchildren. For this deed is hateful before God. And our lawgiver ordered the bodies of suicides to lie unburied until sunset. In other lands they even cut off the right hands of such dead. It is fitting, O friends, to live righteously, and not to add the Creator’s wrath to human misfortunes. This is the end of my speech. I cannot betray myself. Foreigners care for my salvation. Shall I throw myself on my sword? May this not happen to me! If the Romans break their oath and kill me, then I shall perish as one crowned with a victor’s wreath, counting their treachery higher than a kingdom.” Joseph spoke much to them in this way, forbidding them to kill themselves.

But the Jews stopped their ears in despair, and they surrounded Joseph, and wanted to kill him, and reviled him, and accused him of weakness. He, calling one by name, looking threateningly at another, pulling a third by the hand, drove them away from him each time with various words. But they were ashamed and still respected him as their commander, and no one dared raise a hand against Joseph.

And then Joseph, entrusting his salvation to God the Creator, said: “Since God has commanded us to die, let us be killed by a numerical lot. On whomever the end of the count falls, he shall be killed by the one standing before him.” Having said this, he began to count the numbers with great wisdom, and by this he outwitted them all, and all were slain by one another, except one and Joseph—
