The 33rd Reign, of Valerian, Who Was Enthroned in Rome
in the Year 5761 from Adam (261 from Christ)

After Aemilian, Valerian and Gallienus his son reigned for 15 years, and he began a war with King Shapur.

He was taken captive by the spear in the Samaritan city, having 400,000 soldiers, and King Shapur killed him, having flayed off his skin. Gallienus after him was the first to introduce cavalry units, for the Roman soldiers were for the most part on foot. And he was killed by soldiers. In his time God sent great wrath upon all the earth, and a deadly plague was destroying the human race to its annihilation. For fumes rose both from the earth and from the sea, and besides, by the blowing of the winds, river and lake smells were borne along, as though their vapors contained the putrefaction of the dead. And because of this such destruction and grievous incurable diseases seized the earth that the number of human deaths was countless. Wailing and groaning were everywhere because of the multitude of the perishing—no less than when the firstborn of Egypt perished. For there was no house in which the stench of unburied corpses did not linger, since merciless pagan people, fearing infection by the deadly disease, threw out people who were not even dead yet, sparing neither kin nor friends nor any other neighbors, but striving to escape the deadly affliction. Many who feared the Lord, without guarding themselves, tended the sick and accepted suffering, departing this life together with them. Others among those not themselves sick brought some of the sick into their homes, giving them rest in their illness and taking their death upon themselves. In his time Dionysius, bishop of Alexandria, Porphyry from the city of Ephesus, and Philumenus from the Lucanian land were martyred.

The 34th Reign, of Claudius, Who Was Enthroned in Rome
in the Year 5776 from Adam (276 from Christ)

After Valerian, Claudius reigned one year. He was the grandfather of Constantius, father of the holy Emperor Constantine the Great. The Scythians who came in his time, having taken all the cities, came to Athens and captured it. And having gathered all the books, they wanted to burn them, but one of them, better than the rest, knowing that there was much meaning in them, forbade this, saying that as long as the Greeks were occupied with books, they would not care about war. Claudius, having set Aurelian over his kingdom, died.

The 35th Reign, of Quintillus, Who Was Enthroned in Rome
in the Year 5777 from Adam (277 from Christ)

After Claudius, Quintillus his brother, named king, reigned one year. He, having lived but a little while, did nothing worthy of mention.
