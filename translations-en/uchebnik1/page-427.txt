Stephen, Bishop of Raphia; George, Bishop of the city of Tiberias; Damian, Bishop of Sozopolis: these three were deputies of Eustochius, Patriarch of Jerusalem.

On the Heretics. These assembled against Origen and against Didymus the Blind and Evagrius of Pontus, who accepted his impious traditions, who in ancient times made their investigations and set them forth in chapters. In particular they talked idly, saying that souls exist before bodies, deriving this from pagan teachings; they acknowledged the transmigration of souls; and that there will be an end to hellish torment; and that our bodies will not rise at the general Resurrection; and that the demonic powers will be restored to their former state. They also denied that Paradise existed, holding that there was no natural Paradise from God, nor Adam created in the flesh. These teachings were for a time largely concealed, and many passed them on, and many of the Church’s people were ruined by them; afterward they came to light, and they were anathematized. Theodore of Mopsuestia also was anathematized—he who had been a didaskalos, that is, the teacher of Nestorius, and who was close in thought to the Jews—as was one letter of Ibas, Bishop of Edessa, to Maris the Persian, and also a certain writing of Theodoret, Bishop of Cyrrhus, composed against the Twelve Chapters of blessed Cyril of Alexandria.

To the holy council Emperor Justinian also wrote as follows: “Concerning Origen and those who think as one with him. It was and is our aim that the Holy Catholic and Apostolic Church of God be preserved without disturbance, and that shoots contrary to the Orthodox faith be rooted out and condemned. We have learned that there are certain monks in Jerusalem who follow and teach the impious delusion of Pythagoras, Plato, and Origen—who is also Adamantius. We wished to take thought for justice and to make inquiry concerning them, so that many might not be utterly destroyed because of their pagan and Manichaean delusion. They say—we mention only a little out of much—that certain intellects existed without any number and without name, and that these intellects were a Unity by the power of God the Word; and the Unity was of intellect. When these intellects abundantly received God’s wisdom, they passed over to the worse, for all rational beings are inclined to this. And all these intellects—each in its own way—underwent change, and clothed themselves in finer or coarser bodies, and inherited names; from this also the Heavenly and Ministering powers were established. And the monks in Jerusalem also say that the sun, moon, and stars are from that same rational Unity and through change to the worse became such as they are. These also say further that without the divine love’s inspiration rational beings were called souls and were placed into coarser bodies, which are ours, of humans.
