About the City of Gischala

Titus approached Gischala. He had the opportunity to attack the city suddenly. But Titus took pity on the townspeople, so that the soldiers might not destroy the innocent together with the guilty, and he stood before the city and said to them: “I am amazed at you. On whom do you rely, refusing to submit to us? You can see that all cities have submitted to Roman arms. Those that wanted war have been destroyed to their foundations, while those that chose peace live under Roman authority, rejoice in safety, and keep their wealth. Now I shall not call to mind your disloyalty. If a slave, wishing to be free, rebels against his master, and afterward, repenting, asks forgiveness, one should not remember his former disobedience. But if you do not listen to words that are humane and trustworthy, you will experience merciless weapons and will see how Roman engines destroy these walls in which you so trust.” But the brigands did not allow the townspeople to answer these words, nor to mount the walls, nor to go out through the gates. John, the leader of the brigands, answered: “Your summons is pleasing to me also, O Titus, and I will compel all in Gischala to obey you. But today is the Sabbath day, and therefore I ask you, allow us to keep it in purity, according to Jewish law. For it is impious to raise arms today. It is also considered impious among us to conclude peace on the Sabbath. You will suffer no harm from the delay. What can we do in one night? It will be of great benefit to us that we do not violate the law and custom of our fathers. It is fitting for you too, since you grant us peace and save us who are in despair, to preserve our law in purity.” With these words John outwitted Titus, for he was not thinking of keeping the Sabbath, but of his own safety, for he feared that he would be seized after the city was taken, and he hoped for the night and for escape. It pleased God to preserve John for the destruction of Jerusalem. For Titus then believed his words and gave the inhabitants of Gischala time to think, and withdrew far from the city.

At night John, seeing that there were no guards around Gischala, took his soldiers and other people who wished to go, and with his kin, and fled to Jerusalem.

When morning came, Titus came to the walls to continue negotiations, and the people opened the gates to him and received him solemnly as a benefactor who had saved the city from destruction.

The inhabitants of Gischala informed Titus of John’s flight and begged him to spare them and to find and execute the rebels. Titus sent horsemen after John, but they did not overtake him. However, the Romans caught up with those fugitives who had grown weak and fallen behind, and killed 6,000, and led away 3,000 women and children as captives.
