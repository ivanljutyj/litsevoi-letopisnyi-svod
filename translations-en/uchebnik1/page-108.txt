Some of the foolish, however, reviled and abused the ruler, carried a basket on their heads containing a little bread, and portrayed Florus as a beggar.

Florus was not ashamed by this, but turned to even greater plunder. With horsemen and foot soldiers he went to Jerusalem, to obtain what he wanted by the force of Roman arms, by intimidation, and by threats. The people met him with praise and honor and tried in every way to please him.

Florus sent Capito the centurion and fifty horsemen with these words: “Go back and do not mock me, and do not offer false honor after you have so shamefully reviled and abused me. If you are so brave and bold, revile me now; show that you love freedom not only in words but also with weapons in hand.” The people fled in terror, without managing to greet Florus, and spent that night in fear and despondency.

In the morning Florus summoned the Jews to court, and the high priests and Jewish nobles came and stood humbly before him. Florus ordered them to hand over those who had insulted him, and threatened that if they did not bring the guilty to him, he would punish the nobles themselves. The Jews said: “The people desire peace in every way. Forgive those who dishonored you in word. Among such a multitude there will always be fools and rebels from among the young, and this is not surprising. It is now impossible to know who they were, for they all repent and deny it out of fear. It is better for you to take care to maintain peace among the people and to preserve the city for the Romans. For the sake of the many innocent, forgive the few who sinned. This is better than for the sake of a few wicked men to destroy so many who are obedient to you.”

Florus became even more enraged and ordered the soldiers to plunder the so-called Upper Market. The soldiers were greedy for property, and having received the order from their commander, they not only devastated the place to which they were sent but began entering every house in succession and cutting down the inhabitants. The Jews rushed to flee through the streets, and there was indescribable panic, while the soldiers cut down everyone they encountered. The number killed that day was three thousand six hundred, with women and children. They did not spare even infants. Then was done what had not happened under any governor.
