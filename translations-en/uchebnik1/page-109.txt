At that time King Agrippa was not there; he had gone to Alexandria to take part in the celebrations for Alexander, to whom Nero had entrusted Egypt.

Agrippa’s sister Berenice was in Jerusalem and saw Florus’s lawlessness, and she pitied the Jews greatly, and sent her nobles and bodyguards to Florus and asked him to stop the killings. But Florus paid no heed either to the multitude of the slain or to the nobility of the petitioners. Before Berenice’s eyes he killed many noble Jews.

At last Florus’s soldiers rushed at the queen too and would have killed her if she had not fled hastily to the royal palace. There Berenice spent the whole night, fearing an attack by the soldiers. She had come to Jerusalem because she wished to pray and offer sacrifice to God. It is a custom among the Jews that people striving for piety, or in gratitude for deliverance from misfortune, make a vow to pray for thirty days before offering sacrifice, and not to touch wine, and to cut their hair. This preparation for sacrifice was what Berenice was then performing.

Barefoot, Berenice stood before the tribunal and begged Florus for mercy, and she received from Florus dishonor and the danger of death. This happened on the sixteenth day of the month Artemisios, that is, May.

And afterward the people of Jerusalem gathered, and the people shouted, and they ran to the Upper Market to the square, and desperate wails arose, and lamentations for the slain, and curses directed against Florus.

The Jewish nobles feared this shouting, and the high priests tore their garments, and they threw themselves at the people’s feet, begging them to stop shouting. They feared that these cries would drive Florus into even greater fury. And the people, out of respect for the nobles and high priests, obeyed them, and hoped that Florus would not act outrageously against them.

But Florus, when the sedition had quieted, was grieved and again tried to inflame it, and he summoned the high priests and the leading men of the Jews and said: “As a sign that the people have set aside their anger, let them go out and meet with praise the two detachments of soldiers coming from Caesarea.”

And secretly Florus sent word to the centurions of these detachments to give no answer to the Jews’ greeting, but to turn their weapons against them if they said anything against Florus.

The high priests, gathering with the people in the temple, begged them to meet the Romans with honor and greet them, lest they fall into irreparable misfortune. The seditiously minded Jews did not listen...
