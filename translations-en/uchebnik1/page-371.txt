They strengthened themselves, and for their impiety and for their attacks on John they acquired cruelty. But “the house of David is strengthened and exalted, while the house of Saul grows weak and is put to shame.” As you see, even if they make obstacles for the man of God, he will come forth from the storm, crowned and victorious, and will pass to the highest stillness.”

When Epiphanius was returning to Cyprus, he learned from God of the approach of his death, and, they say, he spoke thus to those accompanying him: “Make haste, and I will make haste. I leave you the kingdom, the city, and forgiveness. I also convey forgiveness to John and foretell to him his repose and the victory that will be in visible exile.” Likewise John, on the ship, foretold to Epiphanius his repose. By the Spirit of God the blessed ones learned of this, so that no one would think that they had enmity between them. Would these theologians and lovers of God, teachers and peacemakers, have departed this life with enmity? They were faithful disciples of Christ, who said: “By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love in yourselves.” And would they have departed having enmity and malice? Do not even think so. Even if there was between them, as was said earlier, a small disagreement partly exposing human weakness, they soon ascended to love and attained the summit of virtues, of which the Apostle Paul speaks: “Love endures much, is kind, is not angry, does not remember evil,” and John the Theologian also: “God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God.”

After John’s death, a certain man named Adelphius, a holy bishop, related: “I had very great sorrow that such a man, teacher of the world, who with his word gladdened the Church of God, died far from his throne. I prayed to God with many tears that He would reveal to me in what state John was, and whether he had been numbered among the patriarchs.

After I had prayed for a long time, one day a sudden terror seized me, and I saw a beautiful man who took me by the right hand and led me into a luminous and glorious place, and showed me all the teachers of the Church. I looked closely to see the one whom I desired, the great John, my beloved. And when the man showed me all of them and told me the name of each, he took me by the hand and led me out. And I, grieved, followed him, not having seen my father among the saints, John. And when I was already going out, standing at the doors, the man took me by the hand and said to me: “What is the matter with you, and why are you sad?” But I gave no answer.
