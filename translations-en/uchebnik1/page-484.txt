The holy fathers of the Sixth Council also set forth their own rules, confirming the apostolic rules and those of the holy councils, and correcting some things. They found the Savior depicted in the form of a lamb, coming to the Forerunner, and the fathers commanded that from that time the Savior be depicted in human form.

There was also a certain custom: on Great Thursday, after supper, to celebrate the service and receive communion. But they commanded that the holy service be celebrated on an empty stomach. And many other things they confirmed and established by law, and they completed what was unfinished, and they called their rules the Rules of the Sixth Ecumenical Council.

When those same holy fathers had gathered shortly before this, they did not write then because of some inconvenience. And they assembled again and set everything forth very wondrously.

Emperor Justinian pressured Callinicus, Patriarch of Constantinople, to make a prayer for the destruction of the Church of the Holy Mother of God called the Metropolitan, which stood near his palace. Justinian wanted to make in that place phialae, or lavers—that is, marble bowls—and to build a structure for the Veneti, so that they might meet with the emperor there. The patriarch, being strongly compelled, said: “We have not received a prayer for the destruction of a church, but for its building.” And, much pressured, the patriarch said: “Glory to the sovereign God, who endures always and forever, unto ages of ages, amen.”

The emperor learned of this, and the people gathered and cried out: “Amen.” And then they destroyed the church, set up the phialae, and built the church in another place and named it the Metropolitan.

In those same years Leontius the patrician rose up and by night was proclaimed emperor by the Veneti. When day came, Leontius brought Justinian out to the hippodrome and cut off his nose, and exiled him—that is, banished him—to Cherson.

The Twenty-Seventh Reign, of Leontius, who reigned in Constantinople

After Justinian, Leontius reigned for 3 years. But the righteous Eye did not endure it, and repaid Leontius not only in equal measure but also doubly, that is, twofold. Robbers were seizing the African land; the Arabs were robbers from among the Hagarenes, and they seized the whole maritime coast.
