And the destroyers sent for the family of Elahim, which was a priestly family, and appointed a high priest from it. And it fell to a man named Phannius, son of Samuel from the village of Aphta, to be high priest. This man was like the destroyers in lawlessness. He was not only unworthy of the high-priestly rank, but had never even known or heard of such an office, because he lived in a village. Dragging him out of the village like a buffoon who puts on a mask and imitates other people, they dressed him in priestly garments and taught him what to do at what time. This great disgrace was their amusement. All the priests looked on from afar as the divine law was mocked, and they shed tears and groaned bitterly, because the destroyers had ruined and trampled both the high-priestly image and the covenant of God. Every destructive and vile deed was done among the destroyers, and people understood that desolation would overtake the city, and that the prophecy would be fulfilled, that the abomination of desolation would be in the holy place. The people could not endure such disorder, and all rushed against the destroyers as against those who were laying waste the high priesthood.

But Gorion, son of Joseph, and Simeon, son of Gamaliel, went about all the assemblies and urged the people to avenge themselves on the destroyers and to cleanse the sanctuary of blood. The high priests Jesus, son of Gamala, and Anan, son of Ananus, helped them. And all gathered and were angry at the evildoers because they had seized the temples and had killed and plundered many. Yet the people did not dare rise against the destroyers. And Anan stood up among them, and looked at the temple, and his eyes filled with tears, and he said: “It would have been well for me to die before and not see the temple of God defiled by so many lawless deeds, and the holy places into which no one was permitted to enter, where now blood-drinkers crowd. Yet, clothed in priestly garments and calling upon the glorious sacred Name, I walk under the sun and rejoice that I have not yet accepted a glorious death in my old age. But if I am here alone, as in a wilderness, and there is no one with me who would be zealous with me for God, then I am ready to give my soul to Him. For I do not need life among such people, who do not feel their own destruction. For you, who are robbed, endure; who are killed, keep silent; and seeing your kinsmen killed, you do not even weep for them openly. O bitter torment! O violence unheard of! But why do I murmur against the tormentors? Was it not we who raised them, was it not by our gentleness that they were nourished? If we had forbidden them from the beginning, when they were still few, we would not have brought weapons upon ourselves.
