The glorified miracle manifested in the Church of the Mother of God in the city called Lydda, described in the lives of the holy apostles Peter and John the Theologian who built this church? For many saw that this honorable image not made by hands, imprinted on bright and radiant tablets and piously venerated, only withdrew into the depths of the church wall when certain impious Greeks and Jews in their fury tried to destroy this image. But their efforts were vain. They could cause no harm by this sacrilege: nothing in the image (neither the face, nor the garment, nor anything else) could be scraped away, and to this day it remains undamaged, for the glory of the Most Pure Virgin, according to Her promise to the holy apostles, remains with Her image to this day. Even earlier, as tradition relates, a divine statue was erected, placed before her house by the woman with the flow of blood, delivered from her illness, to the glory of Christ the Healer, as testimony to later generations of the miracle gloriously manifested in her. Concerning it the great teacher Eusebius of Caesarea, in his Ecclesiastical History, recalling manifest miracles, relates (and this also testifies against the iconoclasts) that he himself saw both this statue and the holy God-grown herb at its foot, healing and averting evil. Besides this, he recalls that the first icons of the holy apostles Peter and Paul and of Christ Himself, painted in colors, have been preserved. Thus the truth of icon veneration is confirmed both by this trustworthy witness and teacher, and by the antiquity of the custom of venerating holy icons, and by the splendor of church adornment. Sacred images can be seen on many a liturgical object. They are placed also on the cover of the Holy Gospel (and Christians venerate them together with the Gospel during the service), and the iconostasis separating the Altar is adorned with sacred images. For we not only reverently attend to the words of the Gospel, but also venerate the sacred images, which have the Gospel word as their foundation. But the beast-named one vainly desired to destroy not only all divine icons, but also the very memory of them, and to consign icon veneration to oblivion. Blaspheming publicly, the deceived one boldly said: “As icons have been destroyed and removed from our eyes, so from our hearts must all memory of them be driven out, O friends and servants of God!” For the fool proclaims folly, and his heart teaches him to do lawless and vain things and to call those who rightly worship God deceived.

From that time, when this bloodthirsty foreigner found those who kept and venerated the icons of Christ and His saints, he condemned them to cruel tortures, so that, being tormented and daily put to death, pious Christians everywhere diminished in number. So cruelly and inhumanly did the king treat the Orthodox.
