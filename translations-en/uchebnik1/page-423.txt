On the Holy Altar

The most honored Justinian made the holy altar most precious, entirely of gold and silver and costly stones. Justinian conceived such a thought: he gathered together all earthly substances—gold and silver and every kind of stone, and pearl, and copper, and amber, that is, electrum, and tin, and iron—and he took from all earthly substances and put it all into a blazing furnace and mixed it, and when it had been mixed in this way, he poured what resulted into the form of an altar. And when it had cooled, the altar was cut out and set in place, and this was beyond thought and unlike anything, for the altar took on the appearance of each of the substances used, and such things were done as the human mind cannot comprehend nor behold that beauty, since sometimes the altar appeared golden to those looking at it, sometimes silver or stone, sometimes copper, sometimes tin, and sometimes amber, that is, electrum, or iron. And the feet on which the holy altar stands, and the steps around it, the emperor also made of pure gold and pure silver, so that all who saw it marveled at the beauty of the altar.

Justinian also made three front doors of silver and pure gold, and other doors—some of copper and electrum, and some of gilded ivory—so that the number of church doors of various kinds was 365 in all.

The emperor intended to make the entire lower part of the church, that is, the stone floor, of silver, but Hierotheus the World-Renowned, the Athenian astrologer, dissuaded him and informed him that in the last days people from the dark kingdoms would come and take this city. And the emperor abandoned such a thought. Every day he threw down silver coins, and the builders found them and carried them all off, hastening to the height. The church was built by 10,000 men, that is, a myriad, as was said before. Justinian built the church in 16 years and 4 months.

Justinian made the ambo of stone whose name is sardonyx. In place of the architraves he made the columns all of gold, with jasper stones, and with crystal, and with sapphires. He also made the upper part of the ambo entirely of gold, with pearls and bright emeralds. Above it he made a golden cross weighing 100 litrai and bearing great pearls besides. And in place of the architrave he set gold. The mouth of the holy well was brought from Samaria, from that well at which, as they relate, our Lord Jesus Christ conversed with the Samaritan woman.
