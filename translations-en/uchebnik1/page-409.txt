In the second year of Justin’s reign, the Veneti faction gained the primacy in all the cities, and above all in Antioch the Great, and they slaughtered the Prasini when they met them, and moreover went about and killed those hiding in their houses.

This Justin arranged that the Olympic games should not be held in Antioch. And there were many fires in Antioch the Great.

And again after this fires began in many places in that city, and no one knew whence the fire was kindled. First the fire was kindled from the keramides, that is, the clay roof tiles, so that the princes thought this happened because of civil strife.

At the request of Euphrasius the patriarch, the emperor sent two centenaria of gold for the burned houses.

On the Calamities of Antioch, the Fifth Destruction

It happened in the years of Justin’s reign that by God’s wrath the city of Drach, that is Dyrrachium, fell, from which Emperor Anastasius came, who built much there, including a hippodrome.

Justin sent much gold for the restoration of that city. This city was formerly called Epidamnus. And Corinth also fell then, the capital of Hellas. And Justin sent much gold there for building.

And in the next year, in Justin’s reign, by God’s wrath the city of Anazarbus fell, the capital of Second Cilicia, and the emperor rebuilt it.

In the same year Edessa was drowned by God’s wrath with river waters, a great city, the capital of the province of Osroene. Late in the evening the river called Scirtus, which flows through the middle of the city there and creates beauty and supplies the city with all necessities, by God’s wrath flooded more than the sea, and inundated the city and those living in it, and cast down the houses standing there. And those living in them cried out to be saved, and could not help themselves, and died in those houses in countless numbers. Others, who had fled from that city, said that once this river had also flooded the city, but not so as to drown everyone.

After God’s wrath ceased and the water subsided, when they began to lay foundations for buildings near the river, a large stone slab was found, on which this inscription was carved: “Behold, the river Scirtus plays evil games with the citizens.”

This Edessa was founded by Seleucus Nicator. And this Seleucus named the city so, for in Macedonia in ancient times there was a city with this name. And when the city had fallen before, it was named as it is now called. Justin sent much gold to each of the cities named above, and this emperor sent and restored them, and made them even better.
