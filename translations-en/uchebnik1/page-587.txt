He sent and brought Nicholas back from Galakrin, and removed Euphemius from the patriarchate and seated Nicholas on the throne a second time.

Having assembled a gathering of the boyars in the Magnaura, Alexander brought Euphemius from Blagou and, together with Nicholas, effected Euphemius’s deposition. They immediately, like ungovernable beasts, brought this God-fearing man, pulling at his honorable beard and shoving him in the neck, dragged him, and inflicted other intolerable torments, calling him a criminal, and as though he had attached himself to another man’s wife, and an adulterer.

The holy and worthy man bore all this meekly and silently. Exiled and sent to Agathines, where he also met the end of his life, he was buried in Constantine’s city in his own monastery in Psamathia.

Now Alexander, who even before had no concern but to stay outside the city and hunt, and because of his brother Leo’s suspicion did no royal business, but loved to spend his time in pleasures and shameful deeds, having become sole ruler, did nothing valiant or worthy of mention.

Having become autocrat, he immediately appointed John the papias, surnamed Lazarus, as rector, who ended his life badly after Alexander’s death. Likewise he greatly enriched Gavrilovich and Vasilitsa, of the Slavs, and drained much gold from the palace. They say he often planned to make this Vasilitsa emperor, and to make Constantine, the son of Leo, a eunuch. But this plan was thwarted by those who loved Leo, who said that he was still young and weak in spirit. In his time a huge star appeared from the west; they called it Kopeynik, fearing that this star foretold bloodshed in Constantine’s city. For this Alexander, giving himself over to deceivers and sorcerers, believed them when they said, “The bronze wild boar standing on the portico is your sign. It opposes Leo, your brother,” hinting at his swinish life and folly.

And he, deceived by them, had the boar’s genital member and broken teeth reforged anew. Trusting in such deceits, he built a portico and, gathering curtains and lamps from the holy churches, adorned the portico, and gave God’s honor to idols. Because of this God’s honor was soon taken from him.

And Himerius the logothete, who had returned after a defeat by the Saracens, he sent into confinement in the palace monastery called Kaloupa, threatening him, because Himerius had been hostile to him even in the days of the reign of his brother Leo. After six months of exile he died, consumed by bitter sorrow.
