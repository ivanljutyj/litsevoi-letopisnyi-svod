“Stay with your wife, then you will lose me.” But Pheroras loved his wife very much and said: “It is easier for me to lose my life than my wife.” Herod could do nothing against his brother and his wife, though he hated them greatly. And Herod forbade Antipater to associate with Pheroras, with his wife, and with his close ones.

Antipater obeyed his father’s command, but secretly spent every night with them. And Antipater feared that Salome would find out, and sent a letter to the Roman nobles asking them to write to his father and summon Antipater to Caesar in Rome. They did so. Herod immediately sent Antipater to Rome, and gave him splendid attendants, and everything necessary, and great wealth, and a will in which Antipater was appointed king, and his heir was Antipater’s brother Herod, son of Mariamne, daughter of the high priest.

At the same time as Antipater, the Arab Syllaeus also sailed to Italy to Caesar, and opposed Herod, and gave much gold to Fabatus, the Roman administrator, and gained him as a helper. Antipater gave more and won Fabatus over to his side, and then Syllaeus lost.

Herod did not relent and pressured Pheroras to renounce his wife, and devised how to destroy her, for Herod had many accusations against her. Finally, enraged beyond measure, he drove his brother away with his wife. And Pheroras went to his own region, which he ruled, and swore that he would return to Jerusalem only when news came to him that Herod had died.

And then Herod fell ill, and sent for his brother, wishing to give him certain commissions, and earnestly begged him to come, but Pheroras did not come. And Herod recovered again.

Then illness came to Pheroras, and Herod proved less proud than his brother, and came to visit him, and with a contrite heart sought how to help his brother in his suffering. But Pheroras could not endure the severe illness and soon died. And although Herod loved his brother with all his soul and mourned him, people still said that Herod had poisoned Pheroras. And he was buried in Jerusalem with great honor. And Herod ordered all the people to mourn him. And the death of Pheroras became the beginning of Antipater’s misfortunes.

Some of the slaves freed by the king came to him sorrowful and said that Pheroras had indeed been poisoned, for his wife brought him food in which there was poison, and as soon as he put it into his mouth he immediately began to suffer and be ill. And two days before this his mother-in-law had invited a woman from Arabia, a sorceress skilled in herbs, to prepare a love potion and give it to Pheroras. But the sorceress had secret instructions from Syllaeus and, instead of a love potion, a deadly one.
