But he rebuked them and said: “I will not reveal to you the mystery that is within you, because He did not will it. Therefore an inexpressible destruction has come upon you, and for your sake upon all men.” And having said this, he went away to the other side of the Jordan, and no one dared to forbid him, and there he did the same as before.

Archelaus, having received authority, remembered the Jews’ enmity toward him and brought intolerable burdens upon them, and also upon the Samaritans. In the ninth year of his reign he saw a dream, as if there were nine ears of grain in a field, large and full, and oxen came and ate them and tore them up. And the king summoned soothsayers and interpreters of dreams and asked them about the meaning of his dream, and they interpreted it in various ways. And one man from the Sadducees, named Simon, said: “The ears mean years, and the oxen the mutability of all things. And you shall rule as many years as there are ears in your dream. And then various changes await you, and grief, and death.”

Five days after this dream Caesar summoned Archelaus to trial. For before this the Jews and Samaritans had sent a letter to Caesar complaining of Archelaus’s oppressions. Caesar examined the case and exiled Archelaus to Vienne, a city in Gaul. And Archelaus’s property was brought to Caesar’s palace.

Archelaus, king of Judea, had a wife Glaphyra, daughter of Archelaus, king of Cappadocia; before that she had been the wife of Alexander, son of Herod, whom his father killed, as we have written. After Alexander’s death Juba, king of Libya, took Glaphyra as his wife, and when he too died, Glaphyra returned to Cappadocia, to her father’s house.

And Archelaus, king of Judea, saw Glaphyra and felt such desire for her that he dismissed his wife Mariamne and took Glaphyra as his wife.

Then Glaphyra saw in a dream that Alexander, her first husband, came and threatened her and said: “I endured your abomination when, after my death, you went to the king of Libya. Now, since you have returned to my house, shameless woman, and have married a third husband, my brother, I will not endure such an insult, but I will take you to myself, even if you do not wish it.” After this dream Glaphyra lived two days and died.

Caesar entrusted the administration of Archelaus’s region to his military commander named Coponius. And he gave him power to execute and pardon anyone he wished. With him was a man from Galilee who reviled the Jews. He said that they, the children of Abraham, were free.
