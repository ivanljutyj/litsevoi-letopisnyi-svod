They hanged him. Yet he also called himself a prophet and a foreteller of the future, but did not recognize his own error or what was to come. He had three disciples: Thomas, Addas, and Hermas. But no one considers the Gospel of Thomas true, since he was not one of the twelve apostles, but one of the three evil disciples of Mani. This same mad Mani rejected the Old Testament and blasphemed, saying that every creature and man were created by some evil god, corrupt and vain in essence. But the New Testament he called the work of the good God, who is revealed through revelation and hope, depicting Christ as his. And to this he devised certain immersions, and foul nocturnal sacrifices, and lawless unions, and the unspeakable creation, birth, and movement of bodies, and much else false he did and taught, striving to establish the evil, vain teachings of the Greeks, being opposed to God and hated by God. Theodore, the presbyter of Raphia, also told of him: “Mani, who was the inventor of infinite darkness, or rather himself a creature of the power of darkness, imagined and dreamed that the Lord appeared in a weak and incorporeal human body, who said that what he had expected of his sufferings, and what physical sufferings they inflicted on him, were, just as with us, none of it true in reality, but only invention and deception, in order to deceive the people among whom he supposedly lived. Therefore Mani does not acknowledge two natures in the Lord, saying that it is one—the Divine.”

On Paul the Heretic. Paul, a certain contemporary of Mani, a native of Samosata, a great fellow-bishop in Antioch, called the Lord a lowly man, blaspheming that just as the word of God from above dwelt in every prophet, so also in Him, and that Christ had in Himself two distinct natures existing, not partaking of each other, one of which was Christ proper, and the other the Word of God living in Him. Thus in ancient times there grew up the evil blasphemy concerning Christ, which spoke of one nature in Him, depriving Him now of divinity, now of humanity.

On Apollinaris the Heretic. And after them, a year later, there arose a certain Apollinaris, bishop of Laodicea in Syria, confessing another vain speculation. Since at that time the followers of the Arian heresy said that the Lord’s flesh had no soul at all, he then declared that the Lord took flesh animated with a soul, but did not take our mind, since human intellect is not needed by the flesh if the Word of God has become incarnate in it, and no other rational power besides the Divine can be contained in it. And he reasoned thus: the Word and the flesh have one nature; therefore His flesh should be considered a perfect man, but it is unworthy to call it a nature.

On Theodore the Heretic. Together with him appeared a certain man named Theodore, who received unlimited authority in the city of Mopsuestia in Cilicia.
