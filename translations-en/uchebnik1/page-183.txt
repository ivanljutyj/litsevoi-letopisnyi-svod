The altar had four corners, and on each corner was a projection like a horn. And they ascended to the altar from the south. The altar was built without iron, and iron never touched it. Around the temple and the altar there ran, like a rim, a beautiful stone enclosure one cubit high, separating the people from the priests. Lepers and the gravely ill were not allowed even to enter the city. Women were forbidden to cross this enclosure, whether clean or unclean. And men who had not purified themselves were forbidden to enter the inner court, and priests likewise. Those who were priests by birth but could not serve because they were blind, or maimed, or subject to fainting, entered inside with healthy men and received the priestly portion of the sacrifice on equal terms with healthy priests, but did not put on the sacred garments. To the altar and into the temple ascended blameless priests clothed in fine linen, and they abstained from wine so as not to alter anything in the service. And the high priest ascended with them, but not every day, rather on the Sabbath, and on the first day of the month, and on the great ancestral feasts. When the high priest served, his garments differed from the garments of the priests. The high priest was wrapped to the calves in a girdle, and beneath it in linen. Over it he put a garment that hung down on all sides to the feet and was adorned with hyacinth. And around it hung golden bells and pomegranates likewise; the bells represented thunder, and the pomegranates lightning. And a band fastened the garment to the chest. This band was one fifth gold, and of purple, and of coccus, and of fine linen, and of hyacinth, from which the veil also was made; they signified the same as was said about the katapetasma. And above this the high priest had a shoulder-piece overlaid with gold, having the appearance of armor. Near the shoulder-piece were two settings woven with gold, in which sat large and beautiful sardonyx stones.

On these stones were written the names of the heads of the Jewish tribes. On the other side of the shoulder-piece hung twelve stones divided into four parts: in the first, carnelian, topaz, and emerald; in the second, ruby, jasper, and sapphire; in the third, agate, amethyst, and amber; in the fourth, onyx, beryl, and chrysolite. The high priest’s head was covered by a crown of fine linen adorned with hyacinth, and upon it another golden crown, on which the sacred name of the Life-giver was written crosswise. The high priest put on this garment only when he served in the sanctuary, where no one could enter except the high priest once a year, when by custom all the Jews prepared to serve God.
