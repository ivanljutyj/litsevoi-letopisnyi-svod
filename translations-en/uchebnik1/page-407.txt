Of Judgment Day. When the hour of death comes, a man would gladly lose all the wealth of the world, if only his soul were not separated from his body, for the death of sinners is cruel. But rising from that bed, you go again to the same evil deeds, and even worse. You enter the houses of the rich, you wrong them, rob them, judge without justice, play the hypocrite, take by force, show no mercy to the poor, do not pity the orphan. But when death comes, all this perishes for those who gather possessions. And we do not remember that the power is the Lord’s: to whom He wills, He gives it. Under this Emperor Anastasius, Euthymius the Great departed in the fourth year of his reign. Under this same emperor labored the elders Sabbas the Sanctified and Theodosius the Cenobiarch, and John the Faster, and Cyriacus the Hermit.

The Fifteenth Reign, of Justin, who reigned in Constantinople

After Anastasius Dicorus, Justin from the village of Bederiana in Thrace became emperor, in the consulship of Magnus, in the month Panemus, that is July, on the 9th day, indiction 11; and the year was, by Antiochene or Syriac reckoning, 566. The soldiers with all the people placed the crown on this Justin and set him up as emperor. He was comes excubitorum, that is commander of the emperor’s close guards. Justin reigned 9 years and 22 days. He was of medium stature and valiant in wars.

As soon as Justin became emperor, he killed Amantius, the praepositus, that is the chief of the palace eunuchs, and Andrew Lausiacus, the cubicularius, that is the bedchamberlain, and Theocritus, a comes, a kinsman of that Amantius. This Amantius wished to make that Theocritus emperor, and gave this Justin gold to distribute to the people, so that they might set up Theocritus as emperor, and Justin distributed the gold.

But the soldiers and the people, having taken the gold, did not desire Theocritus to be emperor, but set up Justin. After his accession, Justin killed them both—Amantius and Theocritus—as opponents, in the imperial chambers, within. And the nobles whom Anastasius had exiled before him, Justin brought back.

At the beginning of Justin’s reign, a fearful comet-star rose in the evening in the east, which stretched its rays downward. Some called this star Pogonias, that is the Bearded, and they feared it. In the first year of Justin’s reign, Severus, patriarch of Antioch the Great, fled to Egypt, fearing Vitalian.
