And with a cry he ordered them to call out: “My soul clings to the earth; O Lord, give me life according to Thy word.” And with his hands Theodosius tore at his hair and beard, and striking his head, he wet the ground with tears until the time of Communion. The boyars and all the people received benefit for their souls from this, and they wept and cried with Theodosius.

When the tsar, wishing to approach the Holy Mysteries, stood near the holy doors of the altar, preparing to enter, Ambrose sent to him and said: “Know, tsar, that only priests may enter the inner part of the church; for others it is inviolable and inaccessible. It is fitting for kings to do what is kingly, not what is priestly; therefore remain outside the altar and commune equally with all.”

Theodosius accepted this also with joy and answered Ambrose: “I did not do this of myself, but was taught so in Constantinople, for such is the custom there. With gratitude I accept this healing also.” The archbishop and the tsar shone with virtues.

When the tsar entered Constantinople and a festival was held there, he did not enter the altar. Patriarch Nektarius sent to Theodosius and wished to learn the reason, and the tsar answered: “We have scarcely learned the difference between king and priest; Ambrose told me, the only one who is called bishop by merit.”

On the Idols of Antioch

Certain lawless men in Antioch smashed the royal idols and brought the tsar into great fury.

And the tsar, enraged, sent boyars to torment those living in the city. Learning of the arrival of these boyars, the townspeople fled.

Monks came down from the mountain and turned the tsar’s wrath to mercy. About this Chrysostom says: “When certain vile and most vile men trampled the laws and took down the royal idols, they brought misfortune upon all the rest. And when the boyars sent by the tsar to investigate what had happened gathered for that terrible judgment and summoned all the bold culprits to answer, all expected various deaths.

Then the monks dwelling on the mountains showed their wisdom. For so many years they had remained in their tents and caves, and no one asked for them, nor had they conspired together, but of themselves, since they saw the city enveloped by such a cloud, they left their tents and caves and gathered from everywhere, as if someone had come down from heaven. Then these saints appeared everywhere and with their very faces strengthened the afflicted, who implored them for help, and in every way they averted the assault.
