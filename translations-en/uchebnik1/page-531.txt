"Now, if this is fitting, know that in the morning I will accuse you and tell the tsar everything, and then you will perish." And he gave this message to Papias with the words: "Deliver this to such-and-such and tell them: 'Come this night in the garments of clerics, hiding short swords beneath them.'"

When the time of the service came, the court singers came to sing the canon of the Nativity of Christ. Papias opened the doors for them, and with them entered Michael's accomplices, dressed in clerical garments, and they hid in the corners of the church.

The tsar's singers began to sing and had already sung half the service when the tsar came to matins and also began to sing. His voice was strong and loud, but it sounded harsh and unmelodious. The tsar always imagined that his singing was pleasing to his listeners, and he usually began the singing first. And now, as soon as he entered the church, he began loudly to sing the troparion "With love they reviled the King of all."

And at once the conspirators suddenly surrounded him from all sides and drew their swords. Seeing this, the tsar hid in the altar; they, bursting in after him, stabbed him.

They cut off his head and hands, and threw the body out for all to see. Thus, having ended his vile and impious life, he also destroyed his worthless, accursed soul. Leo was killed by the sharp sword, receiving bitter recompense for the deed of his foul and absurd life. Condemned to death by the conspirators who dared to commit this evil in the holy church, in his life the thrice-accursed hater of God, who had done so much evil and drunk so much Christian blood, himself received an evil death at the hands of murderers.

After this his foul body was wrapped in plain and coarse cloth, placed in a prepared boat, and carried to the so-called First Island, where it was buried. But the bold men who did this did not know that they had taken such a sin upon themselves only for condemnation. "He stretched out firmly, as the word is, these abominable and evil-named men, truly inflaming those things that were lawlessly among them, as they were brought forth, and in certain holy places, if indeed anywhere preserved and known, and some nowhere knew this.
