And soon Herod betrothed Salome to Alexas, and he gave Salome’s first daughter in marriage to Alexas’s son, and the second to his own uncle. Antipater, however, destroyed the orphans’ hopes of the kingdom and arranged marriage alliances to his own advantage.

After this Antipater again began to feel confident and feared no one. To his malice was now added insolence, and he became unbearable. Being unable to avoid people’s contempt, he sought their support by pride and threats. And Pheroras helped Antipater, seeing in him the future king.

Then the women stirred up an unprecedented disturbance at the royal court. Pheroras’s wife, together with her mother and sister, joined with Antipater’s mother. They did much lawlessness and dared to insult two of the king’s daughters, for which the king placed a guard over the daughters. And no one could withstand these women. Salome alone came to the king and informed him that these women were gathering not for his benefit and not with good intent. When they learned of the slander and of Herod’s anger against them, they stopped gathering openly and, moreover, pretended to be at enmity with one another when they appeared before the king. Antipater also, in his father’s sight, began to quarrel with Pheroras. But they began to gather secretly by night and under guard, and continued to be of one mind. Salome learned of everything they were doing and informed Herod.

Herod was inflamed with anger, and most of all against Pheroras’s wife, and he then gathered his relatives and nobles and brought this woman to trial. And Herod accused Pheroras’s wife of having insulted his daughters, and said that she had given great gifts to the Pharisees, setting them against Herod, and had instigated Herod’s servants, and practiced all kinds of sorcery over food and clothing and footwear and riding. And Herod said: “Remember, O Pheroras, what death befell Pesis the commander. When I wished to eat your food, and God saved me, and I did not eat, and sent for Pesis, and when he tasted it, his eyes came out, and his joints fell apart piece by piece, and he roared, and on the third day he died. Another time, when I took hold of the bridle, in that same moment my hand went numb. And if a certain Syrian had not happened to be nearby then, who pierced the horse alive and put my hand into the horse quickly, your wife would have killed me, O Pheroras, with an unheard-of death. And I tortured my groom, and he accused her. And she used every similar sorcery against me. And you, Pheroras, have forgotten both God and kinship and brotherhood and fervent love and the rank and honor that came to you through me, and you were your own master, but now you have taken up arms against me and desire my death.” Pheroras did not dare utter a word in reply and feared greatly for his wife.
