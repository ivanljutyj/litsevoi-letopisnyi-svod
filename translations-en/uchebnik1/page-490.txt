But Conon did not believe it and was perplexed, looking at his great poverty, and those haters of Christ assured him with an oath that it would be so. And behold, they asked him for sworn words, that he would promise them that, when this came to pass, everything they might ask would be granted to them by him without delay.

This was near the church of the Holy Great Martyr Theodore. Then Conon the locksmith agreed to this, and entering, took hold of the holy altar rails, and gave the Holy Martyr Theodore as guarantor and intercessor for himself to those deceivers, that is, petitioners; and the Jews stood in the church courtyard and received the oath from him. Then each of them went his own way.

There was in the Eastern land a certain man holding the authority of a commander, of patrician rank, named Sisinnius; and he was journeying in that land, selecting soldiers, and among them Leo, called “a true cunning fellow in the furnace,” was equipped as a soldier.

And, little by little revealing his boldness of heart, Leo was appointed patrician and great chief commander.

And Leo was sent by Emperor Theodosius to the eastern lands, because in those years Masalma, the Saracen prince, was warring against the Greeks, and was at the fortress of Amorium; Masalma had come there in ships called dromons; there were 120 ships.

And Masalma wrote to Leo, the eastern commander: “I know that you wish to be Greek emperor,” and he sent commanders to him, and Masalma made peace with him, and departed to his own place.

But Leo did not keep faith with Theodosius, and then by the soldiers he was proclaimed emperor, and he found a helper for the wicked design—Artavazd, a man from among the illustrious; Leo promised to reward him, and gave him his daughter in marriage.

But the gentle Emperor Theodosius feared Leo’s boldness and beastly heart, and voluntarily left the throne to the usurper, and, meeting Leo, took the crown from his own head and placed it on Leo’s head, and of his own will left the scepters without weapons or war.

And Theodosius clothed himself in black, and with his son, instead of the imperial purple, became a church cleric; he anointed his lips only with the honey of power, but could not sweeten what is above the tongue.
