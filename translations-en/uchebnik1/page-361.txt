Theodosius ordered the Hellenes to be expelled, or pardoned if they should pass over to Christianity; and he ordered the vessels of the Hellenic temples to be cleansed and distributed to all the poor for their sustenance.

When the temple of Serapis was being destroyed, hieroglyphic inscriptions were found that had a Christian figure. Christians who had come over from Hellenism saw them. And seeing the figure, these Christians came forward and said: “This sign of the Cross says to those who know hieroglyphic writing: ‘Life comes.’” In the temple of Serapis, which Archbishop Theophilus destroyed, the idol of Serapis was also destroyed. Some said it was Zeus, others the Nile, since there was a vessel on his head; and some took him for our Joseph, and for a certain Apis, who, as they told, had been a rich man in the city of Memphis, or a king in the land of Egypt. When there was famine among the citizens of Alexandria, that Apis distributed food from his stores. And when he died, a temple was built where the ploughman’s ox, which had a special mark on its hide, was fed. That same ox also was called by this man’s name—Apis. The coffin of this Apis, in which his body lay, was brought to Alexandria, and the words “coffin” (in Greek “soros”) and “Apis” were joined and made into a name, and the coffin with the body was called Serapis.

After them, people living later began to call the god Serapis. A temple was built to him by Alexander’s command, with very great columns and marble slabs, magnificently adorned within and without. In the middle of the temple was an enormous and terrible idol of Serapis, so that its right hand was held to one wall and its other hand to the other wall. This idol was made of wondrous substances of every kind and of wood. The inner walls of that temple were overlaid with gold and silver shields. In this temple a deception was contrived in some manner, and this wicked deception was as follows: there is a certain stone called a magnet, which has a natural power to draw iron to itself. They made an enormous idol of bronze, and drove iron into its head from within, and placed the stone above in the high vault, and arranged it so that by natural force the idol was drawn by the stone to itself, for the stone hung above, and the idol, held by great force and craft, was above the ground in that temple, and they marveled at it greatly, that it did not fall. When that idol was destroyed and struck down, and the river Nile did not overflow as usual, the Hellenes rejoiced, saying: “For this reason the river did not overflow.”

Having learned of this, King Theodosius answered piously: “So the river and the temple, rejoicing in demonic sacrifices, used formerly to bring water onto the earth.” God accepted his faith and commanded the water to multiply beyond what was required.
