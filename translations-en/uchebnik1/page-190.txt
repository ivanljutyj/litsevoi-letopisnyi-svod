He ordered that those whom the soldiers overtook be neither killed nor taken captive nor plundered. For Titus cared that the city should stand to his glory, and that the temple should stand to the glory of the city and of him.

To the people, however, Titus promised freedom and a free life in their villages and on their estates. And the people rejoiced at these words and were ready to obey these commands, but the Jewish soldiers did not allow them, for they thought that Titus spoke of peace out of weakness, since he could not take the inner city. And they threatened the people with death if anyone mentioned surrendering the city, and those who spoke of peace they killed.

And the rebels attacked the Romans who had entered, and took positions along the narrow streets, while some climbed onto the houses and shot and struck. And the Romans who were entering the city by the wall were swept away, while those crowded in the city fell there like cattle and crushed one another. The Jews grew ever more numerous, and they prevailed, since they knew the narrow streets of the city and all the entrances and exits. But the Romans went about like blind men, not knowing where to pass or where to get out. I think all the Romans would have fallen there, cut down, if Titus had not ridden in to them. He placed archers at the ends of the narrow streets, and he himself took his stand with Sabinus where there were many Jews. Shooting often at the Jews, Titus with his detachment held them back until all the soldiers had got out. And the Romans, having taken the second wall, were thus driven out.

The soldiers of Jerusalem, however, began to pride themselves and boast. Having defeated the Romans and driven them out, they expected that the Romans would now humble themselves and would not dare return to the city or overcome them. God darkened the minds of the rebels because of their lawlessness. They looked neither at the Roman army, which remained outside the city, nor at the famine that was approaching them. They themselves fed on plunder. But those who were good and meek died from lack of all necessities and perished from extreme want. The rebels, however, accepted the destruction of the people lightly and joyfully, for they wanted no one to remain alive except those who wished to fight. When the Romans again tried to enter through the second wall, the rebels hindered them, blocking the ruined place with themselves, and for three days they held firm.

On the fourth day Titus advanced with all his soldiers, and the Jews could not withstand it and were forced to flee where they had before. Titus again took the walls and destroyed the entire northern side, and placed guards on the southern towers. And Titus began to consider how to approach the third wall, and delayed the assault, wishing to give the enemies time for repentance.

And the day came when the monthly wages had to be given to the soldiers for their sustenance. And Titus commanded the commanders each to prepare his regiment, and to count out the silver, and to distribute it to all in order.
