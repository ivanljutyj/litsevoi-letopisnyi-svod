Soon he began to regard Eurycles as a man loyal to him, and made him a friend of Aristobulus, his brother, Herod’s third son. Eurycles was Antipater’s hireling and Alexander’s betrayer.

Eurycles reproached Antipater for not noticing, though he was the eldest son, those who wished to harm him and prevent him from ascending the throne. To Alexander, however, Eurycles spoke thus: “Why do you yield the kingdom to Antipater? His mother was a woman of no distinction, while yours was of royal stock; your wife is a princess, and your father-in-law is King Archelaus.” And young Alexander trusted Eurycles, since he claimed to be a friend of Archelaus; therefore Alexander hid nothing from Eurycles and complained to him with tears against Antipater, his brother, and against Herod, his father, saying: “Herod killed our mother, Aristobulus’s and mine. So it will be no wonder if he also takes from us the right to reign that we received from her.” Eurycles pretended to pity the brothers Alexander and Aristobulus and shed tears with them. Having obtained from these brothers their frank reproaches against their father, Eurycles went to Antipater and passed this secret on to him, and also lied that the brothers were seeking a convenient opportunity to kill Antipater. And Eurycles received from Antipater much wealth as a reward for this lie. Then he began to praise Antipater and to slander Aristobulus and Alexander before their father Herod. Thus Eurycles became a participant in the death of Alexander and Aristobulus.

And Eurycles came to Herod and said: “Today I grant you your life for all the favors you have shown me, and I will open your eyes in return for your hospitality. For long ago a sharp sword has been prepared against you, and Alexander’s hand holds it firmly and has already raised it over you. But I persuaded Alexander not to hurry, pretending to be his accomplice. Alexander says: ‘It was not enough for Herod that he reigns in a kingdom not his own, and that after murdering my mother he possesses her kingdom; now he has also brought in an outsider as heir and is giving the kingdom that belonged to my mother to Antipater the destroyer. The spirits of my great-grandfather King Hyrcanus and of my mother Mariamne call upon me for vengeance, and I cannot accept rule from such a father without a terrible deed.’ Such words, O King Herod,” Eurycles continued, “your son Alexander speaks about you. And if anyone happens to mention noble birth, Alexander says readily: ‘I alone am of noble birth, while my father’s family is ignoble. Despite this, my father treats me badly and cruelly, and shows love only to Antipater. Therefore I will gladly die if my plot is exposed, because I cannot endure this injustice.
