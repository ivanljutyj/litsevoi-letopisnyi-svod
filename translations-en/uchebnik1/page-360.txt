These monks boldly spoke about the guilty, and were ready to shed their blood for all, in order to deliver the afflicted from the executions awaiting them. And so it came to pass.

Where now are those who clothed themselves in temptations, who displayed deep darkness, who held sticks in their right hands, false philosophers, dog-scum, worse than dogs under the table, who do everything for the belly? For those living in the city all fled into the wilderness and hid, and renounced their possessions; but these monks alone showed true wisdom by their deeds, and when all had assembled, they stood in the midst and destroyed evil. They did not gather over many days, but on one day they came together, and spoke, and having destroyed the calamity, they returned to their dwellings. Such wisdom is given to people by Christ.”

On the Head of John the Baptist

In the reign of Theodosius the precious head of John the Baptist was brought to Constantinople; it had first been found by two monks who belonged to the Macedonian heresy, who had come from Jerusalem and were staying in Cilicia.

When Mardonius the eunuch learned of this, he sent word to the emperor Valens, and by the emperor’s command they began to carry the precious head to Constantinople; they sent it, placing it on a chariot.

And those sent reached an inn. On the next day they wanted to continue their journey, but the mules would not move from that place. Then the holy head was placed in the village of Kosila, since God did not permit Valens to be deemed worthy of such a gift.

But to Theodosius, the pious emperor, God, rewarding according to merits, permitted the precious head to be brought to Constantinople. The emperor ordered it to be placed in one place, where he also built a beautiful and large church. Under Theodosius, Capito, Bishop of Cherson, was martyred. At that time too, Cyriacus the Hermit from the city of Corinth struggled ascetically, son of John the presbyter and his mother Eudoxia.

On the Alexandrian Temple

In Alexandria there was a pagan temple; Theophilus the archbishop asked Theodosius for permission to cleanse this temple and turn it into a church. Before the people Theophilus denounced the pagan lewdness, their cunning, folly, and filth.

The pagan people marveled at this, and were stirred up, and committed many murders. Theodosius learned of the murders that had occurred and glorified the slain Christians as those killed for the sake of orthodoxy.
