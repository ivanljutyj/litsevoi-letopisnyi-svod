I seek no deliverance from you, since I speak what is contrary to the judgment of God and try to save those whom God has condemned. For all know the writings of the ancient prophets and that fate which awaits this long-suffering city. The prophets once foretold that the city of Jerusalem would be conquered when kinsman rises against kinsman for battle. And now, as all know, the city and the sanctuary are full of corpses. Since you have laid the blood of the righteous upon yourselves and upon our race, therefore God—God Himself, and again I say, God—has brought the Romans upon us to cleanse the sanctuary with fire. And Jerusalem will be captured, because it is full of unspeakable abomination.” So Josephus spoke with sobbing and tears, and ended his speech, trembling with weeping. The Romans pitied him and marveled.

John, that is, Johanan, and those surrounding him were inflamed with even greater hostility toward the Romans and longed to seize Josephus. Josephus’s words inspired many of the noble Jews, but they feared the city guard and remained in the city, though they knew they would perish with the city. Some Jews, however, found time to flee and crossed over to the Romans. Among them were the high priests Joseph and Jesus, also three of the sons of high priests, and other noble Jews. The emperor received them honorably and sent them to Gophna, understanding that it would be displeasing to them to be among foreigners living by other customs. And Titus commanded these noble Jews to live for a time in Gophna, and promised to restore to each of them his property in full when the war ended. And these Jews did not appear in the Roman camp, because they were in Gophna.

The rebels spread word everywhere that Titus had killed these Jews; they wanted to frighten the people and turn the remaining Jews in the city away from flight. For a time this deception succeeded, for many Jews refrained from fleeing out of fear that they would be killed in the Roman camp. Titus learned of this and brought the refugees back from Gophna to the camp, and commanded them, with Josephus, to go around the city and show themselves to the people. And many then, seeing them, fled to the Romans.

And the Jewish refugees gathered and came up to the city walls, and wept and wailed, and implored the rebels, saying: “Submit to the Romans; by this you will save your fatherland. If you do not wish to surrender, then go wherever you please, but let the temple remain unharmed. The Romans will not raise a hand against the sanctuary without extreme necessity.” And again the rebels did not listen, and with anger and abuse reviled those who addressed them.

And the rebels placed stone-throwing and spear-throwing engines and all that was necessary for battle at the temple gates, and through the holy places, where it was not even permitted to walk, they scurried with weapons, dripping with still-warm blood.
