And Simon the scribe, an Essene by birth, rose up in fury and said to that man: We read the divine books every day, and you have now come out of the thicket like a beast, and yet you dare to teach us and to deceive the people with your unseemly words. And Simon rushed at him, wishing to tear his body apart. But he rebuked them and said: I will not reveal to you the mystery that is in you, because you did not will it. Therefore unspeakable destruction will come upon you. And having said this, he went away to the other side of the Jordan, and no one dared to hinder him, and there he did the same as before.

The Forerunner of the Lord, John, interprets the dream to Herod Philip

Herod Philip was in his own region and saw a dream, that an eagle had plucked out both his eyes; and Philip summoned all his wise men, and each of them interpreted the dream in his own way.

And that man about whom it had been written earlier that he walks in animal skins and purifies people in the streams of the Jordan came to Philip suddenly, though no one had called him, and said: Hear the word of the Lord concerning the dream that you saw: the eagle is your greed, for this bird does violence and plunders; and that sin will take away your eyes, that is, your power and your wife. And after these words, on that same day, Philip died, and his power passed to Agrippa.

In those days Herod Antipas reigned in Judea; he was the most ungodly. And news of John was heard throughout all Galilee and Judea, and many came to him, and 40 disciples gathered to him. Among them were Andrew of Bethsaida and John the son of Zebedee.

And Herod lived in Sebaste.

And Herod sent a commander of fifty to the river Jordan and ordered John to be brought. When the commander of fifty came and saw the glory covering his face, he dared not say anything. But the Forerunner of the Lord understood why this man had come, and said to him: Go, tell King Herod that it is not time now for me to appear before him. But days will come when I shall appear before him of my own accord and rebuke his impiety and his transgression of the law, since he is not satisfied with the evil he has done, and wishes to go up onto his brother's bed, and will be found a transgressor before God.

And the commander of fifty returned and relayed John's words to Herod. Herod listened and marveled, and said to his nobles: Is there anything that could be hidden from this man, this destroyer, for he has exposed my secret thought.
