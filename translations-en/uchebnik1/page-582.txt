A certain Dophilus, a cubicularius by rank, was sent to Sicily on some commission and carried with him 100 litrai of gold. Having fallen ill, he went to Thessalonica to bathe and rest.

The Tripolitan seized him and, learning that he had gold, tortured him for a long time and killed him, though he said he had none. For it had been left on the road, and Simeon, an asekretis by rank, passing along that road, took the gold. This Simeon later became a patrician and chief of the asekretis.

Since the Tripolitan intended to destroy the city of Thessalonica, Simeon sent him word: let him take the gold from him and leave the city. And so it was.

And the Saracen army turned back. Himerius, learning of this, rushed after them.

These came to Crete and gave its inhabitants a part of the plunder.

From there they set off for their own country, leaving Himerius at Limenae with nothing.

There was also with the emperor the patrician Eustathius, deputy of the stratigos of the East, that is, deputy of the commander, from the glorious family of Argyros. He fought against the Ishmaelites and more than once displayed bravery; and there was Andronicus, who was of the Doukas family. And this man, after Eustathius, was sent to the same war and raised to the rank of patrician. The Saracens feared him so much that his name was spoken among them to frighten them.

Samonas, however, under the pretext that he was going to his own monastery called Spira, situated in Damastria, intended to flee with his gold and his horses, cutting the hamstrings of the public horses—in our speech, the post horses—at every station.

And the emperor sent Basil the hetaireiarch, surnamed Kamater, and Krinites George to overtake him. When Samonas had stopped, preparing to cross the Halys, Nicephorus, droungarios of Kamina, overtook him and seized Samonas, saying: “You are caught.” Samonas begged him, saying: “I will pay you well.” But he did not listen to him; then Samonas ran to the cross that is in Siras (Siricha), inventing the excuse that he had come for prayer.

And Constantine, son of Andronicus Doukas, came up and seized him and returned to Constantinople.

And the emperor ordered Samonas to be guarded in the house of Bardas. And the emperor asked Constantine whether it was true that he had wanted to flee to Syria, and hearing that it was true, he forbade Constantine to say this when questioned before the assembly of the boyars, but to say that he had gone to Siricha for prayer. For the emperor wished to set Samonas free.
