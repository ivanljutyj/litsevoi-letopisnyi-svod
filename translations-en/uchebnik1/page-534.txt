…or vainglory—that is, folly—the embrace of every desire, wishing to rule over all and not to be under God’s authority, and either the shame of human glory or, to speak more plainly, the acquisition of created things. From this come insult to God and the saints and persecution of the Church; from this, being oppressed by visible things, he urges obedience; from this he compels submission to laws and will. For this reason this final spiritual thing, this contrived weapon, gathered as a guard for the body by images but destroying the truths of Christ’s Church, was devised to seize by effort and to write Christ’s divinity on an icon, and foretelling, as some show, that the sun’s disk-shaped body is not to be painted in any place, as it is seen somewhere. For he commanded both the substance of light and the dawn to be written and depicted, or that the light of the sun be wholly taken away. Or again, a ray of the sun, or a ray falling somewhere on a tree, when the tree is cut down and the ray is cut off with the tree—they foolishly thought. Moreover they devise arguments about fire and certain other things, so that in truth they may be made sport of by playing children, thinking this without understanding. For if it was not from what is spoken, neither was it visible; and if it has no place to be spoken, how much more concerning the ineffable and divine nature, or concerning the unutterable dispensation of Christ, is it impossible to speak or to know these things, and not to be attempted. For nothing is permitted or guarded for our sake; by these he suffered nothing; and if a man, as we are, is depicted, or, having wholly died, will be separated. What else is death but the separation of the soul from the body? By these it is clearly known that there is nothing impious or reaping. For they do not know how much is written and depicted—that to write and depict, changing the substance, and in this he is deceived, speaks vanity. Where was the body of God not written or depicted, or heard of from all ages? Or how will the bodily ordinance and word be saved, if it does not have these? Therefore, not glorifying the writing or that Christ was depicted in body, or not knowing that it is body, thereby he acquired true glory or, having learned, rejects that body and soul are saved. But whoever confesses Christ ascended in body, as our body is, and that this is written and depicted, let him confess what is with him. If you do not grant the second, neither take the first, which is neither verbally nor mocked. And we depict, but write; if they, wretched, boast vainly otherwise improperly. What the unwritten says, he does not know how from foolishness he perceives the icon to be depicted. For if Christ ought not to be depicted, again their teachers will have something; they received great destruction, Manes and Marcion. For hoping, they glorify that the appearance became a body to the Divine Word; nothing to me; the first lie falls into blasphemy, for if they armed themselves lawlessly against the whole universe, they will not be buried. And some of these, not speaking of praiseworthy things, darkened, in the light, untaught by wisdom, make ungraced things from the theological tongue. At this also Gregory Decapolite and his disciple Hilarion the New of Dalmatia.
