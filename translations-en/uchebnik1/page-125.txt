And they made shelters, the so-called “tortoises,” so that those in the city could not harm them. And the Romans filled up the chasms, while the Jews threw down great stones upon them and tried with every kind of weapon to hinder the Romans, but without success.

Vespasian set battering engines around the city—there were sixty of them—and they hurled stones, and spears launched by special devices made a din, and arrows darkened the light. Therefore the Jews could not go up on the walls and strike the Romans from above.

The Jews would rush out from the gates suddenly like robbers, tear away the shelter-tortoise, and cut down those beneath it.

Vespasian ordered a continuous shelter to be made along the whole wall of the city. Josephus gathered builders to make the walls higher. But they could not build because of the stones and arrows. Then a shelter was made for them from freshly flayed ox hides. The builders worked day and night and raised the walls by twelve cubits.

Because of this Vespasian was greatly troubled, since he could devise nothing against such a height of walls. And he recalled the soldiers, and remained around the city, and considered taking it by hunger. But in Iotapata there was an abundance of every kind of food. They lacked salt and water, since there was neither spring nor well inside the city. Vespasian hoped that the Jews would surrender to him because of thirst.

Josephus wished to break Vespasian’s hopes and ordered many garments to be soaked and hung out on the city walls so that water would drip from them. This threw the Romans into fear and despondency.

The Roman commander despaired of taking the city by hunger and again turned to weapons and violence, which was what the Jews sought, for they no longer hoped to save themselves and the city and wished rather to die in battle than from hunger and thirst. And they fought every day, and all the Jews were killed, and few of them remained.

And soon Josephus understood that there was no salvation for him any longer, and he thought of fleeing from the city. Learning of this, the people surrounded him and begged him not to abandon them in this misfortune, and they placed all their hope in him alone, and said: “If you remain with us further, then we shall all be brave in battle for your sake. But if you leave, then they will defeat us.” And Josephus, thinking of his own life, said to them: “For your sake I am leaving, to gather soldiers and suddenly attack the enemies. And the Romans, learning that I have gone from you, will leave the city and pursue me.” But the people did not listen to these words of Josephus.
