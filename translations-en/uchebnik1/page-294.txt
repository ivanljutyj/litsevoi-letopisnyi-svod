And every kind of filth filled the whole world, while the cities subject to Consta were cleansed of this evil, and through his sole rule and authority there was tranquility in them. The pious in life and all who were persecuted for the sake of our Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ—all the virtuous and those who confessed Christ—gathered under his protection. Consta looked over the multitude of Christians who had assembled and said: “Take courage, all who have not renounced God, for Christ is with Constantine. Others arm themselves against you, but He will protect you from them.” And having said this, he kissed them all.

On the Enthronement of Tsar Constantine in Britain

And embracing the youth, he put a purple robe on him and placed a crown on his head, so that he might resemble David, king of Israel, thereby showing the entrusting of state power and the heir of the realm. And he instructed the youth to be, like himself, a protector of others. Thus the pious Constantine was named Augustus and sole ruler of the Roman realm in the two hundred first year of the Olympiad, striving to glorify still more the kingdom which he had received.

On the Repose of Tsar Consta

His father Consta died in Britain, departing to the other world in goodness. Having reigned over Britain and lived to the age of 56, he left the kingdom to Constantine, the son of Helena. The Christ-loving ruler Constantine, soon after his father’s righteous death, performed the burial of his relics with unprecedented honors.

On the War of the Persians with Constantine

Tsar Constantine heard of the invasion of the Persians, who were attacking Roman cities, plundering them, and taking captives.

And selecting 2,000 strong, sturdy men skilled in battle, honoring them with gifts and arranging a feast for them, he set out to war.

And after some time the two armies met. The Persians, employing every kind of treachery, attacked the army by night, capturing and killing the Roman guard by deceit. And bursting into the camp, they killed many Romans. And they took Tsar Constantine captive, showing honor on account of the royal tent, seizing him with all his attendants.
