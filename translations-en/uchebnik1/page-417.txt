And a certain woman named Marcia gave as a gift to Hagia Sophia eight wondrous green columns from the Roman temple built by the autocrat Aurelian, as Plutarch, Justinian's scribe, reported, and the columns were brought to the church.

Those same eight wondrous green columns were brought from Ephesus to Rome by Constantine the commander. And all of them were made equal in width and equal in length, and all were of one measure.

As for the other columns, kings and princes sent some from Cyzicus, some from Troas, and others from the surrounding, that is the Cycladic, islands.

Likewise all else was prepared that it was fitting for the church to have, and all the other materials; all this was collected over seven years, by the twelfth year of Justinian's reign.

The aforementioned church, built by the great Constantine, Justinian dismantled to its foundations, and he laid its materials nearby, having no need of them, since he had prepared many and countless materials that were superior to those.

Purchase 1. Justinian began to buy up the houses that were nearby there. The first was the house of a certain widow named Anna. This house was valued at two hundred and eighty litras of gold. The emperor sent his nobles to her, asking for her house, but he had no success at all.

Then the emperor himself came and asked the widow about the price of her house. She said to the emperor: "I do not wish to receive gold for my house, but in that Church of God which you wish to build, let me be buried near my house, and let me have my reward on the Day of Judgment." And the emperor promised her to bury her there and, after the church was built, to commemorate her. In the place of her house was situated the whole vessel-treasury, that is, the repository of church vessels.

Purchase 2. There was a certain Antiochus, a eunuch in the rank of ostiary, that is, doorkeeper, and his house was there too. This ostiary was very grieved and did not want to sell his house. The emperor was righteous and did not want to wrong anyone. And the emperor grieved and was saddened, pondering what to do. But the commander magister, keeper of the emperor's estate, promised the emperor to arrange this matter by some cunning.

This ostiary loved horse races and equestrian contests with all his heart; then the magister shut him up in prison. When the day of the equestrian contest came, the eunuch ostiary began to call out in the prison and say: "Bring me out, that I may see the equestrian contest and do the emperor's will."

And they brought the ostiary out of the prison to the place of the emperor's throne, where they paid homage to the emperor, and there they completed the sale of his house for thirty-eight litras of gold.
