O bitter victory, which I win over my own children!” And Herod turned to the judges with a request that they decide. And first Saturninus said: “The young men should be punished, but not killed. For I myself have three sons, and I would be wrong if I condemned other people’s children to death.” And many others spoke likewise—the brothers should be punished, but not killed. But Volumnius was the first to pronounce the dreadful sentence. And after him all condemned the young men to death: some because they wished to please Herod, others because they hated him and wanted him to remain childless, but none of those who condemned the young men to death was angry with them. And all Syria and Judea watched and waited to see how it would end. And no one supposed that Herod would be so cruel and base as to kill his own children. He, however, transferred his sons to the city of Tyre.

And from there Herod sailed to Caesarea, and there he considered by what means to execute his sons. And one soldier named Teron, who together with his son was on close friendly terms with the king and his sons, learned that the king wanted to execute them, and was terribly grieved, so that he fell ill in soul, and went about like a madman, crying: “Truth is trampled down, there is no truth anymore, nature is perverted, life is full of lawlessness! There is no soul in the king, his mind is deranged, his word is like wind, his speeches like morning dew!” And to this he added much else that a man may say in madness. And sparing not his body, not thinking of his life, Teron approached the king and said in anger: “Are you not the most wretched of all, O madman! You listen to slander against your sons, flesh of your flesh. I think you are abominable before God and men. To Pheroras and Salome, who have done much evil, you granted forgiveness, and now, believing their words, you destroy your children, the true heirs of your kingdom. But you leave Antipater, yet he will not be able to reign with severity. Think what hatred the soldiers will have for him—for there is none among them who does not pity the young men, and many of the commanders are angry and do not hide it.” Then Teron named them.

And immediately the king seized these commanders, together with Teron and his son. At that same time Tryphon also, who cut the king’s hair, in his foolishness said: “Teron gave me great gifts so that I would cut your throat with the scissors while cutting your hair.” Herod, learning of this, applied cruel tortures. Teron and his son endured them in silence, while the king’s barber said the same thing. And Herod ordered Teron to be tortured even more severely. But Teron’s son took pity on his father and said to Herod: “I will tell you everything if you pardon my father.” The king released Teron.
