Therefore he became hated and rejected by all; because of murder, bestiality, and vile intentions he called himself accursed, justly receiving retribution for his abomination and ending his foul and abominable life by a shameful death, as was said.

In his time the evangelist John the Theologian was imprisoned on the island of Patmos, and the apostles Timothy and Onesimus were martyred, as were Sebastian of the Thracian city of Sebaste, and Antipas, bishop of Amasea.

On Apollonius the Sorcerer

And Apollonius was renowned, going about cities and villages and working demonic wonders everywhere. Coming from Rome to Byzantium, he was entreated by its inhabitants and did the following: he drove away a multitude of snakes and scorpions from the city so that they would not harm people, and he restrained a mad horse before an assembly of nobles. Then he came also to Antioch, and they begged him there to do the same. Since the Antiochenes were exhausted by scorpions and mosquitoes, he made copper scorpions and, burying them in the ground and setting a small marble pillar over them, commanded the people to take rods and, going about the city, to cry out while shaking the rods: “No mosquitoes in the city!” And so the scorpions and mosquitoes vanished from the city. He was asked also about an earthquake threatening the city; sighing, he wrote on a tablet: “Woe to you, accursed city! For a great earthquake is coming, and you will be consumed by fire. But he who is by the bank of the Orontes will mourn for you.” The great Anastasius of the City of God also said of him: “The works created by Apollonius stand even to this day in certain places—some to ward off four-footed beasts and birds harmful to people, others to restrain river currents flowing violently; but some also stand for destruction and harm to people, intended for victory over enemies.” And not only during his life did demons work thus for his sake, but even after his death, remaining around his grave, they worked signs in his name to deceive unfortunate people, who because of this were seized by the devil. What then can be said of those who practice the works of sorcery? One of them was so strong in the deceit of sorcery that he was accustomed to mock Apollonius as not possessing true philosophical wisdom. He said that Apollonius ought, as he himself did, to accomplish by word alone what he wished, and not to fulfill his commands by action. All this happens by God’s providence and by demonic working, so that by such things our Orthodox faith may be tested—whether it is firm, whether it remains close to the Lord, and whether it is not drawn away by the enemy through fantasies, miracles, and satanic deeds performed by slaves and servants of evil.
