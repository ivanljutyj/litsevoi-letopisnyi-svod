With such words having instructed Arsenius, the pope and the emperor sent him with great glory and honor to the pious Emperor Theodosius. And the emperor and the pope wrote to Theodosius: "At your command we have sent a man who, by the grace of God, can teach your beloved children meekness, all wisdom, and virtue."

Arsenius, having given his sister to a convent, sailed in a ship to Constantinople.

And Emperor Theodosius received the letters and read them, and summoned Arsenius. Understanding and a habit of abstinence were visible in him; Arsenius had a handsome appearance, a submissive gaze, and a humble mind; he was wise in divine and human matters, and, simply put, Arsenius was adorned with every virtue. And the emperor rejoiced with great joy and said to him: "The understanding, conduct, and speech present in you show that virtues fill you and that you are pious, as the Roman emperor and the most holy pope wrote to us about this. So we see: they wrote the truth. Now you well know for what reason you were sent to us, but our majesty will also tell you personally that you were sent to us for the sake of our children, so that you may instruct them in all piety and the fear of God, and furthermore teach them all wisdom in perfection. Do not be timid, and do not doubt, and do not consider that these are our children, and furthermore do not treat them with royal honors, but as your own children and disciples, and if they sin, beat them."

And, having said this, Emperor Theodosius brought Arcadius and Honorius and handed them into the hands of Arsenius, and said: "Behold, from this day he is my and your father and teacher; obey him in everything as a father, and do not disobey him, for I have told him that if you depart from his instructions, or any of you sins before him, he should beat such a one as a disciple." And, having said this, Theodosius gave them in the inner royal chambers a triclinium, that is, a large hall, and commanded them to stay there. He planned this for them so that he might visit them often. And Theodosius surrounded Arsenius with great respect, and made him chief in the palace, so that there was no one in the palace who walked in more beautiful robes than Arsenius, and, simply put, Arsenius became a father to the emperor and one of his household.

Arsenius took Arcadius and Honorius and raised them piously, teaching them all wisdom, both divine and human. Arsenius was humble among the saints, and he did not treat the princes as disciples, but as emperors: he seated them on adorned thrones, while he himself stood before them and taught them.
