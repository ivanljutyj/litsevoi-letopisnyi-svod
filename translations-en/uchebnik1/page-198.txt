The Romans began to make the embankment on the twelfth day of the month Artemisios, that is May, and finished it with great labor on the nineteenth day, and set up the battering engines.

John, that is Johanan, made a tunnel from the city and reached the embankment underground, and laid dry wood with pitch and sulfur, and set it on fire, and returned. The wood caught fire, the earth softened, and with a great crash the embankments fell, together with the towers. At first smoke came out with dust, and the flame could not blaze because it was covered. But when the earth softened and began to cave in, the flame blazed up. The Romans were terrified, seeing sudden fire from the earth, and were greatly dismayed. Two days later sallies were also made with fire against the other embankments. Megassar of Adiabene and Hageir, which means the Lame, were at that time braver than all the Jews. As if to a wedding, they went into battle, and fought without delay, without fear, and passed through the midst of the enemy and set fire to the embankment and the war engines. They fought with them with spears and swords and struggled hand to hand, but did not retreat until the engines caught fire. The Romans tried to save the engines from the fire. The Jews hindered them from the wall, and some ran out and fought, sparing not their bodies, against those who were putting out the fire. The Romans dragged the engines out of the flames, while the Jews leaped through the flames and seized the hot iron and took the engines away from the Romans. The Romans, surrounded by flame, despaired of setting things right and thought only how to escape.

The Jews kept coming on, emboldened by their victory, and did not protect themselves and did not stoop.

And the Jews reached the Roman camp, up to the guards. For armed guard regiments stand constantly by the Roman camp, relieving one another. And there is a harsh law concerning these regiments: whoever leaves the guard post for any reason shall be executed. Therefore the guards chose a glorious death in battle instead of shameful execution and stood firm. And others, ashamed before those who remained, returned to help them. The Jews attacked without fear, sweeping away the Romans with their bodies.

And Titus came from the Tower of Antonia and sternly addressed his soldiers: “You have conquered the walls of foreigners, but why do you not think of your own? You have let the Jews out against you like prisoners from a prison.” And Titus gathered chosen horsemen and attacked the enemy from the flank. The Jews rushed at him and fought. Both armies met, and the dust did not let the eyes see, and the general shout did not let the ears hear. And it was impossible to recognize either one’s own or the enemy.
