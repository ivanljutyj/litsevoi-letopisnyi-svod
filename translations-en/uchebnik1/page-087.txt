He wanted to get up to attend his son’s execution, but illness and exhaustion would not allow it, for he could not take food, and to his former illness was added a tormenting cough. And life became so unbearable for him that he tried to forestall fate. He took an apple and asked for a knife, as though he meant, as usual, to cut and eat it, while he looked around to see that no one would hinder him, and raised his hand to strike himself in the heart. But Achiab, his nephew, ran up and seized him by the hand.

And there was a great cry in the palace, as though the king had died. Antipater heard it and rejoiced, and ordered the guards to unchain him, now threatening, now offering gifts. The guards came to the king and told him of this. Herod spoke with difficulty, but loudly, and immediately sent to kill Antipater, which was done. And Antipater was buried in Hyrcania. Herod wrote in his testament that Archelaus, his elder son, should reign after him.

Antipas, another son, Herod made tetrarch. After the murder of Antipater, Herod lived five days and died. He reigned by the command of Caesar thirty-seven years. Fortune favored Herod more than anyone else: he was not of noble origin, but by his boldness he gained the kingdom, enlarged it, and left it to his children.

Salome, before people learned of the king’s death, summoned the noble men imprisoned by Herod, whom the king had ordered killed, gave them gold, and released them to their lands. Then Salome summoned the rulers and soldiers. Ptolemy, a royal noble to whom the king had given his signet ring, stood among them, and Ptolemy blessed the king, praised and extolled him, comforted the people with a sorrowful and beautiful speech, and opened the royal testament and read it.

In the testament it was written: Philip was to possess the land of Trachonitis, Antipas was to be tetrarch, and Archelaus was to reign. Archelaus also received the ring and the right to bring the testament to Caesar, for Caesar remained ruler and lord of Judea. And then a joyful cry arose from all the friends of Archelaus.

Then they turned to the burial of the king. Archelaus wished to honor his father and ordered that all the royal splendor be taken and carried before the deceased. The couch on which Herod lay was all of pure gold and adorned with precious stones, the bedding was of various rich fabrics, the body was covered with royal purple, and on his head lay a golden crown adorned with precious stones and costly pearls, and into Herod’s right hand they placed the royal scepter.
