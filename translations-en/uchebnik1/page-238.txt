Then the Romans turned to the victory sacrifice; many oxen stood at the altar, and the emperor ordered them all to be slaughtered and given to the soldiers to eat. Titus himself feasted with his nobles for three days.

And then Titus sent the soldiers to villages and cities to rest, each where it had been ordered. The Tenth Legion the emperor entrusted with guarding Jerusalem, and he did not send it beyond the Euphrates, where this legion had previously been.

Titus also remembered that the Twelfth Legion had been defeated by the Jews when Cestius was commander of the Romans. And the emperor expelled this legion from the land of Syria altogether and sent it to the land called Melitene, near the river Euphrates, between the Armenian and Cappadocian lands.

But two legions, the Fifth and the Fifteenth, Titus took with him as far as Egypt. And afterward the emperor went with his soldiers to Caesarea Maritima and there halted to rest, and placed all the booty and the prisoners under guard, since the winter season did not permit him to go to Rome.

When Emperor Titus was still besieging Jerusalem, Vespasian entered a ship and sailed from Alexandria to Rhodes.

In Rhodes Vespasian took oared ships with three rows of oarsmen and sailed along all the coastal cities. The people received him with joy and blessings. And from Joppa the emperor sailed to Hellas, and from there to Corcyra, and then to Iapygia, which is in Italy. There Vespasian disembarked from the oared ships and, mounting horses, continued his journey.

Titus, however, went from Caesarea Maritima to another Caesarea, called Philippi, and remained there a long time. And he arranged various spectacles there, at which many of the captive Jews perished, some thrown to the beasts, others in combats with fellow captives, compelled to this by imperial command.

Then news came of the capture of Simon Niger. This Simon Niger was in the Upper City. When the Romans began to ravage Jerusalem and approached the inner city and took it, then Simon Niger gathered the most loyal soldiers, and stonecutters, and iron tools necessary for this work, and various foods sufficient for many days, and with all this entered an unseen underground passage.
