Theodosius separated Syria Secunda from Syria Prima and created an eparchy, and appointed a prince in it, and gave a metropolitan statute to the city of Apamea.

And Theodosius separated Cilicia Secunda from Cilicia Prima and gave the city of Nazarbus the rightful metropolitan statute, and appointed a prince in this eparchy.

Emperor Theodosius also created another eparchy, cutting it off from Bithynia, which he named Honorias after his uncle. And he gave it a metropolitan statute and appointed a prince in Heraclea, a city by Pontus.

On the Enmity against Empress Eudocia

Empress Eudocia sailed calmly on the sea of this life, borne by bright and gentle winds, but suddenly enmity breathed forth like a storm and shook the boat like a leaf. What misfortune befell her, this account will tell. When Emperor Theodosius lived with her, someone brought a beautiful and very large apple. Theodosius marveled at the fruit’s fair appearance, and was struck by its beauty and size, and gave the poor man who brought the apple 150 gold coins.

Theodosius sent the apple as a new gift to his wife, Empress Eudocia, and this apple became the beginning of great evils.

The empress marveled at the fruit’s appearance and sent it to the handsome Paulinus, the emperor’s friend, because she was fond of him, since he had helped her rise to the heights of imperial rule. And with this apple, bringing sorrow, which Eudocia had received from her husband, she honored Paulinus.

Paulinus sent the apple back to the emperor, not knowing that it had come from the emperor to the empress, for he had not happened to be with the emperor then. The emperor took the apple and hid it.

And Theodosius asked the empress, “Where is the apple?” She said, “I ate it,” and swore oaths. The emperor showed her the apple, and from that fell into evil thoughts, so that he turned his attention more closely to Paulinus, and soon Paulinus met death because of it and was killed by the sword.

When the empress learned of the emperor’s hatred, she asked leave of the emperor and departed for Jerusalem with great wealth.

Eudocia conversed with many fathers, and with the great Euthymius, and renewed many monasteries and churches, and the holy city Jerusalem, and rejoicing she said: “In me is fulfilled what David said: ‘Be gracious, O Lord, with Thy good pleasure unto Zion’—that is, ‘with Thy favour.’”

And after doing much good for the holy churches, Eudocia died there, and with oaths she declared there concerning Paulinus that he had been killed without guilt.
