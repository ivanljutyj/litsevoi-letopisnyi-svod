And again the godless Constantine put on the purple, and again the beast took up the scepters, and again gained dominion over the Greeks. And he was more ferocious than Nebuchadnezzar. What, then, is this luckless and inconstant wheel of life? The rosebud, that is, the rose, barely blooms and soon falls away, while the thorn-bearing plant, the hawthorn, has great strength. Titus the all-good saw only the flowers of power and did not partake of the vegetables, that is, the fruits, and was soon snatched away. But this Constantine the Dung-named, vile of lineage, was long-lived and reaped the fruits of power to satiety.

And Constantine went against the Bulgars with soldiers, having fitted out from all lands 1,600 ships, and sent them to their city of Anchialus.

But they rushed onto the shoals when the east wind blew, and all the ships were quickly, that is, speedily, wrecked, and countless people drowned.

When the Bulgars learned of this, they entered into war with Constantine and fiercely cut down a multitude of his troops; and Constantine returned with great shame and defeat.

On the Frost.

At that same time there was a great and fierce winter, so that from the midnight, that is, northern, side of the Euxine Sea, for 100 versts from the shore, the deep froze like stone to a depth of 30 cubits, and snow fell upon it to a depth of 20 cubits. And the sea became level with the land, and people and livestock walked over it, and wild beasts walked with people without harm.

In the month of February, by God's command, such ice broke into many floes, that is, pieces of ice, and these floes, like mountains, were driven by the force of the wind to Daphnousia and Hieria, suburbs of Constantinople. Then along the Soud, that is, the Golden Horn bay, these floes, these masses of ice, floated to Constantinople, and from there as far as the island of Abydos they filled the whole sea, bearing various animals frozen into the ice, tame, that is, domestic, and wild, which floated to the city. From Chrysopolis, that is, from the other side of the Bosporus strait, all walked as on dry land as far as the Church of Saint Mamas.
