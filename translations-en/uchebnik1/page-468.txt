A Word of Saint Gregory, Pope of Rome, Concerning the Dead. And again Gregory, the glorious Pope of Rome, also spoke a word to Peter: “That the souls of the saints dwell in Heaven, we believe wholly, and that the souls of sinners are in hell, we confess without ceasing. And just as the blessedness of the saints in Heaven gladdens and always fills with fragrance, so it must be believed that sorrow, cramped dwelling, and grief always hold those in hell, and that the furnace flame scorches and burns their souls, as it was with that rich man in the story of Lazarus. And yet each has a sense of some certainty regarding the Judgment, knowing what will be with him for what he has done.”

The Twenty-third Reign, of Heraclonas, who reigned in Constantinople

After Constantine, the son of Heraclius, Heraclonas reigned with his mother named Martina, for four months.

And this Heraclonas brought to Hagia Sophia the royal crown, which he had taken from his father’s tomb. Heraclonas also gave each soldier a golden gift, three gold coins, on account of his brother Constantine.

But the nobles rejected this Heraclonas, cutting off his nose. They also cut out the tongue of his mother Martina, and exiled both, that is, banished them.

They raised to the throne Constans, the son of Constantine, the grandson of Heraclius. This Heraclonas was not buried as a king, but was expelled from the kingdom.
