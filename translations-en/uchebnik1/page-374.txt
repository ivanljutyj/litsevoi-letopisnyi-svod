On the Resurrection of the Seven Youths Who Were in Ephesus

In the reign of this Emperor Theodosius the Younger there was a heresy that there would be no resurrection of the dead. The emperor, seeing that the church was troubled, sat in sackcloth and prayed to God.

God wished to show the Resurrection to those who did not know of it, and seven youths who were in Ephesus arose: Maximilian, Constantine, Jamblichus, Martinian, Antoninus, Dionysius, John. They awoke as if from a long sleep, these youths who had shown fortitude in torments; 300 years and 72 years had passed since these youths fled from the frenzy of the impious Emperor Decius, entered a cave, and were shut in, for by Decius’s command the mouth of the cave had been blocked.

Under Emperor Theodosius the youths arose on the fourth day of August and lived until the twenty-second of October, and were honored by the emperor and all the faithful, and again fell asleep in a long and perfect sleep.

On the Earthquake

At this Emperor Theodosius there was a great earthquake in Constantinople, so that the city walls fell, and many of the citizens fled to various places, and the earthquake lasted three months.

The emperor, Patriarch Proclus, and all the people went around the city with honorable crosses and made supplications for many days. At that time the faithful, at the enemy’s instigation, added to the Trisagion: “Holy Immortal, who was crucified, O God, have mercy on us.”

When they were walking and praying, suddenly a child was caught up from the midst of the people into the air and, after much time, was brought down to the earth again.

And the child said: “The angels who are in Heaven sing the Trisagion without any addition: ‘Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.’” Having sung this, the child gave up its soul to God. And the tremors of the earthquake ceased.

At this Emperor Theodosius there was Sabbas the Hermit from the land of Cappadocia, son of John and of his mother Sophia, and Austocles, and Savda; Atticus, bishop of Constantinople, instructed a certain Jew and gloriously brought him out of the font healthy.
