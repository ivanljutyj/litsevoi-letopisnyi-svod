And thereafter his parricidal seed penetrated our unhappy race and sprouted, and brought forth man-slaying fruits. And only the long-suffering Lord knows whence this evil came into the world, and how his customs and deeds were nourished and grew up together with the evil destroyer. For, having absorbed the vital strength of the Assyrian lioness and the Armenian leopardess, there grew up something beastlike, sprung from lawless and vile parents, from Armenian and Assyrian abomination. And he was named Leo, and like a lion he crushed his enemies, but he was not free from the influence of others. His intimates, a tribe many-faced in its cunning and shiftiness, entangled the weak-willed man with wordy flattery, weaving cunning intrigues and skillfully concealing their true intentions, so as again and again to lean upon the lion’s firmness. And his sin, even if he himself had devised it all, was still not so heavy as the sin of those through whose mouths, merely instructing him foolishly, he was taught. And with a clamour his life perished, like a wretched and insane demonic frenzy, in all things vile and deceitful. When, by God’s permission, he seized royal power, he brought all his fury and cunning to bear against the immaculate faith, following the earlier impious Copronymus. Like him, he trusted all his life in foul and evil men who had won for themselves a reputation by a corrupt and impious life, sorcerers and poisoners destroying soul and body—or, more precisely, workers of every kind of evil and violence, who had renounced Christian piety. He, held captive by sinful captivity, welcomed them kindly and readily drew close to them; and, obeying their vain and soul-destroying apostate teachings, caught on the bait of temptations and seduced by lawlessness, he desired lawlessness and evil. Thus for the third time there occurred an apostasy from our true Orthodox faith, and the destruction of holy icons began, and persecution of those walking in the path of God’s commandments, whereas only he who loves a pious life will obtain a pure and tranquil life for many years. But he, seduced by pernicious temptations, destroyed his own soul—this irreverent, feeble-minded man, with a beastlike, vile appearance (not for nothing did he bear the lion’s name). Depraved in character, proud, unrestrained in his desires, ungovernable and impatient, like a fierce beast, a tormentor. Like wings, this wretch stretched evil and lawlessness over his kingdom, sowing discord around himself. He began by opening his impious mouth against our holy law.

With various torments he mercilessly tortured the defenders of the holy icons, raising persecution against the confessors of Christ.
