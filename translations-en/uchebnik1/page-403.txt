On the Earthquake

When there was fire in Neocaesarea, the greater part of the city collapsed, and a certain man heard a Voice saying: “Save the house of Gregory the Wonderworker.” And the church was saved. Gregory the Wonderworker, whose memory is on the 17th day of November, was bishop in that city.

Under this tsar, from the copper statues set up along the roads, which Tsar Constantine, for their beauty, had brought from other cities to adorn his city, John made a large statue of Tsar Anastasius and set it on the great column that stood empty in the Forum of the Bull. On this column there had formerly been a statue of Theodosius the Great, which fell during earthquakes. Anastasius introduced the so-called follis, that is, the copper coin, and from that time it began to circulate throughout the Greek land.

At the same time the tsar commanded and issued a decree that no one, without having made a written declaration, should have either a son or a daughter, but should make a written declaration and thus may have a son or daughter, and so make his son or daughter a sharer in his property.

On the Righteous Ban on Collecting Gold

This Tsar Anastasius, although he was vile and impious, nevertheless uprooted an evil plantation throughout the Greek land, whose fruit is bitter and whose root is death.

So if Anastasius had not had within him the dark cloud of heresy, which covers every good thing and makes it unknown, this one good deed that he did could have covered all his evil deeds and healed the sinful sores. What was this evil that was stopped? Every man, and woman, and children, and maidens, and slave, and free, and those in obedience, and those not in obedience, for the passage of foul-smelling filth and for the water supply, all gave one silver dinar into the tsar’s treasury. This burden also lay on beasts: oxen, mules, and horses. For every dog and for every ass’s foal the owner gave six folles, for each animal.

Anastasius, however, was entreated by men who led the monastic life, and he drove away such a burden, and uprooted such abomination, and tore it out, and gave it to fire for destruction.
