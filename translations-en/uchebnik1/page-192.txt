Great fortune accompanies the Romans, and God has taken dominion from all peoples and now transferred it to Italy. The law has been established for both beasts and men, that the weak submit to the stronger.

And all submit to those whose weapon is sharper. And our ancestors, who were better than us in souls, bodies, and wealth, would not have endured Roman slavery if they had not known that God helps the Romans. Whom do you trust, then, when you resist them? The most fortified and largest part of the city has been taken. You live in the remaining part worse than captives. It has not been hidden from the Romans that famine is among you, from which the people now perish, and little by little you too will die. If the Romans wish, they will not delay in taking the city. Therefore it is fitting for you to resort to salvation before irreparable evil overtakes you. The Romans will not remember your malice if you do not wish to carry your rebellion to the end. For by nature the Romans are gentle to those who submit to them; they do not wish to exhaust the city or to lay waste the people or the land. Therefore the emperor through me promises you salvation. But if you do not obey, and he takes you by arms, he will spare none of you.” So Josephus spoke, and many from the walls mocked him, many reviled him, and some threw stones at him and shot arrows. But Josephus could not incline the rebels to submission by these words. And he began to speak to them from ancestral history, and cried to them: “O unfortunate ones! Do you trust in hands or in weapons when you resist the Romans? What other enemies have you overcome by these? Or when did God not avenge those who did violence to the Jews? Return to where you went in your war, and see with whom you fight, and what Champion you have defiled. Remember the wondrous deeds of our fathers. What great armies destroyed this place! I am horrified when I speak of divine deeds to those who are unworthy to hear this. However, listen and know that you resist not only the Romans but also God. Nechao, king of Egypt, who was also called Pharaoh, came out with a great army and seized Sarah from her husband, the queen and foremother of our race. Did her husband Abraham, our forefather, take up arms to avenge his offender? Know that he had 318 commanders, and each of them had countless soldiers. Abraham left all those and called God as his helper. And he raised his venerable hands toward this place, which you have now defiled, and called the invincible Helper to war. And in one night undefiled Sarah was sent back to her husband.
