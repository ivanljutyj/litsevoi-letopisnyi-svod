The Jews overpowered the Roman soldiers, since they fought from the ninth hour of the night until the seventh hour of the day, and fought all together, understanding the danger of the city’s complete capture. The Romans were not all there, but a small part of the army, for not all had entered. And the troops ceased fighting. The Romans were content even with having occupied the Antonia Tower.

On the Courage of Julian the Centurion

There was a certain Julian, a centurion from Bithynia, a famous man, with whom I became acquainted at that time. He surpassed all in strength, in skill with weapons, in bodily power, and in qualities of soul. Julian saw the Romans retreating, and he himself, standing near Titus in the Antonia Tower, asked leave of him and leaped onto his horse. The Jews were already prevailing, but this Julian alone rode against them and defeated them, and drove them to the corner of the inner sanctuary. All the Jews fled together, thinking that the power in him was not human. He rode among them, cutting them down and overtaking them; even the emperor marveled at him, and all the others were terrified. But later even this Julian was overtaken by fate, which no mortal can escape.

When his horse was stabbed, this Julian fought on foot as if he were riding a horse, like a spirit. He stepped on many sharp fragments, and went along the stone pavement, and slipped, and fell on his back, and his weapons clanged. Immediately those who had fled from him turned upon him. The Romans in the Antonia Tower cried out in fear for him. The Jews surrounded him all together, pierced him with spears, cut him with axes, pelted him with stones, and beat him with clubs, and by this alone they struck fear into all the Romans. Julian wanted many times to rise, but was surrounded and could not. Yet he stabbed many of those who attacked him. And they could not kill him quickly, since he was protected by a helmet on his head and armor over his whole body. Only when they had cut off his limbs, and no one helped him, did he fall. The emperor greatly lamented him, seeing such a valiant man cut down before his eyes. But the distance of the place where Titus was did not allow him to avenge Julian on the Jews at that moment. This centurion left scarcely any of the enemies standing around him unharmed, and then met a cruel death. With great difficulty the Jews cut off his head. And Julian became very famous not only among the Romans but also among foreigners.
