One of them in outline resembled a beheaded man, and beside it was, as it were, his severed head. This vision was a portent of the division among Christians that soon occurred during the revolt of Thomas the Slav. There were also violent earthquakes, frequent famine, severe drought, and internecine strife throughout all towns and villages. All this began with the accession of the lawless foreigner. Many misfortunes befell then, calamities and diseases, because he became like the founder of the iconoclast heresy. Same-named and like-minded with his predecessor, he became a follower of his impiety as well.

Then Leo, together with his counsellors and learned men, began to seek a theologian capable of speaking against the veneration of icons, and at last found John, called the Scribe—or rather, a new Annius or Simon—known to all for his evil deeds, practising sorcery and boiling magical potions in copper vessels, and also his disciples, other Jannites, Ambrites, and Simonites, filled with the same wicked belief.

Then the emperor sends for Patriarch Nikephoros, wishing to have in him an accomplice to his madness.

The divine Patriarch Nikephoros, taking with him many bishops—Theophylact of Nicomedia, Michael of Synnada, Euthymius of Sardis, Emilian of Cyzicus, Joseph of Thessalonica, and his brother Theodore the Studite—came with them into the imperial palace. The emperor, addressing them before the assembled nobles, said: “Do you know that certain men have appeared and say that it is not proper to venerate icons? If you wish, they will appear before you, and let the truth be revealed in your disputation.” The most holy Nikephoros, making no answer to the emperor, turned to the nobles present: “Tell me, can that which does not exist fall?” They could not find what to answer and remained silent in perplexity. Then the patriarch continued: “Did the holy icons fall in former times, under Leontius the Isaurian and Constantine Copronymus, or not?” At this they nodded their heads in agreement and answered, “Yes.” “Do not revile their perfect original Image. For how can that which does not exist ‘be’ or ‘fall’?” Then all the bishops too condemned the heresy.

Saint Euthymius of Sardis said with boldness: “Listen, O emperor, to what is said in simplicity of heart! For since Jesus Christ came to earth and until now, behold already 800 years, everywhere in frescoes and on icons the image of Christ is depicted, and the faithful worship Him. And what blasphemer will dare, after so many years, to reject or change the apostolic and patristic traditions? The apostle also says: ‘Therefore, brethren, stand fast and hold the traditions which you have received, whether by word or by epistle, or if an angel from heaven should preach to you anything beyond what…’”
