About him the all-knowing Eusebius says: “Here the prophecy was clearly fulfilled: ‘The weapons of Pharaoh and all his host He cast into the sea; the chosen horsemen, three hundred, that is, those who hold three spears in their hand before the king, afterward the deep covered them in the Red Sea.’” So too Maxentius, and with him his armor-bearers and spearmen, drowned in the depths. And when he was pressed back to the river by the power given to Constantine from God, he was caught by his own cunning, and he brought upon himself this destructive cunning, which he had devised. Concerning him the prophetic saying was spoken: “He dug a pit and fell into the hole he had made; and his trouble shall return upon his head, and his iniquity shall come down upon his crown.” For Maxentius, the wretched tormentor, precisely such an end was prepared, and all those who were with him also drowned in the water.

The divine Constantine, having sung the works of God, approached Rome with his army amid many victories and without resistance. The Roman inhabitants—both women and infants, the whole Roman people—with eyes shining with great and ineffable joy and having adorned their city in their souls, met with exultation the precious Cross as victor, deliverer, and savior, and also the great and glorious holy Tsar Constantine.

He, like a garden of piety that he had acquired toward God, was not in the least shaken by the rejoicings, nor did he grow proud from the praises, well understanding that he had help from God. Soon he ordered the image of the Cross, saved by his victorious hand, to be set up as an icon. And from then on, whenever his enemies happened to rebel, they held power only a short time, but soon suffered the torments of the condemnation appointed for them. And the pagans built another entire temple. About it the divine Eusebius recounts, saying: “The sinners have drawn the sword, they have bent their bow to shoot the poor and needy, and to slay the upright in heart. But their sword shall enter into their own hearts, and their bows shall be broken.” In the church of the pious he spoke the Holy Word, saying: “Now do not fear, for you have been condemned; and do not be ashamed, for you have been brought low; for you shall forget eternal shame, and you shall not remember the reproach of your virginity. Lift yourself up, clothe yourself in strength for My glory, shake off fear and arise.”

On the Baptism of Tsar Constantine

This happened in the seventh year of Constantine’s reign. Since until that time the blessed Constantine did not know of the existence of divine baptism, a fearful sign was given to him. It happened that he fell ill, having fallen into elephantine leprosy, and covered all over his body with sores and scabs, he lay scarcely breathing.
