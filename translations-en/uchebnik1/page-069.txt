Archelaus himself, moreover, was still angry with Alexander, his son-in-law, the son of Herod, and threatened to take away his daughter and bring her home. Then Herod began to entreat Archelaus to forgive the young man. But Archelaus cried out all the more: “To any other man of your family, O Herod, whomever you command, I will betroth her, since I do not wish to break the kinship bond with you; but I do not wish to have Alexander as a son-in-law.” Herod answered him: “My son will be a gift to me from you if you do not dissolve this marriage, for they already have children, and the young man loves his wife; and if she lives with him, she will keep him from sins, but if you separate them, my son will despair. The turmoil of youth subsides in a man when he labors and thinks of his old age.” Archelaus did not immediately obey Herod and make peace with his son-in-law Alexander. Thus Archelaus attained his true goal—he reconciled the young man with his father, Alexander with Herod. And Archelaus told Herod to send Alexander to Rome to Caesar for an explanation, since Herod had already written to Caesar about everything that had happened. In this way Archelaus wisely achieved peace and saved his son-in-law. And then they began to feast and make merry.

When Archelaus went to his own land, Cappadocia, Herod honored him with great gifts: he gave him 70 talents of gold, gave him also a golden throne adorned with precious stones, and gave him eunuch servants, and presented him with his concubine, whose name was Pannychis. And Herod honored Archelaus’s close associates with gifts, each according to his worth. Also all the relatives of King Herod honored Archelaus with valuable gifts, by Herod’s command. And Herod, with his nobles, escorted Archelaus as far as Antioch.

A little time passed, and there came a man skilled in cunning, named Eurycles, from Laconia. This Eurycles destroyed the reconciliation of King Herod with his son Alexander that Archelaus had achieved, and ruined Alexander. Eurycles did this in order to get much gold. He came to Herod and brought him rich and varied gifts; by this he wished to earn honor for himself with Herod and to receive rich gifts in return from him; and as Eurycles planned, so it was. And Eurycles took away the king’s reason, acting with flattery, and by the force of his eloquence, and with deceptive praise; he did and said everything that Herod loved, and entered the number of his close associates.

This Eurycles learned of the discord in Herod’s family, of the enmity between the brothers, and of whom the father loved.
