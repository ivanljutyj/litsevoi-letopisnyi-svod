On the left, that is, on the left side, there adjoined a furnace, whose flame rose up to the clouds. This rich man, now dead, stood bound between Paradise and that terrible furnace, and often looked toward Paradise. When he groaned and wept, he saw a certain radiant angel come near to him and say: "Why do you groan in vain, O man? Behold, for the sake of your alms you are delivered from torment, but because you did not turn away from unclean and foul fornication, you are deprived of Paradise."

The patriarch and all the bishops with him heard this, and were seized with great fear, and said to one another: "Truly and rightly did the apostle write, saying: 'Flee fornication, for every sin that a man commits is outside the body, but he who commits fornication sins against his own body.' For God said: 'I am holy'; 'You shall not commit fornication, nor adultery'; and 'There shall be no fornication among the daughters of Israel, and no one committing lewdness among the sons of Israel.' Where then are those who say: 'Even if we commit fornication, yet we also give alms, we shall be saved?' If you are merciful and righteous, then it is fitting first to show mercy and to attest bodily amendment. For without this no one shall see God. For silver given from an unclean hand and from an unrepentant and foul soul, corrupted by unclean deeds, brings no profit at all."

About the Monk Whom Gregory, Pope of Rome, Bound, and Then Sent a Deacon to His Grave, and Loosed

There was in Great Rome the following. A certain monk was cast out by Gregory, Pope of Rome, because he had acted without the command of his abbot and not according to the custom of that monastery. After some days this brother died.

Hearing this, the pope was greatly grieved that the brother had departed suddenly and remained bound by him. And the pope wrote on a parchment a prayer loosing the deceased from the ban, and gave it to a certain deacon, and said: "Go to that brother's grave and read it." And when this had happened, the abbot saw that brother in a dream and said to him: "I grieve for you, that you died bound by the pope. Tell us how you fare there." He answered and said: "Truly, father, I was in prison even until yesterday. Today I was loosed by God." The abbot told this privately to the pope. And all learned that at the very hour when the deacon read the prayer over the grave, that brother was loosed from his bonds. And all who heard this gave glory to God, who gave such power to His servants. Therefore the great Chrysostom said: "We, according to Church custom, impose bonds. If anyone does not concern himself about these bonds, then the time of death and judgment will come upon him and will teach him that the word of the Lord and the law of His Church are not false. Yet I am not guilty in this, for I have done my own work; but he rejected the divine yoke and broke the holy bonds, and he will answer for it."
