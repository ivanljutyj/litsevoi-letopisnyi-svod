The 37th Reign, of Tacitus, who became emperor in Rome in the 5784th year from Adam (the 284th year from Christ)

After Aurelian, Tacitus reigned 2 years. He appointed Maximian, his relative, to Assyria; the soldiers killed him because of the injustices he committed. Fearing that Tacitus would avenge him, they killed him too.

The 38th Reign, of Probus, who became emperor in Rome in the 5786th year from Adam (the 286th year from Christ)

After Tacitus, Probus and Florian reigned 2 years and 4 months. This same Probus lost his mind and killed Florian.

In his time, during rain much wheat mixed with water fell; gathering it, they filled huge granaries. Likewise under Aurelian silver grains fell down. It was told that Victorinus, a friend of Emperor Probus, obtained for his friend a princely title in Britain. He, having come there, rebelled, and Victorinus, having fallen out of favor because of this, was sent to suppress the revolt. Pretending to be hiding from the emperor’s wrath and to have fallen into disgrace, Victorinus killed him.

Returning, while disembarking from the ship he dropped his belt into the sea and entered to the emperor without a belt. The emperor became angry, thinking that this had happened to him through someone else. Victorinus said: “I did it myself,” and asked that no possessions ever be given into his charge, since all power is bound up with misfortune and guilt.
