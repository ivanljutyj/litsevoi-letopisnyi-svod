And from the founding of the most rich city of Constantine, from the month of May, that is Artemisios, on the 11th day, to the aforementioned second consulship of this divine Justinian, to the end of the seventh indiction, there are 199 years, neither less nor more. The years of the ancient kings cannot be counted according to the previously written number of years of their reigns, since two kings reigned together, and fathers also crowned their children as kings while still in childhood and reigned with their children. And others reigned in Rome. The chronicler must record all the years, as many as there are, during which a king reigned, from the time he was proclaimed king. It is good for the reader of chronicles to observe the number of years as they pass, and not only from the previously described reigns.

About Emperor Justinian, how he reigned in Rome

After the reign of Justin, Justinian alone reigned in Rome. From that same time he was named emperor. This was on the 4th day of the month Xanthikos, that is April, in the 5th indiction. The year was, according to the Antiochene chronology, the 75th, in the consulship of Mavortius. Justinian was short in body, "in speech he inclined toward the Roman tongue, and he wrote without fault." Justinian gave consular games in the 6th indiction, and he poured out and distributed so much gold as none of the emperors who had been consuls. In that same year the Persians fought with Tsaphios, king of the Lazi, because he had joined the Greeks.

And the Lazi asked Emperor Justinian for help, and he sent them great aid and three commanders: Belisarius, Cyricus, and Irenaeus; and many of the Persians were killed then.

This same Emperor Justinian renewed the city in the Sophanene district, in Armenia, called Martyropolis, and made new walls for the city in place of the old ruined ones.

In the reign of this Justinian, the Herulian king named Grethis joined the Greeks and came to the city of Constantine with his soldiers. And Grethis bowed to Emperor Justinian and asked to become a Christian, and was baptized on the holy Epiphany, and Emperor Justinian himself raised him from the cross, that is, received him from the font.

His nobles and two children of his brother were baptized with Grethis. And having given many gifts to the Herulian king, Emperor Justinian dismissed him.

Emperor Zeno trusted the eparch Plato, and gave him the rank of magister, and appointed him commander in the East, giving him every authority to do what he wished, and made him dux.
