Infants he sacrificed by unlawful sorcery, and many other evil and shameful plots and incantations he wrought by enchantments, the impious one.

But the benevolent and magnanimous Constantine called upon his God and Father for help, looking to heaven and beseeching Him to manifest Himself as He had already done, and to extend a helping hand in what lay ahead, which indeed came to pass. He stationed himself with his soldiers in a certain field and, entreating, prayed without ceasing.

The Sign of the Cross. At last the Sign of God appeared to him at the noon hour, and rays issued from it, flashing more brightly than the sun, revealing in the sky the image of the Cross, and according to Roman written testimonies, a starry mirage. And a voice came from heaven, saying: “Constantine, conquer by this.” The emperor, perplexed at what had happened, turned to those standing near him, asking whether they had seen anything. And all the army confirmed the vision that had occurred.

When the troubled night came, Christ appeared to Constantine at the place where he was already sleeping, with the Sign he had seen, and said: “Make a likeness of the Sign that appeared to you, and with it go forth before your army; not only will you take all opponents under your hand, but you will also conquer all your enemies.”

On that same day, having reflected and confirmed his purpose and become fully resolved, he arose and told his friends about the vision. And then, summoning a goldsmith, he ordered him to make an image of the Sign of the Cross from gold and precious stones. It was fashioned thus: fixed atop a high spear, made of gold, and in the middle it had a wreath with an inscription and a chrismon with the monogram of the Lord’s name; from the upper end of the shaft hung banners adorned with gold. For the God-loving emperor he made a golden image to wear on his breast, and his soldiers had the same. This image of the saving Sign that appeared from heaven, made by the jeweler’s skill, still lies in the imperial treasuries, preserved as a great gift that resists every hostile military force. And having made the image, he ordered it fixed on a high spear and carried before the soldiers.

The Battle. Then both armies joined battle and mingled, and Maxentius was defeated, and all his military force was crushed, as well as the troops stationed at a distance, the greater part of whom were cut down by swords.

Maxentius fled with the rest and, pursued by the pious emperor, was caught in his own cunning, for the bridge was destroyed by God’s power, and he himself, the wretched one, was the first to plunge into the river in his armor and with his spear, as Pharaoh of old with all his army, so that the river was filled up to the bridge called Fulvius. And together with the bridge, horsemen with spears and all their armor drowned, as he had preferred.
