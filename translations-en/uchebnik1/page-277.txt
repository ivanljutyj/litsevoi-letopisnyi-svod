From all the accounts they informed the tsar about Helen, and how the image and likeness of him born of her was revealed at the place called Serpne—an infant radiant with a royal appearance. Hearing this, Consta, at first astonished by what the elders had told him, proceeded, as was natural, to ask about Helen, wishing to see her. He also remembered the vision he had seen concerning Helen: a dark night illuminated by sunbeams.

Understanding that the vision had come true and learning its fulfillment, he received the messengers worthily and, honoring them with gifts, sent them to the Persian land with a carriage and many wagons, with directions for many comforts and with gifts sent by the tsar, to bring Helen and the youth Constantine.

They, having soon arrived, took Helen with the youth and seated her in the royal carriage, and brought her to Tsar Consta. Seeing them, Consta rejoiced greatly at both, embraced the youth, kissing him, loved the young woman too, and brought them inside the royal palace.

Instructed by his father in the holy books, the wise Saint Constantine had all knowledge; from his youth he had a royal mind and piety, and the wise virtue of manliness.

Rumor of the remarkable event with Helen spread, and this report reached the lawful wife of Consta, named Theodora, the own daughter of Herculius. By her he had three sons and a daughter; their names were Constantius, Annibalianus, Dalmatius, and the daughter Constantia, the wife of Licinius. None of them, however, was worthy of his father’s kingdom.

Consta feared lest out of jealousy they might treacherously kill the young woman with her son, and he prudently sent the youth with letters to Tsar Diocletian and to his son-in-law Maximian, who were then living in Bithynia, in the city of Nicomedia. Having received the tsar’s son at the royal court, they assigned to him a detachment of imperial guards, since he was a royal offspring, that is, a pledge of peace, and he grew up in the palace.

Consta feared the like also concerning Helen, lest his wife Theodora devise something against her, as he had already said before. When the youth Constantine grew up, he was taught Greek learning. But the demon that hates good raised up against him envious idolaters from among the sorcerers and soothsayers living under Diocletian and Maximian Herculius. And foreseeing that he would become not only emperor but also a pious tsar and a destroyer and ruiner of the Hellenic heresy, and also a great preacher of God’s kingdom of Christ, for this reason they devised manifold and all kinds of intrigues against him in order to kill him.
