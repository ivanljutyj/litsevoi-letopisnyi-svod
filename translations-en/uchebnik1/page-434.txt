And a smith came from the west to Constantinople, having a field dog, that is, one yellowish-white and blind. And the smith took from people gold rings and gold coins, that is, golden money, and buried them in the ground, and ordered the dog to dig up the rings and gold coins, and to take them with its mouth, and to give each person his item. And the fornicators and adulterers, the merciful and unmerciful, the evil and the good—this dog discerned them all and showed what each one was like. And it was said of this dog that it had a spirit of sorcery and magic.

And there was a change concerning Easter. Many of the people made a meat-fast, that is, ceased eating meat during Great Lent, before the emperor. The emperor ordered meat to be sold for one more week. The sellers slaughtered cattle and sold meat, and no one bought, for the people no longer ate meat. Easter was kept when the emperor ordered. And it came about that the people fasted one extra week.

On Sodomite Fornication. At that time certain ones were seized, committing fornication with the male sex. The emperor issued a decree that all found in such guilt—some should have their final member cut off, that is, the secret member, and others, after sharpening a reed, should have it thrust into the secret member, into their shameful parts, and also be naked in the square as a mockery. Then many of the nobles were found in that guilt, and not a few of the hierarchs were among them. And so they were led about, and they died cruelly. And there was great fear. And the rest were made wise by what had happened. “For the cypress weeps when the cedar falls.” This means: the fall of the strong and great serves as instruction for the weak and simple. The wise man also says: “If the ungodly are destroyed, the righteous will be frightened,” “if one contends with scoundrels, those lacking understanding will be taught.” That foul demonic unnatural sin the Sodomites committed of old, so that a great cry was raised against the ungodly, and this wailing ascended into the ears of the Lord of Hosts, and much had been spoken before that, and God endured to the last; but because of their inflexible impenitence and their untiring foolish will, at last God in justice brought punishment upon them.
