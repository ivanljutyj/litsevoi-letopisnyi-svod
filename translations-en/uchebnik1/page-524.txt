And the tsar decided to tell Theodotus Melissenus, whom he trusted, about this and to consult with him. The latter had long been a secret iconoclast. Hearing the tsar’s account, Theodotus answered him craftily (since that monk-iconoclast was his friend) and said to the tsar: “Go and consult the monk of whom you speak, and do as he advises you, for this man is holy and by God’s grace sees the future.” And seeing that the tsar had heeded his advice, Theodotus went and taught that monk what answer he should give the tsar. Tsar Leo decided to go with Theodotus alone, without retinue, clothed in rags, so as not to be recognized as the tsar. The monk, having learned from Theodotus that the tsar was coming dressed in old clothes, wished to appear to him as a seer and said to him: “You cannot hide from me behind this poor garment, for it has been revealed to me that you are the tsar of the Romans. But know that you have erred in your piety, since you venerate icons, which are idols. But if you wish to establish your kingdom in prosperity, command that the icons be removed from the church. If you do not do this, know that soon you will lose the kingdom and life itself, and will condemn yourself to eternal torment.” Tsar Leo marveled at his foresight and, listening with delight to that accursed monk, accepted his deception with all his heart, not knowing that he was “prophesying” at Theodotus’s instigation.

And again Leo revives the fierce root of the former iconoclast thorn, and it puts forth shoots of a thorn even fiercer than before, choking and suppressing the golden fruit of piety. And again the serpent’s tail stirred—I speak of the abomination of iconoclasm—and as if countless serpents crawled forth, terribly opening their mouths and hissing, to swallow the Church of God like an unfledged chick in its nest. For by the command of the impious Tsar Leo, icons of Christ and His saints were everywhere torn down and smeared with pitch, the refuges of the Orthodox who hid in caves and in houses were sought out and destroyed; both monks and all who continued to venerate the holy icons were tortured and persecuted. Holy Zion wept; the Church of Christ, the daughter of God, shed bitter tears, exchanging the bride’s raiment for the mourning garments of widowhood. Then the heavenly host of stars also grieved inconsolably and looked upon the earth with darkened eyes.

When the most holy Patriarch Nicephorus was crowning this fierce beast as tsar and placing the royal crown on his head, a thorn pierced his hand, since the crown proved to be made of thorns, from which the patriarch foreknew the impiety of Tsar Leo.
