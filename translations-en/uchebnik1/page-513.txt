After Pope Benedict, the next bishop of Rome was Nicholas, and after him, in the reign of Basil the Macedonian, the Roman see was occupied by Photius.

The third pope was Adrian, the fourth John, the fifth Marinus, the sixth Adrian, the seventh Stephen. All of them were pious and confessed Orthodoxy, like the four Eastern patriarchs.

About Pope Formosus

In the reign of Leo the Wise, son of Basil the Macedonian, the pope in Rome was the impious Formosus, who secretly adhered to the heresy received from the heretics of Charles. Pious people, however, elected him pope, not knowing of his heresy.

He hypocritically wrote a confession of the Orthodox faith and sent it to the four patriarchs.

And when the impious Formosus was elevated to the Roman throne, he began to teach heresies to the people of God. And so all the Latins, themselves unaware, accepted the heretical teaching and thereby cut themselves off from the Greek kingdom and from the four patriarchs.

After Formosus, who became the progenitor of the heresy of Latinism, the second was Pope Boniface, then the Roman see was occupied by Stephen, Romanus, John, Benedict, [Leo, Christopher]. All of them were of one mind with Formosus.

On the Curse of the Latins

In the year 6552 from Adam (1052 from Christ), Sergius was Patriarch of Constantinople. With the consent of the three Eastern patriarchs, he exhorted the Pope of Rome, Christopher, to return to the former Orthodox confession of faith. But he, confirmed in his heresy, did not wish to turn back, but separated from the Greek kingdom and from the four patriarchs and became the sole pontiff of Rome, and an enemy to all Orthodox Christians.

Then Patriarch Sergius and the other patriarchs not only ceased to commemorate the Pope of Rome as the first of bishops, but deposed him from his rank and delivered him to anathema.
