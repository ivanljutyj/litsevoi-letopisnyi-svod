It becomes corruptible, so that it may nourish fruits and nourish bodies. And what can be said of their common usefulness, even during sea voyages, when they, knowing their appointed purposes, replace one another, circling in the midst of the deep and showing the way to ships that sail? One releases and another receives, journeying by opposite paths, and their conflict serves us. And one could name an infinite number of works that the winds perform. But the prophet omitted all that is superfluous, leaving the listener to choose for himself, and showed only one of the conveniences of creation. And what is said, “from His treasures,” does not mean that the winds have some treasures, but that it is serviceable to the One who commands, and is prepared and ordered by His decision. As one who has a treasure brings it out without a word, and if he wishes brings it back in again, so the Creator arranged all things in like manner and handed them over to the living.

Have you seen how many different things there are in heaven? There are changes both in water and in fire, for one water is from springs, another is from the sea, another from a cloud, some from beneath the heavens and above the heavens. There is also subterranean water, from which warm waters issue in various places, wondrously heated by the fire that is beneath the earth. And speaking of fire: one is in the sun, another in the moon, another in the stars, some in the air; among us fire is from wood or in lamps, and from the earth; for often there are fires issuing from the earth, like springs of water, also from stones being rubbed together, and from branches of trees rubbed together, and from the flashing during a storm; another kind is reflected by water and the purest glass. So too the air: one kind, denser and more corrupt, is in us, causing various diseases and death; another, on the contrary, is beneficial to bodies; and a third rises higher and is fiery. So too among the winds there are many differences: one is weaker, another denser, one drier, another wetter or warmer.” Because of this the prophet also, having plunged into the abyss of these and similar good creations, in which the power and wisdom of God are revealed, seeing this vast deep spread out, leaped back and cried aloud: “How great are Thy works, O Lord! In wisdom hast Thou made them all; Thou hast filled the soul with wisdom.”

The Twelfth Reign: Domitian, son of Vespasian, who became king in Rome in the year 5584 from Adam (the 84th year from Christ)

Afterwards Domitian, his brother, the younger son of Vespasian, reigned for 15 years. And he was killed in a Roman church by the plotting of his wife. And so, having become his brother’s successor, he did not follow the virtue of his father and brother, but fell into the crimes of Tiberius and Nero.
