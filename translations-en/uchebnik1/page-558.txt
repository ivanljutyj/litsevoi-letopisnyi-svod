Next morning Tsar Michael returned with Basil to Constantinople. When they reached the village of Akritos, a great crowd gathered to see the tsar. Then a certain monk, climbing onto a stone, cried out to the advancing tsar: “A fine war you have waged, tsar, killing your uncle and staining your hands with kindred blood. Woe to you, woe to you!”

Then Tsar Michael and Basil summoned Theodore, a stern-looking armor-bearer, and sent him to kill that monk. But the people gathered around the messenger intervened for the monk, saying that he was possessed and deranged, and so he barely escaped death.

On the Enthronement of Basil the Macedonian.
Tsar Michael reigned together with him for one year.

On the evening of the Saturday after Pentecost, the tsar sent the protovestiarios (chief of the imperial wardrobe and treasury) Rentakios to Patriarch Photius to say that preparations should be made for Basil’s coronation with the imperial crown.

Next morning two thrones were prepared for the imperial procession. Those present were surprised that two thrones had been set up for one tsar.

The tsar entered the church (the imperial crown was not placed before the Royal Doors, as custom required) and went toward the Royal Doors. Basil followed him, attired as befitted the parakoimomenos (chief imperial chamberlain) in a special cloak, beneath which he wore a sword. Having ascended to the third step of the ambo, the tsar turned to face the assembly.

Basil stopped one step lower, and below Basil stood the asekretis Leo the Philosopher (secretary of the imperial chancery), holding a scroll in his hands. Also in the church were Michael, nicknamed Argun, the elders, and all the people. The asekretis Leo began to read in the tsar’s name: “Caesar Bardas devised evil against me, and wishing to kill me, led me out of the city. If Subatios and Basil had not warned me of this, I would not be alive. However, Bardas ended his days in sin, while we, by God’s protection, are alive to this day. Therefore I desire to make my faithful parakoimomenos Basil, who delivered me from the conspiracy and always cares for the prosperity of my kingdom, its guardian. Let all glorify him as tsar.” When Basil heard these words, his eyes filled with tears.

Then the tsar, taking the crown from his head, handed it to Patriarch Photius, who carried it into the altar, placed it on the Holy Table, and read a prayer over it.
