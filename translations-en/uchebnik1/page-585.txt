Andronicus was seized and put in chains together with all his relatives. Realizing that this had happened through Samonas’s treachery, Andronicus himself and those close to him were compelled to renounce their faith.

Constantine, his son, seeing that it was impossible for Andronicus to escape from there, by his father’s advice fled from Syria and returned to the emperor with his companions. Having given him many gifts, the emperor said this to him before the icon of the Lord God, our Savior, situated on the eastern side of the Golden Triclinium: “Do not be mistaken about yourself. Many have foretold that Constantine would reign over the Greeks. I swear to you by all justice and by the image of our Lord that the kingdom will not be yours, although you are called Constantine; rather, it was given by God to my beloved son from his forefathers, as many holy men have proved to me. But if you attempt to do this, your head will enter these doors without your body.” And so it happened.

After his murder at the hands of the tormentor, his head, soiled with blood and dust, was carried in through these doors to be washed. On the occasion of peace, a certain Abelvakios and Samonas’s father arrived from Tarsus. And seeing their approach, the emperor adorned the Magnaura with great beauty. He also adorned the Great Church with various furnishings and showed the Saracens all the precious vessels, which is an unworthy thing—to show Christian beauty and the vessels of divine service to unrighteous foreigners of another tongue.

Samonas’s father wanted to remain with his son, but the son did not agree to this, and persuaded him to return home and hold to his faith. “I,” he said, “will come to you as soon as I can.”

On the day of Pentecost, Leo crowned his son Constantine by the hands of Euthymius the patriarch.

Samonas handed over his servant Constantine, who had previously served Basil the magistros, to Empress Zoe, so that he might serve her.

Emperor Leo and Empress Zoe loved him so much that Samonas became envious of him and slandered him, saying that he consorted with the empress.

Taking this as true, the emperor sent men to tonsure him as a monk in the monastery of Saint Tarasius, entrusting this to Samonas himself.

Soon the emperor, wishing to bring him back, ordered Samonas to transfer him to the monastery of Spira.

And so the emperor went out to Damatrys and, while dining at Samonas’s monastery, saw Constantine and immediately gave orders, and they clothed him in secular dress.

And they placed the emperor’s cup in his hands at the dinner, and he returned with the emperor to the palace.
