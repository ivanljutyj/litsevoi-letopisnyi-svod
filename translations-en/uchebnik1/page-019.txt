Thus this tormentor was put to shame, and he became still more enraged; and he took the sacred vessels, and gold and silver beyond number, and plundered the whole city, and slaughtered the cattle, and wrought much murder, and boasted greatly, and withdrew to Antioch, and left great lamentation to the sons of Israel.

After two years Antiochus gathered an army against the Persians, and sent a prince to collect tribute from the cities of Jerusalem; and he came with great force, and spoke peaceful words, and entered the city of Jerusalem with deceit. And Antiochus brought great harm upon the city, and destroyed the city walls, and burned and demolished the whole city; and he filled the church with fornication and foul sacrifices; and the Jews who would not turn to the pagan faith and taste defilement he killed without mercy. And so, after torturing many men, women, and children much and cruelly, he killed them, and others he took captive. And Antiochus withdrew, leaving a prince behind to torment the Jews.

About Matthew and Judas

Matthew, a certain priest, had five sons, among whom was Judas Maccabee, who took up arms with fury and rose against the prince, and killed him. And Judas gathered all his kin, and went to the unclean pagan idols, and razed them to the ground, and killed the Jews who had transgressed the law.

And Antiochus was shamefully defeated by the Persians in their land, and again began to arm himself, and from great despondency fell ill, and said to his nobles: Sleep has departed from my eyes, and I have fallen into great sorrow; and now I have remembered the evil that I did in Jerusalem, and I have understood that because of that evil I have found this misfortune, and behold, I perish in a foreign land. And he ordered his armor-bearer constantly to urge on the horses, waving the whip, and he understood clearly that by the Most High Judgment this torment was given to him. Unceasing pain in his belly tormented him, and he endured fierce torments within. And it happened that Antiochus fell from the chariot, because they were carrying him very swiftly; and he fell in a grievous fall, and all the members of his body weakened. And then the disease progressed so far that worms bred, and in great sufferings his flesh decayed; and his wretched and foul body stank so that all the soldiers of Antiochus were burdened by the stench. And so Antiochus, who had boasted much and done much evil, ended his foolish life in shame in a foreign land. And here the unrighteous one was rightly tormented and died wretchedly, and there he was tormented still more.

The Eighteenth Reign in Syria

After Antiochus, his son Antiochus, surnamed Eupator, reigned. In his time Judas Maccabee defeated the chief of the guards of Antiochus.
