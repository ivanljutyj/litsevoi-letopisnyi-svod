Valentinian the Great, perfect in faith and piety and chaste, ruled in the old Roman Empire and governed the life of the country well. He ordered a council to be held in Illyricum, which greatly strengthened the Nicene faith and wrote a rule, and he commanded the bishops in Asia, in Phrygia, and in Caesarea to observe the rule; and all observed it. Valentinian, according to the rule, took as co-rulers his brother Valens and his son Gratian.

The bishops who assembled in Illyricum wrote likewise. When the bishop in Mediolanum died, there was a great strife between the orthodox and the heretics. Both sides with great zeal strove for different things, and there was great dissension between them, and both sides would have brought destruction upon their city if the imperial will had not restrained them. And their opposition still continued.

At that time a certain Ambrose governed that region. He soon entered the church and wished to stop the strife. The people suddenly abandoned the angry bickering and with one voice began to ask for Ambrose as bishop—he was still a catechumen—and many said: “Let us unite; there will be one faith for both sides if Ambrose is appointed bishop for us.”

When the pious Emperor Valentinian learned of this, he ordered Ambrose to be baptized and become bishop, having learned of his many virtues.

And when Ambrose became bishop, the emperor raised such a song to God and said: “I thank Thee, Lord Almighty, our Savior, that I have appointed the body for this man, but Thou hast ordained the soul; Thou hast made my naming right.” And he also said that Ambrose wrathfully denounced unjust princes before the emperor.

Meanwhile Valentinian the Great went to war against the Sarmatians, and from the region of Gaul he came into Illyricum and began to arm for battle.

The Sarmatians sent envoys and asked for peace. Valentinian saw them and asked them: “Are all Sarmatians such in body?” They answered: “Truly those who are best among them are such.” And Valentinian cried out loudly and said: “Hard will it go for the Greek kingdom if these Sarmatians, who are the best, dare so to fight the Greeks.”

Then blood vessels burst in Valentinian’s nostrils, and much blood flowed out, and in a certain fortress in Gaul he died, seized by illness, and passed away suddenly.

Valentinian left as heir to his kingdom his son Gratian, who was Augustus. Valentinian’s other son, also Valentinian, was still young and was not deemed worthy of imperial consideration. Necessity compelled Gratian to occupy the throne. For they were already beginning to try to seize the royal place while no one was upon it. Brother
