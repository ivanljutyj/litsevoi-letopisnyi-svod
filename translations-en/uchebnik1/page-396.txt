And immediately Ariadne ordered the chamberlain Uvrikios to find someone to kill Illus. And when the horse races were being held, Emperor Zeno summoned the patrician Illus to bring the Roman ambassadors into the imperial palace.

And when Illus was ascending the stairs, one courtier holding the office of scholarios, named Sporakios, struck him with a sword, wishing to cut Illus in two. But Illus’s sword-bearer was nearby, and raising his right hand he turned aside the blow, so that only the tip of the sword came down and cut off Illus’s right ear. And the one who attacked Illus was killed.

But Illus’s men and servants lifted him up and carried him away, and he departed to his own house. When Emperor Zeno learned of this, he swore that he had known nothing of that plot. But Illus did not believe him and was in great grief.

When Illus had recovered, he crossed the sea, considering what to do. While being healed of the pains caused by the sword wound, Illus went about wearing a cowl on his head. And Illus went in to Zeno and asked leave to go to the eastern lands for a short time, to change the air for a more favorable one, for he was suffering from the wound.
