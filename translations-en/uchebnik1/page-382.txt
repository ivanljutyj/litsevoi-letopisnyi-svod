The divine Pulcheria, the sister of Theodosius, instructed him much and wished to deliver him from this sin, but had no success at all, and then she decided to catch her brother by her wisdom; she wrote a document in the name of the king himself, that he granted his empress as a slave to his sister Pulcheria.

And Pulcheria gave this document to Emperor Theodosius, her brother, so that he would sign it with his royal signature in red. The king signed it. Pulcheria laughed at the king, that he had his empress as a slave, bought for silver, and wished to take her into service to herself. Theodosius was offended by his sister’s mockery and was indignant at her. Then Pulcheria brought out the royal document, and the king was ashamed and abandoned such folly.

On Nestorius the Heretic

In the reign of Theodosius there was also Nestorius, who was bishop in Constantinople. And there was turmoil when Nestorius began to preach. And this Emperor Theodosius was compelled to convene a council of 240 bishops in Ephesus against Nestorius and to depose him from the episcopate.

On Emperor Valentinian and the Taking of Rome by Gaiseric

There was also Valentinian the Younger, and his love of pleasure and unrestrained indulgence of the flesh deprived him of life and kingdom, and his participation in fornication brought him to disgrace and to spiritual and bodily death in this manner.

A certain Maximus was a ruler and was first in Rome. He had a beautiful wife, adorned even more with chastity. Seeing her, the king was captivated by her beauty and set about every kind of intrigue to obtain what he desired.

And Emperor Valentinian took from Maximus, the beauty’s husband, a gold ring, supposedly for some need, while he himself sent the ring to Maximus’s wife as a sign that her husband commanded her to come to the empress.

The wife believed this, recognizing her husband’s sign on the ring, and came to the royal chambers, and then the king took her and dishonored her by force.

Learning this, Maximus was wounded in heart and armed himself for battle, and in the end killed the king with a sword; Maximus also dishonored his wife Eudoxia, daughter of Theodosius the Younger.

The empress grieved greatly over the shame done to her, and was insulted, and sent to Gaiseric, the Vandal king, and arranged his coming against Rome.
