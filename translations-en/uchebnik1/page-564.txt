The brave Persian Jacob was eaten by worms and died.

Constantine the archer, who was at Kivireotai, was cut to pieces by swords and died in torment.

Marian, the king’s brother, fell from his horse and broke his leg, and since his leg festered and swarmed with worms, he ended his life in torment.

Let people know what judgment befalls those who with hatred and envy dare such things and do not leave vengeance to God. For it is written: “Vengeance is mine, and I will repay,” says the Lord to those who act unjustly.

The king, entering the Great Church on the day of the Nativity of Christ, baptized his son Stephen. Coming out of the church, he sat in a chariot harnessed with white horses, with him also the empress, and the praepositus John with the child in his arms. And the king scattered money to the people all the way to his palace.

On the day of Saint Polyeuctus a terrible earthquake began. And from that day for 40 days and nights the earth shook; then also the statue of Apollo, standing on a column in the marketplace, fell.

And the church of the Most Holy Theotokos, located in Sigma, filled with singers, shifted. Leo the philosopher, who was there, ordered everyone to leave the church. Those who did not obey him were all crushed; he himself, standing under the wall, escaped death with two others, and another 5 people standing under the ambo were saved.

And when the king came to the church to receive the pure sacrifice, Patriarch Photius did not allow him to the divine communion, calling him a murderer and a robber, and unworthy of the pure mysteries.

But Basil, angered by those words, took care that bishops come from Rome, who brought a scroll from the pope, and expelled Photius from the throne, as one who had allegedly unlawfully occupied the patriarchal throne.

And he restored the divine Ignatius to the throne a second time.

And a son was born to the king by Eudokia Ingerina, named Alexander.

After this the king set out to war against the Saracens, who were stationed at Tibrikia (Tephrike).

And after fierce battles he was defeated, and many of the Greeks perished.

When he fled from the enemies, Theophylact Abastaktos saved him, the father of Romanus, who reigned after this. For the Saracens almost seized him.

After this many told the king that anyone could have rescued him, but the king sought the true savior who had been near him when he fled. And so he learned of Theophylact and wished to bestow honor on him. But he, refusing the honor, asked the king for a place, which he received.
