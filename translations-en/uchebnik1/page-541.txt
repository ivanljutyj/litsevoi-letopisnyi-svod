The saints bore branding on their faces, as women wear a pearl crown shining with precious stones. On the way into exile they met a certain fisherman who was bringing food to Patriarch Methodius.

Then they wrote to the great Methodius, who was in prison, a brief note in verse: “To the living dead man and the life-bearing dead man, who lives on earth and walks in heaven, greeting from the branded, captives to the captive.”

He wrote back to them: “The one buried alive greets the holy orators inscribed by name in the heavenly books, chaste, bearing branded faces, persecuted like him for the Truth.” And these golden words were like life-giving streams flowing from a Divine source.

Saint Theodore departed to the Lord in exile, and at the hour of his death the singing of angels was heard, and his voice sounded together with them. But the good ascetic, the great Methodius, imprisoned in a dark, terrifying cave, endured the crushing of his teeth, the shattering of his jaws, and all the other torments with which the impious inhumanly tortured him, and he was like hard iron on an anvil under striking hammers.

Tsar Theophilus, though immeasurably cruel and inventive in every evil against the veneration of icons and its supporters, was at the same time very wise and magnanimous in matters of state governance, and cared for the adoption of just laws and their execution in the courts. A testimony to the greatness of his reign was also the renewal of the walls of the reigning city, which had been ravaged by the Hagarenes and had also suffered from severe frost in the year when the sea froze, from the heat that succeeded the cold, and from constantly blowing hot winds. Just as great mountains crumble, so the walls of the city began to crumble. He not only renewed them but gave them an even more majestic appearance, exhausting numerous treasures on this.

He created a wondrous organ, elaborately adorned with trees wrought in gold and with birds sitting on their branches, executed so skillfully that in the sounds of the wonderful breath of music one seemed to hear the sweet-voiced singing of songbirds sitting on poplar branches or on pointed cypresses. Thus do the wondrous works of gifted people’s hands seem to come alive.
