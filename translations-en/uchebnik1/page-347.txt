Sixth Reign, of Gratian, Son of Valentinian, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Valentinian, his son Gratian reigned alone for one year. In those times there was a great and terrible earthquake, so that at Alexandria the sea receded far, and the ships were left lying on dry land.

A multitude of people gathered for the wondrous spectacle; then the water returned again and rose beyond its usual place, and five thousand people drowned. And the water covered people traveling to Alexandria in ships.

And the ships that were on the Nile River were cast onto dry land by the river with great swiftness over 180 stadia.

Moreover, very many lands of Crete, Achaea, Boeotia, Epirus, and Sicily suffered when the sea rose. And many ships were lifted onto mountains a hundred stadia inland when the water level rose.

The British islands and the African lands also suffered even worse. And the whole world swiftly endured calamities: some lands from plague, others from portents, and still others were drowned by the sea.

And moreover the Adriatic and Libyan seas parted far, and the water stood like a wall, and dry land appeared. And then many ships that were under sail sank to the bottom of the abyss and settled, and afterward rose when the water returned.

Seized by the earthquake, a certain city in Bithynia was wholly destroyed, and the villages around it, and much labor was lost. Likewise the city called Herm in the Hellespont was destroyed completely to its foundations.

Also many chasms appeared in various places, so that out of fear people remained in the mountains. And from the long drought people and cattle perished.

Seventh Reign, of Theodosius the Great, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Gratian, Theodosius the Great reigned with his wife Placidia for 17 years.

Then Theodosius’s father, also named Theodosius, having taken up arms, defeated huge regiments of barbarian warriors, and diligently traversed the Greek territories, and swiftly set them in order.
