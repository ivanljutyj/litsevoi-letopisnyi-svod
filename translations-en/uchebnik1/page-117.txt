And Eleazar entered the upper city with the rebels, and attacked the houses of the nobles, and burned them, and plundered the royal palaces, and burned the debt records, so that the debtors, freed from their debts, might help him. And Eleazar burned all the principal buildings in the city.

And the rebels went against Jerusalem against the Romans on the fifteenth day of the month of Loos, that is, August, and they defeated all the guard detachments, and besieged the Romans remaining in the city.

And the rebels dug an underground passage from afar to the city, and undermined the city wall, so that it collapsed.

But the watchmen of the besieged sensed the undermining, and managed to build another wall before the undermined one collapsed. And the Jews who were in the city sent envoys to Eleazar with a request that they be allowed to go out to their own in peace.

And the Jews came out, and the Romans remained in the city, and in fear they fled to the royal palaces. And the Romans sent envoys to Eleazar, asking that he let them go alive, if only he would spare their lives. And Eleazar promised and swore that he would release them.

But when the Romans came out unarmed, the rebels surrounded them and killed them all except the chiliarch Metilius, for he had accepted circumcision and joined the Jewish law, and he was released.

This defeat was insignificant for the Romans, for their vast army lost only a small part. But for the Jews it was a prelude to their destruction, and the wisest among them were gloomy and sorrowful, expecting for themselves, besides vengeance from the Romans, also the wrath of God. For that impious slaughter of the Romans was committed on the Sabbath, when the law commands abstention from work for the sake of piety.

On that same day and at that same hour, by Divine Providence, as if in vengeance for the treachery of the Jews of Jerusalem, the inhabitants of Caesarea killed the Jews living among them, and more than twelve thousand were killed at that hour. And those who fled from Caesarea Florus seized and put in chains, and confined on ships.

Then the whole land of the Jews was thrown into confusion. And the Jews divided into four parts, and began to seize the Syrian cities and burn them.

And they captured the following cities: Philadelphia, and Sebonitis, and Gerasa, and Gadara, and Pella, and Scythopolis, and Hippos, and Gaulan, and Kedasa, and Ptolemais, and Gaba, and Caesarea, and Sebaste, and Ascalon, and Gaza, and Anthedon.
