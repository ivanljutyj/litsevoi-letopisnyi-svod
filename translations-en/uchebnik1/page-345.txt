She even lingered to adorn herself according to the custom of women, as was fitting, diligently. The woman was carrying a small infant with her, and she ran swiftly, trying to outrun the governor’s soldiers. He could not restrain himself and said: “Take this woman and bring her here to me.” And he said to her: “Wretched woman, where are you hurrying so early?” She said: “To the field where the Orthodox people are gathering.” He answered: “Have you not heard that the governor is going there to kill all whom he finds?” She said: “I have heard, and that is why I hasten to be there.” He said: “Why are you dragging the infant with you?” She answered: “So that this infant too may be counted worthy of martyrdom for Christ.”

Hearing this, the governor ordered his subordinates to turn back, and he ordered the imperial carriage returned to the palace.

The governor went in to the emperor and said: “If you command me to die, I am ready; but the task you entrusted to me I cannot carry out.” And he told him everything about the woman and restrained the emperor’s wrath.

At that same time, because of the persecutions of the impious Emperor Valens, which he brought down upon the churches of God, wars with the enemies began again. The Goths, who had come in close order from their own land, now scattered throughout all the land of Thrace and with arms began cruelly to ravage cities and villages. Then the inhabitants of Constantinople began to revile Valens because of the cruel calamities, and they said to him: “Why do you not go to war?”

Concerning the Monk Isaac

When Valens went out to war, the monk Isaac met him.

And Isaac took Valens’ horse by the bridle and said: “Where are you going, emperor, armed against God? Open the Christian churches and you will return with victory.” The emperor said to him: “If I do not open them, what will happen?” Isaac said: “You will not be able to stand against your enemies, but you will flee from their face, and hide in a hayloft, and be burned alive by fire.” Valens was enraged at Isaac and ordered him thrown into prison, and threatened him with death, and said: “When I return with victory, then I will execute him.” But he did not return, like Ahab in the time of the prophet Micaiah. Saint Isaac was filled with the grace of a like prophecy, and like him he foretold the emperor’s death before all the people.

Then there was a battle with the enemy at Adrianople, and Valens was completely defeated by the Goths.

Valens fled with a small retinue and hid in a hayloft with a commander. His pursuers set the hayloft on fire, and so the accursed one burned, according to Isaac’s prophecy. The impious Valens reigned for 3 years.
