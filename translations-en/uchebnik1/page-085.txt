But most of all the gaze of the Divinity is turned upon those who fulfill His Law. And Abraham, the forefather of our race, was led out of his land because he wronged his brother when dividing his lands: in that in which he sinned, in that he was punished. Then, for Abraham’s obedience, God gave him the Promised Land.

Herod, however, fell ill. And instead of the former will he wrote another. And he named as king Antipas, his son by Malthace. To Caesar he bequeathed 1,000 talents of gold, and to Caesar’s wife and children, 500. And to others he distributed much wealth. And Herod grew sicker and sicker, for the illness came upon him in old age and in grief. Then there was a rebellion among the people against him.

There were two teachers of the Law in the city of Jerusalem; they studied the laws of the fathers and for this were famed throughout Judea—Judas, son of Sepphor, and Matthew, son of Margal. Many young men came to them, asking about the Law of the fathers, and they gathered every day like an army. When it became known that the king was overcome by grief and illnesses, these teachers of the Law sent to all who revered the ancestral Law and said that now the convenient time had come to serve God and to destroy what had been done contrary to the ancestral Law. The Law forbade any image of a man or of another living creature in the temple.

At that same time Herod made an image of a golden eagle above the main gates of the temple in honor of Caesar, whom he called the golden-winged eagle. The teachers of the Law ordered the people to cut down this image of the eagle. And they spoke thus: “It is possible to die for the ancestral Law, for those who so die will inherit immortal glory, and after death their souls will have eternal joy. But he who is cowardly and loves his body, who instead of a martyr’s death prefers death from illness, he will receive no glory and will accept endless torments in hell. Come, people of Judea! Now is the time for heroic deed. Let us show how zealous we are for the Law of Moses. Let us not disgrace our lineage, let us not put to shame Him who gave us the Law. We have examples of martyrs—first Eleazar, and the seven Maccabean brothers, and their mother, so courageous. Antiochus conquered and seized our land and reigned over it, but these seven youths, and the old teacher of the Law, and the aged woman conquered him. Let us too imitate them and not prove more feeble than a woman. If they torture us for divine zeal, they will thereby weave for us an even more glorious crown; and if they kill us, our souls, having left the prison of the body, will depart to the forefathers, to Abraham and his descendants.” These words caused a wondrous stirring among the people, and at noon people girded themselves with thick ropes, climbed up to the gates, and cut down the golden eagle.
