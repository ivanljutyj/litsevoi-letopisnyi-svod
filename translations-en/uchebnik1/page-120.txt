Even earlier King Agrippa had come to Antioch with his soldiers and waited there for Vespasian’s arrival. Having joined with Agrippa, Vespasian went to Ptolemais, in order to move from there into Galilee. And the inhabitants of the Galilean city of Sepphoris met him there with honor and praise, for they were thinking of their own salvation.

Even before Vespasian’s arrival the inhabitants of Sepphoris had trusted Cestius, and sworn loyalty to him, and received a detachment from him. And now they said to the commander of the Romans: “We will be swift helpers to you against our own kinsmen. Only leave us in support your foot and horse soldiers, who can repel the predatory raids of the Jews. Swear loyalty to us. We will not stir ourselves to mad rebellion like others, because of whom our fatherland is going to ruin. Our city of Sepphoris is the strongest of all in Galilee, and the greatest, and it is needful to you, for from it you can wage war throughout that land and do what you consider necessary.” And Vespasian praised the inhabitants of Sepphoris, and swore loyalty to them, and confirmed that Rome would continue to be at peace with them, because of their humility and meekness.

There are two Galilees: Upper and Lower. They are surrounded by Phoenicia and Syria, and on the west by Ptolemais and Mount Carmel, formerly Galilean, now Tyrian.

At Mount Carmel is situated the city of Gaba, called the City of Horsemen, since Herod, when he dismissed his horsemen, sent them there for sustenance. On the south Galilee is surrounded by Samaria and Scythopolis as far as the waters of the river Jordan. On the east Galilee borders on Hippos and Gadara. The northern lands of Galilee end where the Tyrian land begins.
