And again, taking off their white garments, they go about their business and work until evening. And again they return to the dining hall. Neither by shouting nor by laughter do they defile this place. The Essenes speak in turn, without interrupting one another. To outside observers, the silence of the community’s inner life seems a fearful mystery. They do nothing without the command of the overseers. Only in two cases may they do something without a command: if mercy and help are required. They may not give anything to their relatives without the command of the overseers. They are righteous; they do not let fury show, and they restrain anger. The Essenes are defenders of the faith and peacemakers. They do not break their word. Everything they say can be believed more than what is said under oath. Oath itself is considered by them worse than any sin. If a person can be trusted only when he swears by God, this is condemned and rejected by them.

And they seek out the most ancient books, and read them, and study what happens in foreign lands, and draw wisdom from this, paying attention to what is useful for soul and body. They know herbs, roots, and stones: what is obtained where and for what ailment it is needed. And the Essenes know how to wage war from these writings. If someone wishes to join them, they do not accept him quickly, but first order him to live in a building outside the gates, give him an axe, a loincloth, and white clothing, and test him for one year. If the person shows self-restraint, he is admitted to the inner life of the community, and he participates in purification with water. After the person has shown patience, his character is tested for two more years, and if he proves unworthy, he is expelled; if worthy, he is accepted among the brothers. And before acceptance they adjure him with terrible oaths, and he, standing before the doors, makes dreadful vows, invoking the Living God, and His almighty right hand, and the incomprehensible Spirit of God, and the all-seeing seraphim and cherubim, and all the Heavenly powers as witnesses that he will fulfill all that he swears. And he who wishes to join the Essenes swears to the following: to glorify and honor the Divinity; to be just to people; neither by his own will nor by command to do evil to people; to hate injustice; to be truthful always, and not only when he swears; to obey the authorities, for without God’s command authority is not given to anyone; if he himself should happen to rule, to do so worthily; not to dress or adorn himself too luxuriously; to love truth with all his soul; to expose liars; to guard his hands from theft and unjust gain, and his soul from bribery; to conceal nothing from his brothers; not to disclose secrets to those of other faiths, even if they torture him to death; not to take part in robbery; to keep the books and the angelic names. After such vows are made, the one who comes to the Essenes is confirmed.
