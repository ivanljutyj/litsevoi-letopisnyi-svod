The 20th Reign, of Phocas the Tormentor, that is, the Tyrant, who reigned in the City of Constantine

After Maurice, Phocas reigned, a Cappadocian by birth, and he was a tormentor, and he reigned 8 years. Then soldiers who were of one mind with him came, and all the people of Constantinople gathered there as well, and they proclaimed Phocas emperor in the church of St John the Forerunner. Thus this evil entered the imperial palace.

On the Death of Maurice

After that the popular factions rose up, the Greens and the Blues, shouting: “Maurice is not dead, but abides in prosperity!”

And then Phocas set himself to the murder of Maurice, and ordered him sought out, and then Maurice was brought bound to the harbor of Eutropius. First tormenting him with the sight of killings, Phocas ordered the heads of his five sons cut off before Maurice. Reflecting on this sight, accepting this wretched lot, Maurice then said: “Righteous art Thou, O Lord, and righteous is Thy judgment.”

But the wet nurse, that is, the nurse, stole one of the emperor’s five sons and gave her own child to be killed. Maurice did not concern himself with that, but asked that his own child be brought, so that he too might be cut down, for thus it is “lawful by nature, and in the best and most hidden manner.” For Maurice wished to show his true repentance to all, himself changing his way of life. Then, with much patience and thanksgiving, Maurice himself was executed, and so became an heir of eternal blessings.

But wretched Phocas ordered the bodies of the slain to be thrown on the Tribunal Field, so that those leaving the city might look at them, according to his word, until the bodies stank; after this he allowed those who loved them to take the bodies of the executed.

They took and buried Maurice with his sons in a good place. And Maurice’s wife with her three daughters was shut up in a monastery. A certain pious calligrapher in Alexandria, coming from a memorial service, when it was the middle of the night in which Maurice was killed, saw idols come out of the pagan shrine and cry out that Maurice and his children had been killed. The calligrapher noted this and wrote it down.

When ten days had passed, a man came to Alexandria and told of Maurice’s death. The villain Phocas, after a short time, killed Maurice’s wife and her daughters at the hill of Eutropius. Not only these, but also Maurice’s relatives the lawless one killed, and doomed many to great destruction. And from that time in the Greek Empire various calamities did not cease, but one calamity followed another.
