Therefore there was a time when He was not, and the Son is a creation and a creature. Nor is the Son like the Father in essence, nor is the Father truly by nature; the Word is not, nor is His Wisdom true, for no one born of creation for use was called Word or Wisdom, and that One is His Father’s Word and Wisdom. Just as God created all things, so also He created that One; and He was incarnate, and mutable in nature just as all words are. And the Word was separated, alienated, and cut off from God’s essence. And the Father was not visible to the Son, and the Word does not fully know the Father, nor can He see Him in any way, and He does not know His own nature either. The Son was created for our sake, so that through Him, as an instrument, God might create us. And He would not exist if God had not willed to create us.” Someone asked the Arians whether God’s Word can change or be joined to the devil; they were not afraid to say that “it can indeed change so, for created nature is mutable.”

Concerning how a miracle occurred at the council. The dispute of Spyridon with the philosopher. It would not be just to pass over in silence that, by the highest royal decree, philosophers and rhetoricians skilled in speaking beautifully came to the council. Among them was one Greek who astonished the bishops, greatly censuring them in defense of Arius, so that many came near and listened to his speech with great attention. Yet not one of the bishops was able to refute the words of the wise rhetorician, who answered those around him with ease, skillfully explaining what had been said. As an eel cannot be caught, so that philosopher could not be overcome by any word. When it seemed to them that he was convicted, he twisted about and resisted more strongly, displaying lofty intellect and eloquence. But let God show that the Kingdom of Heaven is not in words but in power. A certain Cypriot man, one of the saints and bishop of the city of Trimythus, named Spyridon, of humble origin and not a scholar, asked permission to speak with the philosopher. The fathers, knowing his simplicity and that he was not a scholar, forbade him, lest he be mocked by the sinners. Nevertheless he approached the philosopher and said: “In the name of Jesus Christ, listen, philosopher, to the true teaching.” He answered him: “If you speak, I will listen.” The saint said: “Let it be known to you that God is One, who created heaven and earth and made man; and He formed all things visible and invisible by the Word and the Spirit. Therefore we, acknowledging this Word as the Son of God, worship and believe that for our salvation He was born of the Virgin at the end of the ages and through Crucifixion, Death, and Resurrection freed the human race. And we believe that He will come to judge all the living and the dead in righteousness. Do you believe this, philosopher?” He was terrified and, as it were, struck dumb, finding no words for objection, and from terror fell silent like one mute and deaf, and uttered only one thing: “I think it is so.”
