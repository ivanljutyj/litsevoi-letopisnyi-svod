Moreover, there is signified, as it were, the time of the Last Trumpet, in which the great Angel must sound and awaken all nations to the Terrible and impartial Judgment; and then, having sung the divine hymn Trisagion, he will at once depart to where he appeared. And many, pure in thought and blameless in life, having been deemed worthy by God’s great Providence for us to see an angel on the precious cross announcing the future miracle—so that no one might be caught in the nets of unbelief by godless writings. For God, when He wills, will move even a stone, and it will roll, giving notice; so too did the holy precious cross of the Lord, Anikita, give notice, which King Constantine the Great set upon a high column.

In the days of Maurice and Constantine, Christ-loving rulers, in the 18th year of their reign, there occurred in Constantinople a great earthquake, lasting seven days and nights. Many buildings and structures fell, shattered on the ground, but the glorious column stood with the elevated cross. Thus fell and was crushed the statue of Emperor Theodosius the Great and his wife Plakida, and many other houses and columns from this terrible punishment and shaking leaned and fell. It happened that the precious and holy cross Anikita, by the force of God’s wrath, over seven days and nights because of our sins, loosened on the support on which it stood at the top of the column, and shifted.

And not otherwise than by God’s Providence, the great Emperor Heraclius later, by a certain ingenuity of skilled stonecutters, raised it up, and ordered the cross to be strengthened and set with tin and iron joined by certain devices, writing on it: “The great Emperor Heraclius established the foot of the cross by a God-pleasing deed.” And as he wrote, so to this day it is read on that precious cross, and that holy life-giving cross Emperor Heraclius called Anikita, as was already said, from the name “Victory,” which the great Emperor Constantine gave it, for by its power he conquered and cast down all visible enemies. And enough has been said about this precious and life-giving cross, and also about the wondrous miracles that came from it upon the weak and infirm.

On the Repose of Helena

The blessed Saint Helena piously ended her life, departing to the Holy Consubstantial and Undivided Trinity. And she spread the Christian faith in every place she came to. Having returned to old Rome, the blessed one accepted her blessed end, shining with goodness, chastity, and righteousness. She was buried in a purple coffin outside the city of Rome in a certain holy church, being 80 years old.
