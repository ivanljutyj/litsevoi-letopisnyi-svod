He was angry at the abductor and ordered the accursed one to be beaten; from this fear fell upon Belisarius’s soldiers, while the Libyans felt still greater love for Belisarius, and the Libyan cities themselves opened before him. And the fair city of Carthage opened its gates, and all the townspeople eagerly received Belisarius as children receive a father.

Belisarius found in that city many treasures of Gelimer, the Roman king, and the wealth of sea and rivers, and he drew in much property, and distributed the spoil to his soldiers honorably.

With King Gelimer himself Belisarius joined battle and filled the Libyan fields with blood, and covered the earth with barbarian dead, and took King Gelimer himself with his wife and children in pitiful form and captured him.

And from there the much-victorious commander Belisarius returned, and presented Gelimer, king of the Romans, bound in iron fetters, to Emperor Justinian, and Rome, the ancient city, which the Goths had once taken, he returned again to the Greeks under Justinian’s dominion.

The emperor therefore honored the brave Belisarius magnificently from his own property, and rewarded the soldiers with gold and silver.

For this reason Justinian held horse races, at which King Gelimer was led before Belisarius, and Belisarius himself, the victorious commander, appeared to all, shining with many military successes. And Khosrow, the Persian king, feared Belisarius’s spear and made peace instead of war.

And when the accursed ones wished to take life and power from Emperor Justinian himself, Belisarius delivered him from the hands of the evil people. During the horse races, the whole multitude of the evil people heaped many insults upon the emperor and set up another king and autocrat, pointing to the patrician Hypatius, a kinsman of Anastasius, who had reigned before.

At this Belisarius was wounded in heart and armed himself firmly with many soldiers, and the mad people were cut down by the army, and their gladness turned into weeping; and there were found among the people 35 chiliads, that is, 35,000, slain.

Belisarius seized Hypatius himself and presented him to Emperor Justinian; the emperor ordered Hypatius’s head to be cut off, because he had dared to place the imperial crown upon himself.

And envy envied this great Belisarius in his victories. He who had frightened Khosrow, king of the Persians, he who in battles was fiercer than a lion, was conquered by envy, a savage beast, without soldiers and armor-bearers.
