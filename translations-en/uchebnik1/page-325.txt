It is told that Saint Athanasius, having learned that Emperor Constantius intended to adhere to the Arian faith, and that the Arians with evil intent were unjustly slandering him through an unrighteous trial.

Without telling anyone, suddenly at midnight, being sought, he ran to a certain pious maiden. She was surprised and perplexed at his coming. The blessed patriarch said to her: “The disciples of Arius are seeking me, and they slander me with improper things. And to avoid absurd rumor and not lead into sin those who intend to torture me, I decided to flee. And God directed me on this night, saying: ‘You cannot be saved anywhere except with this maiden.’” For this God-bearing man had foreseen even before, by God’s revelation, the plot being prepared against him, and he fled, because the enemies of truth had declared him a villain and poisoner. The maiden, casting aside all doubt, said: “The saint has been sent by God,” and she hid him for six years.

They did not know where the patriarch was. Having returned to his flock after the death of Constantius, Athanasius was forced to leave the throne also during the persecution of Christians under Emperor Julian the Apostate. His close friends asked him: “Where are you going again, good shepherd? To whom are you leaving us, like sheep without a shepherd?” The good shepherd answered them: “Do not weep, children, and do not wail. The little cloud melting from the heat—this small tumult that we see—will quickly disperse. Do not be dismayed, children; by God’s will I shall soon return.” And so it happened.

A little time passed, and the Hellenes, seeing crows flying over the temple of Serapion in Alexandria and cawing loudly, asked the saint mockingly: “Tell us, wicked old man, what are the crows cawing about?” The saint answered them: “The crows say ‘kra,’ and ‘kra’ in the Assyrian tongue means ‘morning.’ So tomorrow you will see God’s help and our glory.”

And in the morning they learned of the death of Julian the Transgressor; he was brought from Persia dead and stinking.
