On the Scroll of the Orthodox and of the Heretics

The 630 holy fathers, for greater accuracy, made a scroll of the Orthodox faith; and they also copied out the heresiarchs and made another scroll, and placed both scrolls in the shrine upon the relics of the holy and all-praised martyr Euphemia, and, having sealed it, they prayed for three days. And after three days they opened the shrine and found the scroll of the Orthodox in the hand of the saint, and the heretical one beneath her feet. And the Orthodox glorified God, while the heretics were put to even greater shame.

On the Scroll. Afterwards Leo, Pope of Rome, entreated by the council, set down on a scroll the confession concerning Christ our God, and placed it in the tomb of the holy apostles Peter and Paul. And for a long time he prayed with fasting that it might be corrected. And the holy apostles appeared to him and told him of the correction of that scroll.

This scroll Leo joyfully sent to Flavian, Patriarch of Antioch, and to the other patriarchs; and when they received it, they called it the Pillars of Orthodoxy.

In the reign of this Emperor Marcian, Simeon the Stylite of Antioch in Syria ascended the pillar. But the council of the holy fathers censured him, because he had undertaken the standing first, and the fathers had not permitted it. Afterwards, however, the fathers learned of his life, and then they permitted him the standing, and they honoured his mother Martha. At that time also laboured Sabbas the Sanctified, and Theodosius, head of the common life, and Cyriacus the Hermit, and John the Silent.

And in the reign of this Marcian the ladies Eudoxia and Placidia were returned by Genseric, king of the Vandals, and came to Constantinople.

And Olybrius received his wife Placidia and his daughter Juliana in Byzantium. This Emperor Marcian executed Chrysaphius Tzoumas, the chamberlain beloved of Emperor Theodosius, because he had slandered many and was a helper and protector of the Greens.

Emperor Marcian gave his daughter, whom he had by his first wife, in marriage to Anthemius, and appointed him emperor in Rome.

By Marcian’s daughter Anthemius had a daughter, whom he gave in marriage to the general Ricimer.

This Emperor Marcian favoured the faction of the Veneti, who, when the Green faction raised a revolt, established the rule that they should not enter the city for three years.
