The Earthly Life of Jesus Christ

And Alexander (of Macedon) died.

And afterward four of his servants reigned. And Arrhidaeus, Alexander’s brother, who was called Philip, received Macedonia, and he reigned in Macedonia. Antipater reigned in Europe; in Egypt, Ptolemy Lagus; and Seleucus ruled Phoenicia, part of Cilicia, and Syria.

Cilicia was ruled by Philotas, Asia by Antigonus, Caria by Cassander, the Hellespont by Oleon, Paphlagonia by Eumenes, and Phalcisia by Lysimachus; and these rulers never ceased rising up against one another.

Khuzifa, or Olympias, Alexander’s mother, withdrew to her own patrimony, to the land of Myria.

And Byzas, who founded the city of Byzantium, sent across the sea to Phol, king of Myria, his eldest commander Germanicus, to make peace with him; and he also wrote to him about Khuzifa, Alexander’s mother, wishing to take her as his wife and to reign with her.

And Phol received the letters from Germanicus, and examined the gifts he had sent, and was glad.

And Phol gathered four thousand inhabitants of the land of Myria from all Indian Ethiopia, and set out, bringing his daughter with him.

And Byzas met him at sea, and received him in the city of Chalcedon with great love, and gave many gifts to those who came with him. And Phol entered the city of Byzantium, and, having received many gifts, rejoiced.

And King Byzas took as his wife Khuzifa, or Olympias, daughter of Phol, king of Ethiopia, wife of King Philip and mother of Alexander. And a daughter was born to Byzas and Khuzifa, and he named her Byzantia.

And Romun Ermilai, king of Rome, took Byzantia as his wife, and came to love her greatly because of her beauty—he himself was also handsome and wise—and he gave her Rome as a dowry; and for this the Roman nobles were angry with him. And Byzantia bore three sons: the first she named Ermilai, after his father; the second, Urban; and the third, Claudius.
