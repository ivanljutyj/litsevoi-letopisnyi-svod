At that time, by the providence of God, the holy hermit Arsacius came to the Venerable Joannicius the Great, who was laboring in the Olympos mountains, and said to him: “God has sent me to you, so that the two of us may go to Nicomedia to the venerable man, the recluse Isaiah, and learn from him what is pleasing to God and useful to His Church.”

And when they came to the Venerable Isaiah, they heard from him: “Thus says the Lord: ‘The final conviction of My enemies is at hand!’ But you, going to the Empress Theodora and to Patriarch Methodius, say this: ‘Remove from yourselves all those of wrong faith, and then with the Angels offer Me sacrifice, venerating the image of My face and of the Cross.’” And when they heard this from the Venerable Isaiah, they hastened to Constantinople and announced what had been said to Patriarch Methodius.

Then the patriarch assembled all the archbishops and the whole priestly and monastic order, and they all came to the empress. And the empress agreed with them in all that had been announced to her, since from childhood she had been raised in piety and love for God.

And taking out the image of Christ and the Mother of God that she wore on her breast, the empress kissed it in the sight of all, saying that “if anyone does not venerate icons as is proper—that is, not with the worship due to God, not as gods, but as images of the Prototypes, out of love for Them—let him be anathema!” And then all rejoiced with great joy.

And the empress, falling at the feet of Patriarch Methodius, implored him to command all bishops, abbots, and monks, and also all priests, to pray for Emperor Theophilus, that God might forgive him and deliver him from eternal torments. But the patriarch was in doubt, not daring to pray for such a man. The weeping empress barely persuaded him, and then the patriarch said: “The will of God be done!”

And Patriarch Methodius commanded all bishops, venerable men, priest-monks, and monks—the whole clergy and the hierarchs of the Greek Empire—to assemble in the Great Church.

Among them were such soldiers of Christ as Joannicius the Great and Arsacius of Olympos, the disciples of Theodore the Studite, Theophanes the Confessor from the Great Village, Michael of the Holy City, syncellus and confessor, Theophanes and Theodore the Inscribed, and many others; and all of them offered all-night prayer to God for Theophilus. And this earnest prayer was offered with one accord and with tears throughout the first week of Great Lent. The Empress Theodora herself prayed with them, together with other women and all the people. And so the Empress Theodora prayed continually, and toward morning on Friday of the first week she dozed off, exhausted by the stricter fast and the long vigil. And she saw in a dream that she was at the pillar of the Cross...
