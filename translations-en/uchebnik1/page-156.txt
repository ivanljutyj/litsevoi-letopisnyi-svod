Does he make preparations, or send for helpers? No. He destroys himself in his own dissensions. Every day the enemies suffer greater damage than we could inflict on them by coming to the city. If anyone wants security, this is secure—to leave the enemies to destroy themselves. If anyone wants glory, there is little glory in fighting a sick man. For the Jews are now suffering the disease of internal discord. And if we go against them now and conquer, our arms will have no glory; everyone will attribute it to these discords.” So said Vespasian, and the commanders agreed with him. And many from Jerusalem fled secretly from the Zealots to Vespasian. It was hard to escape, because at all the gates the Zealots placed guards to watch that no one should leave. If anyone tried to flee or planned flight to the Romans and was caught, the penalty imposed on such a one was death by the sword.

Many wealthy citizens gave gold, and they were allowed to go wherever they wished. The poor, since they had nothing to give, perished as traitors. And heaps of the dead grew like stacks along the roads and in the city, for the impious did not allow them to be buried, but as if they had sworn to defile all things divine and human. They cast aside both the law of God and the law of nature, and when they saw men cut down and stinking in the sun, they laughed. If anyone dared secretly to sprinkle earth from his sleeve on his own dead lying there, that bold man was immediately stabbed. They had no mercy, and they transferred wrath from the living to the dead and from the dead to the living. And such fear fell upon all that the living envied the dead. And the Zealots trampled all human laws, mocked the divine laws, and laughed at the prophetic teachings, counting them fables. For the prophets of old called to virtue and restrained from wickedness. The Zealots transgressed all this and fulfilled the prophecy about Jerusalem. For it was written that then the city of Jerusalem would be conquered and the sanctuary burned, when there should be discord in the city, and people with their own hands would defile the altar of God. And this word the Zealots fulfilled.

John the robber, who had fled from Titus from the Galilean city of Gischala, having become a very influential man in Jerusalem, strove for greater glory. He received men from the criminal ranks and surrounded himself with ever greater veneration. And John did not obey the commands of other men, and he allowed no one to express his opinion, but always insisted on his own decision. So it was clear that he desired to rule alone. And people submitted to him, some because they feared him, others because they were well disposed toward him. He was skilled in various kinds of deceit. Many robbers resisted him.
