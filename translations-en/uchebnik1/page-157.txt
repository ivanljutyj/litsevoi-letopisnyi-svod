And the rebels divided in two, and quarreled among themselves. And they fell upon peaceful people, and tried to outdo one another so as to seize more plunder from them by robbery. And many townspeople fled from their own kin and went over to foreigners and found among them salvation, which they could not obtain from their own.

On the Fortress of Masada

The fortress was strong, not far from Jerusalem, built by former kings to preserve their wealth and armaments. The fortress was called Masada. Then the evildoers, the so-called sicarii, came and seized it.

And from this fortress the sicarii made sorties into the surrounding villages, and ravaged them, and cut down the people living in them, and took property and grain and carried it off to Masada. And every day similar evildoers came to them from everywhere and joined them. Robbers also gathered in other places of Judea. As with a dangerous disease—if one of the members that sustains life and breath in the whole body is afflicted and inflamed by disease, then the other members will not remain healthy, but will fall ill with the same disease. So too in Judea—since the capital was seized by revolt and robbers, throughout the whole country evildoers gained the opportunity to do what they wanted, and fulfilled their will, and walked in crooked ways, and did not want to know either the law of God, or the instructions of David and Solomon, or the threats of the prophets, or the words of the righteous. The righteous both spoke and wrote that glory and honor would be for good deeds, but for evil deeds—blasphemy, and mockery, and torment. And the righteous sought to inspire people with their words and move them to good deeds, while the wicked they admonished to loathe their deeds and turn their faces away from them. But the evildoers of whom we speak cast the teachings of the righteous away from themselves like an unbearable burden, and did whatever their heart desired, and did not remember either the captivity that the Jews suffered under Nebuchadnezzar, or the destruction that Antiochus brought upon them, or the Egyptian slavery, or God’s deliverance. And then, at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, when the Jews remember their exodus from Egypt and liberation from Egyptian slavery, the robber-sicarii entrenched in the fortress of Masada, which is located not far from Jerusalem, at night ravaged the surrounding villages, and burned them, and killed seven thousand local inhabitants, and returned to Masada, howling like wolves for joy, since they had shown their desperation, the accursed.
