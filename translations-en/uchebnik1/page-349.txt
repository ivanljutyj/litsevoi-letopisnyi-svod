These monks spoke boldly about the guilty and were ready to shed their blood for all, to deliver the suffering from the executions awaiting them. And so it came to pass.

Where now are those who have clothed themselves in temptations, and displayed deep darkness, and hold sticks in their right hands, false philosophers, dog’s offscouring, who are worse than the dogs under the table and do everything for the belly’s sake? For those who lived in the city all fled into the wilderness and hid, and renounced their property; but these monks alone by their deeds showed true wisdom, and when all had gathered, they stood in the midst and destroyed evil. And they did not gather over many days, but on one day they came together, and spoke, and having destroyed the calamity, they returned again to their dwellings. Such wisdom is given to people by Christ."

On the Head of John the Baptist

In the time of Theodosius the precious head of John the Baptist was brought to Constantinople; it had first been found by two monks who were in the Macedonian heresy, who came from Jerusalem and stayed in Cilicia.

When Mardonius the eunuch learned of this, he sent word to Emperor Valens, and by imperial command they began to carry the precious head to Constantinople; they sent it placed on a chariot.

And the messengers reached an inn. On the next day they wanted to continue on their way, but the mules would not move from that place. Then the holy head was placed in the village of Kosilae, since God did not permit Valens to be deemed worthy of such a gift.

To Theodosius, the pious emperor, God, rewarding according to merits, permitted the precious head to be brought to Constantinople. The emperor ordered it to be placed in one place, where he also built a beautiful and large church. Under Theodosius Capito, bishop of Cherson, was martyred. Also at that time Cyriacus the Hermit struggled, from the city of Corinth, son of John the presbyter and his mother Eudoxia.

On the Alexandrian Temple

In Alexandria there was a pagan temple; Archbishop Theophilus asked Theodosius for permission to cleanse this temple and turn it into a church. Before the people Theophilus denounced the Hellenic fornication, and their cunning, and madness, and filth.

The Hellenic people marveled at this, and were stirred up, and committed many murders. Theodosius learned of the murders and glorified the slain Christians as those killed for the sake of orthodoxy.
