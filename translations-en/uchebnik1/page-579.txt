When the tsar entered the Great Church and went into that metatorion, he found this in the place where he prayed, read it, and great sorrow seized him, and he sought the one who had done it.

It happened that there was an eclipse of the moon. And the tsar summoned Panteleon, metropolitan of Synnada, wishing to learn about the moon’s disappearance. When he was entering the church, Samonas asked him: “Upon whom will the evil be?” He said: “Upon you. And if you survive the thirteenth day of the month of June, from that time no evil will befall you.” To the tsar he said that this calamity rose to the second person.

The tsar understood that this evil would come upon Alexander, his brother. Then Tsiriphon confessed privately to the tsar that Samonas had composed the letter.

He immediately took Samonas into his house, tonsured him as a monk, and led him to the monastery of Euthymius the patriarch. Then, when he was accused again, he led him to the monastery of Martinakios.

He made Constantine parakoimomenos and arranged a monastery for him in the place called At the Venerable Ones.

And he came there together with Euthymius the patriarch, and consecrated it.

In the month of October there was a naval battle of Himerios the logothete with the Saracens Damian and Leo. The stratigos in Samos was Romanos, who afterward reigned. Himerios was defeated and barely escaped; all who were with him perished.

And the tsar fell ill with a disease of the bowels, reaching the last extremity of weakness, so that he barely could address the people during the fast, saying: “You see me unwell and exhausted by illness; surely I will not remain with you until Christ’s Resurrection.”

And there was a fire in the Great Church, where candles are made; the whole archive was burned, and the sakella itself.

And in May, on the eleventh day, Tuesday, fifteenth indiction, the tsar Leo died, handing the kingdom to Alexander, his brother. And seeing him coming to him, as they relate, Leo said: “Behold, an evil time is coming after thirteen months.” He earnestly urged Alexander, asking him to protect his son Constantine. In his time in Rome the pope was Formosus the heretic, who turned the Germans away from the Greek faith.

Reign 47th, of Alexander, who reigned in the city of Constantine

After Leo the Wise reigned Alexander, his brother, son of Basil, one year and twenty-seven days, with Constantine, son of Leo.
