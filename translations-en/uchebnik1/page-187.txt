And the emperor, at the head of the strongest cavalry, joined battle with the Jews and killed the twelve men who were in front, and drove the rest back to the city, and saved the engines from fire.

It happened then that a Jew was taken alive, and by Titus’s command he was crucified before the walls, so that by this sight the townspeople might be terrified and inclined to submit to the Romans. When the Jews retreated, a soldier named John, that is Johanan, was talking on the city wall with an acquaintance of his, and one of the Arabians struck him in the chest with an arrow and brought great lamentation to the Jews, for this John was famed for strength and wisdom.

And at night there was sudden confusion among the Romans. Titus ordered three towers to be set up, each fifty cubits high, and at midnight the towers fell by themselves, and there was a great crash, and fear fell upon the soldiers, and they thought the Jews had come out against them, and they seized their weapons and struck one another, until Titus set heralds who announced to all: “The enemy has not attacked. Why do you kill your own?” And the confusion did not soon cease.

And again other towers were set up. The Jews yielded before them, for the Romans struck them from above, and the Jews could not throw their missiles as far as the Romans because of the height, nor could they set the towers on fire, since they were bound with iron. And if the Jews wished to avoid the arrows shot from the towers, they could not hinder the “rams,” which by their constant blows broke through the walls. At last one great “ram,” which was called the Victor, broke the wall. The Jews were worn out by frequent battles and vigils and decided to flee behind the inner walls of the city, and they said: “It is superfluous to fight for the outer walls, if two more walls remain, stronger still.”

And the Romans entered behind the wall where it had been broken by the Victor, since all the Jews fled, coming down from the wall, and they ran to the second wall. And the Romans who had passed behind the wall opened the gates to the other soldiers. And so the first wall was taken, and they fought for it for fifteen days, and this was on the seventeenth day of the month Artemisios, that is May. And the Romans destroyed half the wall on the south side, in the same place where Cestius had done so.

Titus then rose from his former camp and moved to the place where the Assyrians had once stood, and at once began to test the new barrier. And the Jews divided and struck hard from the walls, some from the Tower of Antonia and the northern passages, while others went around the tomb of John and fought at the gate through which the water came in. And the Jews often ran out from the gates and fought with swords. In close combat the Romans prevailed; from the walls, the Jews. The Romans had strength and experience, the Jews rebelliousness and despair.
