While such was the situation inside the city, Titus rode around Jerusalem on the outside with chosen horsemen and looked from which side to attack. And Titus could not find a convenient place, since ravines and gullies surrounded the city everywhere, and the first wall was invincible. And he planned to attack opposite the tomb of John, that is Johanan, the high priest. There the walls were low, and Titus thought that if he broke through these walls, he would take the Upper City from there, the tower of Antonia and the temple.

And then Titus’s commander Nicanor approached the walls with Joseph, trying to speak with the townspeople about peace, and was wounded by an arrow in the left shoulder. And from this shot from the city Titus understood that the townspeople were raging against those who spoke what was useful to them. And Titus began to burn and cut down and destroy what was before the city, and he ordered the soldiers to make a siege mound. And they divided into three parts and began to make the mound. Between the workers Titus placed archers and javelin throwers, and before them throwers of short spears and stones, so that they might prevent enemy raids on the rising mound and not let the fighters stick out from behind the wall enclosures. And they cut down all the trees, and soon laid bare the place before the wall, and carried trees for the mound, and all rushed to its making. The Jews also did not sleep. The people who endured robberies and bloodshed then sighed with hope, since they saw the Romans advancing and their own in confusion before the external enemy.

John, that is Johanan, wanted to go out with his soldiers and fight the enemies, but he feared Simon Niger. Simon Niger was closer to the enemy and against his will prepared for battle, and placed military engines on the walls, which he had taken earlier from Cestius. And other engines were obtained from the tower of Antonia after the murder of the guards there. But the rebels did not know how to set up projectiles and throw them, and did not use the engines; stones and arrows they launched at those making the mound.

Some rebels ran out beyond the city walls and clashed with the Romans. For those making the fortifications, a cover from stones and arrows was ox hides placed above them. Roman shooters and throwers of spears and stones resisted the Jews running out from the city. They threw painted stones, with which they killed not only the front but also the rear. For a stone flew two versts and struck those it hit. And some long-range engines struck thirty people at once.
