The Tale of the Latins, How They Fell Away from the Orthodox Patriarchs

Under Emperor Constantine and his mother Irene, after Adrian, Leo became Pope of Rome. Through the envy of Adrian’s relatives, Pope Leo was deprived of his see and expelled from Rome.

He sent to Emperor Constantine and his mother Irene in Constantinople, asking for help, but received none.

Then he turned to Charles, king of the Franks (the Frankish princes call their kings “Fruzi”). Charles helped him greatly, returned him to Rome, and restored him to the papal throne.

Then Pope Leo began to urge the Roman princes to appoint a pious ruler for themselves, “so that Rome might again become the capital of the empire.”

And so, in gratitude to Charles, Pope Leo summoned him to Rome and crowned him emperor according to the Greek rite, adding (I do not know from what motive) anointing with consecrated oil from head to foot, as was customary in Old Testament times.

Thus for the first time the unity of Rome and Constantinople was broken, and, as it were between a mother and her daughter—a comely young maiden—the dividing sword came in. Fiercely it cut New Rome, the reigning city, away from ancient Rome grown old. Although Charles himself was a Christian, like his father and grandfather, and honored the commandments of Christ, priests and teachers came who secretly professed heresy, and began to corrupt the flock of Christ and to teach their heresies not only secretly but openly, enjoying the favor of the king, saying that the Holy Spirit proceeds not only from the Father but also from the Son, and that the Liturgy should be served on unleavened bread, and introducing many other heretical errors.

Pope Leo, although he saw that his flock had readily accepted the teaching of Charles’s priests and was walking the path of perdition, had no wise teachers in Rome capable of resisting this wicked heretical teaching. Nor could he send for such men to Constantinople, because at that time the iconoclast Theophilus reigned there.

Then he sends to Patriarch Thomas in Jerusalem, asking him to send learned men to him who could expose and root out this heresy.
