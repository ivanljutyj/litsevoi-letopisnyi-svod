Reign 36th, of Constantine, who reigned in Constantinople

After Leo, his son Constantine reigned, 17 years, and his mother Irene.

Under that emperor the pious hopes began to be fulfilled, and judgment began with boldness, and the word of God began to spread, and monasteries began to be built in all quietness.

In the Long Walls, that is, in the Thracian porches or forecourts, a certain man, while digging, found a hand with an inscription: “Christ is to be born of Mary the Virgin, and I believe in Him. But under Constantine and Irene, under the Orthodox emperors, O sun, you will see me again.”

Also the body of the all-praised martyr Euphemia, previously sunk in the sea in a coffin under the persecuting emperor who hated the saints, was found on the island of Lemnos. After Vespers at night, taking it with due honor, they placed Euphemia’s body again in her church, into which that Constantine called Dung-named had ordered dung, that is, manure, to be thrown. But this Constantine together with his mother Irene removed the filth and cleansed everything again.

After this Aaron, the Saracen prince, armed himself for war and came to Constantinople with a great force, and stopped at Chrysopolis on the other side of the Bosphorus.

The emperor sent soldiers, and Vania received the man who conveyed Aaron’s request that there be peace between the Greeks and the Saracens.

And Constantine believed this, and a man named Staurakios, by rank logothete, and Peter the magistros, and Antony the domestikos, and their children and elders came to Aaron without weapons, and were taken by him.

Therefore they became alarmed in Constantinople, and gave many gifts to the Saracens, and the Saracens withdrew, having made peace with the Christians.

And when this true peace had come, Emperor Constantine went out with his mother into the Thracian lands with musical organs, songs, and many people.

Those same rebuilt Beroea and Anchialus and returned with great joy and gladness.

Patriarch Paul the Cypriot fell ill, and learned from this of his deposition by divine revelation, and left his throne and went to the monastery of Saint Florus and became a monk.
