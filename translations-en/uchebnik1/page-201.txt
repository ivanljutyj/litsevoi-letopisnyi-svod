And their souls and bodies were as if alien to them: the souls knew no suffering, while the bodies did not notice wounds or weariness. They tormented the dead like dogs, and filled the prisons with the sick.

Simon Niger also did not leave Matthew without torture—the man who was high priest and enjoyed the people’s trust and reverence. Even earlier, when this Matthew saw that the people were suffering from the zealots and from John, that is Johanan, he advised them to summon Simon Niger to crush John, and they did so. When Simon Niger entered the city, he became equally hostile both to the people and to the one who had advised them to let him in. And they brought this Matthew the high priest, and slandered him, and accused him of being friendly to the Romans. Simon Niger did not even allow Matthew to answer, but condemned him to death together with three sons. His fourth son had fled to Titus before this. Matthew begged Simon to kill him before his children, in gratitude for the city gates having been opened to Simon. Simon Niger did not listen, but cut down Matthew’s children before him, and then him as well. He did this on the walls, so that the Romans would see. And the brigands mocked the Romans, saying: “Come and take your friend.” Simon Niger did not even allow the bodies of Matthew and his sons to be buried.

After Matthew and his sons, Simon Niger executed Ananias the priest and Aristeus, and fifteen other people. He shut Joseph’s relatives in prison. And Simon Niger ordered that no one should associate with his own friends, and that people should not gather together. Those who were caught in an assembly, or who wept for any reason, Simon Niger ordered to be killed. Seeing these deeds, a man named Judas, one of Simon Niger’s subordinates, was horrified at the murders without guilt, and summoned the men loyal to him, and said: “How long shall we endure this monstrous malice? What hope do we have of salvation? How long shall we remain loyal to a brigand? Has not famine crept up on us? Are not the Romans already within the city? Simon Niger does not trust even the one who did him good; he has repaid his benefactor with evil. And we all tremble before Simon and await execution. The Romans’ promises are reliable and saving for us. Let us go, surrender the city to the Romans, save ourselves, and bring just retribution upon Simon Niger.” And those gathered agreed to this. In the morning they met one another secretly, so that the rest would not know, and at the third hour of the day they called to the Romans from the tower. But the Romans did not hurry to come, some out of pride, others because they did not trust the Jews. Many delayed because they hoped in a short time to take the city without loss to themselves.
