So Philippicus went to the emperor, leaving his wife sitting on haircloth and ashes, weeping.

Then Philippicus entered the imperial chamber and threw himself at the emperor’s feet. The emperor ordered all his men to go out, and then, rising from the throne, he fell at Philippicus’s feet, saying: “Forgive me, brother, for God’s sake, for I have sinned against you. Until now I thought that you were plotting against me, but now I have learned that you were innocent of such a thought. I beg you, brother, tell me, do you know of a certain commander Phocas who is in our lands and ranks?”

Philippicus answered: “I know, emperor, the commander Phocas; he was sent by the soldiers who oppose your dominion.” The emperor asked: “What is he like?” Philippicus said: “Young, timid—that is, fearful—and quarrelsome.” And the emperor answered: “If he is timid, then he is a murderer.” And Maurice told Philippicus his dream.
