But Emperor Theophilus ordered them to be seized and tortured. Saint Michael the Protosyncellus, and also his disciple Job, in those torments gave up their holy souls to the Lord.

But after many tortures Emperor Theophilus ordered the faces of Theodore and Theophanes to be branded, for which he commanded an iron plate to be made and these verses to be inscribed on it.

Verses of Emperor Theophilus. “To everyone who wishes to visit this city, that is, Jerusalem, where the most pure feet of Christ trod for the salvation of the world. There appeared in this holy place vessels of deceit and demonic delusion (so Theophilus calls Saints Michael, Theophanes, and Theodore). Having by their false faith wrought many foul and abominable deeds, they, like apostates, by their impious reasoning drove themselves far away from Jerusalem, but even upon coming to this sovereign city of Constantinople, they did not abandon their evil madness. Therefore their faces, as of malefactors, are branded, and they themselves are condemned and expelled.” This inscription Theophilus burned with red-hot iron on the faces of Saints Theophanes and Theodore and sent them into exile, where after some time the great Theodore departed to the Lord. At the hour of his death the singing of angels was heard, and together with them his voice was also heard.

Theophanes remained in exile until the very death of Theophilus. For this reason neither Michael, the protosyncellus of the holy Church of Jerusalem, nor his disciples, Theophanes and Theodore, reached Rome.

But Pope Leo, seeing that the flock of Christ was being led to perdition by the aforementioned heretics of Charles, was perplexed as to why his request had remained unanswered. Then he ordered a confession of the Orthodox faith to be written on tablets and fixed to the wall in the church, so that everyone entering might correctly confess the Christian faith: the Son, begotten of the Father, and the Holy Spirit, proceeding from the Father. This was done to confirm the Orthodox in the faith and to denounce the heretics of Charles.

After the repose of Saint Leo, Benedict became Pope of Rome. A Latin by birth, he knew the works of the holy fathers of both the East and the West, and he wrote a confession of the Orthodox faith in both Latin and Greek, and he ordered all Christians, Greeks and Latins, to read what was written and confess Orthodoxy, denouncing the aforementioned heretics and not listening to their false teachings.
