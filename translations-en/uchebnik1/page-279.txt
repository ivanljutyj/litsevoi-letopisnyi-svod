The 36th reign, of Aurelian, who was enthroned in Rome in the year 5778 from Adam (the 278th year from Christ)

After Quintillus, Aurelian reigned for 6 years. And he was killed by soldiers between Heraclea and Byzantium in the so-called New City, and was buried there.

Aurelian had a certain informer who knew everything that was done and said. Once he was punished by him for some reason, and this man, having forged the imperial signature so that it resembled a document, wrote an order concerning the king’s nobles, as though sending them to death. They, in fear, killed him. Under him Saint Chariton was martyred, and from Iconium Mamant, son of Theodotus and his mother Rufina, from the city of Gangra, and Dionysius, bishop of Alexandria.

Concerning the accursed Manes.

In the time of Aurelian, as Eusebius says, the accursed Manes appeared, through whom the Manichaean name spread among many, whereby Mani, foul and accursed, took root, and in his madness portrayed himself as Christ and the Holy Spirit. For this reason he led about with him 12 disciples, like Christ. And having gathered all evil from all heresies, he came from Persia to the land of Morea by God’s permission. This same mad Mani, who was also called Scythianus, was by origin from the Rachmans; and he had as teacher Uddan, formerly called Terebinthus. He, taught by that Scythianus who glorified the Hellenic faith, loved the heresy of Podkleos, which taught that two principles are opposed to each other. Having entered Persia, he began to tell about himself that he was born of a virgin and raised in the mountains. And he wrote four books: one he called Mysteries, another Gospel, another Treasure, the fourth Chapters. For this Budas, who is also Terebinthus, perished, crushed by an unclean spirit. A certain woman with whom he lived and died, having inherited the property and foul books of the wicked man, bought a seven-year-old slave boy named Kuverik, taught him letters, and, having freed him, made him heir of all she had. He, taking Budas’s books and belongings, went about Persia, calling himself Manes and imagining that he was bearing witness to the faith, and passed the books off as his own writings. The Persian king had him flayed alive, since he was guilty of the death of his son. A child of the king fell ill and was treated by many physicians. Mani said: “I will heal him even without medicines.” And so, having driven the physicians away, he killed the youth through neglect and vanity.
