After this, chosen holy men restored the holy and honorable icons in their former places and returned to the Church her former splendor. A new miracle of God was proclaimed to all. From that time the holy confessors decreed that this celebration be annual on the first Sunday of Great Lent, to the glory of God. We also now honor the memory of this council and celebrate the glorification of the holy and honorable icons, while we anathematize the godless heretics.

Then the empress arranged a solemn banquet in the palace for the holy men and herself honored it with her presence. The golden-mouthed Theophanes, Bishop of Nicaea, was also here, his face disfigured by torture. Empress Theodora shuddered as she looked at him and marveled at his courage and long-suffering. Seeing this, he said to her sternly and with bitterness: “Do you see, empress, these wounds on my face? For them I will contend in judgment with your husband there where the Judge of All judges impartially, without regard to persons or royal raiment, and fearing not the mighty of this world.”

The empress trembled when she heard this and cried out from the depths of her soul: “Is this what you, O glorious man, and the whole council promised me concerning the emperor?” Then the divine Methodius, the golden-mouthed shepherd of the flock of Christ in New Rome, answered Theophanes’ bold words and comforted the pious empress.

As with softening oil he healed her soul with his words, driving away the clouds of tears and calming the heavy storm in her heart, saying: “Let us marvel, brothers, at the love for mankind of the Lord our God! A man so defiled was shown mercy after death! And understand what the prayers of His hierarchs can do, if they delivered Theophilus from eternal torment! Let us marvel also at the faith and love for God of the blessed Empress Theodora. How many sorrows she endured, how she cared for the salvation of her husband. She spared neither gold nor silver, giving all to the needy. To deliver her husband from torments, she called all to pray to God for him—not only hierarchs but also monks, the poor, widows, and orphans—and so after death she delivered her husband from torments by her alms. Of such wives it is said that such a one will save even after her husband’s death. But it is not easy to find such among the wives of today; more often one meets those who do not grieve over their husband’s death and in all things always seek only their own advantage.
