These slanders, composed by the scribe Samonas, which they threw into the metatorium, that is, the monastery.

When the tsar entered the Great Church and went into that metatorium, he found this in the place where he prayed and read it, and great grief seized him, and he sought the one who had done this.

It happened that there was an eclipse of the moon. And the tsar summoned Panteleon, Metropolitan of Synada, wishing to learn about the moon’s disappearance. When he was entering the church, Samonas asked him: “Upon whom will the evil fall?” He said: “Upon you. And if you survive the 13th day of the month of June, from that time no evil will come to you.” To the tsar he said that this calamity pertained to the second person.

The tsar understood that this evil would be fulfilled upon Alexander, his brother. Afterwards Tsiriphon confessed to the tsar privately that Samonas had composed the letter.

He immediately, having brought Samonas into his house, tonsured him as a monk and led him to the monastery of the patriarch Euthymius. Afterwards, when he was again denounced, he led him to the monastery of Martinakios.

He made Constantine parakoimomenos and arranged a monastery for him in the place called At the Reverend Ones.

And he arrived there together with the patriarch Euthymius, and consecrated it.

In the month of October there was a sea battle of Himerios the logothete with the Saracens Damian and Leo. The strategos of Samos was Romanos, who afterwards reigned. Himerios was defeated and barely escaped; all who were with him perished.

And the tsar fell ill with a disease of the intestines, reaching the last infirmity, so that he barely could address the people during the fast, saying thus: “You see me unhealthy and exhausted by illness; surely I shall not remain with you until the Resurrection of Christ.”

And there was a fire in the Great Church, where candles are made; the whole archive also burned, and the sakella itself.

And in the month of May, on the 11th day, on Tuesday of the 15th indiction, Tsar Leo died, handing the kingdom to his brother Alexander. And seeing him entering to him, as they relate, Leo said: “Behold, an evil time is coming after thirteen months.” He earnestly urged Alexander, asking him to protect his son Constantine. In his time, the pope in Rome was Formosus the heretic, who turned the Germans away from the Greek faith.

The 47th Reign: Alexander, who reigned in the city of Constantine

After Leo the Wise, his brother Alexander, son of Basil, reigned one year and 27 days, with Constantine, son of Leo.
