And taking boldness from the boldness of the Bishop of Alexandria, the townspeople, seizing power in the reign of Theodosius, burned Hypatia at the stake, a woman famous as a philosopher, of whom it was said that she was great; and that woman was old.

In the reign of Theodosius, Crete, the great island, received punishment from God’s wrath; it lies in the midst of the sea and has 100 cities, as Euripides wrote of it.

All the surroundings of Crete suffered also. Its public bath in the capital of Crete, the city of Gortyn, also fell; that bath was built by Julius Caesar. Its vaults were twelve in number, according to the twelve months, and for each month tribute was given from a vault. Except for one stone masonry, all twelve vaults were burned, and one could see them in such a strange state.

Theodosius also built many other things, including the Golden Gate in Constantinople, which Nymphian gilded, the governor of Valentinian, the Roman emperor, whom the boyar Maximus killed and took the kingdom in Rome.

This same Emperor Theodosius offended Antiochus, a nobleman in the rank of praepositus, that is, chamberlain, and a patrician, influential in the palace and governing the palace; he had been Theodosius’s tutor while his father was alive, and as chief chamberlain he governed the whole city of Rome under his father.

And this Antiochus continued, after the death of Theodosius’s father, as a patrician, to lord it over Theodosius. And the emperor became angry with him, and brought him out before the people, and had him shorn, and made him a priest at the Great Church of Constantinople, and ordered that eunuchs should not enter the rank of patricians.

The chamberlains were deprived of their rank, that is, of the palace praepositure. And this Antiochus died while being a presbyter. Theodosius appointed as governor the patrician Cyrus, a philosopher, a man wise in all things, and this Cyrus held both offices for 14 years.

This Cyrus the prefect would enter the chariot, then again, with his assistants, the imperial halls. And he would ride out in the chariot of the city prefect, and attended to every matter, and renewed all Constantinople.

This Cyrus was pure in all things; and the townspeople cried out about him thus when Theodosius was watching at the hippodrome: “Constantine founded it, Cyrus renewed it; he is the Augustus.” And the emperor was angry that the people cried out thus and said of Cyrus that after Constantine he had renewed the city, and the emperor was angry with Cyrus. And he was slandered, that the patrician Cyrus alone ruled, and he was cast out of his rank.

And Cyrus fled and was tonsured a priest, and they sent him to Asia and appointed him bishop in the city of Smyrna.
