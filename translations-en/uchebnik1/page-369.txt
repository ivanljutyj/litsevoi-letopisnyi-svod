Concerning the Maiden Who Killed Attila

In the reign of Theodosius and Valentinian, Attila turned against Rome and Constantinople; he was of the Hun tribe and had a multitude of warriors, tens of thousands.

And Attila sent a Gothic envoy to Valentinian the emperor in Rome with these words: “My lord and your lord Attila commands that you prepare for him your royal chambers.”

Likewise Attila sent one of the Goths as envoy to Theodosius the emperor in Constantinople. The chief counselor of Valentinian in Rome heard Attila’s words, full of boundless insolence, and feared to answer Attila; and he went to Alaric in Gaul, who was surrounded by soldiers because of Honorius, and the Roman counselor persuaded Alaric and brought him against Attila, since he had destroyed the Roman cities. And immediately Alaric suddenly attacked Attila, who stood encamped near the Danube, and cut down many thousands of his warriors. In the clash Alaric also received a wound from an arrow and died. As the writings tell elsewhere, that King Attila came from the Northern lands with a vast army and occupied the Roman cities.

And Attila besieged the city of Rome with many thousands of warriors, many times desiring to take the city of Rome. And there was then a princess, a beautiful maiden; she lived in an annex to the tower, and she was thirty years old. Then King Attila, learning of her, that she was very beautiful, asked for her for himself and said: “If you do not give her to me, I will destroy your city of Rome.”

Then all the Roman elders and governors went to the maiden and implored her, saying: “Come out quickly to King Attila, so that he does not cruelly destroy our city of Rome and our city does not perish.”

The maiden, since they had entreated her, went to Attila. When she sat at his feet and he lay reclining, blood came from his nose, and from that he died. The maiden took Attila’s cloak and went and told the townspeople that King Attila was dead.

Attila’s warriors took his body and carried it through the lands of the Germans and the Vlachs and laid it in the land of the Hungarians. And they supposed of that maiden that she had killed Attila. The wise Priscus the Thracian wrote the story of that maiden. Others said that Aetius, a Roman nobleman who held the rank of patrician, persuaded Attila’s swordsman, and he stabbed him, and Aetius returned to Rome, having defeated Attila.
