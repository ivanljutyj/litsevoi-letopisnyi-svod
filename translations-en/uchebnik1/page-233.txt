Then one could see humble and frightened those who had formerly been proud and violent. Such a change aroused pity, though those who had changed were great villains. And some of the robbers who fled first cried out, “The western walls are destroyed!” Others wept and wailed, “The battle is already within the walls!” To the leaders it seemed that they already saw the whole Roman army. Fear prevented them from seeing what was actually there. And their last battle stood before their eyes. And falling to the ground, the rebels bewailed their madness and could not flee, as though their sinews had been cut.

Then everyone could realize that the power of God led the Romans against the ungodly and granted them good fortune in battle and a most glorious victory over the rebels. For the leaders of the rebels themselves surrendered their strongholds and left the towers. Even if the rebels had sat in these towers for many years, prepared, the enemy could not have taken them by force. Unless they had been forced to surrender for lack of food.

The Romans had shed so much sweat taking less strong walls, but these strongest structures they took thanks to good fortune. They could not have been taken either by “rams” or by other devices, so that these three towers were invincible. The rebels abandoned them—or, one may say more, by Divine judgment they were driven out of them. Then the leaders, surrounded by the remaining rebels, fled to the ravines near Siloam and there recovered a little from their fear. And they had little courage, less than before, for need and fear had broken their strength.

And when the rebels suddenly attacked the Roman guards, they were defeated by them and scattered through the underground passages, each wherever he could. The Romans thus conquered and, having taken these walls, set their standards on the tower, and shouted, and rejoiced, and clapped, and sang, as they usually did in victories. And they exulted and were proud, since they had reached the end of the WAR.

It was easier for them at the end of the war than at the beginning. And when without a single wound they took the last walls, they themselves did not believe what had happened and, seeing no enemy, marveled. And the Romans went through the streets with swords and cut down all whom they found, and burned houses filled with people hiding in them.

And Roman soldiers entered houses to plunder and saw dead people lying there, who had died of hunger, and—horrified by what they saw—went out empty-handed. On these dead the Romans looked with pity, but they did not spare the living. Therefore streets and roads and courtyards were piled with the slain. A lake of blood stood throughout the city, so that both the flame and everything burning were quenched by blood.
