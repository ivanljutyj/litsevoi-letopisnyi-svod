His father had appointed over him as guardian of the kingdom the first and wisest of his counselors, Yezdigerd, prince of Persia.

When the tyrant Yezdigerd learned of the conspiracy being prepared against Theodosius, he gathered to march against the conspirators with a great army. But they, fearing the danger of an invasion by the barbarians, ceased preparing the conspiracy against the young emperor.

This Theodosius had a sister, pure in countenance and pure in life, named Pulcheria. This maiden chose to preserve the inviolable treasure of purity, renounced all association with men, and showed warmth of soul in every good work. Not only did she herself do so, but she also instructed her brother in every good.

Concerning Eudokia the Empress

In Athens there was a man named Leontius, who in old age had attained all wisdom. He had two sons by one wife and a daughter named Athenais; the daughter was beautiful and cheerful.

When this man was preparing to depart this life, he left all his wealth to his sons, for he was very rich. To his daughter he ordered only one hundred gold coins to be given, and said: “Her beauty is inheritance enough for her, for it will bring her excellent feminine glory.”

Having made this bequest, Leontius died. But Athenais’s brothers were evil-natured and left the girl altogether without inheritance. Wounded in heart, this maiden Athenais came to her mother’s sister and revealed to her her grief and suffering.

Her aunt generously endowed the maiden and went with her to the reigning city, and came to Empress Pulcheria, adorned with virtues, asking help for the maiden, who had been insulted by her brothers. Empress Pulcheria marveled at the maiden’s beauty and the brightness of her face, at her prudence and wisdom, and, having questioned her, learned that the maiden had kept her beauty undefiled in purity.

And at once Pulcheria cleansed the maiden with the Bath, that is, Holy Baptism, and named her Eudokia.

And Pulcheria gave the maiden in marriage to her brother, Emperor Theodosius; Paulinus also assisted in this, for he was the emperor’s first friend.

This Eudokia bore to Theodosius Eudoxia, whom he gave in marriage to Valentinian. Valentinian held the scepter of the Roman Empire after Honorius, his uncle, and was born of Placidia, daughter of Theodosius the Great.
