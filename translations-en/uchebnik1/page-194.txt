Come to your senses and acknowledge your guilt. O your iron hearts! Throw down your weapons, take pity on our fatherland. Look around, see what beauty, what a city, what a temple, what gifts from all peoples! All this you now consign to flame in your stubbornness and petrification. Spare your families. Set before your eyes your children, wives, and parents, whom destruction awaits very soon, either from hunger or from the sword. I know that my glory too will perish, and my noble family, and my mother, and my wife, and my house. If you think that I beg you for the sake of all this, then kill them. Let my blood be shed for your salvation. If you think of me, I will gladly die for you.” Thus Joseph cried out to the rebels, but they did not yield and firmly resolved that they would not surrender.

The people, however, inclined toward the Romans, and people sold their property and precious things for gold. And they swallowed the gold so that the rebels would not see it, and thus fled to the Romans. There they discharged the gold through the anus and used it for what was necessary.

Titus sent the refugees away to the lands, wherever each wished. John, that is Yohanan, and Simon Niger then ceased to allow anyone to leave the city, but killed all the refugees.

Then the famine increased every day. There was no wheat for sale, and soldiers began to go about searching for it in the houses. If they found it, they killed the owners because they had hidden it before; if they did not find it, they killed the owners all the same, claiming that they were hiding wheat now. And many shut themselves in inner rooms and did not grind the wheat but ate it as it was. Their food was pitiful and worthy of tears. Famine was worse than all misfortunes. Wives snatched food from husbands, children from fathers, and mothers from infants; they squeezed it out of the throats of their loved ones and tore it from their hands. No one gave even a drop of life out of mercy. But even living thus, people could not hide. Fighters went everywhere, and if they saw a closed house, they understood that food was being taken inside, and at once they broke down the doors and rushed in and squeezed the food out of their throats, and beat the old men who would not let go of the food, and the women hiding bread, and took from the children, and dashed the children themselves against a stone. And they tortured those who hid food with unprecedented tortures: they thrust peas into the secret member and a sharp stake into the anus—only so that they would say where a handful of flour or a piece of bread was hidden. The torturers did not go hungry, but satisfied themselves with all that they obtained in this way.

Some went outside the walls and gathered wild herbs, hiding from the Roman soldiers. If on the way back the rebels met them, they took away what they had gathered. They begged them and invoked the dread name of God, that they might give them at least part of the herbs, since they had labored and brought them at peril to their lives. But the rebels gave them nothing.
