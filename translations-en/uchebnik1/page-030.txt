He ruled over all the land. In his reign he built a sanctuary to Dios in Rome and renewed the Capitol, making these two buildings magnificent. In the thirty-ninth year of his reign and in the tenth month he established and gave command to make a census of all the lands subject to him, which the Romans then held, during Agrippa’s second governorship. And all that was subject to Rome was described by Eumenius and Autalus, Roman nobles.

Great was the fear Augustus inspired, for he was very fierce and wrathful, and quickly pronounced condemnation, but afterward came to repentance and gentleness. Once he sat administering justice and condemned many people to immediate and swift death.

A certain wise man could not approach and give instruction to Caesar because of the crowd, so he wrote on a sheet: “Murderer, cease judging,” and sealed it and threw it to Caesar; he took it and read it.

And Caesar rose and departed from the throne and left all affairs unfinished; and with him was a wise teacher from Alexandria named Athenodorus. He leaned to Caesar’s ear and said: “O Caesar Sebastus, one thing is lacking in you—do not pronounce condemnation quickly, but first count silently to twenty-four, and when rage has subsided in you, then without anger judge each according to justice.”

This wise Athenodorus saw that Caesar was possessed by lust and was wholly lecherous, and that desire for married women tormented him most, and that by his authority he ordered husbands to bring their wives to him. Athenodorus wished to deter Caesar from such passion.

And one day Athenodorus saw a certain counselor weeping because he had been ordered to lock his wife in a litter and send her to Caesar. Athenodorus comforted him and ordered himself, instead of the counselor’s wife, to be locked in the litter and sent to Caesar. And Athenodorus entered the litter with a sword.

Caesar received the litter and broke the seals, and then Athenodorus came out, brandishing a sword. Caesar was amazed when he saw Athenodorus and grew numb with fear. Athenodorus said: “I did this to teach you. If another had done this, one of those whose wives you have taken and defiled, he would certainly have killed you.” And Caesar bound himself by oaths that he would do such things no more.

When Athenodorus was departing to the land that had brought him forth, Caesar sighed from the depths and said: “I still need your good counsel; stay with me, and Rome shall have you.”

This Caesar drove buffoons away from him, honored only the wise, and honored them diligently. And for this reason they honored Athenodorus as they had honored no other of the nobles who died. And Athenodorus said: “It is well for me that I die leaving Caesar alive.”
