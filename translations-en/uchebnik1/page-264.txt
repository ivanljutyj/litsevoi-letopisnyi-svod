Having divided fire and water just as He divided light and darkness, He distributed them throughout His creation. And there is water above the firmament, and fire, that is, brightness, beneath the firmament; and there are also water and fire beneath the earth. But the water gathered in one place on the surface of the earth was called the sea; and the water that remained beneath the earth was called the abyss, from which, like certain whirlwinds, springs issue forth for the life of us and every beast, and from there hot waters also come up. Water located farther from the fire comes forth cold by God’s command, while water located near the fire is warmed there and comes forth together with steam. But if in some places water does not come close to the fire, then it flows out there neither very warm nor too cold.

But the fire that is beneath the earth is for the torment of demons and sinful people. The water of the netherworld is the coldest and is thickened into pieces of ice, which is also called the cold place, being likewise a torment for those who do not follow the Lord’s commandments. And that there is fire beneath the earth, let the fire in Sicily persuade you, and the fire coming out in Libya; and in other various places too there is fire similarly manifested, burning in dreadful Gehenna all who have done deeds worthy of fire. Of it the great Moses also spoke on behalf of God: “For fire is kindled from My wrath, it shall burn down to the lowest hell, it shall devour the earth and its fruit, and it shall scorch the foundations of the mountains.” Isaiah also bears witness, clearly foretelling to sinners the torment prepared for them in unquenchable fire, and cries: “Who shall declare to you the eternal place? You who walk by the light of your fire and by the flame which you have kindled; and they shall go out and see the bones of men who have transgressed against Me, says the Lord; for their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched, and they shall be a sight for all flesh.”

And the divinely eloquent Chrysostom also says: “Look also at another wonder—how opposite natures meet. For what is more fiery than lightning, or colder than water? Yet they touch, and are not fused, and are not mixed, but each of them preserves its own constitution: fire in water, and water in fire. And neither the one nor the other has dried up or quenched the other; and lightning is hotter and brighter and stronger than the fire of the sun, as is testified by eyes accustomed to the sight of constantly shining rays, but which cannot endure the sight of lightning at all.” For the sun goes around heaven all day, but this illuminates the whole universe in a short time, as Christ also testifies, saying: “As lightning comes from the east, so it appears even to the west.” Then he says: “He brings forth the winds from His treasuries.” And this other phenomenon also brings us no little benefit, for it accomplishes much in our life, restoring exhausted bodies.
