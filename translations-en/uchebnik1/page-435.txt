The divine Chrysostom says: “If anyone does not believe in Gehenna, let him think on Sodom and Gomorrah and understand the torment that was in these cities, which is still there now. This shows a clear likeness of eternal torment. For there not only the earth but the very air and water received punishment. As the form of a burnt body remains, and the form is discerned in the presence of fire and keeps its dimensions, but there is no life-giving power in it, so there too the earth can be seen, yet it has nothing earthly; but all there is dust and ashes, both trees and all fruits; the air and water can also be seen, but there is nothing there of air or water, since all there has become dust, so that there is nothing else; only air, as in a furnace, and all water barren and unproductive. And everything there suffers torment from the wrath received beforehand, which is an image of future torment. Understand then how great was the sin, that Gehenna was compelled to appear before its time. And that rain was wondrous and unnatural, since that sinful mingling was contrary to nature and lawless; the Lord covered the earth with that rain, because a wild and accursed desire had covered those souls. But even wilder was the corruption of mind in those who did this, for they were more senseless than beasts and more shameless than dogs. For there is no such act among animals, but they know the ordinances of their nature. I say that those who do this are worse than murderers, and whatever sin you name, you cannot name one equal to this lawlessness. For a murderer separates the soul from the body, but this one destroys soul with body. From where was this great evil born? From abundant food, and because they did not fear God and the future Judgment. When some cast the fear of God out of their heart and forget it altogether, then unreasonable desires enter them, and folly, and contemptible sins. Often many, abandoning desire for ordinary food, feed on earth and small stones, and some, possessed by a great thirst for water, often desire foul mud; so too those abandoned what is natural, for the reasons already stated, and were inflamed with vile desire. If they do not feel themselves wretched but take pleasure, do not wonder at this. For those possessed by the disease of folly, when they rave, bring much sorrow upon themselves and make themselves wretched—so that some, looking on them, shed tears—while these sick ones themselves laugh and are drunk on what they do. And what Gehenna will suffice for such, what torments? If you laugh and, hearing of Gehenna, do not believe in that fire, remember again the divine fire that was in Sodom, that fearful flame.
