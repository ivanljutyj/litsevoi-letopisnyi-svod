Therefore, concerning Christ’s second legislation, the great Isaiah declared: “Out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem, and He shall judge among the nations.” By Zion here Isaiah calls the land and the metropolis situated in it, that is, the Jewish capital. From there, as adorned swift horses from some enclosure, the apostles were sent into all the world. From there they began to work signs. There was the saving Passion of Christ, and the Resurrection, and the Ascension, and the beginning of our salvation. There the ineffable Theology began to be preached. There the Father first appeared, and the Only-begotten Son was made known, and the great grace of the Spirit was given. And there incorporeal beings began to glorify Him, together with gifts, and powers, and the promises of future good things. And all this Scripture calls “His beauty,” for the splendor and beauty of God are His goodness, and love for mankind, and all His other benefactions. There too the Lord healed the sin of Adam’s transgression; there too was the swift, as it were winged, gathering of the saints, “a hastening to the Second Coming,” which He foretold beforehand, saying: “Where the body is, there the eagles gather.” “Nor is it unknown to the ruler of all men that even Noah’s tomb could not contain him, since after the flood glory was handed down from him.” Therefore the Lord, knowing the origin of human death, suffered His Passion at the so-called Place of the Skull; in that same place all corruption began for men, and from there the preaching of the Kingdom of Heaven took its beginning. Likewise the God-voiced Athanasius, speaking of Christ’s Passion, says: “For this reason Christ did not endure His Passion somewhere else, nor in any other place, but in that place where, as the Jewish teachers say, Adam’s tomb was. ‘For there they buried him even under the curse, I testify. Therefore I marvel at the peculiarity of the place.’ It was fitting for God to renew the first man and to suffer His Passion in that same place. And since Adam heard, ‘Dust you are, and to dust you shall return,’ he was likewise laid there again: in the place where he received the curse, there he would also receive release.” And the Great Ephrem also said of Cain: “And Cain, groaning and trembling, was condemned, tormented by conscience, and he will also endure in eternal torment, for ‘you shall be groaning and trembling on the earth’; and he shows to all in that land the place of condemnation and the valley of lamentation, as Holy Scripture says.” For the earth is not for judgment’s sake, nor is judgment for the earth’s sake, for not the earth sinned, but man. The earth becomes the punishment of God’s judgment, as a throne for judges and a sword for executioners; and, it is said, judgment can exist even in temporal things. And human flesh is now mortal, but then it will be immortal. And Paradise, by sensible understanding, has also an eternal perception. It is not in a parable that Paradise is spoken of; it was not planted as a figure, but appears eternal.
