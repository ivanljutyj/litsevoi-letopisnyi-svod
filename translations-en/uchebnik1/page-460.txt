Every day he taught and entreated Evagrius, and earnestly urged him to the Christian faith, so that Evagrius might believe in Christ and receive understanding concerning Christ. But that philosopher not only did not listen to Synesius, but also argued with him greatly.

And once the philosopher came to converse with the bishop and said: “Truly, O lord bishop, what I least of all believe is that, as Christians say, there will be an end to the whole world, and resurrection for bodies, and recompense for those who have lived, and that then all people who have been since the beginning of time will rise, and receive incorruptible flesh, and live forever without death. They also say that he who has mercy on the poor lends to God, and stores up treasure for himself in heaven, and will receive a hundredfold from God in eternal life. But I think that these stories are nonsense, fables, and deceit.” The bishop, however, spoke and testified to the philosopher that the Christian mystery contains no lie or mockery, and he confirmed this to him with many indications. And Evagrius departed, having much thought within himself.

After many years, with God’s assistance, the bishop persuaded the philosopher to become a Christian, and he, taught by the bishop, believed in Christ; and the philosopher was baptized, and his children, and all who lived in his house.

And then Evagrius gave the bishop three hundred litrae of gold, for he was very rich, and said: “Receive this, bishop, and distribute it to the poor, and write for me yourself a handwritten note, that is, a receipt, that I shall receive from God and that for this gold Christ will repay me a hundredfold in the age to come.” Bishop Synesius took the gold with joy and wrote Evagrius a document as he desired. And the bishop distributed all the gold to the poor. Evagrius lived several years in piety and virtue.

After several years the philosopher fell ill with a mortal sickness, and as he was dying he said to his children: “When you bury me, place this document also in my hands, and so bury me.” When he died, they buried him with joy.

And on the third day after his death the philosopher appeared to the bishop in a dream and said: “Come to my grave, bishop, and take your handwritten note, for I have received the debt that Christ repaid me a hundredfold, and I have no reproach against you for the former transaction; and in order to announce this to you, I wrote the document with my own hand.” The bishop, however, did not know that the handwritten document had been buried with the philosopher.

When morning came, the bishop summoned the philosopher’s sons and said to them: “Did you place anything with the philosopher in the grave?” They thought he was asking about property, and said: “Nothing, lord, except the special burial garments.” He said: “And did you not place the document with him?” And the sons said: “Yes, lord, he himself gave us the document,”
