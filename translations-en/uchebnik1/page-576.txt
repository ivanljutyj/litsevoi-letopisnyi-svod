Simeon asked the emperor for peace through the drungary Eustathius; the emperor agreed with him and sent Leo Svinoboyets to conclude the peace, and ordered the domestikos Nikephoros and the drungary Eustathius to return with the army.

Simeon did not deign to honor Leo Svinoboyets even with a word, but cast him into prison.

And he warred with the Hungarians, and cut them all down, and the Hungarians could not obtain help from the Greeks.

Returning with victory, he boasted before Leo, saying: “Unless I first receive all the captive Bulgarians, I will not conclude peace.”

The emperor ordered them to be handed over. And so a Bulgarian, Theodore, a close associate of Simeon, came with Leo Svinoboyets and took them.

Since the domestikos Nikephoros was greatly loved by the emperor, the emperor’s father Zaoutzes considered him powerful and wished to make a marriage alliance with him. Nikephoros began to refuse this, so as not to arouse any suspicion in the emperor.

Zaoutzes was angered at him because of this and expelled him from that office. In his place a magistros named Katakalon was appointed domestikos of the scholai.

After Nikephoros had long remained without office, he was appointed strategos of the Thrakesian theme. Having shown many valiant deeds in his military life and won many victories over the Hagarenes, he ended his life in good old age, leaving sons, Bardas and Leontes Leo.

This Bardas was the emperor’s closest assistant; remaining in agreement with him, he rendered the emperor great service.

The emperor, as was said, appointed Katakalon domestikos and sent him against Simeon, and with him he sent Theodosios, a patrikios and protovestiarios.

When all the themes and troops had crossed over from the East, and battle with the Bulgarians began at the place where the Bulgarians had fled, the Greeks were cruelly defeated by the Bulgarians and all perished; with them the protovestiarios Theodosios also perished.

On Oleg’s Campaign against Constantinople

In the reign of this same Emperor Leo, Oleg went to war against the Greeks in the year 6415 from Adam (915 from Christ), and left Igor to rule in Kiev, and took with him many tribes: the Varangians, Slovenes, Chud, Krivichians, Merya, Polyans, Severians, Drevlyans, Radimichians and Vyatichians, Croats, and Dulebes, and Tivertsi. The Greeks called them all Great Scythia.
