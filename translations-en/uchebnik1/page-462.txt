It was the third hour when they buried the warrior, and when they sang the ninth hour, they heard a cry from the grave saying: “Have mercy on me, servants of God.” And coming to the grave, the monks found that it was that dead man, and they quickly dug open the grave and found the warrior sitting there and calling out, and they quickly drew him out of the grave and loosed his bonds, and questioned him about the cause of such a miracle, wishing to learn what had happened to him there. But he could not tell of it, for he was choking with many tears, and he asked them to bring him to the servant of God, Gelasius the bishop.

When the warrior was set before him, the bishop asked him, saying: “Tell me everything, so that I too may glorify the Holy God.” And for three days the bishop urged the warrior to tell him all that he had seen there, and barely on the fourth day did the warrior confess everything to him with many tears, and he said thus: “I, fathers and brethren, lowly and wretched, when I was dying and departing in my sins, saw certain murins, that is, Ethiopians, coming and standing before me, whose appearance was very terrible, more terrible than all torments. Seeing them, my sinful soul fell into great inward confusion from fear. When such terrible Ethiopians stood before me, I looked up and saw two youths in white robes, very beautiful, and my soul leaped into their hands.

And those youths took my soul at once, as it were from the earth to heaven, and swiftly flying up, we ascended with them, and passing through the air, we encountered tollhouses, that is, places for collecting taxes, which guarded the entrance to Heaven and with much testing and investigation hindered the ascent, and they stopped the ascending souls, testing every person, and reproached each according to his deeds, each tollhouse for its own sin: one for lying, another for envy and slander, another for boasting. Simply put, thus, one after another, for each sin they detain souls in those tollhouses and have their own testing. Through the air those youths led me and carried a small casket with certain good deeds of mine, and I saw in it all my good deeds, which the angels had, and taking from those deeds, they made payment for my evil deeds, and so we passed the tollhouses.

But all my good deeds were insufficient, and we came to the Heavenly gates, where we were passing the tollhouses for fornication, and I was taken by those dark Ethiopians, and they brought out and presented all my fleshly and fornication sins, which I committed from two years of age and until now, wretched as I am. And the angels leading me said: “All that this man committed of bodily sins in the city, God forgave him, for he repented of them and went out of the city.” But the evil adversaries said: “But after leaving the city he again fell into adultery in his estate—he fornicated with the wife of his plowman, and immediately died, and did not repent of this.” And hearing this, the angels were dismayed—
