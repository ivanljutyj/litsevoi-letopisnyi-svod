Rome. Byzantium (345–463 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 2

Constantine, the elder son of Constantine the Great, rose against his younger brother Constans, who was in Rome; and there was war between the brothers; and the elder brother Constantine was killed; so that he who desired another’s possessions lost his own.

And the region that Constantine had ruled Constans joined to his own possessions, and Constans became king of all the western lands, and reigned alone for 16 years. In the time of the great king Constantine, Shapur, king of Persia, tortured for the confession of the Christian faith Akindin, and Pigasius, and Anempodistus, and Aphthonius, and his courtier Elpidiphorus, and with him seven thousand nobles, and twenty-two more, and also his mother. Later Shapur also tortured for Christ his son Gaveddai and his daughter Kazdoa.

In the time of Constantine the relics of the first martyr Stephen were brought from Jerusalem to Constantinople. In the time of Constantine the Great, by revelation Anthony the Great found in the desert Paul of Thebes, who had gone into the desert in the reign of Decius and Valerian.

Anthony the Great went into the desert in the reign of Diocletian and remained there until Constantine the Great, and departed this life in the reign of his sons.

In the reign of Constantine, Paphnutius the Confessor found Anuphrius in the desert. In those same years Ammon of Nitria and Palamon also struggled in asceticism, to whom Pachomius the Great came. Then too Mark struggled in asceticism, who was called the Immortal because for 60 years, from Sunday to Sunday, he partook of the Holy Gifts, and ate no bread, and did not spit on the ground.
