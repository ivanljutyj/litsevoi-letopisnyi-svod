The maiden, adorned with virginity, was seized because she reviled the kings and the idols. Taking her, they tortured her much, and since she did not submit to their impiety, they sold her for fornication, ordering the brothel keeper to take her and to bring in three gold pieces a day for her. But he, wishing to receive gold, gave her to those who desired her. Learning of this, the womanizers began coming to her, sinners, and burst in on her offering silver. She, entreating them, first said: “I have a foul-smelling discharge; I am ashamed of it, because it will infect you too. Therefore it is better that you wait a little — a few days, until I am healed, and then I will always be in your power.” So she deceived them, and with prayers she begged God to deliver her from such defilement and to preserve her virginity without turmoil. And the Lord God heard her prayers and saved her thereafter in the following way. Once a certain noble and very pious young man came to the harlots of her master and gave him five gold pieces, saying: “Leave this maiden with me until morning.” And entering the place where she was confined, he said to her: “Rise and go in peace.” And he clothed her in his own garment and covered her head, and she went out undefiled, and unexpectedly the woman was free. By the end of the next day what had happened was discovered, and the young man was condemned to death. And when this happened, the word of the Lord was fulfilled, which says: “No one has greater love than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends.” Likewise another monk was seized by these inhuman and impious men. After many tortures and wounds, finally they devised the following impiety to corrupt the chastity of the righteous man. Having placed a bed in a garden and bound the saint to it, they left a harlot with him, so that in this pleasant place he could not, against his will, escape the harlot’s advances and be drawn into unlawful union. But the divine man, while the woman embraced him, spoke indecent words, and inflamed him with sweetness, bit off his own tongue and spat it in her face, causing himself pain and weeping instead of sweetness; he astonished her with the flowing blood and filled her with shame. The Hellenes learned what true monastic chastity is. The impious woman also was defeated. But the torturers Diocletian and Maximian, because of the strength and steadfastness of the Christians and the multitude killed, issued a decree that captured Christians should have their right eye torn out, so as not only to cause them pain but also to disgrace them, making them outcasts among the Roman inhabitants. But God’s judgment struck them with a punishment that justly overtook them: Diocletian was killed at a meeting of the nobles, and Maximian’s entrails flowed out.
