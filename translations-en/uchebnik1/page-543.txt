Who does not know the zeal of the king, who desired to comprehend what is hidden in Scripture? If the thorns of heresy had not ruined his heart, then his love of justice alone, and also his zeal for comprehending the Word of God, could have covered all his sins.

And, wishing to quench his thirst for knowledge, Emperor Theophilus releases Saint Methodius from the cave, like a pearl from its shell, or a star from the clouds, or the radiant sun. And this lamp shone forth, blazing with bright rays, and illumined all around, driving away the darkness of heresy and the gloom of ignorance. I believe that it pleased Him who sees the abysses and sits upon the Cherubim to set Saint Methodius as an impregnable city on a mountain, so that this treasure might not be hidden, like a lamp under a bushel, in the darkness of the cave.

Emperor Theophilus, having released Saint Methodius, settles him with honor in the palace, as a precious possession, as a treasure that cannot be stolen, as a precious shining stone, as a lamp of knowledge for the foolish and unenlightened. And the king stayed near him, like one thirsting at the life-giving streams of the Nile, abundantly drawing the water of his teaching and understanding. Such was Emperor Theophilus’s zeal for the knowledge of truth. But he could not free his heart from the thorn of the iconoclast heresy, and it choked all the good seeds of his aspirations and deeds. And the wrath of God came upon him for all the evil that he had done to His saints.

Theophilus, feeling that his time was approaching to cross the river of this corruptible life, which no one wearing mortal flesh, neither good nor evil, can avoid, places the crown upon his young son Michael, and appoints his mother Theodora as co-ruler. Until this time she had secretly confessed the veneration of icons and was like Nicodemus: according to tradition, timid in heart and hesitant in mind, yet when the time came he began openly to preach Christ, and his proclamation was bold and loud.

On the Death of Theophilus.

And a severe mortal illness came upon Theophilus, exacting its due, and it began quickly to destroy his body. It was so agonizing that he wished to be released from this life as soon as possible. It was terrible to see how his mouth and jaws opened so wide that his innards became visible.

The empress, seeing all this and suffering grievously, wept bitterly for her husband, who had lived so impiously. One day, when the king’s life was already drawing to its end, the empress, having slept for a short while, saw in a dream the most pure Mother of God holding the eternal Child, surrounded by bright angels, and her husband Theophilus being mercilessly subjected to torments and mockery. And at once she awoke from sleep.
