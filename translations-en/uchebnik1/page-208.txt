Thus also this Sabinus, when he had already overcome all and shown his invincibility, then fell to the ground with a great crash, catching his heavy weapon on a stone. And the Jews turned back, seeing that he was alone and had fallen. They came, surrounded him, and pierced him all over with spears and swords. But Sabinus, having barely risen to one knee and covered himself with his shield, stabbed many Jews and wounded many others.

When all the blood had drained from Sabinus, he grew weak and lowered his arms, and while he was giving up the ghost, arrows and spears and swords covered him. This man deserved a better fate for his courage, but then he fell, for so it was destined, or else he fell because he did not guard himself. The other three who had gone with him and were the first at the wall barriers were crushed by stones. This exploit was accomplished on the 3rd day of the month Panemus, that is July.

Two days later, twenty Romans guarding the rampart gathered and drew to themselves the standard-bearer of the ninth regiment, two horsemen, and one trumpeter. And at the ninth hour of the night they went secretly and climbed onto the Tower of Antonia, and cut down the nearest guards while they slept, and occupied the walls, and ordered the trumpeter to sound. And the rest of the guards heard the trumpet, and leapt up, and fled before they even saw those who had climbed onto the tower. The sound of the trumpet struck fear into them; they thought that many enemies had climbed up.

The emperor heard the sound of the trumpet and quickly raised all the army, and was the first to ascend the walls with chosen commanders. The Jews fled to the temple. The Romans rushed along the underground passages that John, that is Yohanan, had earlier dug against them. And all the Jewish warriors blocked their passage, and to prevent the Romans from passing they brought all their strength and all their courage into play, for they understood that they would be finally conquered if the Romans entered the sanctuary and saw what must not be seen. And the battle was fierce: the Romans strove to occupy the temple, while the Jews pressed them back toward the Tower of Antonia.

And then, hurling spears and shooting arrows, with drawn swords, the enemies closed. And in that battle it was impossible to understand or tell who was fighting whom, for confusion arose among the soldiers, and in the streets a mingling, and a noise in which nothing could be made out. A great multitude of soldiers gathered, and blood flowed from both sides like a river. And the bodies of the fallen and their armor were shattered in the battle. And always where one side prevailed in the battle, the urging cries of those who were overcoming were heard, and the wails and groans of those who were fleeing. There was no place for either flight or pursuit, but in the general turmoil of the army they now pursued, now fled.
