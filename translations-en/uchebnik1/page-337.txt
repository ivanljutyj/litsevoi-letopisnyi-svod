And Saint Domitius sent him this reply: "I have committed my soul into the hands of God, and have shut my body in this cave for many years; but the people who come to me with faith I cannot drive away."

And Julian ordered the exit from the cave to be walled up for the righteous man, and so Saint Domitius died. And Julian took up arms against the Persians, defeated them, and wished to go as far as Babylon.

But the Persian king Slunarsakiy was afraid and fled into the land of Persarmenia, and in order to deceive the Romans he sent two of his nobles to them, ordering their noses to be cut off, and sent them thus to Julian, to deceive him and keep him from pursuing the Persians.

These two Persians, with their noses cut off, came as though they had fled from the Persian king, who had supposedly punished them, and Julian was deceived by them.

These two Persians swore that they were telling the truth, and Julian listened to them and went after them with his soldiers. And the Persians led the Romans into a waterless desert for 150 stadia, luring them. On the 25th day of Daisios, that is of the month of July, the Romans found there the ruins of the old walls of a city called Vuvim, and another village with houses in it, also deserted, and the village was called Asia.

There King Julian and his soldiers made camp. While still in Persia, Julian sent a demon to the west to bring him swift news. And on the way the demon was held back and could not move farther, because certain monks stood with their hands raised to Heaven and prayed to God for 10 days. So the demon could not pass farther and returned again to Julian. And Julian asked the demon: "Why were you delayed?" And the demon answered him: "I was delayed and did not carry out the task you entrusted to me, and I returned because on the way I was stopped by the monk Poplius; for I waited for him to stop praying, but he prayed without ceasing for 10 days and did not let me carry out your will."

And Julian heard this and was greatly enraged, and began to threaten, and said: "When I return, I will destroy all the monks." Learning of this, one of Julian's nobles was struck with awe and turned back, and gave all his possessions to the poor.

In those places where the soldiers had come there was no food for them, and no grass or water for the horses, for it was a desert. And all the Roman soldiers understood that the king had been deceived and had led them into the desert, and they were in great confusion.
