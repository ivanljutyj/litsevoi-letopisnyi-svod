Tsar Michael with Caesar Bardas and parakoimomenos Basil the Macedonian decided to go to war against Crete. But Leo the Philosopher begged Bardas not to go with them, saying: “If you go, you will not return.”

After the feast of Holy Easter the tsar set out with a large army and came to Thrace. When the army halted at a place called Vertograds, Basil gathered his intimates, and they began to consult how to destroy the Caesar. At that council were Marian, Subatius, and Bardas, Basil’s brothers, Asuleon, his nephew, and also Peter the Bulgarian, John the Chaldean, and Constantine the Archer.

But Count John, having learned of this, came after sunset to the Caesar’s tent and told Procopius, the Caesar’s servant, that when morning came his master, Caesar Bardas, would be cut to pieces.

Then Procopius went in to the Caesar to warn him. Bardas, having heard the servant out, said: “Go and tell John: ‘It is empty talk. You are young, and it is too early for you to wear the rank of patrician. You spread these rumors to get what you desire.’” But the Caesar did not close his eyes all night.

And as soon as dawn broke, Bardas summoned his intimates and told them everything, expecting to hear sensible advice from them. Then Philotheus, his friend, a protospatharius and genikos by rank, said: “In the morning, my lord, put on your gold-woven garment and appear before your enemies, and they will flee from your face.”

When the sun rose, Bardas, having put on his shining garment, mounted his horse and, surrounded by numerous intimates, headed for the tsar’s tent. With him was his chief saddler Eustathius, called Argus, that is, the Silversmith, a faithful and strong man.

But Constantine the Archer came out to meet Bardas with an obeisance (such was the agreement with Basil) and, returning, informed Basil of the Caesar’s arrival.

Basil came out, bowed to him, and, taking him by the hand, entered the tsar’s tent together with him.

Caesar Bardas, sitting down beside the tsar, said to him: “All the army, my lord, is assembled. Just give the command, and it will set out for Crete immediately.” At that time Basil, standing behind his back, gave a sign with his hand, pointing at the Caesar.
