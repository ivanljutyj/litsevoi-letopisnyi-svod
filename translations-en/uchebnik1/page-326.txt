He especially glorified the power of the Honorable and Life-Giving Cross and rendered it many honors, for he was confident in it because of the miracles that always took place: by it he defeated and overthrew the impious Maxentius, and by the power of the Cross he trampled the united enemies of Byzantium, and he subdued the Scythians, guided by divine zeal.

After Saint Helena returned from Jerusalem to Constantinople, the Christ-loving Emperor Constantine made three large crosses, according to the number of visions that appeared to him in battles: the first in Rome, when he drowned Maxentius; the second in Byzantium, when he took Byzantium; the third when he built a bridge over the Danube and subdued the Scythians. In accordance with the three visions, he made three honorable crosses of pure copper and gave them these holy names: “Jesus,” “Christ,” “Conquers.” The one called “Jesus,” having been made of cast gold, he set facing east on the arch where the Market Square now is, warmly manifesting his piety to all, since by the power of the Cross he had conquered the Greek rebellions.

The all-honorable and divine cross called “Christ” he placed on top of the red Roman column in the Brother-loving quarter of Constantinople, which stands to this day in the same place where it was set. And another life-giving, wonder-working, divine cross, named “Victory,” later called “Anikitos” (“Invincible”) by Heraclius, the faithful and Christ-loving emperor—and so it is called by all who believe in the Name of Christ from then until this day: the life-giving Cross of the Lord, “Holy Anikitos,” by which the Power of Christ is invincible. Great Constantine raised it upon a tall marble column, in a beautiful place, on Bread Street. Many powers and signs were wrought through it at the place where Christ our God was nailed to the Tree of the Cross, granting us all healing of infirmities, deliverance to those suffering from cold and terrors, and health and sight to damaged, unseeing eyes.

And let no one believe me, but what I wish to tell will be not for the cunning-minded nor for heretics, but for faithful souls and those wise in goodness; the overly reasoning are worthy of no miracles. Many who have heard of this honorable cross relate that the angel of the Lord, late at night, three times a year, like the light of flashing lightning from heaven, descended upon it with a starry sign: in the month of May on the 7th day (the sign of the Cross over Jerusalem); on the honorable and holy Exaltation of the Cross to heaven by faithful hierarchs, on the 14th day of September, and also at the Adoration of Christ our God at the midpoint of the holy and bright Fast. On these three days they walk around the honorable Cross of Christ, Holy Anikitos, and cense it with sweet singing, and make a delicate ringing around it.
