Romanos sent John Tuvakios and Leontios, his kinsman, to bring him to Constantinople.

Having seized him, they put out his pupils and the lights of his eyes, blinding him and darkening him. And nothing had been said to them about punishment, but they did this of their own will, at which the emperor’s father was indignant.

In the month of August it became known that there was a conspiracy of Constantine Ktematines, David Kamoulians, and Michael, the overseer of the Mangana palace. They were beaten, publicly shamed, and led through the whole city and sent into exile.

They brought Leontios, the magistros and domestikos, to Constantinople, and he processed amid a gathering of people, seated on a female mule.

Empress Zoe, who had planned to prepare poisoned food for Romanos, was taken by Theoklitos the notary, as was part of his official duties. She was led out of the palace, brought into the monastery of Saint Euphemia, and tonsured.

And Theodore, the attendant of Emperor Constantine, and Simeon, his brother, were invited to dinner by Theophylact, patrikios and komes.

When they were eating, John, the droungarios of the guard, called Kourkouas, entered with many people and, seizing them, sent them into exile in Opsikion, to their villages, as those plotting against Romanos.
