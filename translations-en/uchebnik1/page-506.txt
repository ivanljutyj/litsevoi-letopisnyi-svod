King Theophilus, however, ordered them to be seized and tortured. Saint Michael the protosyncellus, and also his disciple Job, in those torments gave up their holy souls to the Lord.

But after many tortures King Theophilus ordered that the faces of Theodore and Theophanes be branded, for which he commanded an iron plate to be made and such verses inscribed on it.

Verses of King Theophilus. “To everyone who wishes to visit this city, that is, Jerusalem, where the most pure feet of Christ trod for the salvation of the world. In this holy place there appeared vessels of deceit and demonic delusion (so Theophilus calls Saints Michael, Theophanes, and Theodore). Having by their evil faith wrought many foul and God-abhorrent deeds, they, as apostates, by their impious thinking drove themselves far away from Jerusalem, but even when they came to this sovereign Constantinople, they did not abandon their evil madness. Therefore their faces are branded as those of malefactors, and they themselves are condemned and driven out.” With such an inscription did Theophilus burn the faces of Saints Theophanes and Theodore with red-hot iron and sent them into confinement, where after some time the great Theodore departed to the Lord. And at the hour of his death the singing of angels was heard, and together with them his voice was also heard.

Theophanes, however, remained in exile until the very death of Theophilus. For this reason neither Michael, the protosyncellus of the holy Church of Jerusalem, nor his disciples, Theophanes and Theodore, reached Rome.

Pope Leo, seeing that the flock of Christ was being led to destruction by the aforementioned heretics of Charles, was perplexed why his request had remained unanswered. Then he ordered a confession of the Orthodox faith to be written on tablets and fixed to the wall in the church, so that everyone entering might rightly confess the Christian faith: the Son, begotten of the Father, and the Holy Spirit, proceeding from the Father. This was done to confirm the Orthodox in the faith and to expose the heretics of Charles.

After the repose of Saint Leo, Benedict became Pope of Rome. A Latin by birth, he knew the works of the holy fathers, both of the East and of the West, and wrote a confession of the Orthodox faith in both Latin and Greek, and commanded all Christians, Greeks and Latins, to read what was written and confess Orthodoxy, exposing the aforementioned heretics and not listening to their false teachings.
