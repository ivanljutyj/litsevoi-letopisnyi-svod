And so, having conquered, the Saracens with their subject peoples and in great strength, that is, with a great army, came to Damascus and took it by the spear.

Likewise the Saracens subdued all the Phoenician lands and settled in them—foul and abominable as they were—by God’s permission, for our sins.

The Saracens also took the holy city of Jerusalem and all the Promised Land, a land flowing with milk and honey. Of this holy land Moses spoke to Israel: “Behold, the Lord your God will bring you into a good and great land; in it streams of water and springs flow from the deep through the pastures and mountains; that land is of wheat and barley, of vines and fig trees, and pomegranates, that is, pomegranate trees, are in it, and palms; that land is of olive trees, yielding oil and honey; a land in which you shall eat bread without poverty and shall lack nothing in it; a land whose stones are iron, and from its mountains shall come gold; a land flowing with milk and honey; that land is better and greater than all the world; a land which the Lord your God visits, and His eyes are upon it from the beginning, from year to year.” And truly that land is blessed, fruitful in all things, greater than all lands, and comparable to the Paradise of God. “And Lot lifted up his eyes and saw all the country around the Jordan, that it was all watered, like the Paradise of the Lord, like the land of Egypt,” and this land was chosen by Lot. The wise Aristotle also bears witness to this, saying: “The Promised Land is populous; it is dense with trees and fruitful with every kind of produce; it is fruitful with vineyards and good trees; and its many palms continually produce oil; and there is very much cattle of various kinds; and their pastures are abundant from year to year because of the great mildness of the land; there is a great abundance of plenty there; and the Arabians bring aromatics, that is, sweet spices, and various stones, and gold into that land.” Josephus also says: “That land is exceedingly fruitful because of the mild rain and abundant moisture; in the winter season it brings forth nuts, and in the heat, dates; it produces much oil, that is, olive oil, and wine; and the figs growing there abound for ten months of the year. The other fruits are gathered twice from year to year.” Concerning the greatness of the grape clusters in that land, it is clear from Scripture, which tells of the spies, that is, the messengers whom Moses sent: when they came even to the Valley of Grapes and cut off a vine there, and took one cluster of grapes, two men carried it on their shoulders. That holy land was known to all for its good fruitfulness. Through it Alexander the Great journeyed and saw fruitful trees; among them he also saw the peganon, that is, rue, equal in height and thickness to a fig tree, and he marveled greatly at the good fruitfulness and abundance of that land.
