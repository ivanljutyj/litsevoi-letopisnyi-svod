On the Autardocetian Heresy. Emperor Justinian was a zealot of true faith and raised a great struggle against the wrong-believing, and most of all against the pagans.

Although afterwards Justinian too was seduced by certain vile men and accepted the evil Autardocetian faith, that is, the incorruptibilist faith, according to which Christ’s flesh, conceived of the Most Pure Virgin, was incorruptible until the Passion. When Patriarch Eutychius exposed this evil heresy, Emperor Justinian removed him from the patriarchal throne and sent him into exile. And Justinian the Great reigned thirteen years after the Fifth Council.

The Seventeenth Reign, of Justin, Son of Justinian, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Justinian the Great, his son Justin reigned for twenty years. He built imperial palaces outside the city and a harbor near the city, and named the harbor Sophinias, and the palaces he named Sophia, after his wife Sophia.

In the reign of this Justin the dome of Holy Sophia fell. It happened in the second year of Justin’s reign: on Thursday, at the sixth hour of the day, the glorious church roof, that is, the dome, collapsed, and then the glorious and wondrous ambo was shattered, and all its ornaments were destroyed: sardonyxes, sapphires, pearls, pure glass, golden architraves above the columns, the columns, and many other ornaments. The other parts of the dome remained unmoved.

Emperor Justin summoned the master architect who had built it before and asked him the cause of such a collapse of the dome. He said to him: “Your father built this church with great haste; therefore he completed it quickly, and hurried, and quickly removed the scaffolding, and quickly adorned it with mosaics. He made the church tall, so that it might be seen from everywhere. And when the craftsmen cut away the scaffolding and threw it down, from that throwing the foundations of the church cracked, and the dome did not hold, and from this came such a collapse of the dome.”

Emperor Justin sent to the Isle of Flowers, that is, Rhodes, as his father had done, and had ceramides made, that is, fired clay tiles, as before, and cut fifteen sazhens from the height of the church, fearing that it might fall as before.
