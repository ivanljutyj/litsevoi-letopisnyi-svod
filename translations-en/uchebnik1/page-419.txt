On the Laying of the Foundation of Holy Sophia

Tsar Justinian, having measured the place, found in the foundation an eternal stone, from the altar even to the lower side.

On the Creation of Holy Sophia

The tsar began to create the foundation of the church. And Justinian summoned Patriarch Eutychius with his council. And all were in vestments, and the patriarch offered prayer over the foundation of the church. When the prayer was finished, Tsar Justinian took the work in his own hands, and took a vessel with clay and lime, and looking up to heaven, thanked God, saying: “Look down, O Lord, upon this temple, and make in it a dwelling for Thyself, and hear in it the prayer of Thy servants.” And Justinian laid the foundation before all, while the people stood around, and after the tsar all the workmen began to work.

Then Justinian also made a passage from the royal chambers to Holy Sophia, so that he might come often and unseen by anyone. There were 100 skilled masters, having a hundred hired men each, so that all the hired men were 10,000.

The first builder was Mikhanik, the second Ignatius, and there were 30,000 carrying stones. They began to make the eastern side with 5,000 men, and the western side with 5,000 men, and the southern side with 5,000 men, and the northern side with 5,000 men, since they did the work with great haste.

An angel of the Lord showed the tsar in a dream what the church would be like. In cauldrons they boiled barley with water and mixed it into the lime and clay instead of water, and that lime became sticky. And from a tree called willow they cut pieces, and threw what was cut into the cauldrons, and boiled it with barley. And they made four-cornered troughs, eight cubits wide and eight cubits long.

And the foundation of the church was 20 cubits thick, and the walls were 100 sazhens long, apart from the altar and the porches, and with the four porches 170, and the width was the same. And the foundation could then be seen to hold together as if by iron, and they made the walls above the foundation two cubits thick. And 355 centenaries of gold were given. A certain magister, the keeper of the tsar’s property, supervised this construction and recorded it.

Silver pieces, that is, silver coins, were brought from the royal chambers and placed in the chapel. Those who raised stones onto the wall received one silver piece for each stone. The tsar ordered that no one be wronged at all. And this is how it was: one of the bearers reviled the tsar and, sighing, grumbled, and immediately fell to the ground and died.

The walls all rose higher, and the great Roman columns already stood; they were green, of marble, and all the other columns were set in the church under the top and under the galleries, eight columns, besides those already made; and there were 360 doors throughout the church.
