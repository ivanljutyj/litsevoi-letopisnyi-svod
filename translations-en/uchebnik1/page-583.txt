The emperor summoned the boyar rank and, bringing Constantine in, asked thus: “By God and by my head, did Samonas flee to Syria or not?” And he, fearing the oath—before he had spoken without an oath, but simply when questioned had said what was not true—confessed that he had fled to Syria.

The emperor angrily expelled Constantine, and removed Samonas from his sight for four months to the house of the Caesar; after this he was granted the sight of the emperor and conversation with him.

A son named Constantine was born to Leo by Zoe, his fourth wife. At his birth there appeared a huge star, sending rays to the east and shining for forty days and nights.

He was baptized in the Great Church by Patriarch Nicholas on the day of the Epiphany, and Emperor Alexander received him from holy baptism, and Patrician Samonas, and all those in power. Then Kipha became a home for the aged, and the harlots were expelled from there.

Presbyter Thomas, who had been deprived of the priesthood, blessed Emperor Leo with Zoe. And the emperor named her empress.

Because of this the patriarch forbade the emperor to enter the church; therefore he entered from the right side, into the metatorion, that is, into the dwelling.

Samonas was appointed parakoimomenos, for he taught the emperor lawlessness and every evil, and they began to show zeal against the church.

For he summoned Patriarch Nicholas on the first day of February and long asked him to accept the fourth marriage. And they could not persuade him, even at dinner.

For Nicholas, then the chief shepherd, spoke boldly with the emperor, fulfilling the words of the prophet by testifying to God’s knowledge before emperors, not fearing threats and not trembling; thus the fear of God far drives out the fear that comes from men who judge and rule. Therefore Patriarch Nicholas was driven from the flock and from the throne, and condemned to exile.

Putting him into a small boat, they transported him to Ieria, and from there he barely reached Lakrina on foot, because the snow was very deep.

And in his place Euthymius the syncellus is appointed patriarch, a godly man, temperate and pious, who, they said, accepted the episcopate by divine revelation.

For the emperor intended to establish an evil law: that a man have three wives or four, and many wise men assisted in this.

In the month of June Emperor Leo was invited by Constantine, surnamed Livas, to his monastery near the Holy Apostles, so that he might perform the consecration and dine.
