Therefore, while our hands are not bound and still hold swords, let them render us a glorious service. Let us die without knowing slavery under foreigners; let us depart this life free, with our children and wives. The law urges us to this; our wives and children implore us for this; such a lot has been prepared for us by time and God. The Romans alone do not want this, but they watch lest any of us die before being taken captive. Let us hasten to put their hope to shame. And instead of the joy they hope to receive by capturing us, let them experience terror and marvel at the strength of our hearts.”

And while Eleazar still wished to speak, all who heard him rushed toward death and were filled with joy, like men possessed, wishing to outstrip one another in killing, counting it proof of their manliness and courage if one ended his life before the others. Such a desire to die came upon them that they completely banished from their hearts all familial feeling. And at one and the same time they embraced their wives and children, kissed them with a last kiss, wept over them, and, as if with strangers’ hands, killed them. And no one refused this deed, but all stabbed their kin like cattle.

And then the Sicarii gathered their many treasures and, leaving nothing at all, threw everything into the fire, and all burned. Then they cast lots and chose ten men who were to kill all the rest. And each of the Sicarii, embracing his wife and children, offered his neck to the sword and went to his own.
