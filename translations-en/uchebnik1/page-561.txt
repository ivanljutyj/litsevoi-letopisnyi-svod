Shaken by the inconstancy of the darkened mind of the Greek emperor, who lived in fornication and drunkenness and had exhausted the whole treasury with his henchmen, Basil formed a conspiracy to kill Michael.

Once, when the emperor was hunting, a certain monk handed him a letter from his mother Theodora, from the monastery in Athymia where she was living. The letter spoke of Basil's conspiracy against him. But the emperor did not believe it.

He summoned Rentakios, the chief of the servants, and ordered him, when they had taken game, to send it as a gift to his mother.

Basil then began his preparations. One day the emperor Michael invited Basil and Eudokia to the evening meal, and that evening he drank beyond measure. Then Basil, pleading a natural necessity, went out and damaged the door locks in the imperial bedchamber so that it would be impossible to shut the doors.

Then he returned and continued the meal with the emperor. When he had drunk his fill, the emperor rose and, supported by Basil under the arm, went to his bedchamber. Basil kissed his hand and went out.

The emperor Michael ordered Basiliscus, whom he intended to make emperor, to spend the night in his bedchamber. Basiliscus, obeying the emperor's command, settled on Rentakios's bed.

When the citonites Ignatius, guard of the imperial bedchamber, came to lock the doors, he found that the door lock was damaged. Realizing what was afoot, in despair he sank onto the bed, tearing his hair. The emperor lay sunk in drunken sleep.

Suddenly Basil appeared at the entrance to the bedchamber, leading the conspirators behind him, and flung open the doors. Ignatius rushed to meet them, blocking the way.

Peter the Bulgarian, secretly brought in by Basil, hastened to the imperial bed. Ignatius, trying to stop him, began to wake the emperor. Then John the Chaldean struck the emperor with a sword and cut off both his hands.

Jacob the Brave, a Perseian, killed Basiliscus and threw him from the bed. At that time Marian and Barda, father of Basil the rector, and Subatius, Basil's brothers, and Asuleon his nephew, and Constantine the Archer stood guard before the doors. And there was no one with Michael who knew what was happening.
