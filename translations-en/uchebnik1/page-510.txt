Byzantium (805–875 AD).
Book 3

From the Sixth to the Seventh Ecumenical Council, 122 years; from Adam, 6305; and from the Lord’s Incarnation, 805 years.
On the Seventh Holy Ecumenical Council

The Seventh Holy Ecumenical Council was convened in Nicaea of Bithynia in the reign of the ever-memorable Empress Irene, wife of Emperor Leo, son of Constantine Copronymus, and under Constantine, son of Leo and Irene. First among the bishops was then Adrian, Pope of Rome; the patriarchs were Tarasius of Constantinople, Politian of Alexandria, Theodoret of Antioch, Elias of Jerusalem, and others, in all 367 holy fathers.

They assembled against the iconoclasts, who slandered Christians as though they had deified icons. We do not deify them, but in venerating the image we render honor to the Prototype; as Basil the Great says: “The honor of the image ascends to the Prototype; the Prototype is in heaven, and Him we worship in the image.” The originators of the heresies were Leo the Isaurian and Anastasius, who was Patriarch of Constantinople under him, and also Leo’s son Constantine Copronymus and the Patriarchs of Constantinople whom he appointed, Constantine and Niketas, and with them the metropolitans Basil Tricocavus of Antioch and Sisinnius Pastillas of Perga, the bishops Theodosius of Ephesus, Sisinnius of Pisidia, and Constantine of Nacolia.

All these originators of the heresy the holy fathers anathematized, and they decreed that our Lord Jesus Christ be confessed as uncircumscribable in His divinity and circumscribable in His humanity; therefore they established veneration of His most pure image, and of the image of His Mother, and of the icons of all the saints, and confirmed the Orthodox faith.
