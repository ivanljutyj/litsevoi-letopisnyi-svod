Their ears intently await commands, their eyes are fixed on the standards, their hands are ready for battle. That is why the Romans quickly do what they wish, and inflict wounds more often than they receive them. They cannot be conquered by a multitude of troops, nor by the narrowness of the place, nor by cunning. Therefore it is not surprising that in the east the Euphrates obeys them, and in the west the ocean, and in the south fertile Libya, and on the northern side Rome. And I wrote this not to praise the Romans, but to comfort the conquered and condemn the rebels, and also to give food for thought to those who love labor and reading, so that they may know about Roman life.

Vespasian, with his son Titus, was in Ptolemais, testing the soldiers and recording who was capable of what.

Placidus, whom Vespasian had sent to conquer Galilee, desiring glory, rushed to Jotapata, the city that is the capital of Galilee and is better fortified than the others. When the townspeople learned of the Romans’ approach, they stood before the city and armed themselves.

Having entered battle with the Roman regiments, the inhabitants of Jotapata soon repulsed them and wounded many, but killed only 7 men, since the Romans usually retreat if defeated, but retreat calmly, so that it cannot be known that they are withdrawing.

After Placidus’s actions, Vespasian himself moved from Ptolemais into Galilee. And he ordered the army to march as the Romans usually march. Archers and light-armed troops in front guard the rest of the army from a sudden attack by the enemy, and learn whether enemy soldiers are hidden in the forests. Then come the heavily armed soldiers: one part mounted, and the other on foot.

After them come the workmen making roads; where it is narrow and hard to pass, they cut a road and level it, so that the soldiers may not grow weary from the difficult way. Then comes the baggage train of the commander-in-chief and of the detachment commanders under the guard of mounted and foot soldiers. Then come asses and mules, dragging siege engines for taking cities. After them move the commanders of detachments and regiments, surrounded by chosen soldiers. Then they carry the standards and the so-called eagle. For the eagle is the bird of all birds, and the strongest, and its image signifies victory. Then come the trumpeters. And then the main army, six men abreast, accompanied by centurions who watched over order. At the very end moved mounted and foot soldiers with weapons ready, guarding the rear. And the Galileans saw the Roman army and were all terrified, and many in the circle of Josephus, who writes these lines and then commanded the Galilean army, began to repent.
