The Romans burned the near and far parts of the city. And so Jerusalem was captured in the second year of Vespasian’s reign, on the 8th day of the month Garpeus, that is, September. This city was captured for the sixth time and laid waste for the second time.

Asocheus, king of Egypt, and after him Antiochus, and then Pompey, and after them Sosius with Herod captured this city, preserving it without destruction.

Before these captures, the Babylonian king captured Jerusalem, destroyed it, and laid it waste, 1468 years and one month after the founding of the city.

The first founder of Jerusalem was a Canaanite prince, called in his native tongue “righteous king”; and he was indeed righteous, and therefore became the first priest before God, and established a sanctuary, and named the city Jerusalem; previously the city had been called Solyma.

David, king of the Jews, drove the remaining Canaanites out of this land and settled it with his people. 477 years and 6 months after King David, the Babylonians destroyed Jerusalem.

After King David, who was the first of the Jewish line to reign in Jerusalem, and until the present destruction wrought by Titus, 1179 years passed. From the first founding of the city to the last destruction there were 2177 years. Neither the city’s long existence, nor its immense wealth, nor the multiplication of the Jewish people throughout all the earth, nor the great glory of the service in the Jerusalem temple could keep Jerusalem from destruction.

Thus the destruction of Jerusalem was accomplished.

Book 7 of Josephus on the Capture of Jerusalem

Since the soldiers had no one to kill and nothing to plunder, for nothing remained, the emperor ordered both the whole city and the temple to be destroyed to the foundation. Only three towers, which were the highest — Phasael, Hippicus, and Mariamne — as well as the western walls were preserved for the remaining guards and so that later generations might see and marvel and praise the fortitude and strength of the Romans, who had taken such a mighty and fortified city. The destroyers then leveled with the ground everything else — the walls, the houses, and the temple — so that no building could be recognized. And if someone came from other lands and this place were shown to him, saying that a city had been here, he would not believe it. For no traces of the former city remained except those that have been mentioned. Jerusalem endured such misfortune because of the madness of those who lived in it. This city was very great, and it was renowned in all cities and lands.
