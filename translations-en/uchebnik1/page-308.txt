“I will call and raise the bull.” And Zambrius said: “If you do this, we will give our heads to the king, and all the Jews, abandoning the Law, will join the Christian faith.”

Then Sylvester, raising his hands to heaven and offering prayer with tears, went up to the bull and said in a loud voice: “I call upon Your name, O Christ, before all with a cry, so that all people may know that the devil’s name killed the bull, but by Your holy name let it live!” He came close to the bull and said loudly: “In the name of Jesus Christ, whom the Jews crucified, arise and stand up in all strength.” And at once the bull stirred and stood up. Sylvester untied its bonds and released it, saying: “Go to the place from where you came, and harm no one on the way.”

Seeing this miracle, the Jews were astonished and fell down before the divine Sylvester, asking for holy baptism. And there was great joy. The king and the people for two hours cried out: “Great is the God of the Christians, and great is His power!”

In the time of that blessed Constantine, the inner Indians and the Iberians received holy baptism.

And the Armenians believed in Christ with their king Tiridates, through the many sufferings of the great man Gregory the martyr, their archbishop.

Under Constantine there was also a strong earthquake: in the Kamennian country 13 cities were destroyed, and there was an eclipse of the sun at the third hour of the day, so that stars appeared in the sky.

And in the Melitene country snakes of every kind gathered and, standing opposite one another, fought among themselves for 5 hours, killing one another, so that that country was filled with the stench of their bodies.

Concerning the Jews Who Wanted to Build a Church

The Jews gathered and made a revolt to restore the temple in Jerusalem. The divine Constantine punished many for disobedience, driving them out; and to others he cut off their ears and, hanging them on their necks, had them carried about everywhere, so that the rest of the Jews would not dare to do such things.

Concerning Constantine’s Victory over Maximian

But let us return again to the Orthodox Great Tsar Constantine and to the account of his valour and brave deeds. The great Christ-loving Tsar Constantine, reigning in Rome, unceasingly glorified all the great works of our Lord and God Jesus Christ. Maxentius, son of Maximian Galerius, attacked then with boldness, himself wishing to reign, but he was put to shame and died in Cilicia.
