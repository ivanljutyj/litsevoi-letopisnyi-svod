And only on the next day, when Titus came, he took with him two hundred horsemen and a thousand foot soldiers and entered the city secretly. The guards heard and seized their weapons; then all the inhabitants of Gamala learned of Titus’s arrival. Those who were exhausted fled to the edge of the mountain, as before, dragging after them their wailing wives and children. Those who were still strong resisted Titus and tried to flee to the mountain. But Titus got ahead of them and blocked their path. So all the inhabitants of Gamala fell, and everywhere was the cry and groaning of the slain, and blood flowed from above and drowned the city.

And Vespasian brought up the whole army and helped his son. There was at the top of the mountain a place all of stone and very high, and around it great chasms, and from above the Jews fought back with stones and arrows. Suddenly a storm rose against the Jews and turned back what they threw. And dust rose from the tower and prevented them from seeing, and the storm swept the Jews into the chasm.

Then God’s help was with the Romans. And they climbed the mountain, and, remembering what the inhabitants of Gamala had done to them before, spared no one. Many Jews, surrounded by the Romans, embraced their wives and children and threw themselves into the chasms.

And there were four thousand slain, and thirty thousand threw themselves into the chasm. Only two women escaped death, who were the daughters of Philip’s sister. And that Philip was a commander of King Agrippa. Thus Gamala was taken on the twenty-third day of the month Hyperberetaios, that is, October.

Gischala alone of the cities of Galilee was unconquered. The people there were peaceful and disposed to submit to the Romans, for all were farmers and cared only for the fruits of the earth. However, many of the rebels came to Gischala. And John, son of Levi, a sorcerer, a wicked and arrogant man, who sought war in order to rule over all, stirred up the inhabitants of Gischala to revolt.

Vespasian sent Titus against him with a thousand horsemen. The tenth legion went to Scythopolis.

Vespasian himself with the two remaining legions went to Caesarea. There the legions could rest after long labor and strengthen themselves and their horses with abundant food. These legions Vespasian prepared for the campaign against Jerusalem. And the commander understood that great labor awaited him in Jerusalem, for it was the reigning city of the Jews. All who fled from the war gathered there. The place was fortified by nature, and its wall structures greatly troubled Vespasian.

The fortress of the people of Jerusalem, their rebelliousness and courage, were considered invincible. Therefore Vespasian gave his soldiers rest before the coming trials.
