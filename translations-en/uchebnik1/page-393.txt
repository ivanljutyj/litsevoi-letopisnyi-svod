And Zeno appointed Stephen Patriarch of Antioch in place of Peter. And Zeno gave many gifts to all and took up his kingdom.

In the reign of Zeno, Bishop Stephen, whom Zeno had appointed, was killed with sharpened reeds by certain of the Antioch clergy. This happened when Stephen went out of the city with a prayer procession to the Church of the Holy Forty, to the baptistery named in honor of the holy martyr Barlaam. This Stephen was accused of Nestorianism, and his body was thrown into the Orontes River.

And having learned this, Zeno appointed another patriarch, whose name was Calandion. But he too was exiled as a Nestorian.

And Bishop Peter was brought back from exile by Zeno, because the people of Antioch pleaded for him, crying out. And Peter came from Euchait and again became patriarch, and died on his throne, wearing his hierarchal vestments.

In the reign of Zeno, Armatus was appointed general, and his son became Caesar. And they sat with Zeno in the hippodrome, watched the spectacle, and paid honor to the riders, both the emperor and the Caesar.

And Zeno thought: “Armatus the general, father of the Caesar, has trampled on his oath, for he swore by holy Baptism to King Basiliscus that he would not turn away from him, but through my promise Armatus betrayed Basiliscus, and Basiliscus died. How then will this Armatus be faithful to my reign? A little time will yet pass, and when his son the Caesar comes of age, Armatus will in any case turn away from me too. Yet I did not turn away from him, but even appointed him to the post of general and made his son Caesar.” And Zeno ordered Armatus to be killed as an oath-breaker. And they killed him in the imperial palace as he was going up the stairs to the hippodrome to watch the spectacle.

And after the killing of Armatus, Zeno appointed his son, the Caesar—he was still a child—bishop in Cyzicus, capital of the Hellespont, since he had been clothed in the imperial purple as Caesar. And Zeno divided up all the property of Armatus.

In the reign of this Zeno, seizing a convenient opportunity, people of the Samaritan nation in Palestine turned away from the authority of the Byzantines and crowned as king the chief of brigands, named Justus, a Samaritan.

And this Justus entered Caesarea in Palestine, and he burned the Church of Saint Procopius, in the time of Timothy, bishop of Caesarea.
