There was planted in the royal palace the plant peganon, that is, rue, worthy of wonder for its size: in height and thickness it resembled a fig tree. This plant had been there since the time of Herod and remained until now, and it could still have remained there for a long time, if those who occupied Machaerus had not cut it down. On the southern side of Machaerus, within the gorge, there is a slope called Baaras. It is named after the shining root that grows there. This root has the appearance of fire, and at night it shines and sheds light upon those who approach and touch it. It is not easy to seize this root, for it flees and does not stand still until it is poured over with a woman’s urine or menstrual blood; then it ceases to flee. If anyone suddenly pulls it out, he will die.

There is a way to obtain this root without harm to oneself. They dig around the plant, even to the last rootlet, and then tie a dog to the plant, and place food for it in such a way that it cannot reach it. And the dog, when it grows hungry, strains toward the food and thus pulls out this root. And at once the dog dies, as if given in exchange for the man who wishes to take the root. After this there is no danger in taking the root and touching it. And this root must be obtained with such difficulty because of one of its properties. For when unclean demons enter a man, this root can drive them out if it is applied to the nostrils of the one possessed by demons.

On Various Springs

Warm springs also flow near Machaerus, with different tastes: some of them salty, others fresh. There are also many wells of water there. There is also a cave, not very deep, sheltered by rocks, and above this cave are two hills like women’s breasts, standing opposite each other. From one hill gushes a spring colder than ice, and on the other hill is a hot spring. At the place where the two streams join, a bathing place has been made, bringing healing from ailments to those who bathe in it, and also beneficial for the vessels. In the same land where Machaerus is, both sulfur and asphalt are produced.

Bassus, having inspected the fortress, planned to make an assault from the eastern gorge and began to fill it up, striving quickly to raise a mound and besiege the city.

The local Jews of Machaerus, finding themselves surrounded by the Romans, separated themselves from the newcomers and forced them to remain in the Lower fortress and hold back the enemy’s onslaught, since the local Jews considered the newcomers alien and impious. The inhabitants of Machaerus themselves entered the Upper fortress and lived there, trusting in its strength. They thought of their own safety and hoped to obtain pardon from the Romans if they surrendered to them.
