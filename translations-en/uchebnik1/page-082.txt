He set off for Rome with greater pomp and honor than this Antipater, and no one returned more dejectedly and ingloriously. Already he had begun little by little to understand that there was misfortune in the house, but he did not give himself away by a word. And inwardly he was mortally frightened, but he made his face proud. He had nowhere to flee, and he had no firm assurance in anything. He hoped, however, that his deeds were not disclosed, and if they were disclosed, he would conceal them with deceitful speeches, a shameless tongue, a false oath, and flattery.

With all this Antipater armed himself as with weapons and came to his father. At the very first gate those accompanying him were not admitted and were not allowed to pass. At that time the Syrian governor Varus was in the king's chambers. Antipater, adding audacity to his pride, approached his father and wished to greet him. But he threw up his hands, turned his head aside, and exclaimed: "O earth! O heaven! O sun! Can a parricide embrace a father against whom he has plotted so much evil! Die, impious head! Fall, lawless one! Do not touch me until you have justified yourself in what you are accused of! I am instituting judgment over you, and here is your judge—Varus, who has arrived just in time. Go and seek your defense until tomorrow; I give you time for your fabrications." But Antipater could not say a word from horror and went away.

And Antipater's mother and wife came to him and told him all that had happened, and informed him of the tortures and the uncovering of the conspiracy. Then Antipater began to think hard about how to answer at the trial in order to save himself.

The next day the king gathered his rulers, his relatives, and Antipater's friends; he himself sat on a raised place, and Varus beside him somewhat lower. And by the king's command all the witnesses were brought in. Among them were the maidservants mentioned above, and Antipater's mother with a letter in which it was written: "Everything we have done has been revealed by your father. Do not come to him until you receive some protection from Caesar." When all had been brought in, Antipater also entered. And he fell at his father's feet and said: "I beseech you, father, do not condemn me before you learn the truth, but hear my defense with wisdom, and I will prove my innocence, if you wish it." Herod ordered him to be silent and said to Varus: "I know that you, Varus, and every righteous judge will recognize Antipater as dishonorable. But I fear that he will wish to blacken me, and you will condemn me also, and deem me worthy of every sorrow, since I have raised such sons. Yet I am also worthy of mercy, since even to such accursed children I was still a generous and merciful father. I honored my first sons with royal rank and made them friends of Caesar, and they were envied. And after that I discovered that they were plotting to kill me.
