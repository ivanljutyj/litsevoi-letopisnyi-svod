So it was here also: God showed signs of His wrath, so that people might understand that God was angry, and might turn away from their wickedness, and thereby entreat God to be merciful to them. The Jews had a prophecy that because of a four-cornered image the royal city would be left desolate. Yet the Jews themselves began making crosses for crucifixions, which, as was said, are four-formed, and after the destruction of the Tower of Antonia they made the temple four-cornered. The Jews were also stirred to war by an ambiguous prophecy, which can be found in the holy books, that in those very times someone from the land of Judea would reign over the whole universe. This prophecy was given various interpretations: some thought it concerned Herod, some the crucified Wonderworker, some Vespasian. But it is impossible for people to escape fate, even if they know what is prepared for them. The Jews interpreted some signs as they wished,

and other signs they disregarded, and in the end, when they had destroyed both themselves and their fatherland, they were convicted and put to shame, and their folly was revealed.

When the rebels fled to the city, while the temple and all the surrounding places were burning, the Romans brought their idols and set them in the temple before the eastern gates, and there offered sacrifices to them, and glorified Titus with great honor, and called him autocrat.

All the soldiers had much wealth, both acquired and seized by them. Because of this, in the land of Syria gold was exchanged at half its former price. The priests fled to the walls and waited there. One young man among them, driven by thirst, begged the Roman guards to spare him and let him drink. The guards took pity on him and promised to spare him. Then the young man came down to them and drank water, and filled a vessel, and carrying it fled back to his own. The guards could not catch him and began to accuse him of treachery. He answered: “I have not broken my promise. For I asked you to preserve my life only on condition that I go down and draw water, and not remain with you. I went down and drew water, so I did not break my word.” Thus the guards were deceived by the young man and marveled at his cunning.

On the fifth day the priests, exhausted from hunger, came down, and when they were brought to Titus, they begged him to grant them salvation. Titus said to them: “The time of forgiveness has passed, since there is no longer that for the sake of which you could be preserved. It is fitting for priests to perish with the temple.” And having said this, Titus ordered that they be tortured and killed.
