Do you not submit to the Creator of the earth and of all that is on it, but to another who cunningly philosophizes?

Answer of Bishop Hilarion.
For the Lord says: “Our Father, who art in heaven, Thy will be done, as in heaven so on earth”; and again: “I confess to Thee, Father, Lord of heaven and earth”; He showed Him to be Lord not only of heaven but also of earth.

Question of the Manichees.
Why do you call the most pure Mother of God the heavenly Jerusalem, and call the Lord’s flesh descended from heaven, and say that Christ became man only in appearance?

Answer of the Bishop.
From what Scripture or from which prophets did you learn to call the Mother of God the upper Jerusalem? For the evangelist Matthew clearly says: “The birth of Jesus Christ was thus: when His mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Spirit.” And since the Mother of God Mary bore our Lord Jesus Christ according to the flesh, therefore the Savior in the Gospel calls Himself the Son of Man. Likewise, if Christ’s body were heavenly and incorruptible, it would not have been subject to human passions—I mean hunger and thirst, sleep and labor, sorrow and tears, and other such things. And if He were a heavenly man, to whom the Word was united in composition, He would not be corruptible, mortal, and earthly; for heavenly inhabitants are incorruptible and immortal. And if He brought down flesh from heaven, what need was there for Him to be incarnate of a virgin? The Lord showed that He truly took our flesh from the Virgin, neither in appearance nor in imagination, as you say. After the Resurrection He said to the disciples: “Touch My hands and feet, and see that a spirit has not flesh and bones, as you see Me have.” Likewise the Lord’s flesh was not from heaven, as the apostle declares plainly: “For He did not at any time take hold of angels, but He took hold of the seed of Abraham; therefore He had to become like His brethren in all things.” This plainly shows that Christ took corruptible and mortal flesh from the most pure Mother of God Mary, and that He was not incarnate in imagination, but truly took human flesh; therefore He was circumcised on the eighth day. And this alone is sufficient to show that Christ’s flesh is akin to our flesh, and not brought down from heaven. Likewise the evangelists nowhere called the holy Mother of God the upper Jerusalem; from whom, then, did you receive such an impious teaching?

Question of the Manichees.
We say that the ancient Law was from the evil one, and we call the cross hostile, and therefore we do not venerate it.

Answer of the Bishop.
Arise, people, from the darkness lying over you and hear: if the ancient Law was from the evil one, how then does Christ say: “Search the Scriptures, for in them you will find eternal life”? The Scriptures meant are those of Moses and the prophets, for at that time the books of the new grace did not yet exist. But in an evil lawgiving, how can one find eternal life?
