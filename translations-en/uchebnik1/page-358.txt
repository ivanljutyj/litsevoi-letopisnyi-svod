The church of the Lord? With which feet will you step onto the holy platform? How will you lift your hands, from which the blood of those killed without guilt still drips? How will you receive the Master’s Body with such hands? How will you receive the precious Blood with your lips? For because of one word from these lips, spoken in lawless fury, so much blood was shed. Therefore depart and do not try to multiply the first lawlessness with a second, and do not come to Communion, and accept these spiritual bonds, and God Most High be witness to this.”

Hearing this, the emperor, weeping and wailing, withdrew to the imperial chambers in Milan, and remained in them for eight months, repenting greatly and weeping.

At the feast of Christ’s Nativity, Rufinus the magister saw that the emperor did not wish, according to custom, to make the proeleusis, that is, the procession, and said to him: “I will go, lord, to the priest and beseech him to loose you from these bonds, if you command.” Theodosius said: “I know Ambrose’s severity, so he will not listen to you.” Rufinus spoke much and urged the emperor, and said: “I will persuade Ambrose.” And the emperor ordered Rufinus to go to him.

Hope also arose in Theodosius, and he did not restrain himself long, and followed Rufinus. Ambrose saw Rufinus and said: “You imitate a dog’s shamelessness, O Rufinus, becoming a participant in so great a murder! But I, as before, say that I will not again allow Theodosius to enter even the forecourt of the sanctuary.”

And hearing this, Rufinus quickly sent a messenger to the emperor with this news. The emperor was going and was on the way to the church, and hearing this, said: “I will go to the church and immediately receive the punishments I deserve.”

And Theodosius came to the church and begged the bishop to loose him from the bonds. The bishop said: “You do this by the power of your authority and rise up in war against God.” The emperor said in a contrite voice and with many tears: “I have not come in fury to the holy feast, but I have come to entreat that you loose me from the bonds.” Ambrose said: “What repentance have you shown after so many lawless deeds? With what medicines have you healed the incurable sores?” The emperor said: “It is your task to prepare the needed remedy, and mine to accept its bitterness.”

Then Ambrose said: “Since, overcome by fury, you quickly, without investigation, committed such bloodshed, write a law that nothing be done in fury and without investigation, but that all human cases concerning riots and murders be investigated justly for up to thirty days.” The emperor ordered such a law to be written and sealed it with his ring.
