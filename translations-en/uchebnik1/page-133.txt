And Titus came and took the city. And 15 thousand were killed in it, and three thousand were taken captive.

But the Samaritans gathered on Mount Gerizim, which is considered by them a holy mountain, and wished to fight with the Romans. And Vespasian learned of this, and sent to them the commander Cerealis with six hundred horsemen and three thousand foot soldiers.

But the Samaritans, who were surrounded on the mountain by the Romans, were left without water, and unbearable heat scorched them, and some of them could not endure the thirst and surrendered into slavery. And Cerealis learned that the Samaritans were all exhausted from thirst, and attacked them, and killed 20 thousand Samaritans. On the 4th day of the month Panemus, that is, July, Vespasian returned from Jotapata to Ptolemais, and from there went to Caesarea, in which the greater part of the population were Greeks. And the inhabitants of Caesarea honored the commander with great gifts. But they shouted about Josephus that he should be tortured. Vespasian did not listen to them. And Vespasian left two regiments in Caesarea for provisions, and sent two to Scythopolis.

And then all the fugitives from the cities ravaged by the Romans gathered in Joppa, which had been destroyed by Cestius, and rebuilt it again, so that it might be a stronghold for them, and they built ships, and sailed as far as Phoenicia and Syria, and plundered everywhere.

And news of this came to Vespasian, and he sent foot soldiers and horsemen against Joppa, and took the city by night.

And a very strong wind blew from the sea, and all the inhabitants of Joppa who had taken refuge on the ships fell into the hands of the Romans, and all were killed; not one escaped. And there were 8 thousand of them. Thus Joppa was in a short time a second time subdued by the Romans.

And news came to Jerusalem of the destruction of Jotapata and of the Roman conquest, and they could not believe that such a great misfortune had happened, because there was no man who had seen it with his own eyes. For not a single messenger escaped from Jotapata. However, the news of this spread by itself, and it was added that Josephus also had been killed. And there was a great cry in Jerusalem, and each wept for his own relative, and all lamented Josephus for 30 days.

But when after this they learned that Josephus was alive and was with Vespasian, and enjoyed honor, they became furious with him, calling him a traitor. And the Jews began to prepare for a clash with the Romans, in order to seize Josephus.

But Agrippa summoned Vespasian to his region to show him honor and glorify him. Then also two cities of Agrippa came out from under his authority, Tiberias and Taricheae.
