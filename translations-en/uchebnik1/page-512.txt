Nikephoros’s son Staurakios, wounded in the right thigh, barely escaped and was brought on a litter to Constantinople.

On Nikola the Warrior. With Emperor Nikephoros, who was leading an army against the Bulgars, was a certain soldier named Nikola. Overtaken by darkness on the way, they stopped at an inn for the night. And at night the innkeeper’s daughter came to him and began to urge him to shameful intercourse. But he rejected her. After a time she came again, and he sent her away again. Tempted by the devil, she came a third time. He said to her: “Wretched woman, full of every shamelessness! Do you not know that demons inflame you to destroy your soul, while I am going to the pagans for bloodshed?” And having said this, he sent her away again.

When he fell asleep, he saw in a dream a Certain Fearsome One sitting, having placed His right leg over His left. And He says to him: “Look!” Nikola answers: “I see, Lord, that the Greeks are overcoming the Bulgars.” Then He moved His left leg over His right, and Nikola saw that now the Bulgars were overcoming the Greeks, and behold, the whole Greek army was overthrown. And he saw a field strewn with dead bodies, and among them an empty place, large enough for one single man. And That Fearsome One said to him: “This place was prepared for you; you were to lie there beside your companions. But because this night you conquered the crafty serpent who three times tempted you to shameful intercourse, by this you saved yourself from spiritual and bodily destruction, and left a place in the field without prey; and from now on you will serve Me.”

In the morning the battle took place. He, remaining one of the few alive after the battle, mourning the fallen soldiers, took monastic vows and later became a great and clairvoyant elder. That is why one should abstain from fornication not only during battles but always, since it destroys both body and soul. And during battles it is fitting to repent and partake of the Holy Mysteries of Christ, and so with all zeal stand for the holy churches of God and for Orthodox Christianity.

The 39th Reign, of Staurakios, who reigned in Constantinople

After Nikephoros, his son Staurakios reigned for one year and two months. Because of the wound in his thigh he lay in his chambers and could not take part in the affairs of state.
