The 33rd Reign of Valerian, Who Became Tsar in Rome
in the Year 5761 from Adam (261 from Christ)

After Aemilian, Valerian and Gallienus his son reigned 15 years, and he began a war with King Shapur.

And he was taken captive by the spear in the city of Samaria, although he had 400,000 soldiers, and King Shapur killed him by flaying him. Gallienus after him was the first to introduce cavalry detachments, for the Roman soldiers were for the most part on foot. And he was killed by soldiers. Under him God sent great wrath upon all the earth: a deadly disease destroyed the human race to annihilation. For fumes rose both from the earth and from the sea; moreover, by the blowing of the winds the smells of rivers and lakes were carried, as if their vapors contained the putrefaction of the dead. And because of this such destruction and severe incurable diseases seized the earth that there was a countless multitude of human deaths. Wailing and sighing over the multitude of the perishing were everywhere—no less than when the Egyptian firstborn perished. For there was no house where the stench of unburied corpses did not stand, since merciless pagan people, fearing to be infected by the deadly disease, threw out people even before they had died, sparing neither relatives nor friends nor any other neighbors, but striving to escape the deadly affliction. Many who feared the Lord, without protecting themselves, tended the sick and accepted suffering, departing with them from this life. Others among those who were not themselves ill brought some of the sick into their homes, giving them rest because of their illness, and thereby took on their death. Under him Dionysius, bishop of Alexandria, Porphyrius from the city of Ephesus, and Philemon from the land of Lycia were martyred.

The 34th Reign of Claudius, Who Became Tsar in Rome
in the Year 5776 from Adam (276 from Christ)

After Valerian, Claudius reigned one year. He was the grandfather of Consta, father of Great Constantine, the holy tsar. The Scythians who came in his time, having captured all the cities, came to Athens and seized them. And having gathered all the books, they wanted to burn them, but one of them, better than the others and knowing that there was much meaning in them, forbade this, saying that as long as the Greeks were occupied with books, they would not care for war. And Claudius, having appointed Aurelian to his kingdom, died.

The 35th Reign of Quintillus, Who Became Tsar in Rome
in the Year 5777 from Adam (277 from Christ)

After Claudius, Quintillus his brother, proclaimed tsar, reigned one year. He, having lived but a short time, accomplished nothing worthy of mention.
