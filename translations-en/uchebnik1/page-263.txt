He shed even more tears when he saw the divine church burning with fire, praying God to grant him mercy and forgiveness, since this had not happened by his will but because of the god-abhorrent wickedness of the Jews. Therefore, after the destruction of Jerusalem, to those who proclaimed him victor and destroyer, he said, speaking of the killing, that he had not himself wrought it, but had been obliged to serve and render aid to the divine punishment sent by God. Because of his virtuous mercy and wise humility, he was renowned for his prudence to the very end. And such great weeping seized Rome after his death that for a long time afterward they remembered him, and reasoning of his virtue, wept in public and in their homes, as though each of them were afflicted by his own sins.

In his time, also, at Mount Vesuvius in the west, the summit split open, and boiling fire poured out so that it burned the surrounding country with its cities. Seeing this, the Greeks were greatly amazed and asked certain wise Christians: “How and from where did fire come out from the bottom of the earth?” They answered: “The fire came out from the Gehenna prepared for the devil and his servants and for all sinners and ungodly people, pointing out to those who sin against chastity and reason.” Our glorious philosopher Plato also told of this in the Phaedo: about the dwelling and fate of souls, that those who lived evilly are afterward punished to such a degree and cast into weeping and scorching fire, into the lower place that is in the depths of the earth. Thousands of Greeks at that time marveled at this.

The divine Patricius, bishop of Prusa and martyr, was asked publicly before the people by the judge, the anthypatos, about the hot springs that flow of themselves: “From where do they issue?” And he answered thus: “God created fire and water from non-being into substance, and from fire He fashioned by the great Word, as also light, the sun, and the other luminaries, and commanded them to shine by day and night, since His power is the same as His will. From water, having spread out the firmament of heaven and created the earth upon the water, He also created all living things in them by His provident action, so that man, whom He intended to create, should lack nothing of the best and most useful.

In them He afterward prepared two eternal places, arranging that one should shine with light and be filled with endless goods, and creating the other for darkness and eternal torment, so that those who pleased Him and did not slide down into the devil’s deceit after the Resurrection, having received a place of eternal goods, might reign with Him forever. But those who angered Him and followed the common enemy will receive, with him, a place of dark torments, so that they may be filled with them forever and endlessly.
