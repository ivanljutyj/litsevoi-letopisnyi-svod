Other children, the strongest, were still alive even after thirty days. And others sucked at their dead mothers and lay beside them; living children lay by them and sucked from dead women. And there was much else more wondrous than this and incredible, which human tongue cannot tell, but only the one immortal God knows His mysteries.

On the third day after the fall of the city, that is, on the Lord’s Day, at the eighth hour, the Precious Cross appeared in the sky in a cloud, turned toward the western side of the city.

And all saw this and remained weeping and praying, and for a full hour the vision also remained. After the fall of the city there were also very many other earthquakes; one might say they occurred every hour. That deadly day continued for a long time—a year and a half. And nothing that remained fell, neither house nor church nor anything else. And there was no further collapse at any hour, but, as someone said, it was as if God, the Lover of Mankind, had commanded them to stand thus and not fall, and these buildings did not fall from the earthquakes that occurred after this.

Seleucia and Daphne, and their surroundings for twenty stadia in length and breadth, perished even more from collapses during the earthquakes.

And this divine emperor Justin sent many centenaria of gold, as no other emperor had, for the rebuilding of what had been destroyed. As soon as what had happened was heard of, the emperor immediately took off his crown and purple and grieved over it for a long time and wept, and did nothing, nor held any spectacles in Constantinople, for Justin knew that city, Antioch, and honored and loved it.

Also, on Holy Thursday of Easter, Justin entered the church of Constantinople, and came out from the imperial chambers to the cathedral without a crown, wearing only purple, and lamented what had happened before the nobles and the townspeople. And all the nobles put on garments of mourning, and, looking at Justin, also lamented the destruction of Antioch.

This emperor first sent from Constantinople the comes Carinus with five centenaria of gold to dig out whatever might have survived of the citizens’ property, and to inspect the destruction carefully, and to come and tell the emperor what should be done.

Then Justin sent the patrician Phocas, son of Craterus, a rich man, and with him he sent the patrician Asterius and the city eparch, a wise man, and Justin said: “Take care also of the other people who survived, and also that the remaining buildings of the city be rebuilt quickly.” And he gave them much gold, and told them about other places in the city, and about the public baths, and about the aqueduct, and about the districts of that city, since Justin himself knew that city well, for he had lived in it a long time, when he went with a voivode...
