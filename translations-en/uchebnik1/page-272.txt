He gathered them at his place, divided them, and, comparing them with one another and with the Hebrew Genesis itself, skillfully and very reasonably, after much testing, produced the aforementioned Hexapla. But his glory did not remain unquenchable to the end. It happened that he, so greatly experienced in his work, fell so terribly that he even became a cause of temptation and perdition for many. Wishing to leave nothing in Divine Scripture uninterpreted, he cast himself into the pit of sin by interpreting mortal words. For from him Arius took his foundation, and after him all the other impious and wicked. For he reasoned thus, having first dared to assert that the Only-begotten Son cannot see the Father, nor the Holy Spirit the Son, nor the angels the Spirit, nor men the angels, and that the Son cannot proceed from the substance of the Father, but is a creation, and is called Son by grace. And for sinful souls, after the end of the ages, punishment will come to an end when their sins are cleansed, and demons, he says, will again be angels, and demons and men will be together, and will not be tormented, but will become free. And many other blasphemies he uttered. Thus he set down instructions in each of his works. To the extent that his discourses, spoken in parables about the habits and nature of irrational animals, were rich in many grace-filled explanations, to that same extent his reasonings about the faith prove completely absurd. He led such an ascetic life that his skin, they say, grew weak from excessive fasting and exhaustion. They also say of his body that he himself cut a vein because of its pleasure, so that it would not trouble him, while others say he applied a herb to his shameful part, bidding it dry up. Others slander him, saying he invented a medicinal herb for memory. It is also said that, having had a good education and church upbringing, he suffered much for Christ. Out of envy he was seized by rulers in authority: by the devil’s plotting, in an evil thought they accused the man, of whom it became known from these envious men, that an Ethiopian was being assigned to him for the anointing of his body. He, unable to endure such a vile invention, cried out when he was offered to choose one of two things. And when he agreed to offer sacrifice, they put incense in his hand and then poured it on the sacrificial fire, and so those who judged him delivered him from torture, and he was driven out of the temple. Having left Alexandria because of the slanders, he came to Judea. Entering Jerusalem, as a learned confessor he was invited by the priests to speak in the church, for he had formerly been a presbyter. And urged many times by the priests, he stood up and uttered only one saying: “But to the sinner God says: Why do you declare My statutes, and take My covenant in your mouth?” He closed the books and sat down weeping and with tears, and all wept with him too. There is also much else that they tell about him, praising him for his great knowledge and as a writer of books. Therefore he learned to compose, because he wrote many books, not listening as he ought.
