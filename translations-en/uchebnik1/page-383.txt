Geiseric came and took Rome and enslaved it cruelly. He enslaved the empress herself and her daughter Placidia, a maiden, and led them away captive to Carthage, a city of Africa.

Geiseric gave to his son Huneric the younger daughter of Empress Eudoxia, a maiden, and kept her with him in great honor.

Geiseric also killed Maximus, who had killed Valentinian and made himself emperor in Rome, and enslaved all the nobles and their wives. And so Rome completely lost imperial authority and began to submit to barbarian kings.

After Maximus ruled Anthemius and Olybrius the Great; after Olybrius ruled Majorian, then Glycerius, then Nepos, and also Orestes; and after Orestes his son Romulus received power, and he was the last of the Roman emperors. And the city, great among cities, Rome, having Romulus the first king at its beginning, again placed power upon Romulus, the last king. And from that time kings were no longer ruled by emperors, and the rulers and satraps of the Roman lands became princes unto themselves. This befell old Rome. But our New Rome, Constantinople, stands and grows and strengthens and grows young. May it grow even to the End, O King, who reigns over all.

Emperor Theodosius learned that through the wish of Eudoxia, his daughter, Rome had been betrayed, and he was grieved for her, and left her in Africa with Geiseric, and did not send for her, but made departure and went out from Constantinople to Ephesus, a city of Asia, and bowed before St John the Theologian, asking him who should reign after him.

And in a dream Theodosius saw Marcian, his son-in-law, and came from Ephesus to Constantinople.

After a little time, Theodosius went out to hunt and fell from his horse while hunting. And Theodosius entered the city, summoned his sister Pulcheria, and told her about Marcian: “This one is to reign after me.” And she brought Marcian, and Theodosius said to him before all the nobles: “You are to reign after me.” And two days later Theodosius died; he was 51 years old.

The Tenth Reign, of Marcian, who reigned in Constantinople

This Marcian was not of noble birth, but held military rank; and when there was a battle, Marcian was taken captive by Geiseric, prince of the Vandals.

Geiseric wished to see the captives and saw Marcian sleeping, and eagles made shade over him with their wings from the heat of the sun. And from this Geiseric understood that Marcian was a divine man and worthy of the honor of the Greek Empire, and did not order him killed, but agreed to be at peace with him and released him.
