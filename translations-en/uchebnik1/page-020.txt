And Antiochus came to Jerusalem, and he had 20,000 horsemen and 100,000 foot soldiers, and 300 chariots, and 55 elephants trained for war. And Antiochus distributed the elephants among the detachments, and assigned to each elephant 1,000 men in armor, with a single iron chain, and with bronze helmets on their heads, and 500 chosen horsemen assigned to each beast.

And wooden parapets on each beast were fashioned with skilled craftsmanship; and on each elephant were brave men, under arms, 32 in number. When the sun shone upon the golden shields and weapons, the mountains shone from that radiance like burning lamps. And then all who saw this were in confusion, and those who heard the multitude of voices and the creaking of chariots, for the host was strong and exceedingly great, and a countless multitude of warriors had been gathered, armed for a long battle.

Eleazar, the brother of Judas, saw the enraged elephants and the archers who were on the elephants; and he saw one elephant, the tallest and clad in royal armor, and thought that the king was on it, and ran out from his regiment, and drew near to his enemies, and killed many around the elephant, and crept under it, and pierced the elephant's belly from below; and the beast fell upon Eleazar and at once crushed him.

Judas saw this. And he saw that the enemies were prevailing in the battle, and he fled to Jerusalem; and Antiochus pursued him and encamped around the city.

Then they made a peace agreement with an oath, and Judas received Antiochus within the city. But Antiochus entered and broke the oath, and tore down the great city wall, and took captive those living in the city, and returned to Antioch.

Having brought the captives to Antioch, Antiochus tormented them greatly and put them to death. And he scattered the Jewish high priests, and called Solomon's church the temple of Olympian Zeus, and defiled it with swine's flesh, and separated the Jews from the faith of their fathers, and by tortures forced them into paganism; and so he tormented the Jews for 3 years.

Then Judas, in three years, restored the faith of the fathers, and fought many battles, and won great victories over Antiochus's people, and drove them from his lands.

Antiochus tormented the Jews on the mountain of Iakleont before Drios Kasios; and this Antiochus died.

The Nineteenth Reign in Syria

After him his son Antiochus Zekry, surnamed Hierax, reigned 2 years.
