And when the emperor awoke, he was in confusion and summoned to himself Amantius, the chief chamberlain. And Amantius said to Anastasius: “Live forever, O emperor.” And he also said to the emperor: “I saw this night how a great one came, and took you by the edge of the purple robe, and shook you, and cast you to the ground.”

And the emperor summoned Proclus, the Asian philosopher and dream-interpreter, and told him of the dreams. And the dream-interpreter said to the emperor: “Within a year you both will die.”

And in the reign of Emperor Zeno, his boyar Illus, of whom it was said earlier, having gone away to the eastern lands, permitted Empress Verina to be freed, and took with him the patrician Leontius, that he should go with him, and that the mother of Empress Ariadne, Lady Verina, should come to them.

This Illus set out, and other boyars with him, and he entered Antioch the Great, having with him a multitude of soldiers and the aforementioned Leontius, a patrician of Thracian lineage. And many other princes and commanders were with Illus.

And Illus remained in Antioch for two years, building certain parts of the city; and he built much for the Antiochenes.

And Illus left Antioch for Isauria, after Lady Verina, and brought her out of the fortress, and told her to install as emperor, in the Church of Saint Peter near the city of Tarsus in Cilicia, the patrician Leontius.

And Illus persuaded Verina to place the crown on this Leontius. Leontius was handsome, curly-haired, hairy, young, clean, fair-skinned, with a straight nose and beautiful clear eyes.

And this Lady Verina wrote letters in her own name to the Antiochene Syrians, that they should receive Leontius as emperor and not oppose him.

And to the princes and soldiers of the eastern and Egyptian domains Verina wrote letters. These letters were read in Antioch first before the commanders, and they had this content: “Peaceful Verina, perpetual Augusta, to the Antiochenes, our citizens, the Syrians! You know that the kingdom is ours, and that after the death of Leo of good life we appointed as emperor Traskolissus, named Zeno. And all the military ranks desired that he should rise to this station. But seeing now the city with all our subjects going backward, because of the greed of this Zeno, we have well considered to set over you as emperor a pious Christian adorned with righteousness, that he may enlarge the Roman cities and end all wars, and preserve for the obedient all that is established by law. And behold, we have appointed the pious Leontius as emperor to the Romans, who will deem you all worthy of his care.” And immediately all the people cried out with one voice: “God and the Lord have through you done good and useful things.”
