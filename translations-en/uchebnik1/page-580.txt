And the city of Demetrias in the region of Hellas was captured by the Saracens.

And Emperor Leo crowns Anna, the daughter of Zoe, who was of the Zautzes family, because by custom it was impossible to hold receptions without an empress.

The emperor brought a girl from the Opsikion region, beautiful and fair, whose name was Eudokia; he took her as his wife and crowned her.

A child was born of her, and both she herself and the newborn boy died.

Near the Church of the Holy Apostles the emperor built a beautiful church in the name of his first wife, Theophano.

And he built the Church of Saint Lazarus near the so-called Places, and established in it a monastery for eunuchs, where he transferred and laid the body of Saint Lazarus and the body of Mary Magdalene.

While the naval troops were occupied with the building of these churches, Tauromenium, a city in Sicily, was seized by the Africans, and many Greeks were killed.

And Lumnos (Lemnos) was captured by the Saracens, and many soldiers were captured.

When the emperor was making his entrance to Saint Mocius on the day of Mid-Pentecost, at the entrance, near the holy gates, a man leaped out from the ambo and struck him hard on the head with a large stick; he would have killed him had not the end of the stick, catching in a panikandilo, been checked by some protection and restrained the force of the blow.

Blood gushed from the emperor’s head, and in the confusion that arose the nobles fled.

His brother Alexander, pleading illness, did not go to the entrance; because of this he was suspected of complicity in the conspiracy.

The man who wounded the emperor was seized, and after enduring many tortures he said that he had no accomplices; finally, with his hands and feet cut off, he was burned on the wheel in the Hippodrome.

After that, such an entrance was no longer made. After some time Mark, a most wise monk and the custodian of that church, who on Great Saturday had performed the four-ode hymn of the Great Cosmas, at a meal in conversation with the emperor asked him not to refuse such an entrance.

Since the emperor did not agree, Mark said that it had been foretold by the prophet David that you were destined to endure this.

For he said: “How greatly has the enemy acted wickedly against thy holy one, and those who hate thee have boasted in the midst of thy festival” (Ps 73:3). It is fitting for you, lord, from this day to reign and hold power for ten years.” So it was, to the end of the tenth year: on the day on which he was wounded, he died.
