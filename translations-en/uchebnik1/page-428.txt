“To Your Holiness, having gathered together, diligently to discuss the correction commanded by us, and to condemn each of those chapters, and to anathematize with the impious Origen all who have thought, or think, or shall think such things, even to the end of time.” The council heard these words of Emperor Justinian and put to shame all those of whom it had been spoken. The divine fathers, crying out, said: “We anathematize all those of whom it has been said, together with all who have spoken or thought, or think, or shall think such things, even to the end of time.” Likewise, concerning Theodore of Mopsuestia and others, it was then said in the imperial edict: “It was the care of the Orthodox and pious ancestors who reigned before us that at all times the shoots of heresy should be cut off by councils of venerable fathers, and the right faith should be purely preached in the world, and the Holy Church of God preserved.”

On the First Council. Thus Constantine the Great stood against Arius, who blasphemed and said: “The Son is not consubstantial with God the Father, but is a creature, and was created out of nothing.” And Constantine gathered 318 holy fathers in Nicaea, and himself came there, and when Arius had been anathematized, he took care to establish the Orthodox faith. For this reason the divine fathers confessed: “The Son is consubstantial with God the Father.” Even to this day we so sing and believe.

On the Second Council. Under Theodosius the Elder, Macedonius rejected the divinity of the Holy Spirit, while Apollinarius and his disciple blasphemed against the dispensation of God the Word and said: “The Word of God did not receive a human mind, but was united with flesh having an irrational soul.” And Theodosius gathered 150 holy fathers in Constantinople, and himself took part in the council, and anathematized the aforementioned heretics, and destroyed their impious teaching and those who thought with them, and established the preaching of the Orthodox faith.

On the Third Council. And under Theodosius the Younger, the impious Nestorius said that God the Word is one thing and Christ another; and he argued that God the Word is the Son of God the Father by nature, while Christ is the Son of God by grace; and this Nestorius rejected that Holy Mary is the Mother of God. And Theodosius gathered 200 holy fathers in council at Ephesus, and sent for the nobles who wished to be present at the council, and commanded Nestorius also to come, and the holy fathers to conduct an inquiry concerning him. And the inquiry was thorough, and they condemned Nestorius and anathematized those who thought with him.

Concerning the Orthodox. After this event, those who were among the rejected Nestorians rose up against the venerable Cyril, and they tried, as far as they could, to have the condemnation that had occurred reviewed.
