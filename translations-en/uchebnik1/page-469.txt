The 24th Reign, of Constans, Son of Constantine, Who Reigned in the City of Constantine

After Heraclonas, Constans, son of Constantine, reigned for 27 years. Under this Emperor Constans a great wind blew fiercely and uprooted many great trees, and overthrew many columns of stylites.

About the Heretic Bohmit

A certain wicked Saracen leader, the false prophet Bohmit, was by birth from one tribe of Ishmael, son of Abraham, born of a slave woman. They were children of Ishmael, and Ishmael was called father to them all. He begot two sons: Mudar and Ravia. Mudar begot Kusar, and Kaniton, and Afimimian, and Asadon, and some others whose names are unknown. These inherited the Midianite desert, fed on livestock, and lived in booths, that is, in tents. There were also inner tribes among these, that is, distant ones; they were not from the tribe of these, but from the tribe of Nektan; these were called Homerites. The aforementioned Bohmit was poor, and it happened that he hired himself out to a rich woman, his kinswoman, named Digana, to conduct commerce on camels, that is, to trade, with his fellow tribesmen in Egypt and Palestine. Then, gradually growing bold, this Bohmit seduced the woman, for she was a widow, and took her as his wife. While staying in Palestine, Bohmit lived with Jews and Christians, catching some teachings from their writings. He had an illness—he fell into swoons. Bohmit was afflicted, for his wife grieved over this, since she was from a great family and rich, and had married such a husband, not only poor but also falling into swoons. Having devised a story, Bohmit said to her: “I see a fearful vision—the archangel named Gabriel—and I cannot endure his vision; I grow faint and am tormented.” His wife came to believe him when a certain Arius falsely testified about Bohmit, falsely calling himself a monk for wicked gain, that is, for base profit. This completely deceived her, and so Bohmit’s wife was deceived and spoke to other women, her fellow tribeswomen, and preached to them that Bohmit was truly a prophet. This Bohmit also deceived, and with his lie he came to the man, the prince of that tribe, named Vuvakhar. The wife died, and she left Bohmit as the receiver and heir of her property and wealth, and from that time Bohmit became famous and rich. And the evil delusion of his heresy spread in the Ephrivian lands.
