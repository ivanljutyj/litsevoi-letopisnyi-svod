But the Idumeans, having found no treachery in the city, as the slanderers had told them, repented that they had come. Then one of the Zealots, who had a little fear of God, gathered them and told them that they had committed lawlessness together with those who had summoned them, and he humbly asked and entreated them to stop the bloodshed and not to support those who were destroying the fatherland.

The Idumeans listened and first of all released from the prisons two thousand prisoners whom the Zealots had not managed to kill. Then the Idumeans departed to their own lands.

Then the Zealots went about the city and, if anyone was noble or brave, they seized and bound such people, so that none of the best men would remain. They killed also Gorion, a noble man, and Niger, a brave man, who cried out much and showed them the wounds he had received in battles for the fatherland; and they dragged him through the city. Then Niger despaired of living and begged for burial, but the Zealots, before killing him, did not promise him even that. As he was dying, Niger cursed them, calling down upon them famine and pestilence and discord and fear and war, because they had struck down their own defenders and repaid evil for virtues. And all these curses were fulfilled by God. Not because of Niger alone, but also because of other righteous men killed under Herod, and under Archelaus, and under Antipas, and under Pilate, and under Agrippa, and until now. All that blood gathered together and drowned the city. And no one escaped the hands of the Zealots except the most wretched and poor.

The Romans learned of what had happened in Jerusalem and advised all to go into the city for plunder, saying: "God is our helper, who has raised up the hands of our adversaries so that they kill one another. But we must attack them more quickly, lest they soon come to agreement against us."

Vespasian said to them: "You have departed from your former understanding if you wish to show your courage without losses, as for show. We must look to what is useful and what will strengthen us. If we go against the city now, the enemies, seeing this, will make peace among themselves and turn all their forces against us. But if we wait, those forces will exhaust themselves. And we shall fight with a smaller army. God, a better commander than I, delivers the Jews to us without struggle and grants us victory without losses on our side. Therefore we ought to sit as at a spectacle and watch those cutting one another down, rather than engage with men who seek death. If anyone thinks that our victory will not be glorious, I answer him that victory by arms is unreliable and unpredictable; it is better to obtain victory without noise. And if we delay, it is not to our harm but to the enemies'. Is the enemy fortifying walls, or weapons?"
