The Jewish War.
Flavius Josephus

The Book of Josephus, Son of Mattathias, a Jew

We now turn to the account of the Herodian kings, and to what history tells us about them. We shall speak also of those who held lesser rank under the Herods, of princes and rulers. Many of them — Archelaus, Herod, Antipater, Alexander the son-in-law of Archelaus, Pheroras, Salome, and others — had much vain pride. They dared to go against justice and did things contrary to truth, and often by their own intrigues brought harm upon themselves, as will be told further on. In due time the malice of the evildoer is revealed, how he spread slander and uttered words full of deadly poison. Because of such vain pride Archelaus, king of Cappadocia, was angry with Alexander his son-in-law, the son of Herod. Herod also, king of Judea, was angry with Pheroras his brother and Salome his sister because of the multitude of their wicked deeds.

Then King Herod exposed Pheroras his brother in his crime. Pheroras put on dark clothing to show his grief, and weeping fell at Herod’s feet, and asked forgiveness, and called himself a criminal and a wretch. Pheroras admitted that he was the author of all the evil that Herod’s son Alexander had written against him; Pheroras acknowledged his folly and explained it by love for a woman. And Pheroras turned Herod’s anger away from himself. And Archelaus, king of Cappadocia, who was present, supporting Pheroras, addressed Herod: “I too suffer from my brother, and endure even greater evil from him, but I acknowledge our natural kinship and do not return evil for evil. In kingdoms, as in great bodies, swellings constantly arise in various members because of their size, and one must not cut off the swollen member but heal it with gentleness.” And with such words Archelaus turned Herod’s anger away from Pheroras.
