On the Second Capture of Jerusalem

In the holy city they lived in common; and one of the priestly lineage, Honi, hated wickedness and held to piety. And soon throughout the whole city—and this lasted 40 days—there began to appear in the air riders on horses, in armor and clothed in golden garments; and troops were on every side, and they fought with weapons, and gleamed with gold, and shone in every form, clad in armor. For this reason all prayed that this apparition might be for good.

But the evil was revealed—the coming of Antiochus. After great victory he ordered, since he had a savage soul and out of great madness and pride, that the city be taken by spear and destroyed. And he commanded the soldiers to cut down without mercy all who were in the city, and 18,000 people were killed.

And Antiochus dared to enter the sanctuary with great pride, and he set up an idol for the Jews, an abominable image. And there was desolation in the temple and defilement with unclean sacrifices, and he called it the temple of Zeus. And then Antiochus went up to a high place and sat down, and his soldiers stood around him in armor, and he commanded each of them who bore a spear to seize one Jew apiece and force them to eat swine’s flesh offered to idols; and if any of them would not eat the defilement, he ordered them to be tormented and killed.

On Eleazar

Among the multitude of those seized was a man named Eleazar, an elder of the scribes, a man already of advanced age; and they forced him to eat the defilement. But he chose rather to receive death with good repute than life with hatred, and he himself went to torture, and so, after first receiving many wounds and blows, he ended his life. By his death he showed a good example not only to the young but also to the aged, and left a good memory after himself. With him were seven other brothers: Abib, Antoninus, Gurias, Eleazar, Eusebon, Samon, and Marcellus, with their mother Solomonia; they endured various torments and were crowned with the crowns of passion-bearers. Concerning them Gregory the Great says: Thus, having received the priestly rank, the divine Eleazar learned himself and taught others heavenly things, and having sanctified Israel not by the sprinkling of reckoned blood, but by his own blood, he accomplished the Mystery to the very end. Likewise the young youths did not become slaves of pleasure, but prevailed over sins, and made the body pure, and departed to the Sinless Life. Likewise Solomonia, mother of many children, was adorned by her sons even during their life, and departed with them into rest, with those whom she had borne in the world, and presented them to God.

Antiochus marveled at this miracle and feared lest his soldiers also marvel at the manly virtue of the martyrs, and then their fury would end, and they would no longer fight fervently and would leave him without help.
