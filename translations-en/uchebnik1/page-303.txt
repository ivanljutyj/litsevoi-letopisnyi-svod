By Sylvester, in the name of the Holy and Life-Giving Trinity! Suddenly a radiance as from the brightest light shone forth, and a Voice rang out, sounding like bronze. And at once the good-natured great King Constantine came out, having become completely healthy and clean, leaving his bodily scabs in the water like fish scales. And the king said: “I seemed to see and feel how a certain Hand, sent from heaven, touched me.” And all the assembled boyars and all the people, seeing how he had received healing, cried out with the words: “The God of the Christians is one, great and awesome. And from now on we all believe in Him and will be baptized, for we have seen great wonders today.” To them the king said: “Human things are compelled, but divine things are great, for God must be honored with pure love and good will. Therefore not by compulsion, but by free choice we command that whoever wishes be a Christian, since one cannot compel the service of God by human fear.”

All who heard this, being kindled to faith with love, and also thanks to the most gracious royal command and gentleness, were immediately baptized—his mother Helena too, and his first son Constantius, and his relatives, and his friends were baptized, all having changed into white garments.

They also say the following, telling of the divine baptism; and I have heard from certain men who love piety that some of them in old Rome to this day use the baptistery as a testimony of the baptism performed by our holy father and teacher of the whole universe, Sylvester. And the great King Constantine, having been baptized in Rome, by the vision of the Most Honorable Cross in the heavens completely destroyed the tormentor Maxentius, and Great Constantine reigned. And let no one be surprised that before baptism the glorious King Constantine was deemed worthy of such grace, and also heard the voice of God, and saw the image of the Honorable Cross, and saw the divine apostle of Christ Peter, and with him Saint Paul, seeing their likenesses face to face in a vision. For from the beginning the Cross signified the image of Christians, and in heaven it was written in place of the word “Christianity.” And this sign appeared not only for the unbelievers but also for the believers. But the evil-believing fellow-thinkers of the Arians living in the East in Nicomedia say that Constantine, being near death, was baptized by Eusebius of Nicomedia, where he also happened to die, and that while awaiting baptism he said: “I hoped to be baptized in the Jordan River.” But let their vain and diabolical plan and misleading trick not come to pass—there is no truth from a madman. It seems to me more correct that he was truly baptized by blessed Sylvester in Rome. I accept this with undoubted faith, without hesitation and openly, and I advise all to do so.

Likewise, in their name, commands were sent to Miltiades, Pope of Rome, that the deceived man should become an Arian and secure glory for himself here.
