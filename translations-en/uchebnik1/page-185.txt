While such was the situation inside the city, Titus rode around Jerusalem on the outside with chosen horsemen and looked from which side to attack. And Titus could not find a convenient place, since ravines and gullies surrounded the city everywhere, and the first wall was impregnable. And he planned to attack opposite the tomb of John, that is, Johanan, the high priest. There the walls were low, and Titus thought that if he broke through these walls, he would then take the Upper City from there, the Tower of Antonia, and the temple.

And then Titus’s commander Nicanor approached the walls with Joseph, trying to speak with the townspeople about peace, and was wounded by an arrow in the left shoulder. And from this shot from the city Titus understood that the townspeople were raging against those who spoke what was useful to them. And Titus began to burn, cut down, and destroy what was before the city, and he ordered the soldiers to build a ramp. And they divided into three parts and began to build the ramp. Between the workers Titus placed archers and spearmen, and before them javelin and stone throwers, so that they might hinder enemy raids on the ramp being raised and not let the fighters lean out from behind the wall defenses. And they cut down all the trees, and soon laid bare the place before the wall, and carried the trees for the ramp, and all rushed to its construction. The Jews also did not sleep. The people who had endured plunder and bloodshed then breathed with hope, since they saw the Romans advancing and their own people in confusion before the external enemy.

John, that is Johanan, wanted to go out with his soldiers and fight the enemies, but he feared Simon Niger. Simon Niger was closer to the enemy and against his will prepared for battle, and placed military engines along the walls, which he had earlier taken from Cestius. And other engines were obtained from the Tower of Antonia after the guards there were killed. But the rebels did not know how to set up the engines and fire them, and did not use the engines; they hurled stones and arrows at those building the ramp.

Some rebels ran out beyond the city walls and clashed with the Romans. For those building the fortifications, oxhides placed above them served as cover from stones and arrows. Roman archers and javelin and stone throwers resisted the Jews running out of the city. They threw whitened stones, with which they killed not only the front ranks but also those behind. For a stone flew as far as two versts and struck those it hit. And some long-range engines struck thirty men at once.
