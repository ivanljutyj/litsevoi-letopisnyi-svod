The rebels, having chosen a convenient time, rushed to the royal palace, into which many Jews, because of its strength, had brought their wealth. And the rebels joined battle with the Romans, and defeated them, and drove them out of there, and killed 8,400 people, including Jewish refugees.

The robbers took two Romans alive, one a horseman and the other a foot soldier. And they dragged the foot soldier through the city, and all the rebels gathered, and killed him, as if he were not one soldier but the whole Roman army. The horseman begged them, saying: “I will tell you something useful for your salvation. You will not learn it if you kill me.” And they brought him to Simon Niger, but Simon did not know what to say, and they handed him over to Ardal for execution. He bound his hands and led him to execution.

Then Ardal bound his eyes with a strap and led him out before the Romans to cut his throat; and he drew his sword. But when he raised it, the Roman got ahead of him and ran away to his own. Titus wanted to kill this Roman, but did not kill him. And Titus took his weapons from him and drove him out of the army, and said: “He is not worthy to be a soldier, since the enemy took him alive.”

And for that Roman soldier this disgrace was worse than death.

On the next day the Romans attacked the robbers and drove them out of the Lower City, and burned everything even as far as Siloam. And when Jerusalem was perishing in the fire, the Romans rejoiced. But they got no plunder at all, for everything had already been plundered beforehand. The rebels fled to the Upper City. And they did not repent in the least of the evil they had done, but became even more proud. When they saw the city burning, their faces were joyful, and the rebels said: “We await our end with joy, for we have left nothing whatsoever to the enemies, since the people have been slaughtered, the temple burned, and the city is burning up, and the Romans will have nothing to boast of.”

Josephus, however, did not cease until the last day to entreat the robbers, and he reproached them greatly for their stubbornness and their lawlessness, and he gave them many instructions for their salvation. And Josephus received much abuse from the rebels.

The rebels could not surrender because of an oath, nor could they resist the Romans, and they were shut in as in a prison. Yet they did not abandon their custom, but rushed into battle.

And the rebels dispersed among the collapsed buildings left by the fire on the former site of the city, and lay in wait for those who wished to desert to the Romans, and they seized many such people and cut them all down, and the refugees could not escape because of hunger. The rebels threw the bodies of the slain to the dogs. And people considered any destruction and any death easier than hunger. Therefore they fled, hoping either to receive death from their own or mercy from foreigners. And people willingly fell into the hands of the rebels.
