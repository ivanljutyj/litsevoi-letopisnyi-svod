And Titus sent an order to all the regiments that they should cease enriching themselves villainously. But the love of gold overcame every threat and every execution. The desire for wealth sits firmly in men, and there is no passion like it. Yet I think that even the passion for wealth has measure and satiety, and can be subject to law. But God Himself condemned all the Jews then, and therefore sent various deaths upon them and took from them every path of salvation, so that all might perish. What the emperor forbade, they did secretly with deserters: they waylaid them farther off and killed them cautiously, so that no one might see, and slit open their bellies and drew out the vile treasure. Not in every fugitive did they find gold, but many were killed because of a vain hope of loot. This calamity kept possible deserters from fleeing.

John, that is, Yohanan, saw that the people had become so impoverished that there was nothing left to plunder. Then he stretched out his hands to the temple treasure, and broke many sacred things and melted down what had been dedicated to God: both the sacred dishes and the cups and the altars. He did not preserve even the wine vessels that Caesar Sebaste and his wife had formerly sent. The Roman emperors had shown honor to the sanctuary and adorned it, but this Jew destroyed the foreign beauty. And John, that is, Yohanan, said to those who were with him: “It is fitting for us to spend the Divine treasure on God’s work. For we fight for the temple, and therefore it is permitted us to eat from the temple.” With wine from the inner temple, which John divided among the people, they drank themselves drunk, and anointed themselves with oil, and used up more than a hin, that is, a six-litre measure, and acted without trembling and without fear. I am not afraid to speak of this, for suffering compels me to speak. I think that if the Romans had delayed still longer in attacking the wretched, then either the earth would have opened and swallowed them, or a flood would have drowned the city, or heavenly fire, as in Sodom, would have burned them. Even godless Herod did not commit greater crimes than they. Because of the godlessness of the rebels, all the people perished with them. One cannot tell in full detail the grief of men. In those days there was a certain Mannai, the nephew of Lazarus, whom Jesus raised from the grave when Lazarus had already begun to rot. This Mannai defected to Titus and told him that through one gate, which he had been charged to guard, 115,800 corpses had been carried out, from the day when the city was besieged—it was the 14th day of the month Xanthicus, that is, April—until the first day of the month Panemus, that is, July.
