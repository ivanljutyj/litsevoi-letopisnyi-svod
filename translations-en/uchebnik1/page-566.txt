The emperor then sends to Tephrike with the Domesticus Andrew also Christopher, his son-in-law; having completely defeated the Hagarenes, they captured the city and destroyed it to its foundations.

In those days there was also Phocas, a Cappadocian, who was the great-grandfather of Nikephoros who reigned afterwards; clearly distinguished from all by bodily strength and spiritual valor, he became known to the emperor, who appointed him tourmarch.

And he made his son Nikephoros one of his close associates. He kept this modest youth with him, in whom from his earliest youth he saw courage. Both were with him when he set out to fight the Saracens.

When this Nikephoros grew up and came of age, the emperor honored him with the rank of manglavite. And for his tall stature, firmness of spirit, beauty of face, kindness of character, and the pleasant goodness with which he stood before the emperor, the emperor appointed him protostrator, gave him a large house nearest to the church of the Martyr Thecla, which he passed on to his son Bardas, who afterwards was honored with the rank of Caesar by Emperor Nikephoros, his son and grandson of Nikephoros.

Thus this strategos was in the Charsianon theme, and showed great bravery in the struggle with the Saracens, and became famous for his deeds, and was appointed protostrategos, that is first general, in the western themes, that is in Thrace, Macedonia, Cephalonia, Longibardia, and Calabria. For he was a most powerful and worthy opponent of the attacks of the Africans, who were devastating and seizing Greek possessions, ravaging Sicily itself, Calabria, and Longibardia. For all the cities had been captured by them: Tarentum and Bari, and Santa Severina, and Reggio, and Tauromenium, Tropea, and Amantia. Thus this valiant man, wherever he passed, like some strongest rampart, repelled such a strong attack of the Saracens.

Then, by wisdom destroying their commanders and defeating their military hosts, he saved the Greek cities and brought them under his rule.

Those cities that had been devastated he restored and strengthened under Greek authority, and settled a large number of Armenians in them, and also settled in them a thousand slaves whom the glorious and noble daughter of Danielis gave there to the emperor as a gift.
