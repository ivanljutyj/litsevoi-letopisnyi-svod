And Phocas raises a warlike regiment and arms himself with a mighty force from everywhere: from Lycaonia, and from Thrace, and from Colchis, and from Iberia, and from Pamphylia. And having gathered regiments countless as the sand of the sea, brave men, armed, strong, warriors breathing fire in battles, he approaches Chrysopolis, which lies opposite Constantinople — and if anyone calls it a heavenly city, he will not err — and fills the fields with brave men, and over all the land spears flashed and helmets shone, and shields reflected the light, and the air was lit by javelins. There were golden shield-bearers there, and archers, and good horsemen, and men clad in iron armor — even giants would fear such an army.

And Romanus, the father of the emperor, confirmed with golden seals a letter in the name of Emperor Constantine, so that those who had withdrawn from this conspiracy would leave Phocas and come over to the emperor.

Having given it to a certain woman, Anna, shameless and lewd, who was called Vasilika, and to a certain cleric named Michael, he sent them to the troops of Phocas. And they, taking it, spread the secret throughout the whole world.

But Michael was seized by Phocas, who mercilessly cut off his ears — after this he received a worthy reward from Romanus, as did the woman sent with him.

First the son of Michael the Heavy, leaving Phocas, went over to Romanus, laying the beginning of the destruction of this rebellion and violence, and with him Valentius, both tourmarchs.

And Leo Phocas with a large and well-armed army approached Chrysopolis, stationing his regiments from the stone Telitza as far as Chalcedon itself, terrifying the inhabitants of Constantine’s city.

Therefore Romanus sends in a dromon Simeon the canikleios, with a letter from Emperor Constantine to the whole army, that “I have chosen no one else as guardian of my kingdom except Romanus, vigilant, devoted, and faithful, to whom I entrust my protection, and with God I honor him in place of a father, since he has shown me a father’s mercy and paternal affection.

But this Leo Phocas, constantly plotting against my kingdom, has now carried out his evil design and risen in war against my authority. Therefore from now on I do not recognize him as domestikos, and I declare this rebellion ended, and by my own will I put an end to it, taking possession of my kingdom.”

When this was read, all began to withdraw and join the father of the emperor, leaving the accursed one alone.
