…nii, Andrew, bishop of Crete, from the city of Kyrenia, Peregrinus, Lucian, Pompius, Hesychius, Papias, Satorninus from Italy, and Astius the bishop, Procopius, son of Christopher and of his mother Theodosia, from the city of Ellinsk, and Candius the presbyter from the region of Talmai, Cyricus and his mother Ulita from the city of Iconium, Apudimus from the city of Tenedos, Anthinogenes from the city of Sebaste and with him his ten disciples, Trophimus, and Theophilus and with him thirteen martyrs, Leontius, and Agapitus and those with him from Pisidia in Pamphylia, Marinus, an elder from the city of Nazarv; Euplus the deacon from the city of Catania, Photius and Anicetus in Nicomedia, Diomedes from Tarsus of Cilicia, Irenaeus, bishop of Sarmium, Philonides, bishop of Kyrenia. And a certain woman, distinguished by birth, wealth, understanding, and bodily beauty, with two maiden daughters, after long wanderings was seized; fearing that the vessel of their virginity would be defiled, they threw themselves into a river, thereby giving proof of their being numbered among the martyrs. And another holy and wonderful woman, Euphrosyne, of virgin life and perfect beauty, was seized, and the torturers compelled her by many cunning devices, and when she remained unconquered they ordered her to be given to a soldier for vile union, and if she would not agree to this, to cut off her head. She, being in deep thought, began to ask Anthimus of Nicomedia—whether she should die by martyrdom—and asked his advice as to what he would say. He, a good man, truly wise and sound in judgment, keeping the Lord’s words, said: “A good, O child, and seemly deed is the preservation of chastity, but higher still is the commandment concerning faith, for it is a step for the good toward piety. Do not therefore prefer the better to the secondary. For as for one who is in misfortune and sorrow it is better to preserve the body and lose his clothing, so also in hopeless sorrows it is better, having preserved the soul, to hand over the flesh to all who wish to torment it than to destroy the soul’s nobility.” Having heard this, she went away, reasoning with herself, and neither course pleased her. But since the true gift has a defender by inscrutable judgments, the maiden by chaste reflection preserved her chastity without torments. And being locked in one of the houses, she outwitted the soldier, saying: “Do me no harm, man, and I will give you a reward worthy of your good deed. I will give you for this a medicinal ointment that grants immortality—having anointed your whole body with it, you will be invulnerable in battles. If you wish to test it now, allow me to arrange it.” He with great joy permitted it. And then, taking oil and wax, the blessed one mixed them and kneaded them long with her hands, then, having smeared her neck, said to him: “Strike as hard as you can and you will see the power of the anointing, for you will neither wound me nor kill me.” He raised his sword high and struck her, cutting off her honorable head. And so, having conquered the wicked ones’ cunning, she received a double crown—of martyrdom and chastity. Likewise also another…
