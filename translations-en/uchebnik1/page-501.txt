He ordered dung, that is manure, to be thrown: the dung-minded one did what was worthy of the name of dung.

The impious tormentor set out again against the Bulgars, and was severely scorched to the knees, possessed by a fierce fire and burning, and returned to the city of Arcadius on a litter, and reached Selymbria, and came by boat even to Round Town, and died in soul and body, and cried out, saying: “I am given over alive to the unquenchable fire for Mary’s sake, but from now on let her be honored and praised.” Concerning this the divine Nicephorus, patriarch of the city of Constantine, spoke thus: “Since certain of the troublemakers who rise up against Christ boast of certain things which the father of lies breathed into them, we too must examine these things. They lie and talk idly, claiming that, for the sake of long-lasting vexation of Christ and His Church, this Mammon, that is, Tsar Constantine Dung-named, obtained so long a life and a good living, and attained eukromia, that is, the limit of wealth and prosperity, and they honor him for his victories over the barbarians and for his many brave deeds, that is, exploits, which it is unjust even to utter aloud in the presence of chaste people. But so that the simpler people, or the unlearned, that is, the ignorant, may not experience any harmful perversions from this deception, we must examine each matter and see how they are deceived by empty words. The life of this Copronymus was impious, and it was abominable to God and the angels, and indecent for pious people. For he was drawn by bodily sins, renounced and forbidden even among barbarians and pagans, and the wretch defiled both body and soul, and gave himself over to bestiality with dumb animals.”

Many people who served Copronymus and are still alive can tell of these things, as also of the rest that his body suffered. Diseases and indescribable groanings, such as the rotting suffer, laid hold of him very cruelly, and he lay incurable. Some of his members, that is, limbs, fell away from the pus and decay of his wretched and shameful body. Copronymus was possessed by demons and bitterly held captive by them, and spent much of the night without sleep. We should now leave off and not speak of how he ate indecently and filthily, for what was brought to him as food he devoured with difficulty, and his bowels discharged together with vomit. He treated his servants shamelessly and like a beast, tormenting them with wretched, that is, heavy and foul, labor. Who can recount all that those bodies suffered? Every day he inflicted many wounds on them, beat their faces with boards, and trampled them with his feet, leaping upon them. He acted no more humanely than flesh-eating beasts: beheading, blinding of eyes, and all other evil torments that exist. And he made unjust slaughters of innocent men, and put them to bitter death.
