Vespasian himself was occupied with Alexandria, and he entrusted Titus with selected soldiers to take Jerusalem.

And Titus went along the coast from Nicopolis to Heracleopolis, and from there to Pelusium and further to Ostracine, where there is never any water, and from there to Gaza, and to Ascalon, and to Joppa, and from there he came to Caesarea, and sent messengers so that all the remaining troops would gather to him there.

Book 5, of the same Josephus, on the conquest of Jerusalem

Titus gathered the soldiers, as we said. When God entrusted to his father a new province—Alexandria—a still greater revolt arose in Jerusalem. And Jerusalem was divided into three parts: the Zealots and the brigands fought from the city, and against both of these was Simon Niger. And from revolt revolts were born. As a beast in frenzy turns its teeth against its own body, so these turned their hand against their own kin.

And the Zealots occupied the temple, while John, that is Johanan, occupied the middle city, and Simon Niger the lower. John was between enemies. Against those foes who had entrenched themselves in the temple he directed engines for hurling stones and spears, and so fought with them. And many people were killed by these engines: both those who fought and those who served God and offered sacrifices.

For despite all their impiety, the rebels admitted into the temple those who wished to offer sacrifice to God. The inhabitants of Jerusalem they admitted under guard, but the Jews who came from other places they admitted without guard. The stones and spears that were launched flew with force and struck both those offering sacrifices and the sacrificial altars, and the altar, and the temple itself. And many of those who had labored to come from the ends of the earth to the glorious place honored by all people and to offer sacrifice—they themselves fell dead before their sacrifices during the sacrifice. The altar, which Greeks and foreigners revered, was defiled with the blood of the local inhabitants. And Jewish corpses were mixed with the corpses of foreigners, and the corpses of priests with the corpses of the wicked. And the mixed blood stood in the holy place like a lake. O suffering city! Did you ever receive such defilement from the Romans? On the contrary, they came to cleanse your filth. For you have become a tomb for your inhabitants and have been filled with the blood of strife.
