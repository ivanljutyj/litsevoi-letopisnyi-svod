Those same two women heard the cry and climbed out of the underground passage, and told the Romans what had happened, and about Eleazar’s speech, and about his teaching, and about the massacre, and they told the whole truth. But the Romans could not believe it.

Soon, having put out the fire and made a passage, the Romans reached the palace and found those who had been cut down. And the Romans did not rejoice at the sight of the fallen enemy, but marveled in thought at the firmness of their resolve, and at what they had done, and at their contempt for death. And so the fortress of Masada was taken.

The Roman commander Silva left guards in Masada, and he himself went to Caesarea, for no one remained in all Judea who resisted Rome, but all the rebels had already perished in long battles.

Later, in Egyptian Alexandria, many Jews had to meet death. For certain Sicarii, having fled there, began to do very wicked deeds and incited the Jews of Alexandria not to submit to the Romans, but to resist them with all their strength, and to strive for freedom, and to acknowledge one God as their master. Since many prominent Jews opposed the Sicarii, the latter rose up and killed those who disagreed, and urged the rest to revolt.

But the elders, seeing the shamelessness of the Sicarii, considered that it would be wrong to remain silent any longer, and gathered all the Jews together, and exposed the treason of the Sicarii, and presented them as the cause of all evils, past and present, and said: “The Sicarii do not rejoice that they escaped from the mouth of war, but now they trouble us as well. If the Romans learn of these talks, all sorts of misfortunes will fall upon us, and they will drive us out of Alexandria, and scatter us, though we are innocent. And we will find ourselves in an even more ruinous position than before, if we do not stand firm and seize the Sicarii.” And the people, having considered the magnitude of the misfortunes awaiting them, listened to the elders and rushed upon the Sicarii, and began to seize them, and caught six hundred people.

And the Jews pursued those Sicarii who had fled to Egypt, and caught them, and brought them back.

And there was no man who did not marvel at the strength, and endurance, and desperation of the Sicarii. They tortured them with every kind of torment, and cut them in pieces, if only they would say one word, calling the emperor lord. And none of them yielded or cared for his body, but all with one mind preserved their convictions, paying no attention to any torments, as though their bodies were made of stone.
