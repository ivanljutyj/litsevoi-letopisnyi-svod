He was thinking about a school, but wished to send his sons to a teacher, and was possessed by the longing of which we spoke earlier, and could not find the one he sought.

And Feodosiy sent to Rome to the one reigning in Rome, and asked him to find such a man, and wrote to him: “It will be a great gift to me from you if you do this.”

The Roman king showed the letter of King Feodosiy to the pope and said: “It is not good for us not to fulfill this request of him who reigns on equal terms with us. It would be a disgrace to us and to our realm if we do not find such a man in great Rome.” And they inquired and searched and did not find him. They pondered this much and had no peace because of it.

And the pope said to the king: “We have a certain deacon, a young man, who lives with his sister, a virgin; his name is Arseniy, and he is like the one they seek. Send him to King Feodosiy; the king desires such a man, that he may instruct his children in every understanding of things divine and human. But I do not know whether Arseniy will wish this: he no longer has time for it, since he has renounced the teacher’s office and all books. But if you command, we shall summon him by your authority and direct him to this.”

Having learned of this, the king was very glad, and sent for Arseniy, and summoned him to himself. When he came, the king said to him: “We have learned of your life in God and of your virtuous way of life, and we constantly hear very weighty confirmations of this. Feodosiy reigns with us, and he has sent a plea to us, asking that we send him such a man as can instruct his children with all piety and wisdom. For this reason we have summoned you, since you are from God and can instruct the king’s children. We did not wish to speak of these matters to anyone else, but only to you, with your virtues.” And Arseniy answered the king and said: “Does our most holy father not know, O pious king, our lowliness, that I have no time, and I have not carefully studied secular wisdom, and I received this ordination unworthily, and I have not opened books on such sciences?”

Then the pope began to say to Arseniy: “You have spoken true words to our Christ-loving king, and we know concerning them that it is so. We have truly praised your virtuous life greatly. But since the king’s command is divine, and what he seeks is pleasing to God, we entreat you to go to him for such good instruction. For by the grace of God given to you, you can piously and with love for the good instruct these youths, and not only in wisdom.
