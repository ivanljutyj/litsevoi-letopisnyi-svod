He cut off the heads of all the Persian people, and made cities and buildings food for fire, and in six years utterly overthrew Persia.

When the seventh year of the war came, Heraclius took the life-giving wood of the Precious Cross and came to Jerusalem with great glory. And then the king came down from the Mount of Olives by the same gate through which the Lord, going to His voluntary Passion, had entered. Heraclius was seated on a horse and adorned in royal garments. And when he was about to enter the gate, an angel of the Lord appeared above the gate and said to him: “The King of Heaven and Lord of all the world, of the living and the dead, entered through these gates to His voluntary Passion with great humility, and did not adorn Himself with purple, nor did He make a show from afar with godovaplem, that is, silk, nor did He seek a strong horse to ride, but sat humbly on a colt’s back and left an example to His servants. How then do you come with such pride on foaming horses and in royal array?” When these words had been spoken by the angel, he immediately ascended into Heaven.

Hearing these words, Heraclius dismounted from his horse, and so did all who were with him; the king removed his royal crown and put aside his boots, and in a single garment, girded with a linen belt, taking the Precious Cross, he went on foot and in tears, and so did all who were with him. And Heraclius set the Precious Cross in its place. And at that hour four paralyzed people were healed, ten lepers were cleansed, fifteen blind people received their sight, and many others were freed from demons and from various diseases, and all glorified the power of the Cross.

Emperor Heraclius renewed the deserted churches in Jerusalem, returned the captives to freedom, ordered all things well, and was greatly honored by all. And with great peaceful joy Heraclius sailed to Constantinople and, as a kind of mystery, concluded his wisdom with this. Just as God created all that creation in six days and called the seventh day rest, so this Heraclius waged many wars and labored for six years, and in the seventh year he rested.

When the citizens learned of Heraclius’s arrival, with unrestrained love they all went out to Hieria to the royal palace, together with the patriarch and with Emperor Constantine, son of Heraclius, and carried palm branches, that is, olive branches, and candles, praising Heraclius with great God-inspired wisdom. The son came and fell at his father’s feet, and the father kissed his son with many tears. Seeing this, the people gave thanks to God with tears and thus sang to the king, rejoicing, clapping their hands, and praising him; and they entered the royal palace, and all escorted the king, and the king honored all and dismissed them.

On the Death of Heraclius

Emperor Heraclius was deceived by Athanasius, patriarch of the Jacobites, that is, the Syrian Monophysites, and by Sergius, patriarch of Constantinople.
