At this same time, clashes in boats occurred every day from springtime even until autumn. When winter came, the Saracen warriors sailed away and wintered in Cyzicus. And again, setting out from Cyzicus in spring, Saracen troops also came by sea and began to wage war, and for seven years they fought, and they were able to accomplish nothing at all.

And having destroyed countless numbers of their own men, the Saracen troops returned to their own land.

When the Saracen ships were in the Sylean regions, and great fierce winds and a storm arose there, they drowned in the sea and were utterly destroyed.

Then the Byzantine soldiers who were in the East came to Chrysopolis, a suburb of Constantine’s city, and cried out and said: “We believe in God the Trinity, and we will proclaim three brothers emperors and crown them to the kingdom.” Emperor Constantine was angered at them, because he alone was crowned, while his two brothers had no rank at all. The emperor sent a patrician to them and said: “This speech pleases me,” and he summoned their elders to a council about this with the boyars. When the elders entered the city, they were killed by Constantine; and he cut off the noses of his brothers.

In the days of Constantine, the Bulgarian people from the northern lands of the Euxine Pontus, from the so-called Lake Maeotis, attacked Thrace; they lived in great poverty and wretchedness.

Learning of this, Constantine was greatly grieved, and he rushed against the Bulgarians in many ships with warriors; he sent the infantry by land, and along the Danube he arrayed foot soldiers, and near the shallows he placed the ships. When the Bulgarians saw this, they were afraid and fortified themselves in their strongholds, which were hard to take.

But the emperor suffered from a disease of the legs, and he fell ill, and was forced to withdraw to Mesembria, a Thracian city, for the sake of healing baths, in five ships, and he said: “I will be healed and will return again.” Constantine left his warriors and strategoi, that is, commanders, ordering them to besiege and strike down the Bulgarians.

But the Byzantine soldiers thought that the emperor had fled, and they rushed to flight, pursued by no one. Seeing this, the Bulgarians came out of their strongholds and pursued the Byzantine soldiers, and killed many and took them captive, and they held that land for themselves and ruled over Christian land.

Therefore, perforce, the emperor made peace with the Bulgarians, giving them tribute to the shame of the Greeks, because these Bulgarians attacked and devastated Thrace and the lands around it. Emperor Constantine died of a flux, that is, diarrhea.
