Vespasian learned that many inhabitants of Tarichea had gathered before the city to do battle, and he sent there his son Titus, and six hundred chosen horsemen.

Titus saw that the Jews were countless in number and sent to his father for more soldiers. Some of Titus’s soldiers were eager for battle even without his help, while others were afraid at the sight of the multitude of Jews. Then Titus stood among them on a hill so that all could hear, and said: “Romans! It is fitting at the beginning of my speech to remind you from what city you come, so that you may understand who you are and with whom you are entering battle. No one in all the universe has escaped Roman hands. The Jews have hitherto been defeated, but by this they are neither crushed nor worn out. It would be strange if they should grow strong in their misfortunes, while we grow slack through our successful victories. I see many of you vigorous and ready to go against the enemy, and I rejoice at this. But if any one of you, seeing the multitude of the enemy, has secretly become afraid and is fearful, let him remember of what stock he is and of what stock the enemy is. Although the Jews are inspired and do not fear death, they are unskilled in war and fight in disorder. This crowd of people cannot be called an army. But our military skill and order are known to all. It is for this that in peacetime we exercise with weapons and do not part from them, so that in war we need not occupy ourselves with counting how many we are and how many foreigners. If we, skilled warriors, equate ourselves with the unskilled, then why do we ceaselessly exercise and conduct drills? Consider also that they are in simple clothing, while we are in armor; they are on foot, while we are on horses; they are of inglorious stock, while we are of glorious stock. Though we are few, our great victories increase us. Though the enemies are many, their ceaseless defeats and slavery diminish them. War is won not by numbers but by courage. Though we are few, we can quickly form up and help one another. They, when there are many of them, harm themselves. We must understand that it is not fearful for us to suffer some loss from the enemy, for many helpers are near us. Yet it is also possible for us to gain victory, if we wish. It is foolish for us to wait for the helpers whom my father will send, and to share glory with them and give away part of the victory. Greater praise will be ours if we manage it ourselves. I think the decisive hour has come for my father, for me, and for you. My father has won glorious victories, and I am his son, while you are my soldiers. And if it is my father’s custom to conquer, how can I return to him without victory? Would it not be shameful for you to abandon me, your commander? But follow me and imitate my courage. We all know that God also helps us, and with this help, more than with the help of weapons, we shall prevail in battle.” So spoke Titus, and all the soldiers were filled with a warlike spirit.
