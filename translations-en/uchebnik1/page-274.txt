The 24th Reign, of Antoninus, Who Was Enthroned in Rome in the Year 5712 from Adam (the 212th Year from Christ)

After Macrinus, Antoninus, also called Elagabalus, reigned 3 years and 9 months. He was so enslaved to lustful passion and remained in this evil that he made Hierocles his lawful husband. To cohabit with him, they say, he begged a physician by his skill to castrate him completely. Because of his impious and seditious deeds he was killed, having ended his evil life. And in his place reigned Alexander, his cousin.

The 25th Reign, of Alexander, Who Was Enthroned in Rome in the Year 5716 from Adam (the 216th Year from Christ)

After Antoninus Elagabalus reigned Alexander Mamaean, 13 years and 8 months. And under him there was a famine in Rome, so that they even ate human flesh.

He warred with the Persians and was completely defeated.

And the soldiers proclaimed Maximus emperor. Alexander, with no one guarding him, was killed. Mamaea, Alexander’s mother, as Eusebius reports, was pious; and she summoned to herself Origen, then living in Antioch, wishing to learn from him the mysteries of Christ.

The 26th Reign, of Maximian, Who Was Enthroned in Rome in the Year 5730 from Adam (the 230th Year from Christ)

After Alexander Mamaean reigned Maximus 6 years. He had formerly been a shepherd, then became a soldier, and afterward a commander under Alexander. For his bravery, intelligence, and prudence the people and the council made him emperor. He, reviling Alexander because he had many pious Christians, raised a great persecution against them. He met an end worthy of his impious action, being killed by Maximus and Balbinus.

The 27th Reign, of Maximus, Who Entered the Reign in Rome in the Year 5736 from Adam (the 236th Year from Adam)

After Maximian reigned Maximus and Balbinus 22 days. In the same manner this one was killed by the soldiers. Likewise also Gordian, who had previously been Caesar, who reigned after Maximian, was killed because he forbade Philip the prefect to distribute wheat to the soldiers. This Philip was the father of the holy martyr Eugenia. Under Maximian and Gordian, as Eusebius reports, the writer Africanus became well known.
