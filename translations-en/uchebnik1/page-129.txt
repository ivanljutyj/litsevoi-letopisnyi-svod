And some Jews, seeing their end, stabbed themselves; others fled into the underground chambers. One Jew from such a chamber asked the Roman centurion Antonius to give him his hand and not kill him, and Antonius gave him his hand, without malice and without fear; but the Jew pierced his ribs with a spear and killed Antonius, striking first. On that day all the Jews who fell into the sight of the Romans were killed by them, six thousand in number.

The Romans took five thousand captives, ravaged the city, and burned the towers. Thus the Romans took Jotapata in the month of Panemus, that is, July, in the thirteenth year of Nero's reign.

The Romans searched for Josephus, and were angry with him, and wished to please their commander, and searched among the corpses and in the secret places of the city. When the Romans captured the city, Josephus, by God's help, secretly slipped away from among the soldiers and leapt into a deep pit, from which a hidden path led into an underground chamber; there Josephus found forty noble men hiding. And everything necessary for the body had been provided in that chamber. Since guard detachments surrounded the city, escape was impossible, and two days passed while Josephus and the others all remained hidden in the underground chamber.

On the third day the guards caught a woman and tortured her, and under torture she told them about Josephus and those who were with him. Then Vespasian hastily sent two commanders of a thousand, Paulinus and Gallicanus, to bring Josephus out and to promise him safety with an oath. And these commanders came to the underground chamber and began to urge Josephus to come out, and promised him safety, and swore. But Josephus did not listen to them, fearing torture, since he had done the Romans much harm during the siege.

Vespasian sent a third commander of a thousand to Josephus, an acquaintance and friend of Josephus, whose name was Nicanor. And Nicanor came to Josephus and set before him examples of Roman clemency and mercy toward the conquered, and added also: "Because of your courage and wisdom the Romans do not regard you with hatred, but rather with admiration. Vespasian wants you brought to him not for torture but for preservation. If he wanted to bring you out in order to do you harm, he would not have sent me, your friend, nor would I have agreed to deceive you." But Josephus was in doubt.

Then the Roman soldiers in their anger wanted to set fire to the underground chamber. When Josephus learned of this threat, he remembered the night dreams in which God had shown him the calamities awaiting the Jews. Josephus was skilled in interpreting dreams and could understand what God says in secret, and the holy prophetic books.
