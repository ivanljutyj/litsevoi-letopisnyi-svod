The 5th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 5th Ptolemy, Philopator the Adult, 17 years. The 6th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 6th Ptolemy, Epiphanes the Mother-loving, 25 years. The 7th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 7th Ptolemy, Philometor the Virtuous, 33 years. The 8th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 8th Ptolemy, called the Savior.

The 9th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 9th Ptolemy, Alexander. The 10th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 10th Ptolemy, his brother. These three Ptolemies reigned 20 years. The 11th Reign in Egypt. Then reigned the 11th Ptolemy, by name Dionysius, 29 years; and he had a daughter Cleopatra and a son by name Ptolemy. The 12th Reign in Egypt.

After Dionysius, his son Ptolemy reigned 13 years.

The 13th Reign in Egypt. And then reigned the daughter of that Dionysius, by name Cleopatra, 29 years. In all, 20 Ptolemies, Macedonians, reigned over the whole land of Egypt: from Ptolemy, son of Lagus, to Cleopatra, daughter of Dionysius. The Ptolemies reigned 300 years, until the 15th year of the reign of Augustus Caesar Octavian Sebastus, who defeated Antony and that Cleopatra in the land of Epirus with ships, in the place Leucas; and Augustus slew them and subdued all Egypt, as the wise Eusebius of Pamphylia and Pausanias the Chronicler wrote about this.

But Alexander commanded that Antigonus, surnamed Poliorcetes, should hold and reign over Asia, as far as Cilicia and the river Dracon, which is now called the Orontes, and which divides the land of Cilicia and Syria, which is called Typhon and Ophitis. In Syria, and in Babylon, and in Palestine the following reigned: After the death of Alexander, Antigonus reigned in Asia, the first king, and in Syria, and in Babylon, and in Palestine the first king was Seleucus Nicator. And he killed Antigonus and reigned in Asia. Antigonus built a city near the lake and the river Dracon, and called the city by his own name — Antigonia. And Seleucus overcame Antigonus and received all Asia, and all the wealth, and all the kingdom of that Antigonus.

Seleucus entrusted all the administration in Asia to his commanders: Nicomedes and Nicanor, his kinsmen, from Didyma, the children of his brother, and Antiochus, surnamed Soter, the son of that Seleucus.

This Seleucus Nicator took a wife from Parthia, named Apama; he took the maiden after killing her father; she had been betrothed to a great Parthian commander. By this Apama Seleucus begot two daughters: Apama and Laodice; and Seleucus's wife, Apama the Parthian, died.
