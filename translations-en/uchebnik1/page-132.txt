Having come together with him, Joseph, so as not to defile his hands with kindred blood, implored him to surrender to the Romans.

And both came out alive, and they were led to Vespasian. And all the Romans ran together to look, and they shouted various things. Some rejoiced at Joseph’s captivity, some threatened him, some made merry, wishing to torture and kill the enemy, and others marveled at the inconstancy of human life.

Vespasian ordered Joseph to be put under guard and intended to send him to Nero. When Joseph learned of this, he said, “I wish to say something to you alone.” And when all had withdrawn, Vespasian remained with his son Titus and with two friends. And Joseph said, “O Vespasian, you think that you have taken me captive. But I came to you voluntarily to tell of matters more important. Did I not know the Jewish law, how commanders ought to die? But God sent me to you. Yet you are sending me to Nero, although you yourself are emperor and autocrat, and this son is your heir. So bind me now and keep me for yourself. For you are lord not only over me but over all the earth and sea and the whole human race. If it turns out that I am lying, then devise for me an unheard-of torment.” Vespasian did not believe Joseph, thinking that, in concern for his own safety, he was composing these speeches. But later he began to trust him, when God made Vespasian emperor and placed the staff of dominion in his hand. The two friends of the emperor standing there and Titus said, “It is wondrous that you prophesy about others. But why did you not prophesy about Jotapata and your own capture? You say this wishing to escape torture and wrath.” Joseph said, “I foretold to the inhabitants of Jotapata that after forty-seven days they would be captured, and I foretold concerning myself that I would be alive among you in captivity.”

Then Vespasian questioned the Jewish captives as to whether Joseph had really made such predictions, and he learned that it was true, and he began to believe Joseph, and he assigned guards to him, and put him in chains, and gave him costly garments, possessions, and food as for military commanders; and Titus helped Joseph.

And afterward Vespasian sent against the city of Jaffa the thirteenth legion, led by Trajan, and a small part of the townsmen came out and resisted, and then fled.

That city was surrounded by two walls, and the townsmen fled behind the first walls, and the Romans ran in after them. Seeing this, the townsmen who were inside the city shut themselves behind the second wall and did not let in those Jews who had gone out to fight the Romans, and left them between the walls. And the Romans began to cut down the Jews left between the walls, and they had nowhere to flee, for their inner gates were closed before them, and the enemy was at the outer gates. And Trajan, the Roman commander, sent to Vespasian and told him to send Titus to complete the victory.
