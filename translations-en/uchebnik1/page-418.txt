And the officials recorded the purchase there: both the quaestor and all the counsellors, and this still before the testing of the horses.

The custom was ancient: when the emperor went out to his throne, immediately horses with chariots ran, which at that time remained idle during the negotiations with the eunuch. And to this day the horse-chariots go out thus.

The Third Purchase. At the place where the right side of the church, the women’s side, was built, up to the pillar of St Basil, there lived a certain Xenophon, a craftsman, who, wishing to sell his house, asked the emperor not only for gold for his house, but also that at the next horse contest he be honoured and bowed to by the four chariot drivers. The emperor ordered this to be done for laughter.

And on the day of the next horse contest Xenophon sat in the middle of the barrier at the hippodrome, and the drivers bowed to his back for laughter before they mounted the chariots. And this continued until the days of the pious Emperor Constantine Porphyrogenitus, who instead arranged that there should be venerated the honourable icon of the Lord Christ our God and of the honourable Mother of God who bore Him, and of the holy John the Prophet and Forerunner. That Xenophon was vested below and was thus venerated, vested after the manner of a eunuch in a white woven robe, and for laughter they called him “prince of the underworld.” And the purchase of Xenophon’s house was recorded.

The Fourth Purchase. At the place where the left side of the church was built, from the elevation to the pillar of St Basil, was the house of Chariton the eunuch, also called Chinopol, that is, the Goose-seller, who sold his house with gratitude for 30 litrai of gold. And the purchase of his house was recorded.

The Fifth Purchase. At the place where the lower part of the church was built, and the four porticoes, and the bath around them, there was the house of Damian, patrician of Seleucia. And his house was sold for 90 litrai of gold. And having received the gold for the house, Damian with great joy gave it for the building of the church on its site. And the purchase of Damian’s house was recorded. From the altar and the lower passages Justinian laid there in a circle the foundation of the great dome, from the passages as far as the western portico.

Before the foundation of Hagia Sophia, Justinian made another small church, beautiful, round, covered with gold, in the name of St John the Baptist, calling it the Baptismal Church. There too he placed a baptistery near the chapel. Justinian often stayed there nearby with his boyars, and the emperor dined there.
