He rejoiced and said: “I shall be glad if all the Jews incline to such a thought, and I promise assured safety to all, and I will not destroy the city.” Of the ten men who were with Castor, five pretended to beg for mercy, and the other five cried out: “We will never submit to Roman slavery, for we can die free!” And they argued for a long time. Titus delayed and watched the dispute, and halted the siege.

Castor secretly sent to Simon Niger, asking him to come and see the wonder, how he alone mocked the Roman emperor. And Castor began entreating those five who resisted, and urged them to submit to the Romans. Those five, in indignation, drew their swords and pretended to cut their own throats, and fell as if slaughtered.

Titus and those around him were horrified at the obstinacy of the Jews, and could not see clearly from below what was happening, and marveled at their firmness, and pitied their suffering.

Then someone shot Castor in the nose, and he took the arrow and showed it to Titus, and accused him of injustice. The emperor rebuked the soldier who had hit Castor, and Titus sent Josephus, who was standing near him, to give Castor his hand. Josephus said: “I will neither go myself nor allow any other of those I hold dear, since these petitioners devise injustice.” One deserter, Aeneas, said: “I will go.” Castor called for someone to come and receive the silver he was carrying with him. Aeneas hurried with an outstretched cloth and approached the tower, and one soldier with him. Castor lifted a stone as large as he could lift and threw it at them. He did not hit Aeneas, but struck his soldier. And on the other side, where Simon Niger stood, a centurion named Phocas called to the Roman commander Cerealis and asked him to promise mercy if he would come out with his soldiers. They bound a stone slab with gold and asked Cerealis to order his soldiers to take that slab and not let it be shattered. And those, seeing the golden gleam, came near. The Jews threw the slab from the city walls and struck the Romans.

The emperor understood Castor’s deceit, understood that he himself had spared the Jews, but they had deceived him and mocked him. And Titus ordered the walls to be attacked even more vigorously.

Castor and those with him saw that the walls were collapsing, and set fire to the tower, and slipped through the fire, and entered an underground chamber. And the Romans marveled, and thought that they had thrown themselves into the fire.

And on that day the Romans took the second wall, five days after taking the first wall. And when the Jews withdrew from the walls, Titus entered inside with a thousand soldiers, but he did not order a section of the wall destroyed and the path widened for the army, nor the houses burned, hoping that the Jews would set aside their obstinacy and submit, and would not continue the war.
