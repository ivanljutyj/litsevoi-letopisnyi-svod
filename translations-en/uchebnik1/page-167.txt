The Zealots built four more tall towers, so that they could strike from them without fear. The towers were situated above that chamber of the temple in which one of the high priests stood according to custom and blew a trumpet in the evening, when the day of rest begins, and on the following evening, when the day of rest ends, by these signs showing the people when it was time to work and when to celebrate.

And the Zealots placed catapults on the towers, and archers, and javelin throwers. Simon saw that his fighters were broken by those striking from on high and continued the battle only with difficulty.

Beginning of the Reign of Vespasian

In Rome at that time a terrible calamity occurred. The Roman troops who had come from Germany to help Vitellius saw how richly the inhabitants of Rome lived and began to plunder them.

And news came to Vespasian of the Roman revolt and that Vitellius had become emperor. Vespasian was sorrowful and angry, and did not want to acknowledge as ruler the one who had attacked Rome, forsaken by all. And Vespasian could not endure this; he could not continue the war with foreigners when his own fatherland had been laid waste. Anger urged Vespasian to take vengeance on Vitellius, but the fact that Vespasian was far from Rome, and the sea was unsettled, held him back.

And Vespasian’s commanders learned of the change of power, and each of them with his soldiers pondered this, and all gathered and in anger made an uproar, and said: “This is monstrous and displeasing to Heaven and earth! Those who always live in luxury and pleasures and do not even hear the word ‘war’—they are the ones who appoint whomever they wish as emperors, and, chasing gifts, make him autocrat who gives them more. But we have endured so many hardships, grown old in helmets, and yet yield to others the right to choose the ruler. But we have a man who deserves power more than others. The time has come to repay our commander for his kindness to us. If we let these days slip by, it is unknown when we shall get another. Vespasian is more worthy than Vitellius in strength, in experience, and in valor. We too are better than those who installed Vitellius. For we have endured no fewer wars than they, and have conquered no fewer lands. Moreover, the Roman nobles will not submit to the intemperance of Vitellius and will not want a cruel tormentor instead of a good ruler. Children will not find a better protector than their father. If a king, in his greatness, is also well-disposed and surpasses his subjects in understanding, then one can be certain that he will be useful to the cities of the empire. If the experience of old age is needed for rule, Vespasian has it. If the bravery of youth is needed, who is stronger than Titus, his heir? Not only the Roman soldiers will support our choice.”
