Thus also this Sabinus, when he had already overcome all and shown his invincibility, then fell to the ground with a great crash, having caught his heavy weapon on a stone. And the Jews returned, seeing that he was alone and had fallen. And they came and surrounded him and pierced him all over with spears and swords. Sabinus, however, barely rose to one knee and covered himself with his shield, and stabbed many Jews, and wounded many others.

When all the blood had drained from Sabinus, he grew weak and lowered his arms, and while he was giving up the ghost, arrows and spears and swords covered him. This man deserved a better lot for his courage, but then he fell, for so it was fated, or else he fell because he did not guard himself. The other three who had gone with him and were first at the wall barriers were crushed by stones. This exploit was accomplished on the 3rd day of the month Panemus, that is, July.

Two days later, twenty Romans guarding the embankment gathered and drew to themselves the standard-bearer of the 9th regiment, and two horsemen, and one trumpeter. And at the 9th hour of the night they went secretly and climbed onto the tower of Antonia, and cut down the nearest guards while they slept, and occupied the walls, and ordered the trumpeter to sound. And the rest of the guards heard the trumpet and leaped up and scattered even before they saw those who had climbed onto the tower. The sound of the trumpet struck fear into them; they thought that many enemies had climbed up.

The emperor heard the sound of the trumpet and quickly raised the whole army, and was first to mount the walls with chosen commanders. The Jews fled to the temple. The Romans rushed through the underground passages that John, that is Johanan, had earlier dug against them. But all the Jewish warriors blocked their passage and, to prevent the Romans from passing, used all their strength and all their courage, for they understood that they would be finally conquered if the Romans entered the sanctuary and saw what may not be seen. And the battle was fierce: the Romans strove to seize the temple, while the Jews pressed them back toward the tower of Antonia.

And then, hurling spears and shooting arrows, with drawn swords, the enemies closed. And in that battle it was impossible to understand or discern who was fighting whom, for confusion arose among the soldiers, and in the streets a mingling, and a noise in which nothing could be made out. A great multitude of soldiers came together, and blood flowed from both sides like a river. And the bodies of the fallen and their armor were shattered in the battle. And always, wherever one side prevailed in the battle, were heard the urging cries of those who were overcoming, and the wails and groans of those who fled. There was no place for flight or pursuit, but in the general turmoil of the army they now chased, now fled.
