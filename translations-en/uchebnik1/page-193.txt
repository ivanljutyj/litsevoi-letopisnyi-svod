of the Jews who love God. Should I fall silent, or tell of the migration of our fathers to Egypt? They were tormented by a foreign king and were in slavery to him for four hundred years. They could have saved themselves by their weapons and their own hands, but they did not wish to, and laid the care of themselves upon the Lord and hoped in God. And God sent unseen beasts against Egypt, and the land was filled with dead men torn by beasts, and the Egyptians perished by every kind of calamity, and there was no fruit in that land, and the Nile dried up, and then the Lord sent upon them ten unbearable plagues. And our fathers came out with glory and with foreign wealth, without slain and without wounded. And God gave them even to this day to perform the temple service. When the Assyrians seized the sacred ark, did not the land of the Philistines groan? Was not the abominable idol of Dagon shattered? Did not all the captors groan, and did not their secret parts and inward organs rot? And later the same hands that had seized the sacred ark brought it back, with supplication and great gifts, and with sweet-sounding cymbals, and with every honor. God then granted this to our fathers, because they hoped not in their own hands nor in weapons, but laid the right to avenge and judge upon God. When Sennacherib, king of Assyria, brought all Assyria to this place and besieged the city, did his warriors fall by human hands? No, for the hands of the Jews were raised not to weapons but to prayer. And the angel of the Lord in one night destroyed all the warriors, and when day came and the Assyrian king arose, he found his warriors dead, 185,000, and with the remnant he fled through the forests, and no one pursued him. You also know the Babylonian captivity, in which our people were for seventy years, and the Jews did not go out to freedom until Cyrus granted it to them for God’s sake and released them at Daniel’s request. And the Jews came and served their Deliverer. And one cannot find anything that our fathers accomplished by weapons. But rather they lived in humility and conquered all, if such was the will of the Judge. When they fought, they suffered defeat. When the king of Babylon besieged this city, and Zedekiah, our king, went out and fought with him, not heeding the prophecy of Jeremiah, he himself was captured, and the city was conquered together with the temple.

But how much more humble that king was than your commanders, and his warriors than you! Jeremiah then cried out that God would hate them for their transgressions, and he ordered them to surrender to the Babylonians so as not to perish. But neither the inhabitants of the royal city nor the king was angry with Jeremiah, and the people did not kill him. But you commit lawlessness, such deeds as cannot even be spoken of, and you try to shoot me when I entreat you and tell you what will save you. When Antiochus, called Epiphanes, besieged this city and opened his foul mouth against God, then our forefathers went out with weapons, and they themselves fell in that battle, and the city was plundered by the enemy, and the sanctuary lay desolate for three years and six months.
