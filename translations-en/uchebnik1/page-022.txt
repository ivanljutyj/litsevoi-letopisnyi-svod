In his own work Eutropius, the Roman writer; this same Perseus is also mentioned by Palaephatus. Lucius Paulus, whom Palaephatus also mentions, a friend of that Perseus, the Roman governor, fought with this Perseus the Ship-fighter and killed him.

Under these rulers a certain man named Hannibal, king of Africa, tormented Rome for twenty years; Rome was without a king, and governors ruled it; this same Hannibal destroyed all Italy, a great country, and subdued it by war, and killed the aforementioned Paulus.

And the Roman nobles appointed a governor strong in all things, and most of all in wars, the great Scipio, who even in Hannibal’s time had destroyed Africa and burned the city of Carthage, in which Hannibal was king, and freed the captives, and took the city with his nobles, and returned to Rome.

And Hannibal saw Scipio’s strength and went to Bithynia to Antiochus, king of Asia, and asked him for help. For since the times of Alexander of Macedon the Romans had a sworn agreement with the Macedonians, because the Romans gave Alexander their warriors to help against Darius.

And Antiochus, king of Asia, listened to Hannibal, and joined with him, and went against Rome, and sailed with his army when he came to the sea from Bithynia.

And hearing of this, the Roman nobles appointed a second strong governor named Scipio, brother to the first Scipio, and set him against two kings—Antiochus, king of Asia, and Hannibal, rex, that is, king, of Africa. And Scipio met both kings and joined great battle with them, and many fell.

And Hannibal saw that Scipio’s strength was prevailing, and fled, and killed himself with deadly poison. Antiochus saw that Hannibal had fled, and also turned his back, that is, fled, and Scipio pursued Antiochus to the mountains in Isauria called the Taurus.

And from there envoys came from Antiochus with a petition and said that he had nothing against Rome, but had come to give help to others. And Scipio received the envoys and arranged that Antiochus should be under Rome and obey the commands of the Roman nobles, and give Rome four talents of gold each year until the end of his life.

And Scipio made a feast and summoned Antiochus, and gave him the first place, and showed him honor as a king. And Scipio returned to Rome with glory, as the wise Phronos wrote from the books of Livy the Wise. In the fifteenth year before the so-called Antiochus, king of Syria, the Leper, someone named Tigranes, king of Armenia, came and fought with Antiochus, and there was a great battle, and Tigranes overcame Antiochus, and took Antioch, the great city, and took all the kingdom that Antiochus had.
