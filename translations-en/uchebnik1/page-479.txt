This tsar was troubled, and he assembled a great host against those water-faring robbers, that is, the ship-borne ones. And the Greek naval forces fought them, and those robbers, who attacked with fury and in their malice poured forth, as it were, foam, they drove away from there and returned the captured goods intact.

But the fierce beasts did not stop, and again they made war, began to fight, and put to flight the commanders of the Greek ships, and took the glorious city of Carthage, and took captive all the Libyans, and “as it were, they tore out golden hairs.”

But the naval soldiers and all the commanders could not endure the shame of their bitter defeat, and then they undertook to heal evil with another great evil; they put in at the great island of Crete, and consulted with one another, and “they vomit forth the burden of their audacity.”

The rebels chose for themselves one fellow apostate, called by two names, Tiberius Apsimar, and with him they besieged Constantine’s city.

And having found traitors, the rebels took the reigning city. Then they brought Tsar Leontius out in the midst of all and cut off his nose and shut him in prison, inescapable and bitter.

The Twenty-eighth Reign, of Apsimar Tiberius, who reigned in Constantine’s city

After Leontius, Apsimar Tiberius reigned 7 years. He was double-named, that is, he had a double name, as Jacob, also Israel, as Jethro, also Raguel, as Simon, also Peter, as Thomas, also Didymus, that is, Twin, as Thaddaeus, also Lebbaeus. When Apsimar ruled the Greek realm, frequent and gently breathing winds blew. Only one thing terrified him: lest the scepter of kingship pass again to Justinian, and lest he who had lost it before, and whose nose had also been cut off, return to the crown. Apsimar thought of Justinian as of an amphibious beast and feared this man, and listened to many interpretations and false prophecies of those who practiced stargazing.

Apsimar learned that Justinian had fled from Cherson to the Khazar prince, the kagan, and had taken his sister Theodora as his wife. And then Apsimar began to devise intrigues against Justinian, and sent a request to the fierce barbarian, the kagan, and promised him a great abundance of possessions if the kagan would hand Justinian over to him in chains like a slave, or cut off his head and send it to him.
