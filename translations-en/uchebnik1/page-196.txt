They gave, but only laughed and said: “It will be enough for your labors that you were not stabbed.” And if the robbers heard anywhere that there was a rich man, they gathered slanderers against him. And the slanderer would stand up and, as they had taught him, accuse the rich man and lie that he wanted to desert to the Romans. And Simon Niger and John, that is Johanan, seized him, stripped him naked, drank his blood, and cut apart his sinews. It is impossible to describe in detail all their lawlessness. To speak generally, no other city endured so much suffering, and no generation since the Creation of the world was so filled with sins and every wickedness. In the end they even mocked the Jewish race, showing that they were children of slaves, scoundrels, and vagabonds. To speak plainly, they were the ones who ruined Jerusalem, they forced the Romans to attack the holy city, and they brought fire upon the temple.

Titus made earthworks and sent soldiers, and ordered them to catch those who came out to gather herbs. And they seized them, tortured them, and crucified them before the city walls, a thousand or more every day. The soldiers, angered at them, crucified them not in one manner but in various ways, and subjected them to mockery. And there was not enough space for the crosses, nor crosses for the bodies. The rebels brought their relatives and said: “See what evil the deserters suffer by deserting to the Romans.” They passed off the crucified not as captured men but as deserters. And many of those who wanted to flee refrained from it until they learned the truth.

Titus cut off the hands of many of the captured and sent them back to Simon Niger and John, that is Johanan, and instructed them to say: “Stop resisting and surrender to Titus; then you yourselves will survive, and your fatherland will not perish.” The rebels reviled both Titus and his father from the walls and exclaimed: “We do not fear death. It is better for us to die than to fall into slavery. We will not cease harming the Romans. We do not care about the destruction of the fatherland and the temple, for this world is better than the temple in God’s sight. And this temple will be saved by the God who dwells in it. Having God as our helper, we laugh at all your threats, for God alone knows how it will all end.”

At that time Antiochus, called Epiphanes, came to Titus’s aid. With him were soldiers called Macedonians, tall in stature and armed with Macedonian weapons. Antiochus came up to Titus and said: “I am surprised that the Romans delay in taking the walls.” Antiochus himself was brave but not very intelligent. Titus smiled and said: “Then let us labor together.”
