About the Bulgarian Prince

The Bulgarian Prince Boris came to Tsargrad, asking to be baptized with all his close attendants, and with God’s help this was accomplished. The tsar himself became his sponsor and gave him the name he himself bore—Michael.

About Another Bulgarian Prince and the Baptism of All the Bulgarians, Which Took Place in the Year 6371 from Adam (the Year 871 from Christ)

Tsar Michael and Caesar Bardas, learning that there was a severe famine in Bulgaria, set out with their army against the Bulgarian prince, moving by land and by sea.

The exhausted and doubting Bulgarians were terrified and, without waiting for battle to begin, began to ask for baptism, declaring their submission to the Greeks.

Then the tsar baptized their prince and, being his sponsor, named him Simeon.

Tsar Michael brought the Bulgarian nobles to Constantinople, baptized them, concluded peace, and sent them away with great honor.

Thus all the Bulgarians received holy baptism.

In the year 6377 from Adam (the Year 877 from Christ), Empress Theodora gave the Bulgarians the empty Greek lands from Sidira to Devlet, which they called Zagorie, and from that time Bulgarian princes began to be styled tsars.

Tsar Michael and Bardas went to war against the impious Ishmaelites and Amer, who had brought so many calamities upon the Greeks, against Melitene and the city of Samosata. The tsar came with his army to the city and besieged it. On the third day of the siege (at the time when the Greeks were preparing to partake of the Holy Mysteries of Christ), the city gates suddenly opened. The Hagarenes came out of them with weapons in their hands and put the tsar and the entire Greek army to flight, seizing his tents and their abandoned property.

Two years passed, and Tsar Amer set out with an army against the Greeks. Tsar Michael went out to meet him and encamped not far from the city of Dozimon.

Amer, having approached Zelisa, led his army out to Chonarion. A battle began, and Tsar Michael was put to flight. And the tsar barely managed to make his way through the rocks and hide in a place called Anzi, hard to reach because of the sharp stones. But the enemies caught up with him there too and surrounded him. And the tsar could not think of how to save himself.

Then Manuel, his mother’s uncle, said: “Take off your red boots, tsar, and all the signs of royal dignity. I will take five hundred soldiers, and with them we will break out of the encirclement.” And bravely rushing at the enemies, protected by the power of the Precious Cross, Manuel broke through their line.
