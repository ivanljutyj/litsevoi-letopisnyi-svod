And five arrows flew from the distant archers, and the arrows pierced Eleazar, and, wounded, he fell from the city wall, and with the head of a ram.

Then Netir and Philip from the Galilean village of Ruma showed courage. They attacked the tenth regiment of the Romans with such noise, and with such force and speed, that they broke the formation and put the whole regiment to flight and slaughtered it, for the Romans could not withstand the courage of those they attacked.

Josephus, however, went out with his men and scattered the fifth regiment, and everything the Romans left behind he burned, and he scattered the foundations of the embankment.

On the next day Vespasian gathered the scattered Romans and rebuked them, saying that they had fled not from warriors but from brigands, thereby shaming both the law of war and the glory of their fathers. And then all with one accord set about making the embankment for the third time and repaired the ram.

And the Romans struck the same place with the ram as before. And then someone from the city hit Vespasian in the foot with an arrow. The arrow did not fly straight, but from above, at an angle, and because of the distance it lost the force of its blow. Nevertheless there was great confusion among the Romans.

And the Romans stopped the siege and soon gathered around the commander. Before all others Titus, the son of Vespasian, ran up in fear. But soon the Romans overcame the confusion.

Vespasian bound up his leg and quickly rode along his army so that all could see him. And the Roman soldiers, seeing their commander, rejoiced.

And urging one another on, the Romans rushed at the walls, avenging their commander. And one could see the Jews falling like sheaves from the city walls, but they did not leave them, and, seeing death before their eyes, they rejoiced, and they threw fire and iron and stones at the besiegers, but nothing helped them. Even when night came, the soldiers did not rest; they shot arrows and spears and killed many.

But most of all the stone-throwing engines helped the Romans, which tore off pieces of the walls and shattered the corners of towers. One man’s head was torn off by a stone, so that it flew three versts away.

And a pregnant woman came out of her house, and a stone struck her in the belly, and it threw the infant a verst away. A terrible cry from the mouths of women arose. The wailing of women and the groans of those who were perishing at the walls of the city rose to the heavens. And blood flowed from the walls like rivers. And one could climb into the city over the corpses as if over steps. Many of the inhabitants of Jotapata then bravely endured suffering and fell. Many were wounded. But the wall, battered all night by the ram, was broken and fell.

But the Jews, well armed, strengthened the broken place. In the morning Vespasian gave the soldiers a little rest after the night’s labor.
