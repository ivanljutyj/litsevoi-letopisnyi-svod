A wind called a tempest arose, and blowing thrice, it shook and rocked houses and churches, so that everyone ran to open places, saying: “The end of the whole universe would have come, had not God’s love for mankind stopped such a storm with rain.”

When the Saracen naval force came out against the Greeks, the emperor appointed Mery, logothete of the naval forces, commander of all the naval forces. Andronikos Doukas also prepared to board the ship with him in order to fight against the Hagarenes.

Samonas, however, the implacable enemy of Andronikos, dug a pit for him and spread a net for his feet, because he had hindered him from fleeing. He suborned a certain man to write secretly to Andronikos that he should board the ship, because “Imerius has received an order from the emperor, on Samonas’s denunciation, to seize and blind you.”

And although Imerius long urged Andronikos to board the ship, since the Hagarenes were near, he refused and did not accept his invitation.

And Imerius alone began battle with the Hagarenes on the day of the memory of the holy apostle Thomas, and won a great victory.

Learning of this, Andronikos fell into despair and, together with his relatives and slaves, set out for the city called Kavala in order to retreat.

Samonas, who had long wished to catch him, said to the emperor: “Did I not always say, lord, that Doukas is an enemy and rebel, an adversary to your kingdom?”

The emperor immediately sent Gregory, surnamed Iviritsa, Domestic of the Schools and Andronikos’s kinsman by marriage, to make war on him.

And Andronikos, learning that Patriarch Nicholas had been expelled from the church, fled to the Saracens with all his household.

At the time when he withdrew from the Greeks, Amer, by name Mumenius (Mum), received him with honor and splendor. The emperor was grieved because of Andronikos and more than once wished to write and send him an invitation to return to the Greeks.

Someone advised the emperor to pardon one of the Saracens held in prison and send him to Syria with a letter.

He did so, writing in cinnabar and sealing it with a golden seal, and he sealed the contents with a small piece of wax. When the Saracen came out from the emperor, Samonas called him over and said to him: “Do you know what you are holding?” He showed him the wax. Samonas said that it was the ruin of the Syrian land.

Having given him money, he instructed him to deliver the letter into the hands of Ozir the vizier. And he, having arrived, did so.
