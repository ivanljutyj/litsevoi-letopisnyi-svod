About the Soldier Nicholas. With Emperor Nikephoros, who was leading an army against the Bulgarians, there was a certain soldier named Nicholas. Overtaken by darkness on the way, they stopped at an inn for the night. And at night the innkeeper’s daughter came to him and began to entice him to a vile union. But he rejected her. After a while she came again, and he sent her away again. Tempted by the devil, she came a third time. And he says to her: “Wretched woman, full of all shamelessness! Do you not know that demons inflame you to destroy your soul, while I am going to the pagans for bloodshed?” And having said this, he sent her away again.

When he fell asleep, he saw in a dream a Certain Awesome One sitting, having placed His right leg upon His left. And He says to him: “Look!” Nicholas answers: “I see, Lord, how the Greeks are defeating the Bulgarians.” Then He moved His left leg onto His right, and Nicholas saw that now the Bulgarians were defeating the Greeks, and behold, the whole Greek army was laid low. And he sees a field strewn with dead bodies, and among them an empty place, enough for one single person to fit. And that Awesome One says to him: “This place was prepared for you—you were to lie there beside your comrades, but because in this night you conquered the subtle serpent who tempted you three times to a vile union, by this you saved yourself from spiritual and bodily destruction, you left the place in the field without its prey, and from now on you will serve Me.”

In the morning the battle took place. He, remaining one of the few alive after the battle, mourning the fallen soldiers, took monastic vows and later became a great and clairvoyant elder. That is why one should abstain from fornication not only during battles but always, since it destroys both body and soul. During battles, however, it is fitting to repent and partake of the Holy Mysteries of Christ, and thus with all zeal stand for the holy churches of God and for Orthodox Christianity.

The 39th Reign, of Staurakios, who reigned in Constantinople

After Nikephoros, his son Staurakios reigned for one year and two months. Because of the wound in his thigh he lay in his chambers and could not take part in the affairs of the state.
