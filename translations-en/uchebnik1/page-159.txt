He joined with Trajan the commander, who had brought troops from Perea, since he had already conquered all the lands on the other side of the Jordan.

And many from Jericho, not waiting for the Romans, fled to the mountains opposite Jerusalem. Those who did not flee Vespasian cut down. The city of Jericho is built in a valley over which rise long mountains on which nothing grows; to the north they stretch to the land of Scythopolis, and to the south to the place where Sodom stood and to the Asphalt Lake. Nowhere are these mountains leveled or inhabited, because of the barrenness of the land on them. Opposite these mountains are the Jordan mountains; they begin at Julias and end at Somorrha, where there is a mountain called Iron, which stretches to the region of Moab.

Between these mountains lies a valley called the Great. Its length is 1,200 versts, and its width 120 versts.

And through the middle of the Great Valley flows the Jordan, and there are two lakes in the valley: the Asphalt and the Tiberian, different in nature: the Asphalt is salt and lifeless, while the Tiberian is fresh and rich in life.

At harvest time this valley is scorched, and the air above it is harmful to health, since no rains fall in this region and there is no water in it except that of the Jordan. Therefore the palm trees near the Jordan grow well and bear much fruit, while those farther away do not.

In Jericho there is an abundant well, in the ancient city. That city was the first of the cities of the land of Canaan to be conquered by Joshua, son of Nun, the Hebrew commander. There is a legend about that well. Formerly it was destructive to all fruits: both those that grow in the earth and those on trees, and to infants in their mothers' wombs, and it brought every disease to those who drank from it. Later the prophet Elisha caused this well to bring life and health. The prophet Elisha was known to all and was a follower of the prophet Elijah. And the inhabitants of this country received him and honored him in Jericho, and Elisha wished to repay them with good for their hospitality, and he endowed their land with this grace forever and ever. Elisha saw that everything was barren because of the water, and he came to that place, stood over the well, lowered into it a clay vessel full of salt, then raised his righteous hand over it, and directed a tender prayer to the well, and with tears implored the well to change its water and open useful springs. And Elisha prayed to God that He would command the sky to mix with the water an air favorable to fruits, and he asked God to grant abundance to the inhabitants of Jericho, and the birth of children, and that this water, useful for fruits, should not fail among them as long as the inhabitants remained righteous. To these prayers Elisha joined the effect of many herbs, using his wisdom.
