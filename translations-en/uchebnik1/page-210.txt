From the soldiers of Simon Niger — Malachi, and Judas, son of Merton, and Sosa, son of Jacob; from the Zealots — Simon and Judas, two brothers, sons of Jair the centurion.

Titus ordered the soldiers to raze the tower of Antonia to the ground, to level the road and widen it, so that all the army of Titus might enter.

Titus ordered Josephus to address John, that is Johanan, as before, for Titus had been informed that the sacrifice to God had ceased in the temple, and there was no offering whatsoever, and the people grieved over this. Josephus stood in a high place and said: “O John, that is Johanan! If an evil desire to fight has already entered you and you thirst to shed your own blood, then come out and lead out as many soldiers as you wish. But do not let my city and the temple perish, do not defile the sanctuary, do not provoke God to anger, and let those Jews whom you yourself choose offer the sacrifice. Spare your fatherland, quench the flame that already touches the sanctuary.” The people heard these words, and were sorrowful, and were silent. John, that is Johanan, greatly reviled and cursed Josephus, and at the end added: “I will never be afraid of the conquest of the city, for this is God’s city.” Josephus exclaimed at these words of his: “If this is God’s city, then perhaps you have kept it pure in all things for God, and have not defiled the sanctuary, and have not committed lawless deeds against Him whom you hope to have as a helper, and perhaps you are offering the appointed offerings? If someone should take food from you, a lawless man, even for one day, he will be your enemy and adversary. But from God, from whom you have taken away the sacrifice constantly offered to Him, what help do you expect in war? You lay this sin on the Romans, but they to this day care for our law and urge you to offer God the sacrifices that you have ceased to offer. All groan and weep, seeing how our glorious city has changed. Foreigners and enemies wish to correct your lawlessness. But you, a Jew born in the law, show yourself worse than they. But it is no disgrace for you, O John, that is Johanan, to turn away from evil. King Jehoiachin of Judah gave you a good example. When in former times the Babylonians came against him in war, he, not waiting for conquest, surrendered of his own free will with his family, so that the enemies would not burn God’s house. And to our days all Jews sing sacred songs in his honor, and the memory of him passes through the ages, is constantly renewed, and makes him, as it were, immortal. You ought, O John, that is Johanan, to do likewise, even if misfortune awaits you. I promise you forgiveness from the Romans. Remember that I am your kinsman. And it is fitting for you to consider who is speaking with you, and whence he comes. For it will never be that I forget my kin and fatherland. But you still are angry with me and insult me. Truly I deserve even worse abuse.”
