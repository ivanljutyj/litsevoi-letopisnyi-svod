…, he had a wife who remained in the same heresy. This husband, however, listened to John’s teaching and entered the catholic church to pray and partook of Communion, and he also instructed his wife to partake.

She doubted, and as though obeying her husband, came to the church; but her maidservant, sincerely devoted to her, brought her communion from the Macedonian heresy and gave it to her.

And this woman took the Communion given by John, and again doubted, and bowed down, and received the communion that the maidservant had given her, while the Communion that John had given she gave to the maidservant. And the woman placed the Macedonian communion in her mouth, and found that in her mouth it had turned to stone. And when this was revealed, the woman approached with trembling and fell at John’s feet, and confessed her audacity, and truly entered the Church. And that stone was placed in the vessel treasury for safekeeping.

The venerable John served as bishop for five and a half years and was exiled to Cucusus in Armenia; from there, after three years and two months, he was transferred to Pityus.

And when he was passing by Comana, he reposed there, tormented by great sorrow; he was fifty-two years old.

On the Repose of John Chrysostom

Great Innocent, Pope of Rome, learned of John’s exile and repose and wrote to Arcadius, saying: “The blood of my brother John cries to God against you, O emperor, as formerly the blood of righteous Abel cried to God against Cain the fratricide, and the blood of John will be fully avenged in some manner, since you permitted an unjust persecution against the Church of God and her saints, having driven from the throne of his episcopate the great luminary of the whole universe, and even the teacher, both lawlessly and without examination. With John you have driven out Christ. The new Delilah, Eudoxia, quickly shaved you with the razor of deceit, and procured for herself a curse and righteous hatred, and bound up an uncleansable burden of sins, and unbearable, and added them to her many former sins.”

Great Isidore also wrote to a certain man, saying: “You wish me to tell of the gracious John, but I am at a loss how to speak, for the rigidity of things overwhelms the mind. Learn a little of that which Egypt, as usual, did not understand, despising Moses. Pharaoh did his own will, wounded the humble, caused grief to the laborers, imposed labors, and deprived the workers of their pay. Even to this day they are instructed in this and have received Theophilus, raving over precious stones and a slave of gold, with four helpers and apostates, and have rejected the Theologian truly beloved of God, and were at enmity with him. With malice against my namesake…
