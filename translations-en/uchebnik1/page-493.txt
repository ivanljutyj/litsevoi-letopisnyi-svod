The blasphemous tormentor, hearing this, roared fiercely, was indignant, and drove the righteous man out dishonorably, calling him an idolater, and with his own hands beat the saint, and expelled him from the imperial chambers.

Certain faithful people say that in the royal cistern, that is, the water conduit, situated near Chalkoprateia, there was a small chamber which, after the custom of the first teachers of the whole world, contained certain imperial teachers and counselors, men twelve in number, who studied every kind of knowledge; they also had imperial decrees and books. With them the emperor took counsel, and without them he issued no decrees or laws.

These counselors and learned men the savage beast, evilly named “the crafty cave-dweller,” summoned and wanted them to submit and join his godlessness. But they did not care about this; rather, they even exposed his impiety.

Then Emperor Leo ordered them to be dragged dishonorably and shut up in the place where they had pursued learning.

This happened at night, and then the fierce serpent sent certain strong-bodied men subject to him and ordered a great quantity of firewood to be gathered, a bonfire lit, and those men burned with the house, and with the books, and with the rest of their possessions. This was done, and everything burned to ashes. From that time learned reason among the Romans grew scarce, and there was no rational obstacle to the folly of the rulers even until the days of Michael and Theodora, pious and faithful sovereigns.

Emperor Leo summoned the venerable Germanus again, thinking that he would obey him and sign against the holy icons. But the venerable one did not heed the exhortation and threat of the bitter demon; moreover, he took off his omophorion, renounced the episcopate, and so withdrew from among them.

O woe! The wolf, the demonic Cerberus, drove out the shepherd, the meek leader of the flock, and in his place sets over the sheep a destructive wolf—I mean Anastasius, the wicked presbyter of the Great Church, who submitted to Leo’s impious command—and with him he savagely tears the flock, and destroys the images of the Virgin Mother of God and of all the saints, overturns and burns them, and takes away the beauty from the churches and temples, and covers them with the darkest darkness, and makes a dark and most gloomy day.

The most savage beast ordered the image of Christ that was on the Bronze Gates to be thrown down and torn off. And a swordsman went up the ladder and set to work. But the women—Theodosia the virgin and Anastasia the patrician, and others with them—burning with divine zeal, threw the ladder down to the ground, cast down the wretched man, and, dragging him, put him to death.
