Meanwhile the emperor, who spent his time in hunting, horse-racing, and other impious amusements, reached manhood. And he fell in love with Eudokia Ierosovna. Learning of this, Empress Theodora and Theoktistos, who hated her for her shamelessness, considered what to do. And then they decided to marry Michael to Eudokia Dekapolitissa. The wedding took place in the Church of St Stephen. In the palace at Daphne a bridal chamber was prepared, while in the Magnaura, the hall for solemn receptions, in the Hall of the Nineteen Couches, the invited nobles reclined on banquet couches.

On Basil the Macedonian

It is time to tell of Basil, whence and from what family he came. There once was a village of Lipra near the chief Macedonian city, which was originally called Orestgrad after Orestes. Later the Greek emperor Hadrian, who founded this city, renamed it in his honor Adrianople. Basil’s parents were neither distinguished nor noble, and had neither lands, nor herds, nor villages, but lived by the labor of their hands, and to the very end of their lives they could not escape poverty.

At that time the Bulgarian tsar Krum subdued many regions of the Greek empire and, destroying all the flower of youth and carrying many away into slavery, filled his land with captives. By the outstretched Bulgarian hand Basil too was captured, like a golden-feathered bird in the ruthless snares of a fowler. Having escaped from captivity, as if fluttering out of the Bulgarian nets, Basil at once returned to his homeland (Macedonia was then a province of the Greek empire), hoping to find worthy service, for he had suffered enough from his former beggarly life. Having tried many things, he realized that he had sown his labors on barren ground. Then he left his kin and the land that bore him, and counting all he had undertaken as nothing, came to Byzantium, the reigning city. And since there were many noble and rich people there, Basil, dressed in rags and possessing neither silver nor gold, was despised by all and could rely only on his hands.

Once he was sleeping at the gates of the church of the holy martyr Diomedes on a certain stone elevation. And a voice came to the church guard, awakening him and commanding him to bring the emperor into the church. The sexton jumped from his bed, hurried to the church, and found there the sleeping Basil, a beggar lying in filthy rags with unwashed feet. Considering what he had heard to be the wiles of the enemy, he went back.
