Returning, he found Michael lying on the bed with his hands cut off. He no longer hoped for deliverance and only wept, lamenting the treachery and faithlessness of Basil.

Then the heartless Asuleon plunged his drawn sword into Michael’s heart, and then cut open his belly, after which he returned to Basil, boasting as though he had done something good and worthy of praise.

Having resolved to reach the imperial palace as quickly as possible, they set out and began to descend. All the way to the crossing they went on foot, since the sea had grown rough and a strong wind had risen. After crossing, they reached the house of Eulogius the Persian, then, taking him with them, came to the house of Marian and, entering the city, headed for the palace. A stone slab blocked their way. Basil, with the help of his close associates, braced himself and pushed the slab with his foot and overturned it. All approached the gates of the palace.

The city prefect Eulogius the Persian, who was called Ardanazd, himself announced that “Michael has been cut down by the sword, and Basil has come to reign.” Then they sent for the key and opened the doors to Basil.

Entering, Basil took the keys for himself, and in the morning appointed Gregory, son of Philemon, as papias; he sent him to the palace of Saint Mamas for his wife Eudokia Nerovna and solemnly proclaimed her empress.

Basil ordered John the prepositus, the master of court ceremonial, to send the widow of Emperor Michael, Eudokia Dekapolitissa, back to her parents.

He sent Paul the cetonite, guard of the imperial bedchamber, to bury Michael. Paul, coming to the palace, found his hacked body lying in a pool of blood. Having nothing in which to wrap the body, he wrapped it in the horse blanket of one of the four horses on which Michael had taken part in the races. And Michael’s mother and sisters made lamentation over him, mourning the wretched man.

Then Paul, placing the body in a boat, brought it to the suburban monastery at Chrysopolis and buried it at the end of the same night, at the third hour, at dawn of the beginning of the first indiction, on Tuesday, the day of the memory of the holy protomartyr Thecla. From the Seventh Ecumenical Council to the murder of Emperor Michael, nine years and seven months passed.
