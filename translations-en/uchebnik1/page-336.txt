Flavian said to this elder privately: “We, O elder, have lived a long life and are experienced in spiritual matters, and can understand them and keep them. But these monks are still young and know nothing of spiritual matters, and do not wish to heed spiritual words. Tell me, then, how does the hostile spirit depart, and how does the grace of the All-Holy Spirit come through your prayers?” The elder was emboldened by these words, thinking that Flavian truly wished to hear from him about this, and the elder spewed out the poison of his heresy. But the divine Flavian said to him: “You have grown old in evil; your foul lips have convicted you, not I.” And then Flavian expelled all the Messalians from Syria, and they came to Pamphylia and filled that land with their foul heresy.

Third Reign, of Julian the Apostate, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Constantius, Julian the Apostate reigned for two and a half years. This Julian, a relative of that Constantius, had previously been Caesar. Julian came to the throne under the consuls Mamertinus and Nevitta. Julian was short of stature, broad-shouldered, with a handsome nose and eyes, and was a friend and contemporary of Libanius, the renowned sage of Antioch. And Julian went with his soldiers against the Persians, with a great army, and came down and arrived at Antioch, and ascended the mountain called Casius, which is six stades from Antioch, and on the mountain Julian offered thousands of sacrifices to Zeus Casius.

And from there Julian came to Daphne, a suburb of Antioch, and offered sacrifice to Apollo, and there he lay down and saw in a dream a fair-haired youth who said to him: “You are to die in Asia.”

When Julian came from Daphne to Antioch, the nobles and inhabitants of Antioch met him there, and shouted against him, and reviled him, and with their cries adjured him to leave their city. And Julian was angered at the Antiochenes, and threatened them, and said: “When I return, I will tell of the Antiochenes that they are rebels.” And Julian wrote a composition about the Antiochenes, so that all might know what he reproached them for.

And Julian went out from the city of Antioch and set off to war against the Persians, and passed by the city of Cyrus, and saw that at the cave of Saint Dometius stood a crowd of people gathered for healing. And Julian asked: “Who is this?” And he learned that he was a monk, and that many people gathered to him, standing there desiring to receive healing and blessing from him.

Julian sent to Saint Dometius and, mocking the Christians, said: “If you entered the cave out of a desire to please your God, then do not please people either, but remain alone.”
