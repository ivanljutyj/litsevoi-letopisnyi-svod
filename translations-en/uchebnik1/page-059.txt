There were holy children: Zacchaeus, who had formerly been a tax collector, and Barnabas, and Aquila, brothers of Clement of Rome, who studied with Paul, our co-apostle and fulfiller of the Gospel, that is, the Good News. And for the third time I disputed with him about his prophecies, and about God’s sole sovereignty, and I overcame him by the power of our Lord Jesus Christ, and reduced him to silence, and made him flee to Italy. When Simon was, as I said, in Rome, he greatly disturbed the church and turned many to himself. The people marveled at his cunning sorcery and demonic deeds. And one day after noon Simon entered the theater and said: I will rise into the air. All marveled at this, but I, Peter, prayed inwardly. And behold, he went, and demons flew and carried him through the air, and he said: I will ascend to heaven and from there bestow good on people. The people praised him as God. I too stretched out my hands on high and prayed to God that the destroyer might be cast down, and his power was shattered. And I said to Simon: If I am an apostle of Jesus Christ and not a deceiver like you, Simon, then I command the enemy powers to cease holding you. And behold, as soon as I said this, Simon immediately fell with a great crash and was dashed to pieces. And the people cried out: There is one God—the one whom Peter preaches. Thus the first heresy was destroyed—the heresy of Simon in Rome—and it quenched a great heresy. Therefore the Romans especially observe that Sabbath day. That was the Sabbath on which both prayer and fasting are appointed for the faithful; for it is the custom to pray in church for one’s teacher, toiling and sorrowful, as is also written in the Acts of the Holy Apostles. And earnest prayer was made in the church for Peter. For on that Sabbath day was Christ’s victory, and the Antichrist Simon fell before everyone’s eyes, and the Romans commemorate that Sabbath and fast, and hold bright assemblies, and preserve that custom even to this day. For different Churches keep different customs, as the writers on church ordinances have set down: among the Scythians, they say, all cities have only one bishop; in Cyprus and Arabia there are bishops in every city, as the adherents of the heresies of Novatus and Montanus in Phrygia urged. In the Roman Church there are only seven deacons, while in other churches there is a great number of them. In Rome also the Gospel is read once a year, on the first day of Easter, and no one teaches in the church then, not even the bishop himself. In Alexandria the bishop teaches in the church, and when the Gospels are read he does not rise from the throne. During the forty-day fast some fast six weeks, as in Illyria, Libya, Egypt, and the whole western land. But in Palestine, Constantinople, its surroundings, and as far as Phoenicia, they fast seven weeks. Others fast only three days, scattered over six or seven weeks. Some fast three weeks before Easter. In some Egyptian cities they dine before Vespers and receive the mysteries. Secratus set this forth; all especially observed this at that time. But now still greater changes have occurred, and each church has its own changes in the customs mentioned.
