This Eleazar gathered around himself all the shameless, who were called Sicarii. They considered all Jews who desired peace to be enemies and killed them, seized their property, and burned their houses. And the Sicarii said of those they killed: “By their weakness they betray the freedom for which we struggle, and of their own accord bow beneath the yoke of slavery.” But these words of the Sicarii were only a cover for their cruelty. They wished to hide their addiction to plunder behind these words. Their deeds showed everything as it actually was.

The citizens carried out the will of the Sicarii, and following their instructions waged war against the Romans. The Sicarii, however, showed their true nature, laid false accusations upon the citizens, and oppressed them greatly. That time was abundant in every kind of treachery.

And there remained no vile deed that did not appear in this generation. Therefore the torment of all our land came from the Divine weapon, for thus the wrath of God overtook all the Jews, both individuals and the whole community. And people competed with one another not in doing good but in lawlessness and impiety: who would seize and plunder more, who would devise greater treachery, who would defile another man’s marriage bed, and, generally speaking, who would destroy more people and gain more sin and lawlessness. The Sicarii were the first to begin all this abomination against their own people; they omitted neither any destructive word nor any secret deed. John, that is Iohanan, turned to even greater malice, for he not only killed all who called to what was righteous and useful, but filled all our fatherland with every abomination. He showed an example of how a man created by God completely rejects God. For he took unlawful food and rejected the purity prescribed by the Law. The same did Simon Niger, son of Giora. What evil did he not do? Or what free people did he pass over with his executions? He regarded neither friendship nor love, and spared neither those who flattered him for wealth nor the servants of God, neither old nor young. Likewise the Idumeans strove for disorder and turned to even greater abomination. They killed the high priests, renounced all decency, and brought about complete lawlessness. In such conditions the generation of Zealots grew up. They called themselves by a name opposite to what they did. For those who are zealous for truth and similar things are called Zealots. But these “Zealots” had no zeal for truth. They were zealous for every vile and foul deed. Whatever crimes were in human memory, from Adam to our own days, known either by rumor or by books, all were committed by these Zealots. Therefore such was their end.
