But if you will not heed this, then for the sake of our faith, even if an angel descending from heaven should wish to turn us from the truth, we will listen neither to him nor to you, O king.”

The tormentor, hearing this, was inflamed with anger and, roaring like a lion, drove them all away with dishonor; and he ordered the venerable Theodore to be shut up in a dungeon.

He also expelled the most holy Patriarch Nicephorus from the city and sent him into exile on the island of Prikonis.

The venerable Theophanes, abbot of the monastery in Great Village, foreseeing the patriarch’s arrival, though he had never seen him before, lit many candles and lamps and went out to the shore to meet him.

The patriarch, even before he had come down from the ship, began to bow and greet the abbot, though he did not yet see him. The sailors, marveling, asked the patriarch: “To whom are you bowing?”—for they themselves saw no one. He answered: “I bow to the abbot of Great Village” (so was called the monastery in which Theophanes the Confessor struggled. The patriarch was the first to call it so, because he foresaw that Theophanes would confess the glory of the venerable icons, which indeed came to pass afterward, when the saint, having exposed the impiety of Tsar Leo, suffered many torments from him and was sent into exile).

After the patriarch’s expulsion, Tsar Leo raised to his place his friend Theodotus Melissenus, formerly a swordsman, nicknamed Olovudmets. Alas—in place of a pious patriarch, a champion of impiety!

This Theodotus was also called Kasiphira. He gained the trust of Tsar Leo in this way. In the days of the reign of Michael Rhangabe there lived a certain woman possessed by a demon. Prompted by it, she cried aloud, addressing Tsar Michael: “Leave it, it is not yours, leave it!” She spoke of the kingdom, since Michael did not wish to reign. Many times she repeated these words publicly. The tsar, hearing her, was thrown into confusion.
