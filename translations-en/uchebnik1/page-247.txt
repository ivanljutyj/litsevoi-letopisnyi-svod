The skill with which the images were made was so great that those who had not been at the war became as though they had been. On each image were shown the city commanders in the circumstances in which each of them had been defeated and captured, and it was also shown how one city or another had been conquered.

Then after the images came the ships and vessels. All the spoil was carried in order. Most precious and honorable were the things taken from the Jerusalem temple: a golden throne made of many stones, and a lampstand adorned with gold, unlike those commonly used by the Romans. In the middle of this lampstand was a golden shaft, and from it extended seven smaller golden branches, like a trident; there were seven branches, and on each of them was a forged golden lamp; and there were seven lamps in honor of the Jewish week.

At the very end of the spoils taken from the Jews, the books of the Law of Moses were carried. After this, many more victory idols were carried, adorned with ivory and gold. After the idols rode Vespasian first, and after him Titus. Last rode Domitian, and he was magnificent, both he and his horse. The procession ended at the temple of Capitoline Jupiter; when they came to it, all stopped, for there was an ancient law from the fathers—to remain in that place and wait until they were informed of the death of the enemy commander.

That commander was Simon Niger, son of Giora, whom they were then leading before the people with the captives during the procession. They tied him by the neck with a rope, and dragged him, and struck him on all parts of his body. And they brought him to the square where by law condemned criminals were killed, and there they strangled him. And word was brought to Vespasian of the death of Simon Niger. And then all glorified Vespasian, and began to offer sacrifices.

The sovereigns recited their prayers and went to the palaces, and turned to rejoicing. On that same day Rome celebrated both the victory over its enemies and the end of internal strife and the beginning of abundance, and all the Romans hoped for a good future. After the triumphal procession, and after the confirmation and glorification of Vespasian, he decided to create an altar in honor of peace. And he soon began the construction, and finished it faster than could have been thought.
