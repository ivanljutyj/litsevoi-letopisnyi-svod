After some time, Basil the kaniklios and Nikita Elladikos the protospatharios were sent by Zoe with great gifts, and they won back Adrianople.

And Damian Ameras came to the island called Strovilos with a large force on warships, and would have captured it if he had not fallen ill and died; the Saracens returned with nothing.

And the inhabitants of Athens and Hellas, constantly insulted by Hasey, son of Iuvius, not enduring his debauchery and insatiability, stoned him severely in the altar in the church of Athens.

Zoe, seeing Simeon’s exaltation and his designs against the Christians, together with the elders planned to restore relations with the Hagarenes and conclude peace, so as to transfer the whole eastern army for war and the destruction of Simeon.

And so she sent to Syria to establish relations and peace John Radin the patrician and Michael Luchnik.

Having done this, they carried out the customary distribution of clothing in the regiments; and having joined them to their soldiers, they entered the land of Thrace. The domestikos was Leo Phocas the magistros, who was characterized rather by courage than by a commanding mind.

And Constantine, the protopope of the palace, surnamed Glavatec, and Constantine, son of Malelios, brought out the life-giving and precious Wood in Thrace; all bowed down and swore: “We shall all die together!” and moved against the Bulgarians with the whole army.

The commander of the excubitors was John Pisec, and the commander of the hikanatoi was the son of Marul the hikanatos; Romanos Argyros, his brother Leo, and Bardas Phocas were commanders; with them were Melios with the Armenians, and all the other commanders, each with his own troops.

Among the commanders was Constantine the patrician, surnamed Lips, the adviser of Leo the domestikos in all matters.

The battle between the Bulgarians and the Greeks took place in the month of August, on the 20th day, in the fifth indiction, at the river called Achelous.

Since God’s judgments are unsearchable and past finding out, the Greeks fled with their whole army, and there was destruction and terrible lamentation. Some trampled one another, while others were cut down by the enemy, and there was such a shedding of blood as had not been from the beginning of time.

And Leo the domestikos, having fled to Mesembria, escaped. Also killed in the battle with the enemies were Constantine Lips, John Grapson Pisec, and many other commanders.
