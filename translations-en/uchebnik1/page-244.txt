The inhabitants of Antioch learned of Titus’s approach and out of joy could not wait for him in the city, but went out to meet him. And they went thirty versts from the city, and not only men but also women and children. And they stood on both sides of the road, and when Titus passed between them, they stretched out their hands to him, bowed, greeted him, praised him in every way, glorified him, and went back with him into the city. Along the way the Antiochenes implored Titus to lead out of Antioch the Jews remaining in the city. But Titus did not wish to listen to their request, and did not answer them, but was silent, and withdrew from the city. And great fear seized the Jews of Antioch, for they did not know the emperor’s decision.

To the same place whither Titus had withdrawn, King Vologeses of another land sent envoys to him, and they brought him a golden crown, because Titus had conquered the Jews. Titus accepted the crown and honored the envoys with great honor.

The nobles of Antioch earnestly asked Titus to enter their city, into the hippodrome, where all the inhabitants had gathered and were waiting for him. And Titus graciously heard them out and came to the assembly. And the Antiochenes began to ask very insistently and repeatedly that Titus expel the Jews from their city. Titus wisely refused them and explained the refusal by saying that the homeland of the Jews, whither they could move, had been conquered and laid waste, and no other city would receive them. The Antiochenes, having been refused on the first request, turned to a second, asking Titus to break the bronze tablets on which the Jews’ honorary rights were inscribed, by which they were permitted to live in Antioch. Titus did not heed this request either, but according to the former laws allowed the Jews to live in Antioch.

From Antioch Titus went to Egypt, and he passed by Jerusalem and saw the change that had come over the city: from wealth to desolation, from splendor to a pitiful sight, from majestic buildings to ruins and conflagration. And when Titus remembered the former beauty of the city, he grieved over its destruction. And he did not exalt himself, as another might have done, nor did he boast that he had conquered a city so great and so mighty, but again and again he cursed the leaders of the revolt, who by their malice and deceit had brought disaster upon the city. From such conduct of Titus it was clear that he had reluctantly accepted the glory of conqueror of Jerusalem. The wealth of the city was found in abundance on the burned ruins under the stones.
