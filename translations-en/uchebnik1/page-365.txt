On the other days John was seen by no one among men, but only applied himself to fasting, vigil, and prayers. And one could see that the whole body of this blessed man had withered from an extremely harsh life, for he ate nothing else but only a little grain and vegetables after sunset. Of bread, wine, oil, and things that must be cooked on fire, it is superfluous even to speak — he ate nothing of that sort. In such feats the venerable one spent night and day, and it became known to all that he was filled with prophetic grace. He prophesied of the future and of what people secretly pondered, and also about the Nile River, what summer would be fruitful and what unfruitful, and he preached about wrath or calamity coming from God, and rebuked sinners with boldness. What more to say? It is impossible for human mouths and minds to number John’s miracles and the abyss of his virtues.

To this God-bearer Emperor Theodosius sent, and called him to labor, and even implored him that, proceeding with his prayers, he should begin war against the enemies. John, however, for the sake of continence declined to journey to Theodosius; he made a prayer over the staff on which he leaned, and gave his mantle instead of a helmet, so that Theodosius might place it on his head, and the staff he was to take as a spear in his right hand and go first before his troops.

Emperor Theodosius received what the saint had sent with faith and honor and prepared for war, not so much by weapons and arrows of a numerous army as by fasting and prayers, trusting in the help of the saint. For even now he said thus: “When the armies meet one another, the victory will be without blood, even if both sides are ready to shed much blood.”

Theodosius walked with bishops and with the people through all places of prayer and cast himself down before the doors of the holy martyrs and apostles, and prostrated himself on a haircloth, and prayed to the saints, and asked God for sure help for himself.

The Hellenes in Rome began to offer sacrifices to the gods, and slaughtered every kind of livestock as a sacrifice, and foretold to Eugenius a victory free of trouble. Flavian was then prefect of Rome and did this more diligently than others, and said to all: “Victory has appeared to Eugenius,” and he had much assistance from his wisdom.

When Theodosius, fervently trusting in the help of piety, took the apostolic words upon his lips and spoke, then the demons themselves, with their vile conscience, were the first to rush into flight in terror.
