The image. Petronius answered: “It is my duty to defend the image of my sovereign. If I withdraw and spare you, I shall justly perish myself, and it will no longer be I who contend with you, but the one who sent me. For I, like you, am subject to his commands.” And the people cried out: “We are ready to endure suffering and torment for the law.” Petronius quieted their shouting and said: “Can you wage war against the emperor?” The Jews said: “For the emperor and the Roman people we offer sacrifices to God twice a day. But if the emperor wishes to set up an image in our land, he must first sacrifice the Jewish people. We have prepared our necks for slaughter, with our wives and children.” Hearing these words, Petronius was amazed and took pity on the Jews, seeing that they would go to death and every torment and endure to the end. Then he gathered the leading men and began now to entreat them, now to point out the wrath of Gaius and that he, Petronius, was under his authority.

And for fifty days the people did not work, for the dispute with Petronius did not allow them to till the land and sow. And Petronius saw their invincible patience, and dismissed them with the words: “I shall have to suffer with you. Either God will help me and incline Gaius to fulfill your will, and then I will gladly accept salvation together with you. Or the emperor will be angry, and then I will gladly give my soul for so great a multitude of people.” And with his army Petronius went from Ptolemais to Antioch.

And immediately from there Petronius sent a letter to the emperor about how he had come, and how the Jews had entreated him, and that if the emperor wished to possess the land of the Jews and not destroy the people, he must heed the plea of the Jews and permit them to observe their ancestral law.

Gaius received Petronius’s letter, was enraged, and sent him a reply in which he sentenced Petronius to have his head cut off, since he had been a negligent executor of the emperor’s command. But the messengers who were carrying this letter drowned in the sea, and before them others arrived who reported the death of Gaius. Gaius was emperor for three years and eight months, and was killed.

And the soldiers put forward Claudius as emperor. But the city elders, and also the governors Sentius Saturninus and Pomponius Secundus, ordered the military commanders to guard the city with three detachments, while they themselves gathered in the Capitol and, because of the outrages of Gaius, intended to wage war against Claudius and to transfer power to ten judges, as had been formerly.
