The tsar said this, and the magistros was seized by the judge from among the banqueters, and they dragged him by force from the costly supper and from his table companions, and he stood before the judgment seat, trembling with fear. He who had formerly exalted himself, proud and fierce, who had previously “had an ox on his tongue,” now could not answer, being accused by the woman of plundering. The justice-guarding judge carefully investigated that the woman had suffered grievous misfortunes from him, and ordered that boyar to be beaten with raw beef sinews; he also had his hair and beard cut down to the very skin, and disgraced his face, and seated him on a wretched, barely stepping beast, and brought the malefactor out in the middle of the marketplace to be displayed for all to mock, and gave all his property to the widow he had wronged. So there is nothing equal to a free mind and a soul not defiled by covetousness and venality. This man, bold in good, will not fear kings, will not doubt before an assembly, will not be frightened by rulers and the mighty; he will be ashamed before no one, like an eagle chick, well-winged and soaring high, and is restrained by no earthly thing. But let us return to what lies before us.

This inexorable zeal of the judges frightened many in the courts and turned them away from extortion and injustice, theft and robbery. And calm and righteous action spread in place of the storm of injustice and fierce waves, and the deep of life remained without disturbance. The sweet-breathing Spirit of God breathed directly and calmed the fierce and much-troubled storm of extortion and injustice.

On Queen Sophia and the Remission of the People’s Debt

This Tsar Justin had a spouse named Sophia, equal in mind to her husband, magnanimous and richly gifted, who, learning that the people were grieving and sorrowing, sorely burdened by debts as if by insoluble bonds, gathered all debt records and consigned them to fire, and paid the debts to the creditors from her own wealth. By what other good deeds the tsar and tsaritsa filled the city can clearly be learned from this small instance, for a little leaven is enough for a whole barrel, and the hem, that is, the skirt, of a well-woven garment shows it, and a magnanimous lion is evident by its claws.
