Since Patriarch Nicholas did not approve of this, Theodore, the aforementioned tutor, sent word to Romanus to come with all his army in ships to Vukolent.

Romanus, having consulted with those close to him, led his army on the day of the Annunciation of the All-Immaculate Virgin Mother of God and on Thursday, fully armed, approached Vukolent.

And immediately Stephen the magistros left the palace, while Nicetas the patrician, Romanus’s matchmaker, entering the palace, removed Patriarch Nicholas from there.

Those in the palace, having received assurances of loyalty from Romanus, sent him the Precious and Life-Giving Cross of the Lord.

And he bowed before it and, having assured them with oaths, entered the palace with a small retinue and bowed to the emperor. And he entered with him into the church at the Pharos, and gave him assurances, and immediately the emperor appointed him magistros and great hetaireiarch.

At the same time a stern order went out to Leo Phocas, saying: “Do not plot any rebellion, but rest a little in your own house.”

It was also ordered that Constantine the parakoimomenos write a letter commanding the same: to plot nothing against him, but to obey Emperor Constantine.

Taking these letters, Andrew, primicerius of the imperial court, delivered them to him when he was in Cappadocia. And having taken and read them, and withdrawn to his house, he fell quiet.

And in the fifth week of holy Lent, in the month of April, Emperor Constantine gave the dowry of the marriage agreement with Helen, daughter of Romanus, who excelled in bodily beauty and in mind.

And on Tuesday of Paschal week, called Galilee, Patriarch Nicholas blessed and crowned him with her.

He appointed Romanus father of the emperor, and in his place he set his son Christopher as hetaireiarch.

After some time, the magistros Phocas by name Leo, a man distinguished by height and strength, became commander of all. Seeing that the emperor honored Romanus Lacapenus, he said that it was Lacapenus, the father of the emperor, who had inclined him to revolt.

And at first Leo Phocas, thinking of seizing the kingdom, kept himself hidden until the time, but when the time came, he brought forth from the womb the evil design into the light, like the offspring of a viper. Not wishing to reveal himself completely, he accused Romanus, saying that Romanus, while making war and supposedly caring in every way for the emperor and doing what was useful for him, like a malicious fox, by this deception attracted the army to himself.
