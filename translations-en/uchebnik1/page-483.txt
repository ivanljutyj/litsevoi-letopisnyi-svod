The Twenty-Sixth Reign, of Justinian, who reigned in the City of Constantine

After Constantine the Bearded, Justinian reigned for 16 years. This tsar, when he warred in the western lands, captured a great multitude of Slavs: some he conquered, and others he won over to his authority by word.

And Justinian returned to his own land; from these Slavs he chose 30,000 warriors, whom he named an excellent, that is, extraordinary, army, and having armed them, “made them renowned.”

And relying on the aid, that is, help, of these brave warriors, Justinian wrote to the Saracens that there would no longer be the peace that had been made by signing between the Greeks and the Saracens.

And Justinian took those excellent warriors and even more of the ordinary ones, and took other warriors as well, and went to the city of Sebaste. The Saracens came to him and testified to him concerning the peace previously made with an oath, and urged Justinian not to turn aside from the agreement, or else God would be his Judge or Avenger. But Tsar Justinian did not accept the words of peace at all, but arrayed himself for war; and then, there and then, the Saracens tore up the written peace charter, thrust it onto a spear, and rushed upon the Greeks.

When the regiments joined battle, 20,000 of the Slavs went over to the Saracens. And so the Greeks were cruelly defeated and countless numbers of them were killed—the true Judgment awarded victory to the enemies, teaching that one must never transgress a divine oath, even one given to adversaries and unbelievers.

Tsar Justinian fled to the coastal place Leucate with great disgrace and defeat. When he arrived, he killed the 10,000 Slavs who remained with him, together with their wives and children.

Because of this the Saracens were enraged even more, and they went on ravaging the Greek land, having with them 20,000 Slavs who had gone over to their side. But the tsar entered Constantinople and was not grieved by anything.

On the Second Assembly of the Holy Sixth Council. Under this Tsar Justinian, after the sixth council, certain men set about renewing again the heresy of Sergius, Pyrrhus, and Paul.

Tsar Justinian again ordered a council of the holy fathers to assemble. The Roman pope was ill and sent two bishops in his place. And all the patriarchs came. Then Saint Gregory also came from Jerusalem, who was then a deacon and later became bishop of Agrigento and a wonderworker; and they gave him the place of the bishop who was ill.

And the holy fathers sat in the Church of Saint Irene and condemned the heretics; together with them Saint Gregory, by a written declaration, denounced the heretics.
