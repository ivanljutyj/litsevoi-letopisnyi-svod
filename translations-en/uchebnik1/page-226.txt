Had there not been so many who saw and told of it, no disaster like the vision would have followed. Before sunset there appeared in the air, in the sky, throughout the land of Judea, chariots and armed troops, galloping through the clouds and surrounding the cities. On the feast called Pentecost, at night, the priests, having entered the inner temple according to custom for the service, first felt a movement and a noise, and then suddenly heard a voice saying, “Let us withdraw from here.” And more terrible than these was the sign.

About Jesus, son of Ananus

There was a man named Jesus, son of Ananus, from the simple folk, not a scribe. And before this, for four years, there had been grace in the city, and there was still peace and abundance. And this man came to the Feast of Tabernacles, when all celebrate according to custom, and, standing in the temple, suddenly began to cry: “A voice from the east, a voice from the west, a voice from the four winds, a voice against Jerusalem and against the temple, a voice against bridegrooms and against brides, a voice against all people.” And he said this all days and all nights, and cried as he went around the streets. Some of the noble people became angry at him for his evil words, and seized him, and inflicted many wounds, and tortured him. But Jesus son of Ananus did not notice the wounds, and did not ask mercy for himself, and did not answer his torturers, but only cried the same as before.

The rulers of Jerusalem thought that some higher power was moving him to this outcry, and brought him to the Roman governor. There Jesus son of Ananus was beaten with rods even to the bones, but he felt no suffering and shed no tears, and still, with all his strength, cried in a pitiful voice: “Woe to Jerusalem!” And Albinus—for that was the Roman governor’s name—asked Jesus son of Ananus: “Who are you, and where are you from? And why do you cry such things?” But he answered nothing to the governor’s words and did not cease bitterly weeping for the city. At last Albinus considered him mad and released him. And from that time Jesus son of Ananus did not come to anyone, and did not appear until the war came. And afterward every day, like a memorized prayer, in tears, he exclaimed: “Woe, woe to Jerusalem!” Those who reviled him he did not curse, and those who gave him food he did not bless. This one sorrowful answer was his to everything, and this lasted seven years and five months, and his voice did not fall silent or grow weary until he saw the military events he had prophesied, and he found rest only when he died. He walked on the city walls and cried: “Alas, alas for the city, and the people, and the temple!” At the end he added: “Alas, alas also for me!” And at once a stone flew from a stone-throwing engine and killed Jesus son of Ananus, and he gave up the spirit while still uttering this prophecy.
