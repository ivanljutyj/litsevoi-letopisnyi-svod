As he was dying, he said: “When you bury me, put this charter into my hands.”

Then the bishop also told them the dream that he had seen that night.

And when Synesius learned of this, he went to the philosopher’s tomb, taking with him the clergy, that is, the priests, of that city. And when they opened the tomb—O miracle!—they found the philosopher sitting and his hand stretched out with the document. When the clergy wanted to take the document, not one of them could pull it from his hand until the bishop came and took the writing with his own hand. Then, having given up the charter, Evagrius the philosopher lay down again. The bishop unrolled the charter before everyone, and they found in it a new writing in the philosopher’s hand, as follows: “I, Evagrius the philosopher, greet you with joy, venerable lord and my teacher, Synesius the bishop. Know, father, that I have received the debt that was written in my scroll, and it has been repaid to me a hundredfold, and I have no reproach at all against you for that gold which I gave to you, and through you to Christ God our Savior.” When this happened, all who heard the document being read marveled at this and glorified God, crying out for three hours: “Lord, have mercy.”

That writing which the philosopher Evagrius had written, Bishop Synesius ordered to be kept in the sacristy, and to this day it lies in the city of Cyrene in the church, and on the Lord’s feasts they set it out and read it before the people for all to hear. Let us therefore attend carefully to our life, and understand all that happened with the divine Evagrius, and take thought for our sins, and redeem ourselves by alms from the bonds of sin; for whoever gives into the hands of the poor before his death gives to Christ Himself, and after his death will find eternal blessings a hundredfold.

(9) The Taziote, that is, the warrior who rose from the dead

I will not be silent about what happened by God’s grace to the human race, for what is said is true: “I do not desire the death of sinners, but that they turn and live.” In Carthage, a city of Africa, there was a certain man given to fornication; he was a taziote, that is, a soldier; and he persisted in many sins. And there were many deaths in Carthage, and people perished from a deadly disease—the red dysentery.

The soldier came to his senses and repented, and left the city, and lived apart with his wife. And so that soldier stayed on a certain estate in silence. But the devil, envying the human race, cast that soldier there into adultery with the wife of his plowman, that is, of the farmer who lived with him on the estate.

And after that sin it happened that the soldier died from a snakebite. There was a monastery there at a distance of more than a mile. And the soldier’s wife went to the monastery and entreated the monks of that monastery; and they came, and took the soldier, and buried him in the church.
