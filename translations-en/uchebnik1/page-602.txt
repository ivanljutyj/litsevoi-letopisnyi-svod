He referred them to the commandments, and He Himself fulfilled all the commandments of the ancient Law; “I have not come,” He said, “to destroy, but to fulfill,” and He filled up its deficiencies by delivering the law and commandments of the new grace. But how is it that you call the Cross hostile and blaspheme it? Hear Paul saying: “But far be it from me to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ”; and again: “The word of the Cross is the power of God to those who are being saved.” If the Cross is the power of God, why do you not believe in the power of God? For by the Cross hell was overthrown, the Resurrection was given to us, and we became heirs of God. You said before that all things on earth were the creation of the evil one; and therefore, O bold ones, do you speak of the Lord’s body as of an evil principle? And how, tell me, is good joined to the evil one, and how did the good God endure with an evil body to accomplish the salvation of the world, or how did He set it on the Father’s throne?”

At this the heretics were at a loss what to answer, and were moved to compunction, and received holy baptism. Seeing this, the Armenians stoned Bishop Hilarion, and, thinking him dead, went away. The Orthodox wished to stone them, but the saint forbade them.

The Disputation of Saint Hilarion, Bishop of Meglin, with the Armenians

The Armenians said: “We keep all the traditions of the catholic Church and say: Christ did not assume flesh consubstantial with us, but a certain incorruptible, impassible, subtle, uncreated, and heavenly flesh.”

The bishop’s answer. “The catholic Church never accepted such a statement, nor did it ever hand it on. For if the Lord’s flesh was incorruptible, impassible, and heavenly, then the impassible does not labor, does not sleep, does not eat, does not drink, nor is it touched with hands. But the Lord showed that He assumed flesh akin to ours, corruptible and mortal, and He ate and drank and slept; moreover, He was nailed to the cross and laid in the tomb.”

The Armenians’ question. We say: “That manifested flesh of the Lord, having united with the Godhead, became one nature—Christ; and since we have received this from the catholic Church, and all else besides, we follow it unshaken.”

The bishop’s answer. “You kept the traditions of the catholic Church until the Fourth Council of Chalcedon; but afterwards you fell away from the catholic Church, taught by a certain Ekhany, who was also surnamed Mantakuny, and by the foul priests who were with him, who accepted the blasphemy of Eutyches and Dioscorus. You call the Lord’s flesh impassible and incorruptible, and heavenly, and united with the Godhead, and become one nature—Christ. Hear, foolish ones: if there is one nature in Christ, then this whole is Godhead.
