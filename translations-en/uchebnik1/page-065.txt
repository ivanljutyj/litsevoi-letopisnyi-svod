The Jewish War.
Josephus Flavius

The Book of Joseph Mattathias the Jew

We now turn to the account of the Herodian kings, and of what is known to us about them from history. We shall also tell of those who were of lesser rank under the Herods, of princes and rulers. They had much empty pride—Archelaus, Herod, Antipater, Alexander the son-in-law of Archelaus, Pheroras, Salome, and others. They dared to go against justice and did things contrary to the truth, and often by their intrigues brought harm upon themselves, as will be told below. In due time the malice of the evildoer is revealed: how he raised slander and uttered words full of deadly poison. It was because of such empty pride that Archelaus, king of Cappadocia, was angry with Alexander, his son-in-law, the son of Herod. Likewise Herod, king of Judea, was angry with Pheroras his brother and Salome his sister because of their many wicked deeds.

And then King Herod convicted Pheroras his brother of his crime. But Pheroras put on dark clothing to show his grief, and weeping fell at Herod’s feet, and asked forgiveness, and called himself a criminal and wretched. And Pheroras admitted that he was the maker of all the evil that Herod’s son Alexander had written about him; Pheroras acknowledged his madness and explained it by love for a woman. And Pheroras turned Herod’s wrath away from himself. And Archelaus, king of Cappadocia, who was present, helping Pheroras, addressed Herod: “I too suffer from my brother, and endure even greater evil from him, but I recognize our natural kinship and do not return evil for evil. In kingdoms, as in large bodies, swellings constantly arise in various members because of their size, and one must not cut off the swollen member but heal it with gentleness.” And with such words Archelaus turned Herod’s wrath away from Pheroras.
