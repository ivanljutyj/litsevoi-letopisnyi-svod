He rose and entrenched himself at a place called Storozhevoye. This place is on a hill, and from there one can see and make out the layout of the buildings and of the temple, huge and radiant, for the northern side of the city lay in a hollow. And Titus ascended Mount of Olives, which is six versts from Jerusalem, and there he ordered his men to entrench themselves east of the city. A great ravine called Kedron surrounds this mountain.

Then the townspeople, who had fought ceaselessly among themselves, seeing the external enemy, abandoned their internal strife and watched the Romans with fear as they entrenched themselves in three places. Then the Jews, for a vain cause—that is, to withstand the Romans—began to come to one mind, and turned to one another and said: “What are we waiting for? Why do we delay? From three sides they fence in our very breath, and war falls upon us with all its force. And how long are we to sit inside the city as at a spectacle, as though what we see were things very useful to us? How long will our hands be lowered and our weapons abandoned? Are we brave only in war against one another? O our madness! Will the Romans, because of our madness and strife, take the city without blood and without sweat?”

And so the warring rebels in Jerusalem united, and encouraged one another, and, having armed themselves, ran out through an unnoticed ravine, and crept up to the enemy, and with an indescribable cry attacked the 10th regiment while it was making a rampart for itself. At that time the Romans had laid aside their weapons and were occupied with works, and did not expect any movement or sortie from the Jews. And there was confusion among the Romans: some fled, others rushed for their weapons and, not having time to raise them against the enemy, were killed.

Other Jews, seeing that their soldiers were winning, ran to their aid. The Romans fled and thought the Jews more numerous than they actually were. And victory came to the Jews, and fortune was on their side. But it is the Roman custom to do everything in order and to fight after warning the enemy with trumpet sounds. Therefore the sudden disorder threw the Roman soldiers into confusion, and they scattered. And when the Jews overtook them, then the Romans, turning around, fought with them.

And the Romans saw that more and more Jews were arriving, and they fled from the camp, and they would have perished utterly if Titus had not hurried to them when he received news of them. And Titus came and mocked their weakness and cowardice, and forced the fleeing men to turn around, and he himself with chosen soldiers attacked the Jews from the flank, and killed many, and wounded still more.

And Titus drove the Jews back to the ravine, and they suffered greatly along the slopes, and, crossing to the other side, they turned and fought with the Romans until noon, with the ravine between them.
