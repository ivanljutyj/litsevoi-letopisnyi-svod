Seeing that the Jews would not be appeased unless they received consolation, he condemned the soldier to death. And the Jews, having received vengeance, dispersed. And they gathered at the village of Gema, which is situated in the great plain of Samaria.

And when the townspeople were celebrating, suddenly a certain Galilean, a pious and righteous man, was killed. And news of this came to Jerusalem and to Galilee.

And, interrupting the festival, the Galileans rushed against the Samaritans, in disorder, of their own accord, like robbers and rebels. Their leader was Eleazar, son of Dinaeus. And they attacked the Acrabattine country, and spared neither old men nor children, and burned everything.

Cumanus took in Caesarea a detachment of horsemen called Sebastians, and came to the aid of the Samaritans, and killed many robbers, and seized Eleazar’s close associates. And the notables of Jerusalem came to the rebels, having put on sackcloth and sprinkling ashes on their heads, and entreated the people with these words: “For the sake of your fatherland and the temple, disperse. You are taking vengeance on the Samaritans for one Galilean, but because of this slaughter the Romans may become angry with Jerusalem.” The Jews obeyed these words and dispersed. But many turned aside to robbery. And throughout the whole land the rebels plundered and oppressed.

The Samaritan notables came to Ummidius, governor of Syria, for a hearing against the Jews. Then Jonathan the high priest arrived, and other notables who spoke on behalf of the Jews. They accused Cumanus of not punishing the guilty and not stopping the slaughter at the very outset. Ummidius said: “When I come to those places, I will investigate everything.” Those whom Cumanus had seized he ordered to be crucified.

Ummidius sent the two high priests, Jonathan and Ananias, and other leading men from among the Jews and Samaritans to Rome, so that the emperor might judge them.

Claudius heard them and condemned Cumanus, his official, to exile; and Celer, another of his officials, also found guilty in this matter, the emperor ordered to be put in chains, taken to Jerusalem, and killed there in the sight of all. And from among the Samaritans the emperor condemned three of the most distinguished men.

And afterward the emperor appointed Felix to rule and possess the lands that Archelaus had possessed, and he gave the remaining lands to Agrippa, the son of Agrippa, whose father had died when he was young.
