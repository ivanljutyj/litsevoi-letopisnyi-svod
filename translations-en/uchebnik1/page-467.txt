The Word of Martirius of Antioch Concerning the Dead.
The same Martirius of Antioch also declares concerning this in a certain epistle: “When the soul is separated from the body, immediately after it has left this world the good powers of angels and the wicked troops of demons meet it, and according to the deeds done, evil and good, either the one or the other carry the soul to a worthy place and keep it there until the last Day, on which we shall all stand before the Judgment and be led either into eternal life and rest or into the endless flame of fire. Desiring to show this by a certain testimony, that is, by a bodily figure, God arranged at Moses’ departure that the Archangel Michael should appear to ask for the body of Moses; the hostile demon objected and opposed Michael; the good archangel contended with him and prevailed and drove him off; and Michael did not rebuke him with authority, but the Lord of all relaxed the judgment upon Moses. Therefore let us learn that a certain struggle awaits souls after death, so that we ought to prepare ourselves by good deeds, that we may receive angelic help when demons gnash their teeth at us, enviously finding fault with everything.”

The Word of Maximus the Confessor Concerning the Departed.
The great Confessor Maximus also said to John, by rank cubicularius, that is, bedchamberlain: “Who, beloved, among those beloved, who like me is possessed by sins and defilements, will not fear the visitation of the holy angels? For by this divine visitation of the angels the sinner receives the end of this present life and is compelled to depart from the body unwillingly, knowing his defiled conscience. Who will not be terrified by the barbarian-like encounter with fierce evil demons? For each of them will mercilessly drag the wretched soul through the air to himself after its departure from the body, and will shame it by exposing what it has done, so that it may have no hope of future goods.
