And there came to Titus at Tarichea 300 horsemen from Vespasian as reinforcements. Titus was greatly grieved, since less glory would fall to his soldiers because of the reinforcements that had arrived.

Vespasian also sent to Titus’s aid the commander Anton with two thousand archers, so that they might station themselves before the city during the battle and prevent the townspeople from coming out onto the walls and helping their own.

Titus was the first to spur his horse and advance against the Jews, and after him all the soldiers rode with a valiant cry, and they spread out across the field so that it might seem there were many of them. The Jews were frightened, seeing the swiftness and order of the Romans, and they resisted but briefly; the Romans bore down on them with spears, pierced the Jews, and crushed them with their horses. Unable to withstand the onslaught of the cavalry, the Jews fled, trampling and pressing upon one another. Titus and his soldiers chased and cut down the Jews across the field, and those who tried to flee to the city they overtook and drove back. Few Jews escaped from the battlefield.

And within the city there was a violent dissension. The local inhabitants had not wanted war from the beginning, because they had much wealth and property in the city, and they understood that they would be defeated. But those who had come to Tarichea from other places urged war.

And the townspeople quarreled among themselves, and took up arms against one another.

Titus stood not far from the walls and heard this shouting, and understood that the Jews were quarreling, and said to his soldiers: "Romans! Why do we delay in gaining the victory, when God is delivering the Jews into our hands? Do you not hear the shouting? Those who have escaped from our hands are in confusion. The city will be ours if we make haste, labor, and force ourselves. Nothing glorious is accomplished without labor and without compulsion. Let us outstrip the folly of our opponents, who may soon reconcile with one another if we delay. And help may come to us. Then our victory will be less, and our glory diminished. But if we, who are so few, defeat so great a multitude and take the city, who will be more glorious than we?"

After these words Titus leaped onto his horse and rode forward along the edge of the lake, through the ford, and entered the city by way of the lake, and after him all his soldiers. And fear fell upon all the Jews standing on the city walls, and no one dared fight them, and the people around Jesus fled.

Some fled to the lake, where the enemy met and killed them; others tried to get into the ships; others showed submission to Titus. And streams of blood flowed through the city.

Those who had come from other places, those who had not managed to flee, resisted Titus. But the local population of Tarichea showed submission.
