When the Saracens’ pride had so grown that they drove the soldiers even as far as Malagina, the emperor noticed a certain Andrew, a prudent man of great experience and no less brave; although he kept himself modestly, the deed showed the truth. He was one of the western Scythians, and was appointed assistant to the commander in the Opsikion province.

In battle with the adversaries he won a great victory, for which, with the rank of patrikios, he received the post of commander of the Cholon country, and after that became magistros. But Golden-Handed, together with Kavreios and Kallistos, caused much evil to the Christians, as did his father, who had reached as far as Nicomedia and Nicaea, and had also reached the Thracian region and John the Theologian, into whose church his intimates led their horses. The emperor sent him letters about peace, but he did not agree to conclude peace.

And this Golden-Handed pitched his tents in the Charsianon theme at Agranai.

At Sivora, however, the domestikos Andrew ordered the commanders of the troops in the themes of Armenia and Charsianon, saying: “Take trusted nobles from those subject to you, and within twelve days overtake Golden-Handed.”

Golden-Handed, approaching Deep Stream in the evening, pitched his tents below.

The commanders climbed up to a mountain pass and stopped at a place called Zogoloil. This place, set among rocks, was inaccessible. The commanders pitched their tents there, and disputes about bravery began among them. The leaders argued among themselves as to which of the two themes had the better soldiers, the Armeniac theme or that of Charsianon.

To the Charsianians, who were arguing whether to claim the first place for themselves or for the others, the Armeniacs objected: “Why do you argue? Let it be shown in deeds; let both sides enter battle, and then it will be clear who is braver.”

And so, when the battle began, the Armeniacs drove the enemy from Deep Ravine all the way to the so-called Hill of Constantine, cutting them down fiercely.

Then Puladius, clad in a helmet—for before Golden-Handed, Puladius had come to Tephrike—overtook Golden-Handed, and drew near him and cried out: “The commanders are already here, the domestikos of the Schools is here!” And Golden-Handed said to Puladius: “What harm did I do you?” And he said to him: “You did me much good.”

Golden-Handed rushed away in flight, and in a certain hollow his horse bent its legs for a leap; and when he tried to jump over it, Puladius, thrusting a spear under his ribs, threw him down.
