This Julian died at the age of thirty-six.

In that same night Basil the Great, Archbishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia, saw in a vision the heavens opened and Christ the Savior seated on a throne, surrounded by angels, and the holy martyr Mercury standing before him, clad in shining iron armor. And the Lord said to him: “Go and kill Emperor Julian, who has risen against my servants the Christians.” And at once Mercury became invisible.

And Mercury appeared again, and stood before the throne of Christ, and cried out: “Julian has been stabbed and has died, as the Lord commanded!” And Bishop Basil was terrified by this cry, and leaped up trembling. Emperor Julian respected Basil for his wisdom, and, since he had become accustomed to him, often wrote to him.

And Basil entered the church for matins, and summoned all the clergy, and told them of the mysterious vision, how Emperor Julian had been stabbed and had died that night. And all begged Basil to be silent and not to say anything of the kind. After that they brought the foul body of Julian to the Cilician city of Tarsus and buried it.

And the earth was shaken, and Julian was cast out. But Eutropius the chronicler spoke differently from the others about these events in his writing.

The Fourth Reign, of Jovian, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Julian the Apostate, this Jovian, son of Varronian, reigned for seven months, installed by the soldiers there in the Persian lands; this was in the consulship of Sallust. Jovian was short, pale, and grey-haired. He was a Christian. As soon as he became emperor, Jovian said to all the soldiers who were with him and to his nobles, and cried out in a loud voice: “If you wish me to be your emperor, then let you all be Christians.” And all the soldiers, the nobles, and the commanders praised Jovian. And then this Jovian went with his soldiers from the desert into the Persian steppe, and he pondered how to get out of the Persian lands.

The Persian king Slunarsakiy, not knowing that Julian had been killed, was possessed by great fear, and from the Persarmenian land he sent an envoy to Julian, and entreated him and asked for peace.

And this Jovian agreed, established tribute, and concluded peace, through the mediation of his commander Arintheus, whom this Emperor Jovian sent to the Persian king.

And when Jovian came to Mesopotamia, he strengthened Christianity in every way, and appointed Christians as princes and commanders throughout the East.
