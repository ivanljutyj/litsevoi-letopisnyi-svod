"There is no salvation for you unless you find helpers for yourselves. For Ananus, having taken counsel with the people, sent envoys to Vespasian so that he might come quickly and seize the whole city. To the people Ananus announced a purification, so that in the morning they might enter the temple ostensibly to worship and offer sacrifice, and attack you suddenly. And I was sent to negotiate peace with you, so as to seize you treacherously while you are unarmed. You must therefore take thought for saving your lives: either make peace with Ananus, or send for help. But I tell you, if the hope of forgiveness warms you, you have no sense, for people will not forget what lawless deeds you committed against them." With this lie John intimidated the zealots, and he spoke to them vaguely about help from outside, but his words pointed to Idumea.

And to this John added further that Ananus gnashed his teeth, wishing to tear apart the leaders of the zealots. Having heard all this, Eleazar son of Simon and Zachariah son of Phalek, both priests by birth, consulted with the other zealots about what ought to be done, for a hard time had come upon them, since they themselves were shut in and could not send for help from outside. Nevertheless they decided to send to the Idumeans.

And they quickly wrote a letter and sent it to the Idumeans with these words: "Ananus is deceiving the people and handing the capital over to the Romans. But we have separated from him for the sake of freedom, and now we are shut in and sit inside the temple. And guards stand around us. Little time remains for our salvation. If you do not help us soon, Ananus and our enemies will overcome us, and the city will be surrendered to the Romans." And the zealots gave this letter to two men, loyal, clever, and swift-footed. For the zealots knew that the Idumeans were a violent people, prone to sedition and revolution, and that once a word was said to them they would immediately take up arms and come.

And when the message from the zealots was brought to the Idumeans, the Idumeans were thrown into confusion and sent men everywhere with calls to war, and they gathered as one, twenty thousand men, and went against Jerusalem.

But Ananus did not notice how the envoys from the zealots had gone out, nor did the guards. But the approach of the Idumeans did not escape their eyes, and they managed to close the city gates and post guards on the walls. And Jesus, the high priest, who was second after Ananus, stood on the tower and said: "I am amazed how fortune helps the wicked. For you have come to the aid of impious men. I did not know that you were of the same mind, since you are helping the zealots. If anyone honestly examines their life, he will find that each of them deserves a thousand deaths. For they are cursed by the whole land, and have defiled themselves in everything, squandered their property, and tested their frenzy and greed on the surrounding cities. Now at last they have secretly entered the holy city and defiled the undefiled places, and without fear they drink their fill in the sanctuary with wine and with the wealth of the slain..."
