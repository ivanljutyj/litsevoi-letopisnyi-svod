On the Heretics.

After this, when Cyril died, there arose a certain monk, an archimandrite named Eutyches. He tore away and led after himself no small part of Cyril’s people, and preached the evil teaching of Nestorius, and said: “The Lord’s body is not consubstantial with us.” And soon after that—and the imperial hand helped this—there was a certain council in Ephesus, a robber council and not a holy one. Flavian of Constantinople was also sent there, while Dioscorus of Alexandria had all authority. Flavian the divine, champion of the Orthodox faith, was killed. And at that robber council in Ephesus they condemned the council that had been before. And some bishops signed this condemnation under fear of death, among them Basil of Seleucia. And then they preached the evil teaching of Nestorius, Dioscorus, and Eutyches. And there was much tumult and uproar again in the East, and Proterius, the great hierarch, was killed, and many others.

On the Fourth Council.

After this, Marcian the emperor, raised up by God, rose up and gathered in Chalcedon a council of 630 fathers. Dioscorus and Eutyches came, and the emperor himself walked with them.

On the Heretics.

And they condemned Dioscorus, Eutyches, and Nestorius, and these were again anathematized. Theodoret of Cyrrhus, Ibas of Edessa, and Basil of Seleucia were received only when they set their signatures and repented of what they had done. And the robber council was then condemned and anathematized, and the divine fathers were brought into one agreement, and these four councils thus took place, and they justified those who held to God’s Church. But the followers of Nestorius’s teaching tried to hold to heresy further through Theodore of Mopsuestia, who is guilty of many blasphemies even greater than those of his disciple Nestorius. Therefore we also, following the teaching of our forefathers and wishing to keep the right faith without blemish, command that this Theodore’s blasphemy also be investigated and that, after common deliberation, an answer be given concerning him. Among his other evil blasphemies against Christ God, this Theodore said: “God the Word is one thing, and Christ another.
