Maurice desired this, overcome by love of silver and hatred of his disobedient soldiers; neither Scythian savagery, nor the wondrous resolve of the barbarian kagan, nor the wailing and tears of the captives inclined Maurice’s hardness of heart.

The kagan sent again to Maurice, but he did not incline to mercy, and at this the kagan raged like a leopard and a tiger. O woe! By this refusal Maurice doomed so great a multitude of people to bitter death, and the sword reaped—woe is me!—so great a multitude, and covered the face of the earth with corpses, and the fields were reddened with blood, as food for soaring birds and beasts. O gold, persecutor and most proud tormentor, destroyer of cities! O gold, you soften the cruel and harden the soft; you open the tongue of the speechless and stop the mouths of the eloquent; by your gleaming you catch hearts in desire. If you make even stones soft, who can escape your most powerful strength?

After this, having learned that so many men had been killed because of Tsar Maurice, all the people hated him, and reproached him for hard-heartedness, and all began to revile and abuse him.

Then Maurice repented, and came to himself, and, reflecting on the future Judgment, chose rather to receive retribution here for his sins than there at the terrible Judgment.

And Maurice composed a petitionary letter, and sent it to all the patriarchal thrones, and to the ascetics in Jerusalem, and to the monasteries, and sent much gold, and incense, and candles, and asked them to pray for him, that here for his sin he might receive retribution.

When Maurice prayed to God concerning the sin of murder, he saw such a dream: as if many people, standing before the icon of Christ that is over the Copper Gates, stood in the royal chambers, and a Voice came from the icon, saying: “Give Maurice.” They took him and placed him before the icon.

And the Divine voice said: “And where do you wish, Maurice, that I repay you: here, or in the age to come?” Maurice, hearing this, trembled and in answer said: “O Lord, lover of mankind, here, and not there.” Immediately the Lord commanded to deliver him himself, and his wife, and his children, and all his retainers, that is, his whole household, to Phocas the commander.

And the tsar awoke, and quickly sent for his parakoimomenos, that is, his chief chamberlain, an official who stood before him day and night, and ordered him to bring Philippicus, his son-in-law, urgently.

And Philippicus, seeing the parakoimomenos coming at such an hour, bade farewell to his life, and asked to receive Communion. Thereupon the illustrious and great in battles was seized—O woe!—by a hostile snare, that is, a net, and instead of bright raiment he was clothed in the black garment of a monk.
