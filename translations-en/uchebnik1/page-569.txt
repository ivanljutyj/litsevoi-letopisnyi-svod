Basil considered him a saint and held him in great honor.

The tsar was in great sorrow over the death of his son Constantine. Santavarin said to the tsar: “I will show him to you alive.” And once, when the tsar was walking in one of the gardens, a phantom on horseback met him, resembling Constantine his son, clothed in gold-woven garments. Seeing him with his eyes, the tsar embraced and kissed him and told him that he was his deceased son. And on this occasion the tsar built a church of Saint Constantine in that place and founded a monastery, and he became filled with great faith in Santavarin. But Leo, the son of Basil, called him a sorcerer and magician.

And on the first day of the month of May, the New Church was renewed and consecrated by Photius. The tsar, in the loros, that is, in royal vestment, made a procession and distributed much gold and silver, and called it Nea, that is, the New.

Leo took as his wife Theophano, the daughter of Martinakios, with whom he was wedded, arranging a wedding in the Magnaura with nineteen tables.

And his father made him tsar. Santavarin, however, did not love Leo and plotted against him, so that his father might hate him.

And for this he ingratiated himself with Leo and coached him, saying: “Since you are still young, O tsar, you ought to carry a larger knife with you, and when you are hunting with your father, if a wild beast comes upon you, that knife will be useful to you. And if there is any evil design against your father, you will draw the knife and frighten the evildoers.” But he did not discern the deceit, and believed him, and put the knife in his boot, and carried it.

And having left him, Santavarin said to the tsar, his father: “Let your kingdom know that your son Leo has plotted against you. If you wish to know whether I speak the truth, order a search to be made as to whether he is carrying a knife hidden in his boot.”

And the tsar went out hunting, and with him his son Leo. And when they began to search, they found the knife hidden in his boot, and Santavarin proved that he spoke the truth. And although Leo told of Santavarin’s deceit, they did not believe him.

And the tsar was enraged, and ordered him shut in the palace prison, and he remained there a long time.

They decided to blind him, but Patriarch Photius and Stylianos, nicknamed Zaoutza, by many entreaties persuaded the tsar and annulled this decision.

They tortured Niketas of Hellas, his protovestiarios, who under Tsar Roman became papias, and many others with him were beaten and banished.
