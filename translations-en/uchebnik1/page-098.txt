In Hades, according to the Greeks, are Sisyphus, Tantalus, Ixion, and Tityus. By this teaching the Essenes urge and compel people to do good and to avoid evil. The good become even better, hoping for glory and immortal life after death. But the impulses of the wicked are restrained by fear, for they know that even if in life they hide their malice, after death they will receive immortal torment.

By this teaching about the soul the Essenes bring inexpressible joy to those who heed their wisdom. There are also some among them who foretell the future, since they have read the sacred books and the sayings of the prophets and have purified themselves by various purifications. And their predictions always come true, and it rarely happens that some prediction does not come true.

There is also another branch of the Essenes, who are of one mind with all the rest in manner of life, custom, and law, but differ concerning marriage. They say that those who do not marry cut off an important part of life. If all people unanimously avoided wives, the human race would soon cease. They test the brides for three years and wait until they have been cleansed three times from womanly impurity and are ready to give birth, and then they marry them. When they conceive, they no longer sleep with their wife, showing by this that they did not unite for pleasure. The so-called Pharisees, the teachers of the law, place all their hope in God and in the Judgment, and say that recompense will be for righteousness and for unrighteousness. About the soul they teach that every soul is immortal, but if the soul is good, it passes from its previous body into a purer one, while the wicked soul is led to torment.

The Sadducees do not acknowledge the Judgment at all, and “they do not call God the creator of evil, but the Giver of life, who has set forth in men the will for good and for evil.” They do not acknowledge that the soul receives reward or torment after death, and there is no love among them.

When Archelaus’ region was taken from him, Caesar handed it over to his administrators. Philip and Antipas, who was also called Herod, held their own regions and built many cities: Caesarea, Julias, and Tiberias, in honor of Tiberius. And Augustus, ruler of the world, died; he reigned 57 years, 6 months, and 2 days.
