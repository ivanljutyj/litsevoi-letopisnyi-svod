In the year 6633 from Adam (1133 from Christ)

On the 19th day of May, the right-believing Grand Prince Vladimir Monomakh died, son of Vsevolod, grandson of Yaroslav, great-grandson of the great Vladimir; he reigned in Kiev for 13 years, and lived 73 years in all from his birth. He was adorned with good morals and became famed for victories for the Russian land against the foul Polovtsians; all lands trembled at his name, and report of him went through all lands, because he loved the Lord God with all his soul. But we too imagine ourselves to be lovers of God, yet we do not do his deeds; but if we strive to keep his commandments, then we shall show ourselves to be lovers of God; for “he who loves me,” said the Lord, “will keep my commandments.”

This wondrous Prince Vladimir Monomakh diligently kept God’s commandments and constantly had fear of Him in his heart, and kept His words, which say: “By this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another; and love your enemies, and do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who bring troubles upon you.” Therefore the Lord God delivered all his enemies into his hands, because he did not exalt himself, did not boast, but placed everything on God, and God subdued all his enemies under his feet.

Keeping God’s commandment, he did good to his enemies and sent them away with gifts. He was merciful beyond measure, remembering the word of the Lord that says: “Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy; blessed is he who understands the poor and needy; in the evil day the Lord will deliver him.” Therefore, not sparing his property, he gave to those in need.

He built and adorned churches and monasteries, honored the priestly and monastic order, and gave them every sufficiency for their needs. And he had great faith in his kinsmen, the holy martyrs Roman and David, and built a beautiful church on the Alta in their name, where the blood of Saint Boris was shed.

He was very compassionate and received this gift from God: when he entered a church and heard singing and reading, he stood firmly in mind at the singing and reading, and was moved, and shed tears; therefore the Lord God fulfilled all his petitions and completed his life in virtue.

He died on the Alta, by his beloved church, which he himself had built with great care. All his sons—Mstislav, Izyaslav, Svyatoslav, Yaropolk, Vyacheslav, Roman, Yuri, Andrew—and his boyars carried him to Kiev, and he was laid in the Church of Holy Sophia, beside his father.
