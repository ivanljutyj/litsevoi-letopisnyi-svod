But his mother was Ariadne, and to Zeno, general and patrician, they bowed as to king and father of Leo. And Zeno placed the royal crown on his head and reigned with Leo his son for a short time.

And the Younger Leo was consul. In the 11th month of his consulship, the Younger Leo began to ail, in the month of Dios, that is November, indiction 13. The year was, by Antiochene reckoning, the 23rd, 17 and a half years, as Nestorian the Wise, the chronicler, wrote up to Leo the Younger.

The 13th Reign, of Zeno, Who Reigned in the City of Constantine

After Leo the Younger, Zeno his father reigned 2 years and 3 months. He was of medium build. Under this emperor Martyrius left the episcopate because of Zeno’s corruption and said in the middle of the church: “The clergy does not obey me, and the people do not listen, and the churches are defiled; therefore I renounce it, in order to preserve in myself the episcopal dignity.” When he withdrew, in the second year and the 8th month of Zeno’s reign they appointed as patriarch in Great Antioch Peter, sacristan of the Church of Saint Euphemia in Chalcedon, nicknamed the Fuller, and sent him to Antioch.

This Peter was the first to devise the consecration of chrism in church before all the people, and that the blessing of the waters at Theophany should take place in the evening, and that the Mother of God should be named at every prayer, and that the Creed should be sung at every service of God, that is: “I believe in One God”; previously this was not spoken except once a year, on Great Friday.

This Zeno was grieved over Ardabur while he was alive, for he had asked Leo the Great for his daughter Ariadne; but Leo gave her to Zeno.

But Lady Verina, his mother-in-law, devised evil against Zeno. And Zeno feared that someone among those living in his imperial palace might kill him by command of his mother-in-law Verina; and Zeno went to Chalcedon, and abdicated, and fled from there, and came to Isauria, and was emperor there. Queen Ariadne came to him in Isauria, having fled secretly from her mother, and lived there with her husband.

And after the flight of Emperor Zeno and Ariadne, this Lady Verina immediately appointed as emperor and crowned for rule Basiliscus, her brother. And Basiliscus, brother of Verina, Zeno’s mother-in-law, reigned two years. Basiliscus was of medium build. This Verina proclaimed the emperor whom she had appointed consul.

This Basiliscus began to reign and appointed his son, named Mark, emperor, and the two of them reigned.
