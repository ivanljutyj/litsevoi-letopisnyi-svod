Truly, another great word was also fulfilled in him: “And he destroyed all his strongholds; thou hast made his stronghold fear. All who passed along the way plundered him; he became a reproach to his neighbors; thou hast raised the right hand of those who oppressed him; thou hast made all his enemies glad; thou hast turned back the help of his sword and didst not support him in battle.” And those dwelling in Constantinople also sang: “Some are in arms, some on horses. But we will call upon the name of the Lord our God and be magnified. They stumbled and fell, but we rose and were enlightened.” The divine word, again foretelling the swift fall of their enemies, says: “Children, bear the wrath that has come upon us from God. For your enemy has pursued, and you will see his destruction soon and will step upon his neck. Strive, children, and cry out to God. For when it comes upon you, it will be for a remembrance to you. For He who brought evils upon you will bring upon you eternal gladness and your salvation.” Thus this wretched man labored in vain, shedding blood to no purpose and perplexed why his plans did not succeed and his position grew worse day by day.

After some time Thomas, learning that Emperor Michael had summoned the Bulgarians to help, immediately went against them, lifting the siege of Constantinople.

And he fought with the Bulgarians, and having suffered great losses, fled to the Arcadian city and hid there, remaining in sickness and fear.

Then those with him understood that they had rashly submitted to his evil will, and realizing all the illusory nature of hope for victory and plunder, fearing approaching destruction, little by little began to leave the city and abandon him who had not justified their expectations.

Michael, learning of this, without delay set out from Constantinople with a large army and rushed against him. After a short siege, having taken the city, Michael subjected the fallen Thomas to mockery, trampling his neck with his feet, as was customary by ancient custom, and ordered his hands and feet cut off, his eyes gouged out, and then his body cut into parts.

Emperor Michael, feeling the approach of his death, entrusts the royal scepter to his son Theophilus and soon dies in terrible torments from a kidney disease.

The 43rd reign, of Theophilus, who reigned in Constantinople

After Michael, his son Theophilus reigned for 12 years and 3 months, a new godless Belshazzar, a persecutor of the saints, similar to both beast-named rulers and to the impious Copronymus.

And God, because of his lawlessness, allowed the Saracens to attack the reigning city, capture it, ravage it, and burn it. Of the inhabitants, some they killed, others they took captive.

Also on many ships they went around the surrounding islands, laying waste Crete and Sicily.
