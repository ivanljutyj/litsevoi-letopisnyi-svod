All the other Jews who were still alive were sold with their wives and children at a reduced price, for ten gold pieces, since the number of sellers had increased while the number of buyers had decreased. Titus ordered it to be proclaimed to the Jews that none of them should cross over alone, but with his family. Yet afterward he began to receive everyone: both those who came with family and those who came alone. And Titus appointed men to judge the Jews, who was worthy of death and who of life. Those condemned to death were led away for execution. And there were so many Jews that they could not register them all for the payment of tribute, and 40,000 people were released to go wherever they wished.

On the Temple Vessels

At that time a certain priest named Jesus, son of Thebutus, swore to the emperor that if he granted him his life, he would hand over the temple vessels. Breaking down the wall, this priest took out two magnificent golden lamps, many tables, bowls, dishes, and cups, all made of solid gold. Along with these, this priest handed over to the Romans all the curtains, the garments of the high priests with precious stones, and much other sacred equipment for worship.

On the Priestly Girdles

The treasurer named Pinchas was also seized, who showed where the priestly girdles were, and garments, and much purple, and fine linen, and everything else needed for the temple curtain. There was also a great quantity of cinnamon, cassia, and other spices, which were intended so that, mixing them all together, they might burn incense to God every day. The treasurer also handed over much other equipment. Because of this, although he was taken captive, he was granted pardon.

The Romans finished making the embankments on the seventh day of the month Gorpiaeus, that is, September. They made them in eighteen days. And the Romans set up wall-breaking engines. The rebels despaired of holding the city. Some of them withdrew from the walls to the outskirts of the city, others went down into underground passages. And a few robbers still resisted the Romans. When the Romans broke through a small part of the wall, and the towers began to collapse under the blows of the rams, the defenders of the walls quickly fled. A great fear fell upon the leaders of the rebels.

And even before the enemy entered, the rebels lost their senses, and there was no place for them to flee, and they tried to leave the city and break through the guards and escape, but could not, because the Romans proved stronger than they. And those who had formerly been faithful servants of the robbers dispersed wherever need led each.
