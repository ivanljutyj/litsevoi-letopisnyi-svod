They suffered a small defeat at their hands, by Divine Providence. Those who boast and glory in joy, exalt themselves above all people, and then in grief are terrified and despair show a lack of sense. This is equally foolish, since both joy and grief are changeable. It is better to be restrained in victory and not to lose heart in defeat. What happened to us today did not come about through Jewish courage, but the narrowness and inconvenience of the place brought them victory and us destruction. Therefore we can be reproached only for this: that you rushed at the enemy without caution. When the enemies fled into the upper town, we should then have protected ourselves and not gone up as if to a slaughterhouse, letting ourselves be cut down by those who fell upon our heads from above. But we rushed into danger like beasts. When the walls were destroyed and we entered the city, we should have stayed in place and little by little lured the enemy out to a safe and broad place where we could fight. But we did not restrain ourselves today, and in striving for victory we forgot safety. It is not the Roman custom to fight in fury and disorder; that is Jewish savagery. And now we must return to our manly composure and only be vexed with ourselves that we allowed an error uncharacteristic of us. But you will still be able to avenge yourselves with your own hands, and I, as before, so now, will begin first.” With these words Vespasian consoled his soldiers.

The inhabitants of Gamala themselves marveled at their victory: those who had been under siege drove off the besiegers. They understood that they no longer had hope of a peaceful agreement, that they could not flee, and that there was a lack of food; they grieved greatly, restored the ruined walls, and strengthened what remained of the fortifications.

The Romans rested for one day and again began to build an embankment. Seeing this, many of the townspeople fled from the city through ravines and underground passages. Those of them who remained in the city grew weak. Only those who could fight took all the food for themselves.

On the twenty-second day of the month Hyperberetaios, that is, October, three soldiers from the Fifteenth Legion came at night before dawn and removed three stones from the foundation of the tower. Because of deep sleep the guards did not hear or see this, for the night was moonless, and there was a storm with thunder.

And when these Roman soldiers withdrew, the tower fell suddenly, and the guards with it, and the rest of the townspeople were terrified and fled, and the Romans, entering the city, killed many of them.

Joseph and Chares saw the fall of the tower and the movement of the soldiers, and were filled with fear, and died; indeed, great fear contributed to their death. The Romans, remembering their previous defeat, did not take the city that time.
