And he cast down many bronze idols for the needs of the aforementioned church; much marble and psephidia, that is, crushed marble, and marble columns from many churches and buildings he took for the construction of that church.

He also removed a bronze statue that stood in the senate; it depicted a bishop holding a staff entwined by a snake.

Having removed it, they set it up in the imperial courtyard. The emperor, coming there and finding himself near the statue, put his finger into the mouth of the bronze snake. But in that snake there was a live snake, and it bit him, and he barely recovered, having taken much against this poison.

And the statue of Solomon stood in the so-called imperial place in the basilica; he removed it, and, having made an image of himself, laid it in the foundation of the New Church, as though making himself a sacrifice to God the Word.

He set out again on campaign against Melitene, waged many battles with the Hagarenes, took many captives, and returned to Constantinople.

And from the storm of life the sacred Ignatius fled, and reached good and calm havens, departing to the Lord. The body of this patriarch was placed in the monastery in the Zaturian settlement, which he himself had founded.
