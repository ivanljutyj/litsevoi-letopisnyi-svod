And Julian commanded the Jews to build a temple in Jerusalem, and when the Jews had gathered, Julian sent with them a prince, and many servants, and a builder; and he ordered round lamps and silver chains to be forged and sent.

They came to the place and began to dig and to heap up earth, and there were many tens of thousands of them, and strong winds and whirlwinds rose against those assembled, and they were scattered by the winds and whirlwinds in different directions.

And when they began to work again, fire came out of the excavated foundation and burned very many of them.

That same night there appeared in the sky the image of the Cross, brightly illuminating all that land. Seeing this image, the Jews understood that God was angry with them, and they fled, put to shame, to their own homes.

When the hard-hearted Julian learned of this, he hardened his heart like Pharaoh. Eutychius, the Cappadocian chronicler, was a commander and governor in his province, over the Armenians; he too was then in Julian’s army and wrote the following. Julian came on the 15th day into the Persian land, and he crossed the Euphrates, and overcame the Persians, and captured everything as far as the city called Ctesiphon, where the Persian king sat.

And the king fled into the land of Persarmenia; and Julian planned with his soldiers to go against Babylon, and on the next day they set out to capture it.

And when Julian slept at night, he saw in a dream a certain man clothed in bright robes enter the tent in which he had settled near Ctesiphon, and strike Julian with a spear. And Julian cried out, and was terrified, and awoke. And Julian’s chamberlain, and the eunuchs, and the court sword-bearers, and all the soldiers who guarded the tent jumped up and entered to Julian with the royal candles, and saw the wound from the spear below his chest. And Julian asked: “What is this village called, where my tent stands?” And they said to him: “Asia.”

And then he cried out: “O sun, you have destroyed Julian!” And Julian scooped up his shed blood with his hands, and threw it into the air, and said: “You have conquered, Galilean, you have conquered.” And after these words the vile soul of Julian departed from his body, at the fifth hour of the night.

And then Julian’s soldiers said: “Let not the Persian warriors learn of the emperor’s death.” And they went to the tent of Jovian, the head of the court guards, a commander by rank, who did not know what had happened. And they came for him, and led him to the royal tent, saying: “King Julian summons you.”
