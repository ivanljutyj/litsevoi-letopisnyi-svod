News of this came to Titus, and he went with his soldiers to the walls.

Simon Niger, however, got ahead of him: learning of the conspiracy, he hurried to the tower, seized Judas and his accomplices, cut them to pieces, and threw those pieces down from the tower.

Josephus, meanwhile, went around the walls and urged the Jews to surrender, but they threw a stone at him and struck his head, and at once he fell and grew weak. The Jews quickly rushed out to seize his body and drag it into the city. They would have dragged it in and subjected it to unheard-of tortures, had not the emperor hurried up with his guards. The soldiers came together and fought, and the Romans barely rescued Josephus and carried him to their camp. Josephus was senseless, as if in sleep.

The rebels saw this and shouted for joy, as though they had killed the man they had long desired. And they spread news of Josephus's death everywhere. When the people learned of it, they were sorrowful. They wept for Josephus, because through his help they had hoped to be freed and to cross over safely to the Romans.

Josephus's mother, however, sat in prison, and she said to the prison guards: "This report came from Jotapata before, and I did not hope to see my son alive."

Secretly Josephus's mother wept and said to her maidservants: "This was the fruit of my blessing. I shall not bury the son on whom I hoped that he would bury me." But the brigands' joy and the mother's grief were not long, for Josephus recovered from the blow, came forward, showed himself to them, and cried out: "The day of your destruction is near, and I will avenge the wound." Hope entered the hearts of the people, and terror seized the rebels when they saw Josephus's face.

There was no time when escape was possible past the guards of Simon Niger. Therefore some broke through by force and leaped down from the walls, some went with weapons against the Romans and then crossed over to them. The deserters died among the Romans from eating, for they came running swollen from hunger. Then sensible people began to give them food with restraint, and little by little they grew accustomed to eating again.

A certain Syrian saw a deserter pulling gold out of his dung. This Syrian told all the soldiers what he had seen. And they began to think that the Jewish deserters came with bellies full of gold. At once, secretly from one another, the soldiers lay in wait on the roads and ripped open the Jews' bellies; and in one night four thousand people perished.
