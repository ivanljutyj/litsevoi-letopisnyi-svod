At this time the people living in Korsun killed their strategos Simeon, the son of Jonah, and in Cappadocia Koron was captured by the Saracens.

The tsar went out to the place called Damian together with Zoe, the daughter of Zaoutzes, and with Zaoutzes himself. But Theophano, the tsar’s wife, was not there; she remained in Blachernae at the holy shrine in prayer.

When the tsar decided to spend the night at Damian, his son Tzanes and others formed a plot with Zaoutzes and planned to kill the tsar at night. When Zoe, who was sleeping with the tsar, heard the conversation and looked out the window, they fell silent. Since she had heard the essence of the evil design, she woke the tsar and told him what was happening. The tsar immediately left in the dromon and sailed to Pegae, leaving everyone there, including Zaoutzes.

In the morning, entering the palace, he expelled John, the drungarius of the guard, and placed Pardas, the son of Nicholas the hetaireiarch, in his place. Since this Nicholas was a friend of the tsar and loyal to him, he made all Zaoutzes’s secrets clear and known to the tsar.

From then on Tsar Leo did not enter Zaoutzes’s chambers; only a magistros named Theodotakes reconciled them with each other.

And Tsarina Theophano died, having reigned twelve years; she showed herself a wonderworker, rose above the sin of feminine jealousy, and, conquering by meekness, surpassed Zoe, spending her whole life in mercy and prayer.

Tsar Leo is wedded to Zaoutzes’s daughter Zoe, and the palace priest named Apios blesses him with her. But the one who blessed them was deposed from the priesthood; she, after reigning one year and eight months, died.

And they found a marble sarcophagus in which to place her body, and inside it an inscription was carved: “Wretched daughter of Babylon.”

Musikon the eunuch and Staurakios were denounced before the tsar, that they took bribes from strategoi and boyars, interceding for them before the tsar’s father.

Once, when Staurakios set out with some letter from a strategos to the tsar’s father, the tsar, standing in the vestibule, saw him, followed him in, and, taking him by the shoulders, led him out, pretending that he wanted to ask him about the strategoi; he led him to a single-leaf door, took the documents from him, and pushed him out, and ordered people who happened to be there to take him out of the palace and tonsure him as a monk.

Hearing of this, Musik fell into despair. And the tsar, entering Zaoutzes’s room when Musik was with him, took him by the shoulders, led him out, and ordered Christophoros the Kitionites to tonsure him at the Stoudios Monastery.
