But when, beyond the words, I heard how a certain power went forth from the elder’s mouth, my words could not withstand the words of this elder, for no man can oppose God. Therefore if any of you wishes to understand as I have understood, let him believe in Christ and follow with me after this elder, in whom God spoke.” Thus the philosopher became a Christian, rejoicing that he had been defeated by the elder.

Among the others came also the divine Paphnutius, an Egyptian man, wonderworker and confessor; Maximian had torn out his right eye. He gave a ruling to the council: to regard as a grave prohibition that clergy should have wives, if they wished, and he proposed that only those holding the rank of bishops should act thus, that is, keep themselves; and the council decreed that a bishop should not have a wife. After long and careful reflection they decided unanimously to command all people of the Orthodox faith to confess the Consubstantiality, that is, to confess the essence of the Father’s nature also for the Son, and they agreed to hold to this—except the impious Arius and certain of his like-minded followers. The divine fathers, 318 in number, accepted for all the confession of the Consubstantiality of the Holy Trinity; Arius and his supporters they anathematized, and the emperor expelled them.

Later certain men, lovers of slander, wrote denunciations against other bishops and gave them to the emperor. He, having received them, put them together into a scroll and sealed it with his ring, and ordered it kept. Then, having admonished them, he ordered them to live in peace and concord, and he brought the slanderous writings and burned them before all, assuring everyone by oath that he had not read what was written in them. “I think,” he said, “that it is not fitting to know the transgressions of priests, nor to make them known to many, lest a pretext for scandal be given, that they continually give themselves over to sin.” And he also said: “If I myself saw a bishop committing fornication, I would cover the lawlessness being committed with the hem of my purple robe, lest it harm the sight of those looking on.”

Likewise he besought with entreaty those who brought denunciations to him, calling all to unity and to establish unity of faith and unanimity in all disputed matters. And having thus instructed all, and rewarded the priests with many honors and gifts, he asked all to return—each to his own flock.

The first council of the assembled holy fathers, 318 in number, was in Nicaea; at that time the Great Emperor Constantine had reigned twenty years.
