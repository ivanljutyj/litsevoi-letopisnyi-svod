On the Holy Third Ecumenical Council.
From the second council to the third, 50 years.

The third holy ecumenical council was in Ephesus in the 25th year of the reign of Theodosius the Younger; 200 holy fathers assembled.

When Theodosius the Small, son of Arcadius, reigned, as has already been said, there gathered Cyril of Alexandria, and the representative in that country of Celestine of Rome, and Juvenal of Jerusalem, and the monk Auxentius; and they gathered against Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople. This Nestorius came to Ephesus, and he said that Holy Mary is not the Mother of God but the Mother of Christ, and he told fables about two sons in the one Christ God, and two essences, one of which is God the Word, begotten of the Father, and the other a man born of Mary, joined by a certain bond to God the Word and called by the same name of Son.

And since this Nestorius was summoned to the council, and could not come and object to the council, they deposed him, because he did not wish to assent to the earlier orthodox decrees. And Nestorius went astray in his vain words, and did not consider within himself that “the light did not enter the mother’s womb without flesh, nor did flesh come forth from the womb without the bodiless essence and soul.” And Nestorius stopped his ears and his mind, so as not to hear the word of the Lord who said: “Those in the tombs will hear the voice of the Son of God and will live.” And there are also the words: “Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth will arise.” And it is also written: “Behold, I will open your graves and bring you out of your graves.” But the heretics strayed from the path of life and turned the sweetness of faith into bitterness for their souls, and disturbed and wounded the purity of faith and the faithful mind.

Tsar Theodosius grieved greatly and lamented, and sat on sackcloth and ashes in his bedchamber. But the merciful God does not wish that any of those firm in faith should perish, and God wishes to grant consolation. And the council anathematized Nestorius. The council clearly decreed the one Christ our God as a distinct hypostasis and two natures, confessing that Christ is God and man, and that Holy Mary is truly and without falsehood the Mother of God.

John, bishop of Antioch, came after Nestorius’s deposition and was sorrowful, because Nestorius had been deposed without him, and he gathered with him the bishops who had come, among whom were Theodoret, bishop of Cyrrhus, and Ibas, bishop of Edessa, and they proclaimed the deposition of Saint Cyril and Memnon, bishop of Edessa, since they had held the council not according to church rule, and they reviled those who were with Cyril, because what had happened came from envy and what they had done was lawless.
