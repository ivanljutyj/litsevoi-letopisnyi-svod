They devoured the blood of the slain. And without fear they turned to vices, adopting the custom of women: they curled their hair, dressed in women’s garments, rouged their cheeks, painted their eyes, drenched themselves with fragrant oil, and gave themselves over to much fornication and lawless pleasure, and with one another, as with women, they gave themselves up to lust. These brigands roamed through the city as through a house of fornication, and defiled themselves with every unclean deed. They assumed the appearance of women, yet their hands were in blood. In mockery they mimicked women in their gait, but suddenly they proved to be murderers. They drew the sword to test it and killed those who came in their path.

And if anyone could flee Jerusalem from the inner tormentor, John, he fell into the hands of the outer tormentor, Simon, and the way was completely cut off for those who wished to flee to the Romans.

In the city the army rose against John. All the Idumeans who were in the army rose against the tormentor, hating him for his cruelty. The Idumeans engaged the Zealots and killed many of them, and the rest fled to the royal palace, and from there they were driven out, and they fled to the temple. The Idumeans plundered John’s wealth, for in that house was the storehouse of his bloody treasures. And those Zealots who had been scattered throughout the city then gathered and came to their own in the temple.

And John prepared to go with the Zealots against the people and against the Idumeans. But the people and the Idumeans feared the uprising of the Zealots and their desperation, and held a council with the chief priests, at which they decided how to protect themselves from the Zealots, lest they attack suddenly, slaughter the people, and set the city on fire. But God allowed their decision to be for evil, and that what they sought for salvation should turn to destruction. And desiring John’s downfall, the inhabitants of Jerusalem planned to send to Simon with a petition that he enter the city and save them. They did not know that they were inviting a cruel tormentor to their own destruction.

And the inhabitants of Jerusalem finished their council and sent Matthew the high priest to the one whom they had previously feared. Simon listened to them with pride and entered as a lord to deliver the city from the Zealots, and the people greeted him as a savior and protector. But Simon began to care not for the people but for himself, for how he might rule alone, and he counted equally as enemies both those who called him and those against whom he was called.

But John and the Zealots, shut in on every side so that they lacked necessities, despaired, and they stood on the covered passages and walls of the temple and fought against Simon, and killed many of the people, since they struck from above.
