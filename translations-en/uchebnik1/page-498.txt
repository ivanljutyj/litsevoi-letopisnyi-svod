For the King sits enthroned, and he commanded his attendants and armour-bearers to bind a certain man who had turned aside from Him and to lay bonds upon him; but he not only rejected these, but also shattered the bonds—yet this man is not an armour-bearer, for he has received insult and does not command. But it is more clearly known that the didaskalos, that is, the teacher, assists what is taking place, while the King and Lord Himself arranges all, both the pious and the insolent.

The 34th Reign, of Constantine Gnoiutezny, that is, Dung-named, son of Leo, who reigned in the city of Constantine

After Leo, Constantine reigned, nicknamed Gnoiutezoimenity, that is, Dung-named, in Greek Copronymus, son of that Leo, and he reigned 34 years. From his father the son was—as from a wicked lion a many-formed leopard, and from the seed of the asp a wicked flying serpent, and from the tribe of Dan an antichrist. This impious Constantine, having received the kingdom, immediately fell away from God and the Mother of God and from all His saints, and therefore rejoiced and celebrated all the more in sorcery, fornication, demonic invocation, and other evil machinations; and he was an excellent instrument and a very convenient tool of his father and teacher the devil. For this Constantine came to such madness and pernicious folly that he even decreed by law to all people that no one should speak at all of the holy disciples of the Lord, nor care for relics that are found, nor ask prayers from the saints. “They can help in nothing,” he said.

And to this the sinful and most vile man added more, saying that Mary also should not be invoked in prayers, for she cannot help anyone; and again he did not permit her to be called the Mother of God. And he took a potsherd in his hands, full of gold, and showed it to all, and asked them: “What is it worthy of?” They said: “It is worthy of much.” And, having poured out the gold, he again asked about the potsherd: “What is it worthy of?” And they said to him: “Nothing.” The king said: “So too is Mary.” The godless one did not want to call her the Mother of God, and said further: “While she had Christ in herself, she was honored; but having given birth to Him, she has nothing more than other women.” Flee after such bold blasphemy, you Saracen-believer and Jewish-minded one! He was not a Christian, but a Paulician or, to say more truly, a servant of filth and idols, a worker for demons, and a murderer of men. The most pure Mother of God and all the saints, the vile and godless one, he turned away from, but he believed in Aphrodite and Dionysus, honored by the pagans, a new Julian, and he slaughtered people, offering sacrifices to these pagan gods.
