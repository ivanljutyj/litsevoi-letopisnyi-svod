The same Tiberius sent the generals Maurice and Narses with soldiers against the Persians. They returned with a great victory and much plunder. The emperor received Maurice with honor and magnificence and made him his son-in-law, marrying him to his daughter, whose name was Constantina; Tiberius also married Germanus, a man of patrician rank who had been a general in Africa, to another of his daughters, whose name was Charito; and he made both sons-in-law caesars.

The same Tiberius ate a fruit brought to him from an early sycamore, that is, a fig tree, a wondrous one, which had been poisoned. As he was dying, Tiberius named his general Maurice emperor in his place. In the time of this Tiberius were John the Faster, Florus, son of the father Florus and the mother Euphemia, Gregory, Pope of Rome, and Eutychius the archbishop.

The Nineteenth Reign, of Maurice, Who Reigned in Constantine's City

After Tiberius, his son-in-law Maurice reigned, who came from the land of Cappadocia; and he reigned twenty years. This Maurice built the Church of the Forty Martyrs in Constantine's City.

In the time of this emperor a woman gave birth in Constantine's City to a child without eyes and without hands; at the loins, that is, the thighs, it had a fish's tail, grown on in the manner of a shell-skinned fish. Also a dog was born with six legs, having a head in the likeness of a lion, and it was exceedingly huge.

Also in Thrace two children were born: one with four legs, and the other with two heads. Some chroniclers say that this does not portend good for those cities in which such a thing is suddenly born.

There appeared in the river Nile, when the sun had risen, two human-shaped creatures, male and female, which are called sirins, that is, vily, or rusalki, sweet-voiced, killing all. Their form was: from head to navel a human, the rest bird-like. The male was a wondrous red-breasted one; the female's face and hair were red, that is, fair, likewise the male's; her breasts were without hair, and the hair was even more red. The people with the eparch marveled and cast incantations upon those spouses so that they would not pass out of sight until all had seen that most wondrous sight. And until the ninth hour all the people marveled, seeing those animals, until they again entered the river. When these had submerged, a crocodile, surfacing and leaping out, ate many people. The crocodile is a great beast and at the same time a fish from head even to tail, and in form its back is like black stone, its belly white, it has four legs, its tail is great and sharp. Its back is a single bone to the end, sharpened like thorns, like saw-toothed ends, and it strikes those at whom it is angry, producing many sores and inflicting wounds.
