And another city was in Bithynia, lawless and full of much impurity; and lawlessness was committed in it. In it was a certain deacon, a cruel destroyer, who had received the humble rank, that is the priesthood, and went and took the tonsure, and clothed himself in black robes, and became a monk, that is a black-robed man; yet he did not abandon his evil deeds of fornication.

It happened that the bishop of that city died, and the angel of the Lord appeared to a certain holy man and said: “Go and persuade the people in the city to appoint as bishop that deacon from among the people.” That man went and did what was commanded him.

And when that deacon, chosen by God, had been appointed bishop, he began in his mind to exalt himself and boast. And the angel of the Lord came to him and said: “Do you think, wretched man, that you were truly worthy of the holy office and episcopate? No, but you were chosen because this city was worthy of such a bad bishop.” Therefore, when you see someone unworthy—whether an evil king, or prince, or bishop—do not marvel at it, nor test God’s Providence, but understand and believe that according to our lawlessness we are given over to such tormentors; yet even then we do not renounce our evil.

The 21st Reign, of Heraclius, Who Reigned in Constantinople

After Phocas, Heraclius reigned 30 years. Patriarch Sergius and the boyars with all the people proclaimed him emperor in the Great Church. They received Heraclius from the church, and praised him, and applauded, and glorified him, and led him into the imperial palace. He ruled the people, and with himself he also chose Crispus the eparch, Phocas’s son-in-law.

When some time had passed, the emperor made Crispus a commander and sent him to Cappadocia. Crispus went there and began to slander and plot against Heraclius.

When much time had passed and Crispus came to Constantinople, the emperor said to him before his boyars: “Was this not Phocas’s son-in-law? Wretched man, how do you intend to be a friend to me?”

And then Heraclius placed Crispus as a cleric, that is a priest, in one of the monasteries of that country, and the emperor ordered Crispus to live there, and he soon met the end of his life.

Then Chosroes, the Persian king, sent against the Greeks a commander called Sait with great force, that is an army, and destroyed the whole Eastern land. Sait came to Constantinople and stayed a long time in Chalcedon. And Heraclius, with peaceful intent, summoned Sait and spoke with him about peace.
