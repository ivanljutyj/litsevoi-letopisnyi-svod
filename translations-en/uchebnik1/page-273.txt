The wise Solomon says: “Son, beware of making many books; and do not hasten with your lips, let not your heart hasten to bring forth a word before God, for God is in heaven above, and He is on earth below. Therefore your words shall be few. For many words multiply vanity. And do not be overly righteous, for there is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness, and do not be excessively wise, lest at some time you become wicked.” But he struck through these holy instructions and did not heed the understanding of Christopher in his writings. And the God-like Paul, who was the strongest of all in composing words and surpassed all in understanding, committed to writing not many small epistles, although he could have expressed much more of what he left unsaid: for he even reached the third heaven in heavenly revelations, was caught up to the very God-like paradise, and was deemed worthy to hear the voices that are there. Like him, the other disciples of Christ, the 12 apostles and the 70 disciples, and a multitude of others besides these, were not unskilled in speech, yet of all of them only two left remembrances of the Lord’s life—Matthew and John—since they were compelled to preserve the Word in writing, as Mark and Luke did later. Therefore the great Maximus also said: “He who writes words writes them either for his own remembrance, or for the benefit of others, or for both, or to the harm of someone, or for an example, or out of necessity.”

The 22nd Reign, of Antoninus, who was enthroned in Rome in the year 5704 from Adam (the 204th year from Christ)

After Severus, Antoninus Caracalla reigned 6 years and two months. He stabbed his brother in his mother’s bosom. Heretics. In his time, as Clement says, Origen left various writings, called in Hebrew Hexapla, that is, the Sixfold.

A certain Serapion foretold that Antoninus would soon die and Macrinus would succeed him, and he pointed at him with his finger while in the assembly of the nobles. But Antoninus, out of rage or by chance, did not make out Macrinus’s face and ordered another man standing nearby to be killed. Macrinus, however, hastened to forestall Antoninus. When Antoninus dismounted from his horse at a hunt, having sent the centurion away, Macrinus killed him with a weapon, saying to him: “As you stabbed your brother in your mother’s bosom, so I do to you.”

The 23rd Reign, of Macrinus, who was enthroned in Rome in the year 5711 from Adam (the 211th year from Christ)

After Antoninus Caracalla, Macrinus reigned one year and two months. A certain Eutychian sheltered Avitus, the illegitimate son of Antoninus, placed a crown on him, and proclaimed him autocrat. And having given gold to the soldiers, he rose up and entered battle with Macrinus. And Macrinus was defeated, fled, and was killed with his son.
