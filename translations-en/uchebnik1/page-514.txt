On Peter the Stammerer

Then Peter the Stammerer occupied the Roman see. This lawless man rejected the Orthodox faith and, having sown many evil heresies, disrupted church unity throughout Italy.

He permitted Christians to take up to seven wives and did not consider it a sin to have concubines; he introduced organ music into worship, ordered beards to be trimmed, and permitted the eating of what had previously been considered unclean and was forbidden—he even allowed them to eat with dogs from the same dish. Many other vile things did this lawless man introduce, and because of these apostasies the Latins were deprived of church communion and given over to anathema.

On Vretania and the Alemannic Prince

The pope repeatedly set kings from among the Latins in Rome, but they did not wish to obey him.

Having consulted with his intimates, the pope sends an embassy to Vretania. The inhabitants of the island of Vretania had accepted holy baptism as early as under Constantine the Great, but they had no books in their native language and used books in Latin, while always being at enmity with the Romans.

The pope sent an embassy to Vretania to the Alemannic prince, concluded peace with him, confirmed by many oaths, so that the Alemanni would always be in accord with the Romans, and promised the ruler of Vretania the royal title. By this promise of his and by Latin books sent as gifts, the pope persuaded the Alemannic prince together with his subjects to accept the Latin heresy.

And he summoned the Alemannic prince with all his military commanders to Rome, and made him king of Vretania, issuing a law stating that from now on the Roman pope must be installed by the Latins, and the king of Vretania by the Alemanni, and not by the Tsar of Constantinople, that is, the Greek Tsar.

In the great cities, instead of the four patriarchs, the pope appointed his own four patriarchs, called cardinals. And so he completely separated himself from the Orthodox faith.
