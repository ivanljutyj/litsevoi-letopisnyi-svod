The Facial Chronicle — A Source of Truth
The Facial Chronicle was created in the 16th century by order of the Russian Tsar Ivan the Terrible for the education of the tsar's children. The work on compiling this Chronicle was led by the most educated man of his time — Saint Macarius, Metropolitan of Moscow and All Rus. The best scribes and icon painters of the capital worked on compiling the Chronicle.

What they accomplished: a collection of all reliably known sources from Holy Scripture (the text of the Septuagint) to the history of Alexander the Great and the works of Josephus Flavius — the entire written history of humanity from the creation of the world to the 16th century inclusive. All times and all peoples who had writing are reflected in dozens of books of this collection. No civilization of humanity has created a similar chronicle collection adorned with a huge number of highly artistic illustrations: neither Europe, nor Asia, nor America or Africa.

The fate of the Russian tsar himself and his children was tragic. The Facial Chronicle was not useful to the tsareviches. After reading the Facial Chronicle, part of which is devoted to the period of the Terrible, it becomes clear why.

Over the following hundreds of years, official historiography emerged, often opportunistic and politically biased, and therefore reliable chronicle sources were doomed to destruction or correction, that is, falsification. The Facial Chronicle survived these centuries thanks to the fact that after the death of Ivan the Terrible, during the period of troubles and timelessness, this folio became a coveted object for "enlightened" bibliophiles. The most influential nobles of their time — Golitsyn, Sheremetev, Khilkov, and others — dragged its folios off to their libraries. After all, even then high-ranking collectors understood that such a folio with sixteen thousand miniatures was priceless. Thus the Chronicle survived until the revolution and was gathered with difficulty in several boxes and repositories.

Already in our days, through the efforts of enthusiasts, the scattered books and sheets were collected together from various repositories. And the revived Society of Lovers of Ancient Writing has made this masterpiece accessible to all. As a historical source that has no analogues, it can now be freely obtained by many major educational institutions of the world, national libraries of various countries and, of course, our compatriots for raising children on this treasure of experience and wisdom of millennia.

In such a surprising way, the work that was done for the tsar's children five hundred years ago has come down to our children, dear contemporaries, and we congratulate you on this from the bottom of our hearts!
