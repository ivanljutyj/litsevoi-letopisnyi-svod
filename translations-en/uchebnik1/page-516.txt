From among the courtiers around her, choosing chamberlain-eunuchs, traitors ready to carry out her cunning and inhuman design, she forms with them a conspiracy—alas!—against her beloved son.

The tsar slept, but this sleep already foretold to him the outer darkness that would forever hide the sunlight; his fate was already predetermined. Like ravens ready to peck out an eye, the villains burst in and, piercing his pupils, darkened the light to his eyes, and drops of blood sprinkled his garment. The tsar, tormented—alas!—lay trying to see and begging for mercy. The fierce wolves extinguished his eyes precisely where he had come into the world and first seen the dawn. The chamber in which such evil was accomplished is called “Porphyry.” Who and when poured out such grievous wrath and abomination upon a son? Though the tsar was wicked and himself deserving of much evil, it was not from her that he ought to have suffered. O passion for power! Neither the lynx nor the tiger is so angry at its young, nor do toothy dogs rage so fiercely at their puppies; but I have heard it said that only the sea fish “phina,” alone of all, attacks its offspring and devours them in the darkness of the deep—and even that from its Scythian foolishness. Of the others, neither those living in water nor those walking on dry land, none does such a thing. Then the light-bearing Sun extinguished the light, the light-giving eye, the Moon, closed its eyelids, deep darkness spread over many days, and the elements themselves wept over the sufferings caused by soulless men. She who dared such a thing became autocratic ruler, declaring in writing that this had been done to Tsar Constantine without her knowledge. He died some time later, having lived ten years after the Seventh Ecumenical Council.

Reign 37: Irene, who reigned in Constantinople

After the death of Constantine, his mother Irene reigned autocratically for 5 years.

At that time, in a prison under guard, there were two young sons of the blasphemer Copronymus. They were persuaded to flee and seek refuge in the Great Church, so that later one of them might be proclaimed tsar.

Then by Irene’s command, under the guise of rendering them royal honors, before a great gathering of people they were persuaded to come out of the church. No one interceded for them, and then the empress ordered them seized and exiled to Athens.
