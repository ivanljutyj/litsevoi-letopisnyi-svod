They did not give them room to retreat. Then they broke their swords too, and began to fight with their hands, and every weapon that could be found served the one who found it. The Jews overpowered the Roman soldiers, since they had fought from the ninth hour of the night until the seventh hour of the day, and fought all together, understanding the danger of the city being completely taken. The Romans were not all there, but a small part of the army, for not all had entered. And the troops ceased fighting. The Romans were content even with having occupied the tower of Antonia.

On the Courage of Julian the Centurion

There was a certain Julian, a centurion from Bithynia, a famous man, with whom I became acquainted at that time. He surpassed all in strength, in experience of arms, in bodily power, and in qualities of soul. And Julian saw the Romans retreating, and he himself, standing near Titus in the tower of Antonia, asked leave of him and mounted a horse. The Jews were already prevailing, but this Julian alone rode at them and defeated them, and drove them to the corner of the inner sanctuary. And all the Jews fled together, thinking that the power in him was not human. He rode among them, cut them down, and overtook them; even the emperor marveled at him, and all others were terrified. But afterward this Julian too was overtaken by fate, which no mortal can escape.

When his horse was stabbed, this Julian fought on foot as if he were riding on horseback, like a spirit. And he stepped on many sharp fragments, and went along the stone pavement, and slipped, and fell on his back, and his weapons clanged. And immediately those who had fled from him turned upon him. The Romans in the tower of Antonia cried out in fear for him. The Jews surrounded him all together, pierced him with spears, hacked him with axes, pelted him with stones, beat him with sticks, and by this alone terrified all the Romans. Julian wanted many times to rise, but was surrounded and could not. However, he stabbed many of those who attacked him. And they could not kill him quickly, because he was protected by a helmet on his head and armor over his whole body. Only when his limbs had been cut off, and no one helped him, did he fall. And the emperor grieved greatly for him, seeing such a valiant man cut down before his eyes. The remoteness of the place where Titus was did not allow him to avenge Julian on the Jews at that same time. This centurion left scarcely any of the enemies surrounding him unharmed, and then met a cruel death. With great difficulty the Jews cut off his head. And Julian became very famous not only among the Romans but also among foreign peoples.
