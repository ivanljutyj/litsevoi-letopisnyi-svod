The Paulicians also blaspheme against the divine holy Communion of the precious Body and divine Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, saying that the Lord gave to the apostles His voices, that is, utterances, when He said: “Come, eat, and drink,” and not bread and wine. They also blaspheme against the precious Cross, saying that the Cross is Christ, and that it is not proper to bow to the Cross, for it is an accursed ersan, that is, a creature. They reject the prophets and the other saints, and especially Saint Peter, the great and first apostle, and they greatly blaspheme against them and turn away from them, and say that not one of them is among the saved. The Catholic, that is, universal, Church they will say to us, out of their fear, that they acknowledge, but in saying this they mean their own assemblies. Baptism they regard as the hearing of the Gospel word, for the Lord said: “I am the living water.” They say all this and more than this when they are discovered and seized, and they falter. But one must with understanding and one by one examine all these questions with them, for they carry falsehood on their tongue; by their custom they always lie, especially when they are compelled, and they say what they are ordered or told, and remain guiltless before themselves and their faith, for Mani taught them to speak thus. “For I am not merciless,” said Mani, “as Christ, who said: ‘Whoever denies Me before men, I will also deny him.’ I say: ‘Those who deny me before men and seek salvation for themselves by lying, I receive with joy as not denying me, for before me they are guiltless by confession.’” The Paulicians also reject presbyters and others who are priests. They have their own priests, and these priests are in unity with all the rest; they call them scribes, and they differ from all neither in morals, nor in demands, nor in all other worldly order. All that is in the Gospel and in the Apostle they interpret perversely, not as we do; their Gospel and Apostle are fitted, that is, adapted, to their heresy. As was said, the writing and words are the same as ours and unchanged, but they pervert the meaning. They bow to the Gospel among us, when occasion arises, not to the Cross but to the books, saying: “These are Christ’s words, and therefore we bow.” When one of them falls into sickness and disease, they lay the book on him, and he receives health, “and again, having broken it and cast it into the fire, they burn it or tear it up.” Some of the Paulicians also have their children baptized by our presbyters, when these are captured by the Paulicians and are among them. Others of them enter our church and intercept the Orthodox who have come to the Divine mysteries, and deceive the inexperienced still more by such cunning and flattery, receiving them with all untiring zeal, and without shame continually do foul things against human nature. “And others of them, they say, are not permitted to go to their parents.”
