Seleucus also founded another city in Syria and named it Laodicea, after his daughter Laodice; before that it was a village called Maudan. Having sacrificed to Zeus, Seleucus also founded yet another city and named it Apamia after his daughter Apama; before that it was a village called Pharnacin.

This Seleucus was by birth from Pella, a Macedonian city. He also founded other cities in various domains and in the Persian lands, seventy-five in number, as Pausanias wrote of this. Seleucus named cities after his own name and the names of his children. Pausanias wrote that Seleucus named the city of Antioch after his father’s name, since Seleucus’s father was Antiochus. But no one, when founding a city, names it after a dead man, for that is an insult; rather they name it after a living one. And so Seleucus named the city of Antioch for his son Antiochus. And this wise Pausanias wrote much else with cunning.

The same Seleucus was also in Heracleides, which is now called Daphne, and planted a cypress. From Heracles it was named Heracleides; this city was founded by the forest, by the temple of Athena. And there was an altar of Apollo, called Daphnian, in the middle of the forest; there Raclis first devised his fierce cunning. And this Seleucus died at the Hellespont, at the age of seventy-two, and was buried in Seleucia of Syria.

Second reign in Syria

Then reigned Antiochus, son of Seleucus, nicknamed Soter, for twenty years; he was called the Savior. This Antiochus loved his stepmother, the daughter of Demetrius, and took her as his wife, and with her begot two sons: Seleucus, who died young, and Antiochus, who was called Theos. And Antiochus, son of Seleucus, died.

Third reign in Syria. After this reigned Antiochus Theos, born out of wedlock, and he reigned fifteen years. Fourth reign in Syria. Then reigned Seleucus, son of Antiochus Theos, also called Callinicus, son of Berenice, and he reigned twenty-four years. Fifth reign in Syria. Then reigned Alexander Nicator for twenty-four years. Sixth reign in Syria. Then, after Alexander, reigned Seleucus the Father-loving.

Seventh reign in Syria. After Seleucus reigned Demetrius from Seleucia. Eighth reign in Syria. After Demetrius reigned Alexander Balas. Ninth reign in Syria. After Alexander reigned Demetrius, also called Nicator. Tenth reign in Syria. After Demetrius reigned Antiochus.
