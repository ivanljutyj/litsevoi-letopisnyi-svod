And Saint Dometius sent him this answer: “I have given my soul into God’s hands, and I have shut my body in this cave for many years; but I cannot drive away the people who come to me in faith.”

And Julian ordered the righteous man’s exit from the cave to be blocked, and so Saint Dometius died. And Julian took up arms against the Persians, overcame them, and wished to go as far as Babylon.

But the Persian king Slunarsakius was frightened and fled into the land of Persarmenia, and to deceive the Romans he sent two of his nobles to them, ordering their noses cut off, and so sent them to Julian to deceive him and keep him from pursuing the Persians.

These two Persians, with their noses cut off, came as though they had fled from the Persian king, who had allegedly punished them, and Julian was deceived by them.

These two Persians swore that they were telling the truth, and Julian listened to them and went after them with his soldiers. And the Persians led the Romans into a waterless desert for 150 leagues, luring them on. On the 25th day of Daisios, that is, of the month of July, the Romans found there the ruins of the old walls of a city called Vuvim, and another village with houses in it, also deserted, and the village was called Asia.

There King Julian and his soldiers made camp. While still in Persia, Julian sent a demon to the west so that it might bring him news quickly. But the demon was detained on the way and could not move farther, because certain monks stood with their hands raised to Heaven and prayed to God for 10 days. So the demon could not pass farther and returned again to Julian. Julian asked the demon: “Why were you delayed?” And the demon answered him: “I was delayed and did not accomplish the task entrusted to me by you, and I returned because I was stopped on the way by the monk Poplius, for I waited for him to stop praying, but he prayed without ceasing for 10 days and did not let me fulfill your will.”

Hearing this, Julian was very angry, began to threaten, and said: “When I return, I will destroy all the monks.” Learning of this, one of Julian’s nobles was struck with amazement and turned back, and gave all his property to the poor.

In those places where the soldiers had come there was no food for them, and for the horses neither grass nor water, for it was a desert. All the Roman soldiers understood that the king had been deceived and had led them into the desert, and they were in great confusion.
