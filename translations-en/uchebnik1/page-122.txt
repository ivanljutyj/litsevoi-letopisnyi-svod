One sound means it is time to wake, another that it is time to dine, and a third that it is time to sleep. And without the sound of the trumpet they do nothing. When the Roman army leaves camp, a trumpet sounds, producing a high note. And immediately the soldiers rise and begin to form up, and no one remains idle. And again many trumpets sound, and the soldiers load onto donkeys and mules all that is carried.

And a third time they sound the trumpets as a sign that it is time to leave camp. Then no one may delay for any reason or has the right to leave his place in the formation. And a herald on the right side of the commander stands in a high place and in the soldiers’ native language cries three times: “Are you ready for battle?” And the soldiers exclaim three times, very fiercely and briskly: “Ready!” And then they are full of martial spirit. And with this cry the Roman soldiers mount their horses and raise their right hands, and then they set off. And they go quietly, in strict order, each in his place. The place where the camp was is burned, so that it may not become a refuge for enemies.

The Roman foot soldiers are protected by armor and helmets. On the left they have a very long sabre, and on the right a sword a span wide. And those chosen foot soldiers who are around the commander carry throwing spears and round shields. And the other soldiers carry spears, and long shields, and baskets, and saws, and scythes, and axes, and straps, and sickles, and iron chains, so that a foot soldier bears a weight almost equal to that of a mule.

The Roman horsemen on the right side wear a long sabre, and carry a long spear in the hand; they also have a shield long enough to reach the horse’s ribs, and a quiver with three throwing spears having large iron tips. The helmets and armor of the horsemen are the same as those of the foot soldiers. And all have the same weapons, both the nobles and the simple soldiers. And it is not always one regiment that goes in front, but they take turns.

Without deliberation the Romans do not begin a battle, and therefore they never make mistakes, and if they do make a mistake, they quickly correct it. If they win because of luck and not by design, they consider it not a victory but a defeat. If, acting by design, they make an error, they consider that error a victory, because they were not defeated by chance but were acting by design. If someone accidentally, not by design, wins a victory, they do not consider it a cause for pride. And if someone meets death but did everything as planned, they are proud of this and comforted that he did not die thoughtlessly.

The Romans strengthen by exercises with weapons not only their bodies but also their souls. And their law is very strict. Not only the one who fled from the field of battle do they condemn to death, but also the one who did not arrive in time for the battle.
