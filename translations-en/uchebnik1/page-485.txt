This king was troubled, and he assembled a great host against those water-going robbers, that is, the shipborne ones. And the Greek naval forces fought with them, and those robbers, who attacked with fury and in their malice seemed to foam, were driven away from there, and the captured goods were recovered intact.

But the fierce beasts did not stop, and again they formed battle and began to wage war, and they put to flight the commanders of the Greek ships, and took the famous city of Carthage, and enslaved all the Libyans, and “as it were plucked out golden hairs.”

But the naval soldiers and all the commanders could not endure the shame of their bitter defeat, and then they undertook to heal evil with another great evil; they put in at the great island of Crete, and took counsel with one another, and “they vomit out the burden of their audacity.”

The rebels chose for themselves one fellow apostate, called by two names, Tiberius Apsimar, and together with him they besieged Constantine’s city.

And having found traitors, the rebels took the reigning city. Then King Leontius was led out before all, and his nose was cut off, and he was shut in prison, inescapable and bitter.

Reign 28, of Apsimar Tiberius, who reigned in Constantine’s city

After Leontius, Apsimar Tiberius reigned 7 years. He was double-named, that is, he had a double name, like Jacob, who is also Israel, like Jethro, who is also Reuel, like Simon, who is also Peter, like Thomas, who is also Didymus, that is, the Twin, like Thaddaeus, who is also Lebbaeus. When Apsimar ruled the Greek realm, frequent winds blew, gently breathing. Only one thing terrified him: that the scepter of kingship might again pass to Justinian, and that he who had lost it before, and whose nose had also been cut off, might not return to the crown. Apsimar thought of Justinian as of an amphibious beast and feared this man, and listened to many interpretations and false prophecies of stargazers.

Apsimar learned that Justinian had fled from Cherson to the Khazar prince, the kagan, and had taken his sister Theodora as wife. And then Apsimar began to plot against Justinian, and sent a request to the fierce barbarian kagan, and promised him a multitude of possessions if the kagan would hand Justinian over to him in chains, like a slave, or cut off his head and send it to him.
