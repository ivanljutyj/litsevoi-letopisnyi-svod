The thrice-accursed and cursed Persians became proud three times over because of their victory in the war, and having captured the Roman king Constantine and bound him in heavy fetters, they ordered a day of sacrifice to their gods to be arranged, so that they might cut him down with the sword on the idolatrous altar. O wonder of God’s patience and His waiting! If weeping takes up its lodging in the evening, joy comes in the morning.

Some of the Romans who had fled from the Persians hid in the mountains. And certain Persians who had gone up the mountain for firewood discovered the Romans hiding there. The Romans quickly leaped out of the mountain forest, surrounded them, and caught them. Seeing this, the Persian soldiers grew numb with fear, and when the Romans questioned them, wishing to learn what was known about the fate of the blessed King Constantine, they begged and fell at their knees, pleading for their lives, saying that they were slaves of the Persians, and promising never until death to do any harm to Roman soldiers. And they said: “We have no part in their transgressions; do not kill us, O Romans, have mercy on us. We will tell you the whole truth for your benefit.” Then the Roman soldiers swore to them, promising to preserve their lives if they told the truth. They said: “Hear from us, Roman men: our king and princes have ordered a solemn day to be held, so that tomorrow your king Constantine may be slaughtered on the altar. The place where the altar stands is surrounded by a wall. And there is only one entrance to that altar—through the gate. But by custom the Persians are not permitted to serve the gods with weapons, so they leave their weapons outside the wall and enter inside without weapons. And when they praise and thank the gods, then they slaughter those appointed for sacrifice. And tomorrow it will be so. Arrange us so that we may observe carefully; and when they begin to cry out, we will inform you, and then you shall enter, secretly taking into your hands the weapons lying outside, and begin to cut them down and stab them. And so you will save and lead away your king.” And this word pleased the Romans, and the Romans swore to spare them and to repay with good those slaves who had betrayed the Persians. And they sealed letters of peace, so that their comrades might have them after what was promised had been fulfilled.

And they brought in King Constantine like a spotless lamb to be slaughtered, and laid him on the altar, binding his hands and feet with cords. When they had gone around the altar three times with cries, then suddenly the Romans leaped out of ambush with fury, and snatching up the Persians’ weapons lying outside the wall, and bursting into the altar, they began to cut them down and stab them with unprecedented and terrible blows.
