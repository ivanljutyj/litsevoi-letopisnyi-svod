Quarrels also, and seditions, and envy seized Jerusalem—all that it was fitting for you to receive for your wickedness. I, having received a sorrowful command from my father, came to fulfill it and learned that the people were thinking of peace, and I rejoiced and begged you for peace before the battle. But you preferred battle. For many days I spared you, promised safety to deserters and kept my word, pardoned many captives, reluctantly set siege engines against your walls, and restrained soldiers who always thirsted for your blood; though constantly conquering you, I asked for peace like one defeated. When I was near the temple, I forgot the law of war and begged you many times to spare your sanctuary and save the temple for yourselves, and I gave you freedom to go out, and promised to preserve your lives, and pointed out a place for battle if you wished to fight. But you despised my words and set fire to the temple with your own hands, and after this, vile ones, you summon me to negotiations. What will you say? Can you find anywhere such a creation as you have destroyed? Or do you think you are worthy of salvation after the destruction of the temple? You yourselves still stand under arms and at your last breath do not ask for mercy. O weak ones, in whom do you hope? Is your people not dead? Is the temple not destroyed? Is the city not occupied by me? Are your lives not in my hands? I know that you consider the desire for death glorious and brave. Yet I, not disputing your despair, if only you lay down your arms and surrender, will grant you life. As a gentle householder, I execute the incorrigible and pardon the rest.

To these words of Titus the rebels answered: “We cannot surrender to your mercy, since we have sworn never to do this and will not break our oath. Let us leave with our wives and children. Having gone into the desert, we will leave the city to you.” Titus was angered at them because they, though conquered, answered not as befits the defeated, but like victors set their own terms. And Titus ordered this answer to be given to the rebels: “Let none of you desert to the Romans any more or hope for salvation. I will spare no one. Now fight with all the strength you have, devise what you wish, and save yourselves as you can. I will do all that the law of war commands me.”

And Titus ordered the soldiers to set fire to, plunder, and pillage the city. And at once the royal palaces, and Acra, and the assembly building, and Ophla were burned. And the fire reached the palace of Helena. And the streets and houses were burned, full of those who had died of hunger.

On that same day the sons and brothers of King Izates and many of the noble Jews begged the emperor to spare them. But Titus, although he was angry with all, did not change his disposition and received these people, and shut them in prison; and the king’s children he put in chains and sent to Rome as hostages.
