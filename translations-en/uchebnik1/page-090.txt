And again, having taken off their white garments, they go about their affairs and work until evening. And again they return to the refectory. Neither by shouting nor by laughter do they defile this place. The Essenes speak in turn, without interrupting one another. To outside observers, the silence of the community's inner life appears a fearsome mystery. They do nothing without the command of the overseers. Only in two cases may they do something without command: if it is necessary to show mercy and help. But they may give nothing to their relatives without the command of the overseers. They are righteous; they do not let rage show and they restrain anger. The Essenes are defenders of the faith and peacemakers. They do not break their word. Everything they say can be believed more than what is said with an oath. The oath itself is considered by them worse than any sin. If a person can be trusted only when he swears by God, this is condemned and rejected by them.

And they seek out the most ancient books, and read them, and study what happens in foreign lands, and draw wisdom from this, paying attention to what is useful for soul and body. They know herbs, roots, and stones: what is found where and for what ailment it is needed. And the Essenes know how to wage war from these writings. If someone wishes to join them, they do not accept him quickly, but first bid him live in a building outside the gates; they give him an axe, a loincloth, and white clothing, and test him for one year. If the man shows his self-restraint, he is admitted to the inner life of the community and participates in purification by water. After the man has shown his endurance, his character is tested for another two years, and if he proves unworthy, he is expelled, but if worthy, he is received among the brothers. And before acceptance they adjure him with terrible oaths, and he, standing before the doors, makes dreadful vows, calling upon the Living God, and His almighty right hand, and the uncontainable Spirit of God, and the all-seeing seraphim and cherubim, and he calls all the Heavenly powers as witnesses that he will fulfill all that he swears. And he who wishes to join the Essenes swears to the following: to please and honor the Deity; to be just to people; to do no evil to people either by his own will or by command; to hate injustice; to be truthful always, and not only when he swears; to submit to the authorities, for without God's command authority is given to no one; if he himself happens to rule, to do so worthily; not to dress or adorn himself too luxuriously; to love truth with all his soul; to expose liars; to keep his hands from theft and unjust gain, and his soul from bribery; to conceal nothing from his brothers; not to reveal secrets to those of other faiths, even if they torture him to death; not to take part in robbery; to preserve the books and the angelic names. After such vows are made, the one who comes to the Essenes is confirmed.
