Having received many gifts, this false Alexander sailed to the city of Mylae, where everyone also believed him, and all the people escorted him to Rome with honor and gifts, as a king. And those who had seen the real Alexander alive swore that this was truly he. And all the Jews of Rome gathered to see him, while the Jews of Mylae carried him, lifting him up together with his throne, sang joyful songs before him, and spent much to please him.

Caesar, however, knew well what Alexander looked like, since he had stood before him when he was pleading his case against Herod. And Caesar perceived the deceit: his face was different, and his body was coarser than Alexander’s, which exposed the slave in the impostor. And Caesar was greatly enraged, and seized the young man, and said: “I will spare your life if you tell me who taught you to do this.” Then the captured man betrayed the Jew who had prompted him to this deceit, pointing out his resemblance to Alexander. And the young man said: “We have received as many gifts in the cities as Alexander himself did not receive while he was alive.” Caesar listened to him with a smile and assigned him to his rowers. And the Jews of Mylae, because they had carried him in their arms and shown him royal honor, were put to death.

Concerning John the Forerunner

At that time a certain man from among the Jews went about in strange clothing, having attached animal skins to his body in places not covered by his hair, and his face was like a wild man’s. And this man came to the Jews and called them to freedom, and said: “God sent me, that I may show you the lawful path by which you shall escape from many rulers, and no mortal shall rule over you, except the Most High who sent me.” And people heard this and rejoiced.
