The Lord, lover of mankind, changed wrath to mercy and commanded an unusual rain to fall from the clouds. What descended was like furnace dust, that is, ash, and it fell upon the earth and upon the roofs of dwellings, and it lay more than a span deep, and it burned all that had sprouted from the earth. And afterward such dust was barely washed from the earth by rain.

Under this Emperor Leo the Robe of the Mother of God was brought by Galbius and Candidus from Jerusalem to Constantinople. Under this same emperor Daniel the Stylite also struggled in asceticism.

In the reign of Leo the Great there was a great conflagration in Constantinople, such as had never been, and the city burned from sea to sea.

The emperor feared to remain in his palace in the city, and he came to the monastery of Saint Mamas, and shut himself in there, and remained forty-six days, and built there a harbor and a portico, which is called after Leo to this day.

This Leo, in the years of his reign, warred with Gaiseric the Vandal, king of Africa, and there was a terrible naval war. Leo sent many ships against Gaiseric, and sent Basiliscus, brother of Empress Verina, the wife of that same Leo.

This same Basiliscus, having taken gold from Gaiseric, king of Africa, betrayed his ships, his commanders, and all his soldiers, and he alone escaped and was saved in his own ship. But all his sailors and soldiers perished and drowned in the sea.

Among the soldiers of Basiliscus was Kadimonesk, one of the duces, a commander from the great city of Antioch. He strengthened himself and alone entered the army of Gaiseric, and was taken, and was cast into the sea in his military equipment. And Basiliscus returned to Constantinople defeated.

In the reign of Leo, Emperor Anthemius was killed in Rome. Anthemius had a quarrel with his son-in-law, the commander Ricimer, fearing him as a Goth, and Anthemius himself went and fled to the church of Saint Peter for the sake of his devotions.

And Emperor Leo in Constantinople learned this and sent to Rome the patrician Olybrius the Ethiopian, during his consulship, which he had granted him together with Rusticius, and he sent him for this purpose, to reconcile Emperor Anthemius with his son-in-law Ricimer.

Olybrius was one of the Roman nobles, and Leo commanded him and said: “As soon as Emperor Anthemius has made peace with Ricimer, go to Gaiseric the Vandal, king of Africa, as one who has friendship with him, since Gaiseric has Eudocia, the sister of your wife Placidia, as a bride for his son, and persuade him to be a friend to me.”
