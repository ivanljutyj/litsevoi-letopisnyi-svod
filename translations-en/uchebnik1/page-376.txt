Thus the eastern and Egyptian bishops departed from Ephesus and became divided among themselves.

Later, through the tsar's efforts, they were united and came to think alike, and the eastern bishops agreed with the exposition of the Orthodox faith set forth at Ephesus and with the deposition of Nestorius. And they sent a written message to those in Alexandria with Paul, Bishop of Emesa. Nestorius was exiled to one of the oases of Egypt.

On the War with the Persians

Blasius, ruler of the Persian people, came in war against Tsar Theodosius. Theodosius sent against him his nobleman, who held the rank of patrician, and warriors ready for battle and armed.

When this patrician had prepared for battle, Prince Blasius sent envoys to him, saying: “If you have in your army one brave warrior ready to go out alone to single combat against one Persian fighter, then set him to the fight; and if your fighter defeats my warrior, I will immediately make peace with you for 50 years, with payment of tribute. But if yours is defeated, I will take from you 50 centenaria of gold.”

Then Blasius brought out a Persian encased in armor, and the patrician brought out a Goth, a royal nobleman in the rank of comes; and armed, they stood between the troops for the combat.

The Persian was first to rush into the combat behind his shield; the Goth dodged aside and, according to Gothic custom, pierced him, cast him from his horse, and killed him on the spot. When this happened, the Persian king was put to shame. And the ruler of the Persian people made peace with Theodosius, with payment of tribute, according to his word.

How Tsar Theodosius Dined with a Monk

Once Tsar Theodosius went out hunting, and his retinue was near him; but the tsar hid himself from the retinue and by a distant path came to a certain monk who sat in a cell, living apart from the villages, near Constantinople. When the elder saw Theodosius, he did not recognize him, taking him for one of the soldiers.

The tsar, exhausted by the heat and the journey, sat down. The elder moistened some kovrigi, that is, dry flatbreads, and, pouring vinegar and vegetable oil over them, gave them to the tsar. The tsar ate and drank water.

And the tsar said: “Do you know, father, who I am?” The monk said: “God knows you.” And the tsar said: “I am Tsar Theodosius.” And the monk rose and bowed to the tsar. And the tsar said: “Truly blessed are you monks, and you dwell far from the sorrows of this world. Truly I say to you, father, that I was born in the royal palace, but never have I tasted such food and drink.”
