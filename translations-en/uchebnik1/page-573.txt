A certain Dophil, a cubicularius by rank, was sent to Sicily on some commission and carried with him 100 litrai of gold. Having fallen ill, he went to Thessalonica to wash and rest.

A Tripolitan seized him and, learning that he had gold, tortured him for a long time and killed him, although he said he had nothing. For it had been left on the road, and Simeon, an asekretis by rank, passing along that road, took the gold. This Simeon later became a patrikios and head of the asekretis.

Since the Tripolitan intended to destroy the city of Thessalonica, Simeon sent him word: let him take the gold from him and leave the city. And so it was.

And the Saracen army returned back. Himerios, learning of this, rushed after them.

These reached Crete and gave its inhabitants a part of the plunder.

From there they set out for their homeland, leaving Himerios at Limenai with nothing.

There was with the emperor the patrikios Eustathios, deputy of the strategos of the East, that is, deputy of the commander, from the glorious family of the Argyroi. He fought against the Ishmaelites and more than once displayed bravery; and there was Andronikos, who was of the Doukas family.
