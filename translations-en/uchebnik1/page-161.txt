And Elisha replaced the harmful water in the well of Jericho with wholesome water, and through this water people were given fertility, abundance, and plenty. When the local inhabitants water the land with this water, it has such power that if even a drop falls on some place, it is more beneficial to it than if rain fell without ceasing. The lands watered by it are 70 versts long and 20 wide. On the lands of Jericho grow beautiful and dense trees. The date fruits there differ both in taste and in the power that works in them. The juiciest fruits are pressed, and much honey is obtained. There also grow trees that yield balsam, a healing resin, the most precious thing obtained there. There also grow cypress and myrobalan. If anyone calls Jericho a divine paradise, he will not sin against the truth, since there grows in abundance everything useful and fragrant, such things as are not found in other places. Grain, falling into that land, yields a hundredfold crop. This happens because of the warmth of the air and the nourishing properties of the water: the water fills the roots with strength, and the air promotes growth. It is very hot in this place, so that at harvest time one may perish. Water drawn before sunrise is colder than ice, and with this cold it cools the earth, which is hot from the heat. In winter days this water grows warmer, and when snow lies throughout all Judea, in Jericho it is so warm that one can walk without clothes. This place is 150 versts from Jerusalem and 60 from the Jordan. From Jericho to Jerusalem all is empty and stony, and to the Jordan and the Asphalt Lake all is likewise barren.

On the Asphalt Lake

I shall now tell also of the Asphalt Lake. It is bitter and barren. And everything heavy that is thrown into it does not sink. Vespasian came to look at the lake and ordered bound captives to be brought and thrown into it, and none of them drowned. The appearance of this lake is wondrous: it changes three times a day and shines from the sun’s rays. And pieces of asphalt are born in the lake, a special resin, dense and large as oxen, black; they float on the surface of the water and in appearance resemble headless bulls.

And those who work on the lake row up to them and drag them into boats, and fill the boats with this asphalt, and then they cannot cut it from the boats with iron or with anything else until they pour over it blood that comes from a woman, and only with the help of this blood can the asphalt be cut from the boat. This asphalt resin is used not only for building ships but also for healing the body, and physicians mix it into many medicines. The length of this lake is 580 versts. Adjacent to the Asphalt Lake is the land of Sodom, once rich and abundant in all things, but now burned. It is said that because of the wickedness of the local inhabitants God burned this land with heavenly fire.
