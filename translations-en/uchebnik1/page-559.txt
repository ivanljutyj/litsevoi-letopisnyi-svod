The praepositi brought the royal garments, clothed Basil in them, and put red boots on him.

Basil, clad in the royal raiment, bowed down at the tsar’s feet.

Then the patriarch brought out the crown, handed it to Tsar Michael, and he crowned Basil according to the established custom and gave him the scepter; all glorified Tsars Michael and Basil.

On the Shattering of the Mirror

Tsar Michael, having emptied the entire treasury in amusements with his dissolute retinue, squandered not only the golden griffins but even the famous gold-wrought poplar, that is, a tree with birds perched on it. Each of these birds, made of gold, differed from the others and was contrived so that it could produce songs as if alive, to the astonishment of listeners, who marveled at how ingeniously it was all made. And I weep, for I must recount a deed of the mad tsar worthy of tears, and, compelled to continue, I cannot restrain my sorrowful sighs.

Leo, most skilled in the sciences, made for Tsar Basil the Macedonian a wondrous instrument—a mirror—by means of which the tsar, while in the palace, could at any time see what was happening among the Arabs or Syrians and what they were plotting.

Once, while Tsar Michael was taking part in a chariot contest, he was informed of a sudden invasion of the Syrians, whose forces were rapidly approaching.

Scarcely had Tsar Michael heard this than he feared that the people, on receiving this news, would be thrown into confusion and the racecourse would empty, and he ordered the mirror to be smashed at once. Woe to his madness! Woe to his folly! Alas! Alas! What a Greek treasure did this foul-living man, drunkard, and glutton destroy! But I have already mourned his deeds enough.

Subatius, son-in-law of Bardas, having not received the title of Caesar as had been promised him, realized that he had been deceived by Basil and hated him. Then Subatius asked to be made strategos, but instead the tsar appointed Guver, the logothete of the public road (the official responsible for the condition of roads and the post), and appointed Subatius count in Opsikion. Then he, together with the ruler of Opsikion, George Piganios, resolved on rebellion, and setting out, they began to lay waste vineyards and burn fields (it was then harvest time), glorifying Tsar Michael alone, while dishonoring Basil and subjecting him to abuse. Learning of this, Michael and Basil ordered the other strategoi to march against the rebels.
