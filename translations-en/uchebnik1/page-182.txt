About the Veil, and about Jesus, and about the Guard

Until this generation the veil had been intact, since the people were pious. But now it was pitiful to look at it, for it was torn suddenly from top to bottom when the virtuous Man—and by His deeds, God—was put to death for money. They tell of many other terrible signs that occurred then. That Slain Man, after burial, was not found in the tomb. Some said that He had risen, while others said that His friends had stolen Him. I do not know which of them speaks the truth. For a dead man cannot rise by himself, but only with help and through the prayer of a righteous man, or if he is an angel, or someone else from the Heavenly Powers, or finally if God Himself appears as a man and does as He wills: and He will walk with people, and fall, and lie down, and rise, as He pleases. Some said that He could not have been stolen, since guards were placed around His tomb, a thousand Romans and a thousand Jews. This is what they tell about this veil, and this is the reason it was torn. Those who passed inside came into the chamber of the temple, whose height was 60 cubits, and its length the same, while its width was 20 cubits. This chamber of sixty cubits was divided in two. The first part was 40 cubits. In it were three wondrous objects known to all people: the lampstand, the table, and the censer. On the lampstand were 7 lamps, according to the number of the seven heavenly bodies called planets; and on the table were 12 loaves, according to the number of the heavenly circles that make up the year. On the censer were 13 incenses, which were gathered from all the seas, and from the whole universe, and from all the deserts, and these incenses signified that all is from God and to God. The inner place, of 20 cubits, was invisible behind another veil, and nothing stood or lay in it, and no one entered there or defiled it, and it was closed from the eyes of all. This place was called the Holy of Holies. And near the lower temple, on the sides, stood many three-tiered buildings close together. On the upper side, however, the buildings were not like these, because it was cramped there. To look at the temple from the front was terrifying to both eyes and souls, for it was everywhere overlaid with thick golden sheets, and the radiance of the sun was reflected, and all this had a fiery appearance. As no one can look at the bright sun, so anyone who wished to look at the temple covered his eyes. To strangers who looked at the temple it appeared like a mountain covered with snow, for in those places where it was not overlaid with gold the temple was very white. On the top of the temple were set golden spikes, large, long, numerous, and sharp as arrows, so that no bird would alight and defile the top of the temple.
