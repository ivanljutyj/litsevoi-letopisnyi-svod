The Great Athanasius was exiled not only by Emperor Constantine because of the treachery of the Arians, but also by his son Constantius, who had received his father's throne. It is told that Saint Athanasius, having learned that Emperor Constantius intended to adhere to the Arian faith, and that the Arians with evil intent were unjustly slandering him through an unrighteous judgment,

told no one, but suddenly at midnight, being sought after, he ran to a certain pious maiden. She was astonished and perplexed at his coming. The blessed patriarch said to her: "The disciples of Arius are seeking me and are slandering me with improper things. And in order to avoid absurd rumor and not lead into sin those who intend to torment me, I have decided to flee. And God directed me this night, saying: 'You will be able to be saved nowhere except with this maiden.'" For this God-bearer of God had foreseen even before, by divine revelation, the evil plot being prepared against him, and he fled, since the enemies of truth had declared him a villain and a poisoner. That maiden, casting aside all doubt, said thus: "The saint is sent by God," and she sheltered him for six years.

They did not know where the patriarch was. Returning to his flock after the death of Constantius, Athanasius was forced to leave the throne also during the persecution of Christians under Emperor Julian the Apostate. His close friends asked him: "Where are you going again, good shepherd, and to whom are you leaving us, like sheep having no shepherd?" To them the good shepherd answered: "Do not weep, children, and do not wail. A little cloud melting from the heat is this small sedition that we see; it will quickly disperse. Do not be downcast, children, I shall soon return by the will of God." And so it happened.

A little time passed, and the Hellenes, seeing crows flying over the shrine of Serapion in Alexandria and cawing loudly, asked the saint mockingly: "Tell us, wicked old man, what are the crows cawing about?" The saint answered them: "The crows say 'cra,' and in the Assyrian tongue 'cra' means 'morning.' So tomorrow you will see God's help and our glory."

And in the morning they learned of the death of Julian the Transgressor; he was brought from Persia dead and stinking.

On Anthony the Great
