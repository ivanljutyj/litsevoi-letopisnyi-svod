The priest of the city of Canopus learned of this and devised a certain malicious thing. In the land of Egypt they had thought to make clay vessels with many narrow holes, through which muddy water was filtered and became clear. And from those clay vessels the Canopian priest took one vessel and stopped up those holes with wax, adorned it with various colors, filled it with water, and set it up as a god. And he cut off the head of an old idol—they said it was the idol of a certain helmsman who had been with Menelaus—and carefully fitted it and attached it to the vessel. Then the Chaldeans came and lit a fire before that vessel, and the wax with which the holes had been stopped melted when the vessel became hot. Then the water poured from the holes and extinguished the fire. By such evil priestly tricks he proved the conqueror of the Chaldean god, and from that time Canopus came to be venerated.”

But now, when Theophilus, God’s hierarch, came, not a single drop profited the priests, nor did their malicious contrivances, but all their idols burned and crumbled to the ground.

When all this had been arranged, and word reached the orthodox emperor that the Egyptian idols had been destroyed and churches had been built in their place, the emperor raised his hands to Heaven and said with great joy: “I thank Thee, O Lord Christ our God! Without the destruction of this great city, idolatry has thus been extinguished.” Meanwhile Emperor Valentinian, while reigning in the western lands, hot-souled by reason of his age, despised life and strangled himself, and ended his life. Even to this day we do not know the reason for this. Some said that it was deliberate poisoning by Valentinian’s general Arbogast, and everyone said so. Others said: “The general is not guilty in this hateful affair,” but they laid the blame for this crime on the angry youth, since he was not allowed to hold royal power because he was young and not yet ready to rule over all by himself.

Concerning John the Faster

Theodosius was greatly inflamed by this calamity, took up arms against Eugenius, who had entered into Valentinian’s place, and grasped the sword according to God’s will, which the emperor had learned from John, a monk and faster, an Egyptian. This divine man was then ninety years old and dwelt in a cave; he neither went out of his cell himself nor received anyone inside, but only blessed and through the little door gave a greeting.
