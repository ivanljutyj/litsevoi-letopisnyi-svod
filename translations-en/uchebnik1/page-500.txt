Entering Constantinople again, Tsar Constantine assembled a council in Blachernae against the veneration of the holy icons, where much vain talk was uttered against the Lord and against His Christ. O, the frenzy of those men! The pious patriarch Germanus and all who venerate icons were there given over to a curse by those who themselves are cursed a thousand times. Lawless priests lifted their foul hands on high, and cried out, and uttered that voice, and said: “Today salvation has come to the whole world, for you, O tsar, have delivered us from idols.” O, the utmost boldness of shamelessness! O, the madness and blasphemy of those who rise against Christ and traffic in holy things, and their untiring tongue, and their senseless mind! That impious first ruler imagined himself responsible for the destruction of idols, as though the incarnation of Christ had been in vain, and the apostolic teaching futile. The wretched Constantine grew proud when he heard such things, and rejoiced in soul. John of Damascus, dwelling not under the power of the tormentor but in the city of Jerusalem, in the holy Lavra of St Sabas, pursued the monastic struggle and by many letters exposed the impious one, calling his bishops bishops and slaves of the belly.

After this, by God’s wrath, Anastasius died an evil death, he who was already dead in soul, a fornicator and not a patriarch. The lawless Constantine Kopronymos, that is, Dung-named, in his place set a certain bishop named Constantine, like-minded with him, not by hierarchal election, but he himself ordained him, and, going up to the ambo, cried: “Many years to Constantine, patriarch of the whole universe.” O, the frenzy of those men! He who bears weapons and rejoices in murders, and is filled with every impiety like Julian the Apostate, ordains a hierarch—one whom it is unworthy to call a hierarch. And the abomination of desolation stood in the holy place. Of these truly Ezekiel says: “For thus says the Lord: the priests have rejected My law and have defiled My holy things. Between the holy and the vile they have not spoken, between the unclean and the vile they have not judged.” The great Daniel exposes them still more firmly, saying: “Lawlessness went forth from the elders who judged, who thought to govern the people.” For truly these conceived sickness and gave birth to lawlessness, since they handed the established hierarchal and divine laws to simple folk and commanded them to be kept. And this perversion of hierarchs is no wonder. For those who wished to trample the Son of God, insulting His holy image and inflicting sufferings on the saints, how could they spare His priesthood? Who will not wonder and be afraid, hearing and seeing that one who bears weapons confers the priesthood and makes a hierarch of one devoted to wars and murders? But not long afterward the tsar did a deed like that laying on of hands and appointment.
