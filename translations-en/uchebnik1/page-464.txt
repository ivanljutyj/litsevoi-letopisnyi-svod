All saw the man abased and worn out by hunger, and they shed many tears, and brought him out of the tomb. The great bishop Gelasius told him to take food. But he did not wish to then; thanking them, he refused to eat.

And this soldier went from church to church, falling on his face to the ground, confessing to God, crying out with a great cry, with sighing and tears: “Woe, woe to those who sin and have not repented, for when the time has passed, what dreadful torment awaits them! Woe to those who defile their bodies!”

And this soldier lived forty days, singing prayers, keeping vigil, preaching to many, and turning them to repentance; and he reposed in the Lord and departed pure to God, having learned of his death three days beforehand. The trustworthy fathers, having seen this, told us of it for our benefit.

A Homily of John Chrysostom on the Dead

The God-taught Chrysostom also agrees with this miracle—I speak of human souls departing from here and of their ascent to Heaven. He speaks thus concerning the departure of the souls of the departed: “Many have seen this most ineffable spectacle: as they die, some toss upon their beds, wishing to flee; others cut and scratch their cheeks; some roll their eyes wildly; their hands droop, strength gradually fails, and their tongue burns fiercely. Then they see the angels of God, fearfully and suddenly taking the soul, while evil demons stand by and contend earnestly, desiring to snatch the soul to themselves; and then, after testing, they suddenly draw near to the soul that has been torn from the body, and it journeys with great despondency into places dreadful and utterly unknown. We need many prayers to God, brethren, many helpers, and many good deeds; we greatly need angelic help, and for the ascent through the air we need much guidance and escort.
