One of Herod’s wives, the daughter of the high priest, Mariamne, was found to be involved in it. Since she had already been killed earlier, the king turned his wrath upon her son Herod, whom he had appointed to reign after Antipater.

Now, since he could do her no harm, for she was dead, Herod took from her son the right to reign.

Antipater, however, knew nothing of these events and made merry in Rome. He spent his time and lived as befits a king’s son—surrounded by servants, in rich garments, distributing generous gifts. He gave many gifts to Roman nobles and urged them to write praises of him to Herod. And receiving gifts, the Italians, that is, the Latins, wrote such praises of Antipater as cannot be expressed. They wrote to Herod: “Antipater alone is your intercessor, your guard, your defense, and your savior from your treacherous sons. If it were not for him, your other lawless sons, who are now studying philosophy here in Rome, would have killed you; they often revile you and portray you as a monster.” Such are the Latins: they take bribes, and for gifts they will renounce their oath, and they see no sin in slander, and say: “We only utter words, but we ourselves do not kill.” The accursed think that only he is a murderer who kills with a weapon, while to slander, denounce, and incite is not murder. If only they understood God’s law! They had already been told about the law before, but since they were foreigners, this teaching did not take root among them. Therefore they slandered Herod’s sons, Archelaus and Philip, who were then studying in Rome, and wrote that he should kill them. Herod, however, was confident in the information he had managed to learn about Antipater, and did not trust the letters from Rome.

Many people came to Rome from Judea, but no one informed Antipater of what had happened. There was no one to tell him of his misfortune. Because of Antipater’s lawlessness and impiety, and by God’s providence, all mouths were closed, and no one told him of what threatened him. And seven months passed between the discovery of his conspiracy and his return. Antipater wrote from Rome to his father, joyfully reporting his speedy return and that Caesar had received him with great honor. Herod awaited impatiently the moment when he could seize his enemy in his hands, and feared lest Antipater learn of what had happened and slip away somewhere. And Herod sent Antipater a letter in which he asked and courteously commanded him to try to return as soon as possible.
