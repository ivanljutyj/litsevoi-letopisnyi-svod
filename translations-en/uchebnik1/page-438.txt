For nothing is truly more foul or unclean than those who walk thus and commit fornication.

For just as those deprived of their kindred—flee the final ruin and madness!—both with hand and finger corrupt the wretched women by an improper act, so do the insane and improper do. And here, naming this and showing himself wise, he said: “Blessed is the eunuch who does not commit lawlessness with his hand, and the virgin who has not known the bed of sin.” Thus there have arisen and been named the husbandless, the wifeless, the man-women, the iron-condemned, and the female demoniacs. Let the pure and meek hear these things being called in vain, so that they may not put faith in falsehood, in imagined purity and meekness. “What is clean from the unclean, and what is true from falsehood?”

On the War with the Vandals and with the Roman King Gelimer

After the murder of the Roman emperor Valentinian the New, who received the Roman kingdom after Honorius, son of Theodosius, from that time Rome was subject to barbarian kings even until Justinian the Great. Now, under Justinian, Rome was again united with the Greek power in this manner.

This Justinian the Great was on friendly terms with Hilderic, king of the Vandals, at the beginning of his reign. And from this Hilderic Justinian received a letter that the crafty Gelimer, Roman king, had risen against him and taken away all Hilderic’s domain, and shut up him himself, his wife, and his children.

Having learned this, Emperor Justinian was wounded in heart and grieved greatly for Hilderic, who suffered cruelly, and he assembled an army of many men against Gelimer, the Roman king. As commander and general in the war Justinian appointed the great Belisarius, the glory of Greece, who surpassed all commanders and generals by firmness of mind and wise judgment.
