…and Apollinarius, who helped with a bold soul and a fearless heart. He poured out absurd insults upon the Lord Christ, saying that He was merely a man and one of us, who accidentally received God’s grace, and was called God only after the Jordan baptism, having been deemed worthy among the first to receive the gifts of the Holy Spirit, having been baptized in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. But God the Word dwelt in Him by His own good will because of His exceptional virtue, in order to transmit to Him the divine rank, and chose Him as heir of worship. This man also blasphemed about many other things in the same way, alleging two unoriginate natures in the nature of Christ, although somehow conjoined yet separated from each other. Such is the second offshoot of heresy: that Christ has one nature, and that it is wrong to confess a dual nature.

On Nestorius the Heretic. After this, a certain heretic named Nestorius, from Germanicia in Syria, took the throne of Constantinople. He likewise rejected the dual nature of Christ, maliciously imitating Paul and Theodore, following his forebears, being a son of Cilix and a descendant of him of Samosata. And because of this he raised an irreconcilable heresy against the Holy Mother of God, denying his Lord and vexing his Lady, a wretched and shameless servant. For this malignant Nestorius was the third representative of the Jewish heresy, asserting that one essence is Christ Himself and another is God the Word, following the error of his fathers.

On Eutyches the Heretic. To the refuse left by Mani and Apollinarius there appeared yet a third helper—Eutyches, abbot of a monastery in Constantinople. He, not accepting consubstantiality, proposed that we confess the Lord as one-natured according to the flesh, separating himself from those who say that in Christ two natures are united and conjoined. And he also added certain other empty talk and absurdity, saying: the Lord’s Body came down from heaven, and God the Word passed through the Virgin like water through a channel and clothed Himself in it, deigning to be born of a woman, although He is Unbegotten. This teaching is Manichaean, but far surpassing it in its fantasy; and following his kin, with a perverted heart he prayed to Christ.

On Severus the Heretic. Then a certain man named Severus, having seized the throne of Antioch, wished to avenge Mani, Apollinarius, and Eutyches by heresy, disturbing the peace of the Church as much as he could. When he was driven out of Antioch as an enemy and rebel, like a storm and whirlwind he intoxicated Alexandria with flattery; there another storm seized him and the people, stirring and agitating them all. But a certain man named Julian, bishop of Halicarnassus in Asia, a zealous defender of Eutyches’ foul heresy, troubled them. Severus
