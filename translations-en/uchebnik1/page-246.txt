The art with which the images were made was so high that those who had not been at the war became as though they had been. On each image were shown the city commanders in those circumstances in which each of them was defeated and captured, and it was also shown how one or another city was conquered.

Then after the images came ships and vessels. All the spoil was carried in order. Most precious and honorable were the things taken from the Jerusalem temple: a golden throne made of many stones, and a lampstand adorned with gold, unlike those commonly used by the Romans. In the middle of this lampstand was a golden shaft, and from it extended seven smaller golden branches, like a trident; there were seven branches, and on each of them was a wrought golden lamp; and there were seven lamps in honor of the Jewish week.

At the very end of the spoil taken from the Jews were carried the books of the law of Moses. And after this many victory idols were carried, adorned with ivory and gold. And after the idols Vespasian rode first, and after him Titus. Last rode Domitian, and he was magnificent, himself and his horse. The procession ended at the temple of Capitoline Jupiter; approaching it, all stopped, for there was an ancient law from the fathers—to remain in that place and wait until they were informed of the death of the enemy commander.

This commander was Simon Niger, son of Giora, who was then led before the people with the captives during the procession. They tied him by the neck with a rope, and dragged him, and beat him on all parts of the body. And they brought him to the square where by law condemned criminals were killed, and strangled him there. And news was brought to Vespasian of the death of Simon Niger. And then all glorified Vespasian, and began to offer sacrifices.

The sovereigns recited their prayers and went to the palaces, and turned to rejoicing. On that same day Rome celebrated both the victory over enemies and the end of internal strife and the beginning of abundance, and all Romans hoped for a good future. After the triumphal procession, and after the confirmation and glorification of Vespasian, he decided to create an altar in honor of peace. And soon he began construction, and finished faster than could have been thought. And Vespasian placed all kinds of treasures in this temple, and one could see in it a collection of all unheard-of things.
