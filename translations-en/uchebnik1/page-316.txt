It was all but destruction that arose from the fierce, dangerous, and evil-believing heresy.
The coming of the blessed, holy, and God-wise Constantine from Rome to Byzantium was in the 12th indiction, at the end of the year, on the 13th day, in the 12th year of his reign. After this, by God’s will, certain bishops were found who at that time, by the providence of the Life-giving Spirit, had been preserved from the heresy; and each of them rose up from his church and came to the orthodox village of Nicaea. This was the first ecumenical council of the holy fathers, 318 in number. And they assembled on the 20th day of the month of May; from Adam to the Nativity of Christ, 5500 years; from the Nativity of Christ to the Crucifixion, 33 years; from the Crucifixion of the Lord to King Constantine, 279 years; from the first year of Constantine’s reign to the first Council of Nicaea, 12 years; and from the Ascension of the Lord to the first council, 318 years.

The First Council of the Holy Fathers

And the first council of the holy fathers, 318 in number, was held in Nicaea because, as Constantine the Great said, he saw the Church troubled by Arius. And he began to gather an ecumenical council, and commanded the bishops to come to the council on horses and mules, and he himself also entered Nicaea.

When all had gathered, he prepared a hall in the imperial palace, ordered seats to be placed in it, and asked them to enter.

And he himself entered last with a small retinue, and standing in the middle, he ordered all to sit. Then all the bishops asked him to sit on the small seat that had been placed. After everyone, the emperor himself sat down, overcome with great shame and confusion at the sight of the men. They, with praise, crowned his head with flowers and, for his zeal for God, gave him a blessing.

He delivered a speech about unanimity and concord, spoke of other matters, presented many gifts, and ordered many couches to be prepared and all to dine together. He seated the elders next to himself at the table, and seated the rest at other tables. There he saw that some had their right eye put out. And learning that they had all suffered from Diocletian and Maximian for the confession of Christ, he kissed those scars with his own lips, believing that by the kiss he would receive forgiveness from them; so wonderful was this man, adorned with continence, faith, and humility.

And in the morning, when all the bishops and the emperor had gathered together, they summoned Arius and his like-minded followers to the council, ordering him to cease his fabrications. The teaching of this impious and accursed man is this: “God was not always Father, and the Word was not always God’s Word, and it was not from non-being, and the Existing God created the non-existent.”
