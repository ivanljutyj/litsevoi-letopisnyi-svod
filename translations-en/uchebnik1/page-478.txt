Reign 25th, of Constantine the Bearded, son of Constans

After Constans, grandson of Heraclius, his son Constantine the Bearded reigned for 17 years.

This man reached the island of Sicily with a heavy army, that is, a great one, and stabbed to death all the murderers and slanderers of the king and of his father Constans, grandson of Heraclius, and with them Mizizius, who had tyrannized, that is, seized power.

The people named this sovereign Pogonatus, that is, the Bearded, because, setting out to war against Mizizius, he was a handsome youth and departed beardless, but returned from there a mature man with a beard. And Constantine came to the City of Constantine, and was received by all, and praised, and glorified.

In the years of this Constantine’s reign, an elder of the Paulicians appeared, named Constantine, who called himself Silvanus. This heresy is as follows. The Paulicians, who are also Manichaeans, were named Paulicians instead of Manichaeans from a certain Paul of Samosata, son of a Manichaean woman named Callinice, who had two sons, this Paul and John. This Callinice taught her sons the Manichaean heresy and sent them from Samosata to the Armenian land as preachers of this heresy. These Paul and John came to a certain village, that is, a settlement, in order to sow their heresy; and from that time the name of that village was changed, and they called it Episporis, that is, All-Sown; and their disciples were called Paulicians. These Paulicians remained in the teaching of this Paul for several years, and received another teacher called Constantine. And that Constantine called himself Silvanus, the name of a disciple of the apostle Paul. This Constantine the Paulicians have as their elder and teacher, and not that Paul of Samosata. This Constantine handed his teaching over to the Paulicians, using Gospel and Apostolic preaching, written unchanged in comparison with the writing and words such as we also have, but Constantine presented it to them, turning each chapter of the Gospel and Apostle to the use of his heresy, and gave them a law never to read other books, only the Gospel and the Apostle. Heretics. After Constantine, called Silvanus, the Paulicians had a second teacher named Simeon, who called himself Titus, likewise as Constantine and those after him did, by the name of a disciple of the apostle Paul. The third, an Armenian by birth, called Gegnesius, changed his name to Timothy. The fourth, Joseph, changed his name to Epaphroditus, and after him was the fifth, Zacharias. But some of the Paulicians consider him a hireling and not a true shepherd, and reject him. The sixth teacher the Paulicians had was Vahan the vile; the seventh they had was Sergius, who changed his name to Tychicus. Manes
