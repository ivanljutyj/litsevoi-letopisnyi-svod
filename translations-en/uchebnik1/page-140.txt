And they began to set up wall-breaking engines. Joseph and Chares, who were leaders in the city, saw that they could resist for not many days without water and without engines, and they urged and encouraged their people to leave the city.

When Joseph and Chares led the inhabitants of Gamala out of the city, they stood for a short time, but could not endure the stones and Roman spears flying at them, and retreated to the city.

The Romans set up “rams”—special wall-breaking engines—in three places, and no one hindered them, and they destroyed the walls.

And the Romans trumpeted trumpets, shouted, clashed weapons, entered the city, and attacked the townspeople with noise. They were surrounded on all sides, could not resist, and fled to the upper part of the city.

The Romans followed them, and the Jews turned and attacked them from the height, and killed them, since the place was cramped and inconvenient for the Romans. The Romans below could not help those above in the battle, nor could they run out of the city because of the pressing multitude of their own, and the Romans harmed one another and did not know what to do.

And the Romans began to climb onto the city buildings with heavy armor, and the roofs filled with Romans, and the buildings could not bear their weight, and collapsed, and fell upon one another, and crushed all the soldiers. Some were stoned, others suffocated in lime dust, and many Romans were buried alive under the rubble. Few of those who ran out from there were without injury. Most of all the Romans suffocated from the dust.

The inhabitants of Gamala thought this was divine help, and, not caring that they harmed themselves by breaking their own houses, pressed zealously on the Romans, driving them onto the roofs. The streets were narrow, steep, and slippery. And the Romans kept falling with heavy armor, not knowing where to flee. The townspeople, being above, beat the Romans like cattle; they threw stones from the ruined buildings, and pulled weapons from the hands of fallen Romans and turned them against those still breathing.
