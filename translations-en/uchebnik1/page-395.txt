In the reign of this Zeno, Constantinople suffered a punishment from God's wrath—an earthquake—on the second day of the month of September, extending as far as Taurus and Nicomedia, the capital of Bithynia; it was the sixth punishment.

A similar punishment befell Helenopolis. And Zeno gave much for the restoration of these cities. In the reign of this same Zeno, the patrician Mamian was in Syrian Antioch, of Phoenician stock; he built much in Syrian Daphne.

Under this same Zeno, the patrician Illus the Isaurian rose against him—a friend of Zeno who had given him much help at his second return from Isauria, whither Zeno had fled from Constantinople.

This Illus came to Constantinople with Emperor Zeno as his trusted friend. And Illus agreed with Emperor Zeno that Zeno would drive his mother-in-law out of the imperial palace, so that she would not again plot against Zeno as before.

And Zeno sent Illus to Isauria to bring Longinus, Zeno's brother. And Illus went and began to delay in Isauria.

And Illus wrote in letters to Zeno that Lady Verina, Zeno's mother-in-law, should come and give Illus her word that he would live, since Illus feared Emperor Zeno. And Emperor Zeno, as her son-in-law, persuaded Verina and instructed her, as he had agreed with Illus, to go and give Illus her word that he would live, so that Illus would not fear Emperor Zeno and would bring Zeno's brother Longinus.

And this Lady Verina went down to Isauria, as Illus had summoned her, and he had many soldiers, and Illus shut Verina up in a fortress and ordered Isaurian soldiers to guard her in the fortress.

And Illus took Longinus and came to Constantinople, and Illus became both boyar and consul, and magister, and patrician, and governed the whole city. Likewise Longinus, Zeno's brother, became a great commander and immediately consul.

Empress Ariadne, wife of Emperor Zeno, received a letter from her mother, Empress Verina.

And Ariadne begged the emperor to release Lady Verina from the fortress. And the emperor said: "Ask the patrician Illus." She tearfully appealed to Illus for her mother's sake, that he release her. And Illus did not permit it and said to her: "Will she for whom you plead make another emperor in place of your husband?"

And after this Empress Ariadne said to Emperor Zeno: "Either Illus remains in the imperial palace, or I." And the emperor said to her: "Do what you can; I wish you to remain." Then Ariadne plotted to kill Illus, because he had not allowed her mother to come.
