Domestic Andrew said to the patriarch: “Do you know the monk Theodore?” The patriarch said: “I do not know him.” Andrew said: “Do you not know the monk Theodore Santabarinus?” And the patriarch said: “I know Theodore the monk, Archbishop of Euchaita.”

When Santabarinus was brought, Andrew said to him: “The emperor asks you: ‘Where is the money of my imperial authority?’” He replied: “The one reigning in those days gave it. Now, if the emperor asks, he has the authority to take it.” And Andrew said: “Tell me, whom did you wish to make emperor, when you persuaded the father to deprive his own son of his eyes? Was it not a kinsman of the patriarch?” He said: “I do not know what you are talking about.” And Magistros Stephen said: “Then how was it that you sent a warning to the emperor, to expose the patriarch in this matter?”

And he, falling at the patriarch’s feet, said: “I adjure you, my lord, by God, first tear the omophorion from me, and then, once I am deprived of my episcopal rank, let them torment me as a malefactor. For I did not send such words to the emperor.” The patriarch said: “By the salvation of my soul, Kyr Theodore, you are an archbishop both in this age and in the age to come.” Enraged at this, Andrew said: “Was it not through me that you sent, monk, to the emperor, to expose the patriarch?” He denied it, saying: “I know nothing.”

Returning to the emperor, they reported this conversation. The emperor, seized with uncontrollable anger and fury that he had brought an unjust accusation against the patriarch, sent men to beat Santabarinus cruelly and exiled him to Athens; then he brought him back, blinded him, and sent him into exile in the East.

After several years he brought him back from exile to Constantinople and ordered that he be given maintenance in the New Church. He died under Emperor Constantine and Zoe, his mother.

In the days of Emperor Leo, the Lombard leader Agion, son-in-law of the Frankish king, dared to oppose the emperor and subjected the whole country to himself. Learning of this, Emperor Leo sent Constantine, by rank a trapezites, with all the western troops to fight Agion.

And the regiments fought, and Constantine’s army was defeated and cut to pieces, and he himself barely escaped.

And then there was an eclipse of the sun at the sixth hour of the day, so that the stars appeared; a strong wind arose, with lightning and thunder, and seven people were killed on the trading steps.

Then Samos was besieged by the Saracens, and its strategos, named Paspalas, was taken captive.
