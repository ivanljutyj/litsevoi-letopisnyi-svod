O bitter victory that I win over my own children!” And Herod turned to the judges with a request that they reach a decision. And the first, Saturninus, said: “The young men must be punished, but not killed. For I myself have three sons, and I would be wrong to condemn other men’s children to death.” And many others spoke likewise—the brothers should be punished, but not killed. Volumnius, however, was the first to pronounce the terrible sentence. And after him all condemned the young men to death: some because they wished to please Herod, others because they hated him and wanted him to remain childless; but none of those who condemned the young men to death was angry with them. And all Syria and Judea watched and waited to see how it would end. And no one allowed the thought that Herod would be so cruel and base as to kill his own children. He, however, transferred his sons to the city of Tyre.

And from there Herod sailed to Caesarea, and there he considered by what means to execute his sons. But a certain soldier named Teron, who with his son was in close friendship with the king and his sons, learned that the king wished to execute them, and was terribly grieved, so that he fell sick in soul, and went about like a madman, and cried: “Truth is trampled down, there is no longer truth, nature is perverted, life is full of lawlessness! There is no soul in the king, his mind is corrupted, his word is like wind, his speeches are like morning dew!” And to this he added much else that a man in madness can say. And sparing not his body, not thinking of his life, Teron approached the king and said in anger: “Are you not the most wretched of all, O madman! You listen to slander against your sons, flesh of your flesh. I think that you are abominable before God and men. To Pheroras and Salome, who have done much evil, you granted forgiveness, and now, believing their words, you destroy your children, the true heirs of your kingdom. But Antipater you leave, yet he will not be able to reign with terror. Think what hatred the soldiers will have for him—for there is none among them who does not pity the young men, and many of the commanders are angry and do not hide it.” Then Teron named their names.

And at once the king seized those commanders, together with Teron and his son. At that same time Tryphon also, who cut the king’s hair, in his foolishness said: “Teron gave me great gifts so that I would cut your throat with the scissors during the haircut.” When Herod learned of this, he applied cruel tortures. Teron and his son endured them in silence, and the king’s barber kept saying the same thing. And Herod ordered Teron to be tortured even more severely. But Teron’s son took pity on his father and said to Herod: “I will tell you everything if you forgive my father.” The king released Teron.
