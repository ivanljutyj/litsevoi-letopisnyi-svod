Once Varda, wishing to partake of the Holy Mysteries of Christ, did not receive this divine gift, being excommunicated by the patriarch, for precious myrrh must not be poured into a worthless vessel.

Then Caesar Varda, like a roaring lion, blazed with anger against the patriarch; like a lynx he rushed upon him and, on the advice of Michael, cast the hierarch down from the patriarchal throne. And in his place he installed the protosyncritus Photius, who headed the imperial chancery. O woe! Whom he installed, and in place of whom! A terrible storm was approaching, threatening to drown the hierarch Ignatius. The Caesar's malice, like the breath of a stormy north wind and a fierce west wind, raised it, and the waves foamed menacingly. And with what words can one describe how many devices Photius and Varda resorted to, what slander and violence they subjected the hierarch to, wishing to force the patriarch to write with his own hand that he was leaving the patriarchal throne of his own will!

Then, having brought him down from the throne, they subjected the saint to cruel tortures: like a robber and malefactor they struck him in the face, crushed his jaws and knocked out his teeth, and then, naked and bound, laid him in a cold marble tomb full of filth, in which the vile body of Copronymus had been buried.

But the hierarch was not forsaken by God. The Lord sent him a certain Constantine the Armenian, who, wishing to bring at least some consolation to the righteous man, began secretly to bring him a glass vessel of wine and a few vegetables. And he did this until Varda was killed and the lawless Photius was driven out, as will be told in due time.

On the Baptism of the Bulgars

In one of the battles, the sister of the Bulgarian prince Boris was taken captive by the Greeks. In captivity she received holy baptism.

This came to pass under Tsar Michael. She lived in the imperial palace, and there she was taught letters. Later she was exchanged for a noble man named Theodore Kiphara, who was held captive by the Bulgars.

After returning, she began to teach her brother the truths of the Christian faith. At that time a terrible disease, leprosy, broke out in Bulgaria and began to spread rapidly, and many died. But when the prince, heeding his sister's exhortations, believed in Christ, the epidemic ceased.

Then the prince sent an embassy to Constantinople with a request to send a bishop, desiring to receive baptism.

When the Bulgars learned of this, they resolved to kill him. But he boldly withstood them with the sign of the cross and defeated them, having the Divine Cross before him. Then all the people wished to be baptized.
