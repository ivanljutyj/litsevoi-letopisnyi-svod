Opposite Tarichea, above the lake, where the border of Agrippa’s kingdom was.

There too are Sogana and Seleucia by Lake Samachonitis. The width of this lake is sixty versts.

Lake Samachonitis extends to the place called Daphne, which is filled with every kind of beauty. There are springs there, from which the Little Jordan is born, below the temple of the Golden Treasure, and it flows into the Great Jordan.

Agrippa sent envoys to the cities of Sogana and Seleucia, and promised them peace, and they went over to Agrippa’s side.

But the inhabitants of Gamala did not surrender, hoping in the strength of the city, since the land there is mountainous, and the city stands on a hill as if on a neck and shoulders, and all of it resembles a camel in appearance, and therefore the city is called Gamala, though the local inhabitants cannot pronounce this name correctly — “kamil” — because they hate that animal. On the sides and in front of the city are terrible ravines. Behind, the city is not so strongly fortified by nature, but the townspeople strengthened that place with a deep ditch. Many temples stand within the city, and wells were made on the outskirts of the city. Thus was this city fortified.

Josephus fortified the city still more, making trenches and underground passages, so that one could walk underground as well. The inhabitants of the city hoped in the protection of this place. But they had few troops.

Vespasian set out from Ammaus, where he had entrenched before Tiberias. “Ammaus” means “warmth,” because warm baths are located there, bathing in which all are healed. And Vespasian went to Gamala.

Vespasian could not besiege the whole city—the terrible ravines did not permit it—and he placed soldiers to guard the city from afar.

Vespasian himself entrenched on the mountain near the city, and arranged his camp in the same manner as has been told before.

And behind the city he began to make an embankment, also on the eastern side, where the highest tower was.

Near this tower the fifth and tenth legions were stationed. One defended against the city, and the other filled up the ravines.

Then King Agrippa approached the walls of Gamala and tried to speak with the townspeople about surrendering the city. Someone from the walls threw a stone at him and hit him in the elbow, and Agrippa’s men ran up, surrounded him, and quickly led him away.
