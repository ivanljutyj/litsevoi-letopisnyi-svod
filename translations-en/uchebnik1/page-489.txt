Artemius was a fierce and courageous king, and educated in books. He had formerly been the king's counsellor, whom they call an asynkritos. Artemius learned that the Hagarite robbers were sailing, and he equipped warships and “first-voyaging” boats, and sent them to join battle with the Hagarites.

Those Greek troops sailed as far as the Hellespont—they went swiftly, for the winds were “right-breathing”—and there they rebelled against the king. And in the city of Adramytium, after taking counsel, they made Theodosius king, a native of Adramytium, who until then had been a collector of taxes there.

Learning of this, Artemius fled to the city of Nicaea. Theodosius, however, came to Constantinople with many soldiers, and, making use of treachery, entered the city through the Blachernae Gate.

The 32nd Reign, of Theodosius the Adramite, who reigned in Constantine's city

After Artemius, Theodosius the Adramite reigned for two years. This Theodosius clothed King Artemius in the monastic schema and imprisoned him in Thessalonica. But this Theodosius too, having tasted power but little, soon abandoned royal glory, as will be said below.
