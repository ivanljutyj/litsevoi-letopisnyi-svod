The saints bore branding on their faces as women wear a pearl crown shining with precious stones. On the way into exile they met a certain fisherman who was bringing food to Patriarch Methodius.

Then they wrote to the great Methodius, who was in prison, a brief note in an Amorian verse: “The inscribed greet a living dead man and a life-bearing dead man, who lives on earth and walks in the heavens—captives greeting the captive.”

He wrote them in reply: “The one buried alive greets the holy orators—inscribed by name in the heavenly books, chaste, with branded faces—persecuted like him for the Truth.” And these golden words were like life-giving streams flowing from a Divine source.

Saint Theodore departed to the Lord in exile, and at the hour of his death the singing of angels was heard, and his voice sounded together with them. The good ascetic, the great Methodius, imprisoned in a dark, terrifying cave, endured the crushing of his teeth, the shattering of his jaws, and all the other tortures with which the impious inhumanly tormented him; and he was like hard iron on an anvil under striking hammers.

Emperor Theophilus, though immeasurably cruel and inventive in every kind of evil against the veneration of icons and its supporters, was at the same time very wise and magnanimous in matters of state governance; he cared about the adoption of just laws and their execution in the courts. A testimony to the greatness of his reign was also the renewal of the walls of the reigning city, which had been ravaged by the Hagarenes and had also suffered from severe frost in the year when the sea froze, from the heat that followed the cold, and from constantly blowing hot winds. Just as great mountains collapse, so the walls of the city began to collapse. He not only renewed them but gave them an even more majestic appearance, spending vast treasures on it.

He created a wondrous organ, elaborately adorned with gold-wrought trees, with birds sitting on their branches, made so skillfully that in the sounds of the wondrous breath of the music one seemed to hear the sweet-voiced singing of songbirds sitting on poplar branches or on pointed cypresses. So do the wondrous creations of gifted people’s hands seem to come alive.
