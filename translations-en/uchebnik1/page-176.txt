John had spoken to his men, and so they did. And in the Temple, instead of a festival, there was wailing. The Zealots leaped down from the walls and fled into the underground chambers of the Temple, and from there they pledged loyalty to John and were pardoned. John, that is Johanan, occupied the inner Temple and prepared to fight Simon Niger. Thus, instead of three factions involved in the strife within the city, two remained.

Titus wanted to entrench near the city, and he stationed chosen horsemen and foot soldiers to fight those who ran out from the city, while he ordered the other soldiers to level the ground from the camp to the city walls. Then the soldiers leveled and broke down every kind of enclosure, and cut down the fruit-bearing and beautiful trees that had been planted by the local inhabitants. Rocky places they leveled with iron tools. With what had been broken down they filled ravines, gullies, and pits from the Watchtower as far as the tomb of Herod, which was near the Serpent’s Pool.

The Jews devised this trick against the Romans. A thousand Jews came out before the city and circled near the Women’s Tower, as if they had been driven out by the townspeople who desired peace, and they pretended to be afraid and to beg for mercy. And those Jews who stood on the city walls pretended to beat these men and drive them away from them. And the Jews on the walls began to stretch out their hands to the Romans and bow to them, so that they would come and enter the city, since the evildoers had been expelled and the gates were open. The Romans saw their plea and did not suspect the deception, but believed the Jews, and went to seize those outside and to enter the city. But Titus doubted and marveled at the sudden change in the Jews, and ordered the soldiers not to go to the city.

But some of the soldiers working near the city did not wait for this order; they took up arms and went to the city. Those Jews who pretended to have been driven out first ran, and when the Roman soldiers were between them and the walls, the Jews came out and surrounded the Romans who had rushed in, and cut them down, while from the walls they threw stones at them and killed many of them. And if any of the wounded Romans fled, the Jews struck them down as far as the tomb of Helena. And the Jews returned to Jerusalem with pride, and laughed and mocked the Romans who had yielded to their deception, and the Jews raised their shields and danced for joy.

But the commanders and the emperor were angry at the disobedient soldiers. And Titus said to the soldiers: “Look, the Jews, who have already despaired of remaining alive and have no ruler among them, yet do everything with design and deliberation, and deceive us, and listen to one another. And therefore fortune accompanies them. But you are accustomed to be under orders and discipline, to obey the commander, and to submit to your officers. But now...”
