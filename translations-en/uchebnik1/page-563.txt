Byzantium (875–928 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 4

The 45th Reign, of Basil the Macedonian, Autocrat, who began to reign in Constantinople in the year 6387 from Adam (the year 887 from Christ)

Basil reigned with Michael for one year and four months, and alone for nineteen years.

Having become autocrat, he ordered the eparch to go out to the marketplace and proclaim it. And so he took power and was proclaimed.

Oryphas, commander of the naval forces, having learned what had been done to Basil, being a friend of both Basil and his lord, rose up to avenge Michael.

When the autocrat learned of this, he summoned him, first forbade him with threatening words, and then honored him with gifts, because he had dared such boldness for the sake of his lord.

But after some time he became so confident in him that, going off to war, he left Constantinople in his charge.

Although Michael spent his life in debauchery, God is the judge and avenger of such deeds. All those who with Basil undertook his murder were overtaken by God's vengeance, and they ended their lives in agonizing suffering.

For Yakovets, while hunting with the emperor, dropped his dagger; when he intended to dismount from his horse and pick it up, before he could set his foot on the ground his horse bolted, and his foot remained in the stirrup. And the horse dragged him through ravines, over stones, and through thickets, and tore him to pieces.

And John the Chaldean, who had become governor in Chaldia, because he rose against the emperor, was seized and cut down.

Asuleon, a relative of Emperor Basil, having aroused the emperor's wrath, was exiled to his village and stabbed at night by his own slaves.

However, they too were burned with fire at the Amastrianum.
