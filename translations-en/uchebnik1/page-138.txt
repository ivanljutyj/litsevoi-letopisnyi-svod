And the destroyers sent to the family of Elachim, which was a priestly family, and appointed a high priest from it. And it fell to a man named Phannias, son of Samuel, from the village of Aphtha, to be high priest. This man was like the destroyers in lawlessness. He was not only unworthy of the high-priestly rank, but did not even know or ever hear of such an office, because he lived in a village. Dragging him out of the village like a buffoon putting on a mask and impersonating other people, they dressed him in priestly garments and taught him what to do at what time. This great dishonor was for their amusement. All the priests watched from afar as the divine law was mocked, and shed tears and groaned bitterly, since the destroyers had ruined and trampled both the high-priestly image and the covenant of God. Every destructive and vile deed was done among the destroyers, and people understood that desolation would overtake the city, and the prophecy would be fulfilled that there would be an abomination of desolation in the holy place. The people could not endure such outrage, and all rushed against the destroyers as against destroyers of the high priesthood.

Gorion, son of Joseph, and Simeon, son of Gamaliel, went about all the assemblies and urged the people to avenge the destroyers and cleanse the sanctuary of blood. The high priests Jesus, son of Gamala, and Ananus, son of Ananus, helped them. And all gathered and were angry at the evildoers because they had seized the temples and killed and plundered many. But the people did not dare rise against the destroyers. And Ananus stood up among them, and looked at the temple, and his eyes filled with tears, and he said: “It would be well for me to die beforehand and not see the temple of God, which has been defiled by so many iniquities, and the holy places, into which it was not permitted to enter, and where now bloodsuckers crowd. Yet, clothed in priestly garments, calling upon the glorious sacred Name, I walk under the sun and rejoice that I have not yet accepted a glorious death in my old age. But if I am alone here as in a wilderness, and there is no one with me who would be zealous with me for God, then I am ready to give my soul to Him. For I do not need life among such people who do not feel their own destruction. For you, robbed, endure; killed, keep silent; and seeing your kinsmen killed, you do not even weep over them openly. O bitter torment! O violence unseen! But why do I murmur against the tormentors? Did we not raise them, were they not nourished by our meekness? If we had forbidden them from the beginning, when there were still few of them, we would not have brought weapons upon ourselves.
