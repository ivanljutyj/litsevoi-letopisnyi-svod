If only Herod had left us alive. But it is impossible to escape such a serpent, such a bloodsucker, under whom no one can openly love one another, but all speak to each other in fear. But everything will be different if our hand and mind are strengthened by courage.” So these women spoke under torture, and also that Antipater intended to flee with Pheroras into his region. And Herod believed everything that was said when they mentioned the hundred talents, since he had spoken of them privately with Antipater. And Herod’s anger fell first upon Doris, Antipater’s mother: he took from her all her property and much money and drove her away a second time. And he freed from torment the maidservants of Pheroras who had told everything. And inexpressible fear came upon Herod, and his thoughts spun as if in the wind, and he tortured many innocent people so that not one of the guilty should escape.

Herod also seized Antipater’s steward and tortured him, and he reported that Antipater had sent to Egypt for a poisonous draught, which Antiphilus brought and gave to Theodion, Antipater’s maternal uncle, and to Pheroras. Antipater had told them to poison Herod while he himself was in Rome. Pheroras gave this draught to his wife.

The king sent for Pheroras’s wife and ordered her to bring this draught. She pretended that she had gone for the draught, but threw herself down from a height to avoid accusations and torture. But by God’s Providence everything was so ordered that vengeance should overtake Antipater, and the woman did not strike her head but her thighs, and escaped death.

And they brought her, and the king brought her round with incense, and asked her why she had thrown herself from the height. And Herod swore that if she told the truth he would not torture her, but if she concealed it he would shatter her body and not allow her to be buried. The woman thought to herself and said with a groan: “Why should I continue to keep the secret when Pheroras is already dead? Why should I save Antipater, who has ruined us all? Listen, king, and let God be my witness that I do not depart from the truth. When you sat in tears beside the dying Pheroras, he then called me to him and said in a whisper: ‘O wife, there is not in my soul the prudence and brotherly love that are in my brother. I withdrew from him, and was deceived, and hated one who loved me, and thought to kill my savior, who grieves so for me. My God avenges on me my impiety and lawlessness. But bring the poison that you keep and that you received from Antipater, and burn it before my eyes, so that I may not answer for this in the world to come.’ And I brought the poison and poured it out into the fire before his eyes; a little I kept for myself and kept in secret out of fear of you.” With these words she gave the vessel in which there was very little of that poison.
