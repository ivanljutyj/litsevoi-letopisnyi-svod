In the reign of this Justin there appeared a certain woman from the land of Cilicia, of giant race, with an enormous body, taller and broader than any other person by a full cubit, who went about begging throughout all Greece. And she came to the city of Antioch and took from every shop and workshop in that city one copper coin, and she saved up, if converted into silver coins, forty such coins. The Antiochenes gave her the nickname Phelera, and she departed from that city.

In the reign of this Justin, Tzath, king of the Laz, was in the Persian land. At that time Kavad reigned in Persia, and he held Tzath in subjection to himself. And Tzath, king of the Laz, fled from the Persian pagan faith and came up to King Justin in Constantinople, asking that Justin appoint him king over the Laz and that Tzath become a Christian.

And Tzath took for himself a Greek wife, the granddaughter of John the patrician, who held the court office of curopalates. And this Greek woman, Valeriana, Tzath took with him to his own land. Tzath was appointed and crowned to the kingdom by King Justin. And Tzath began to wear the Roman imperial crown; and he had an okril, that is, a garment, all white and silk, and instead of purple, on his shoulder a flat gold plaque, and on it were small clasps with the eagle emblem of King Justin; his boots were from his own country, crimson, with pearls, in Persian style, and his belt also was with pearls.
