One day the emperor came unexpectedly to Arsenius and saw him standing while his children were seated. Theodosius was greatly indignant with Arsenius and said to him: “Did I not tell you that in all things you should treat the boys not as emperors but as disciples? What are you doing? You have greatly grieved our realm by this.”

Arsenius said to the emperor: “I did not wish to do so, O emperor, for it is not good nor established by God that I should sit while emperors stand before me.” The emperor was angered by these words and answered Arsenius, raising his voice, and said: “Did you make them emperors? Did I not hand them over to you as children and servants? How then do you answer thus?” And having said this, the emperor at once took the crowns from his sons’ heads and cast the crowns to the ground, and made his sons come and bow to Arsenius.

And Theodosius seated Arsenius on the throne, and ordered Arcadius and Honorius to stand before him, and said: “If they fear God and walk in His law and keep His commandments, the eternal King is able, if such be His will, to give them this rank. But if they do not have fear of Him, I will pray to God that He root them out at once, even from their youth, for it is better to die in piety than to reign in impiety.”

And having said this, the emperor went to his chambers. From that time Arsenius, fearing the emperor, no longer allowed the boys to sit, but only to stand before him and thus learn. Living in such circumstances, holy Arsenius grieved and sorrowed, for he was a hater of glory and a lover of God, and he desired above all the monastic ascetic life. So Arsenius often prayed to God with tears that He would arrange for him in some way to withdraw from the world.

One day holy Arsenius found Arcadius fallen into some sin, as any child does. And having brought him, he forbade him to do it and inflicted a cruel wound on him with his hand, so that even until death the mark of that wound remained on his body. And from the day on which he was beaten, Arcadius harbored great malice against Arsenius, and he summoned his sword-bearer and said to him privately, giving orders:

“By whatever means you wish, destroy Arsenius, so that no one learns of it.”

The sword-bearer, having heard Arcadius, agreed, and having the fear of God in his heart, he told Arsenius about the murder that Arcadius had ordered to be committed. Arsenius, overcome with sorrow, prayed to God, saying: “Lord, instruct me how to be saved.” And Arsenius repented of the wound he had inflicted on Arcadius. After a few days, the sword-bearer began to urge Arsenius, saying: “Go and save yourself by whatever means.”
