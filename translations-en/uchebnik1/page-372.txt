And Theodosius loved Cyril, Archbishop of Alexandria. And having taken boldness from the boldness of the bishop of Alexandria, the townspeople, seizing power in the reign of Theodosius, burned at the stake Hypatia, a woman famed as a philosopher, of whom it was said that she was great; that woman was already old.

In the reign of Theodosius, Crete, the great island lying in the midst of the sea, received punishment from God's wrath; it has 100 cities, as Euripides wrote of it.

All the regions around Crete suffered also. There fell also its public bath in the capital of Crete, the city of Gortyn; that bath was built by Julius Caesar. Its domes were twelve in number, according to the twelve months, and for each month tribute was given from a dome. Except for one stone structure, all twelve domes were burned, and they could be seen in such a strange state.

Theodosius also built many other things, including the Golden Gate in Constantinople, which Nymphianus, the administrator of Valentinian, emperor of Rome, gilded; Maximus the noble killed him and took the kingdom in Rome.

This Emperor Theodosius wronged Antiochus, a nobleman in the rank of praepositus, that is, chamberlain, and a patrician, influential in the palace and governing the palace; he had been Theodosius's tutor while his father was alive, and as chief chamberlain he governed the whole city of Rome during his father's time.

And this Antiochus, after the death of Theodosius's father, continued as a patrician to lord it over Theodosius. And the emperor was angered against him, and brought him out before the people, and tonsured him, and made him a priest at the Great Church of Constantinople, and commanded that eunuchs should not enter the rank of patricians.

The chamberlains were deprived of their rank, that is, of the palace praepositure. And this Antiochus died, having been a presbyter. Theodosius appointed as administrator the patrician Cyrus, a philosopher, a man wise in all things, and this Cyrus held both ranks for 14 years.

This Cyrus the administrator would enter sometimes the chariot, sometimes again, with assistants, the imperial chambers. And he would ride out in the chariot of the city administrator, and took care of every matter, and renewed all Constantinople.

This Cyrus was pure in all things; the townspeople cried out about him, when Theodosius watched at the hippodrome, thus: "Constantine founded it, Cyrus renewed it, he is also august." And the emperor was angry that the people cried out thus and said of Cyrus that after Constantine he had renewed the city, and the emperor was enraged at Cyrus. And they slandered him, saying that the patrician Cyrus alone ruled, and he was deposed from his rank.

And Cyrus fled and was tonsured a priest, and they sent him to Asia, and appointed him bishop in the city of Smyrna.
