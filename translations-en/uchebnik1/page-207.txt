They are received and placed among the stars, and afterward they become good spirits and demigods, and they show mercy to their kin with gentleness. But those who waste away in bodily illnesses, even if they are clean from impurity and sin, earthly darkness envelops them, and they remain there without memory. If death is appointed to people and no one can avoid it, then it is better to die by iron than by disease. And he who does not use a convenient occasion for such a death is foolish. Moreover, I said this to you not hoping for your salvation in this matter. However, it also happens that brave men, being in great dangers, are saved. By these stones of the ruined wall you can climb, while the new wall was recently built and will soon be destroyed. If you stand firm in this matter and help one another, the enemies, seeing your resolve, will abate their pride, so that it may happen that without harm and without blood you will win as soon as you approach the wall. I know that the Jews will try to prevent you from climbing the walls, but if you withstand the first onslaught, they will not hold out longer. And if I do not reward the one who first goes onto the wall with honor and gifts, to the envy of all, then shame be mine. If this warrior remains alive, let him command those to whom he is now equal. And if he falls and is killed, he will inherit respect and glory, and to those born of him there shall be eternal honor and recompense. Thus spoke Titus, and all the soldiers were frightened because of the great danger.

But one man, named Sabinus, a Syrian by birth, strong of hand and soul, volunteered to go first. He did not look like a warrior: he was dark, lean, and awkward, but he had a soul like a demigod’s, braver than his body. This Sabinus was the first to stand up and said to Titus: “O king! Now I boldly risk my head for you, and I am first to climb the walls, and I pray that your fortune may accompany my strength and will. If I fall before the work is finished, know that I did not retreat, but resolved to die for you.”

So said this Sabinus, and he took a shield in his left hand and raised it over his head, and in his right he took a drawn sword, and so went to the walls. It was the sixth hour of the day. And eleven more soldiers followed him, inspired by his courage. Sabinus went before all with wondrous zeal. The defenders of the city, seeing him, hurled spears at him from the walls and shot countless arrows, and threw down large stones, and thus struck down eight of the Romans who had come and forced them back.

But Sabinus withstood all that flew at him and, drowning in arrows, did not stop his advance until he had climbed to the top. There he engaged the guards and overcame them. And all the Jews were terrified at his strength and might and, thinking that many Romans had climbed up, fled. But not without reason do some reproach fate, saying that it is always envious of those who do good deeds and prevents them from completing their most glorious exploits.
