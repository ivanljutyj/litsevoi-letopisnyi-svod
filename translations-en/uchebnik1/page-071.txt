First, a letter about the death of Pheroras was delivered to Antipater, and Antipater made a very great mourning for him. And people thought that he did so out of love for Pheroras, because he was his uncle. But it was not so at all. For Antipater did not grieve for Pheroras, nor lament his death. Antipater grieved that he had lost the one who was to poison Herod with venom. And fear fell upon him, lest Herod learn about the poison. And when Antipater was in Cilicia and received from Herod the letter of which we have spoken, he rejoiced greatly.

And Antipater made a splendid feast for those who accompanied him and for the Romans. And the Romans took from him 300 talents of gold, for they are very greedy and insatiable for wealth. If you give the Romans much today, tomorrow they will demand still more. And just as the sea cannot be filled, nor hell satisfied, nor women’s lust, so too the Romans are insatiable for wealth. Truly they are leeches, of which Solomon speaks, giving their life and soul for reward, and they can give up the members of their own bodies, and brothers, and children. Some of them in their folly consider courage madness. Others lust after wealth as crows after carrion. Many for wealth can betray both cities and their own generals. But we shall write about the deeds of the Romans later, and now let us return to our account.

When Antipater arrived at Celenderis, he first of all learned of his mother’s exile, and great fear seized him, for his soul sensed the approach of misfortune. “For the spirit is divine and quickly senses what will be from God, as even the irrational creature does. And having from the airy spirit, man perceives beforehand what is brought about by the air.” The most sensible of Antipater’s close associates advised him not to fall into his father’s hands until Antipater learned the reason for his mother’s exile. Others, less sensible, wishing to see their own country as soon as possible and not thinking about Antipater’s benefit, advised him to appear before his father quickly. And they said to Antipater: “If you delay, your father will think that you are guilty, and the slanderers will have time to blacken you. If they have spoken against you now, it was only because of your departure; but if they see your face, they will not dare to slander you. It is unreasonable, for an unclear cause, to refuse an evident good. Therefore hurry to your father and receive the kingdom which he promised you.” And Antipater listened to these advisers.

But Divine Providence was leading Antipater. And he sailed to Caesarea, and a most glorious emptiness met him: all avoided him, and no one dared to approach him.
