He was there... And when Leo Phocas came to the village called Goliontovo, he was seized.

Romanos sent John Tuvakios and Leontios, his relative, to bring him to Constantinople.

Having seized him, they quenched his pupils and the lights of his eyes, blinding him and darkening him. And nothing had been said to them about execution, but they did this of their own will, at which the emperor’s father was indignant.

In the month of August it became known about the conspiracy of Constantine Ktimatines, David Kamoulianos, and Michael, overseer of the Manganes palace. They were beaten, publicly shamed, and sent into exile through the whole city.

Leontios, magistros and domestikos, was brought to Constantinople, and he paraded amid a gathering of people, riding on a female mule.

Empress Zoe, who had planned to prepare food with poison for Romanos, was taken by Theoklitos the notary, which was part of his official duties. She was led out of the palace, brought into the monastery of Saint Euphemia, and tonsured.

Theodore, the attendant of Emperor Constantine, and Simeon, his brother, were invited to dinner by Theophylact, patrician and komes.

When they were eating, John, the guard droungarios, called Kourkouas, entered with many people and, seizing them, sent them into exile in Opsikion, to their villages, as plotters against Romanos.
