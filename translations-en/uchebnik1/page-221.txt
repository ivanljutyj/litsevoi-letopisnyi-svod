If the temple perishes, it will be a loss to the Romans. If it remains, it will be an ornament to our whole empire.” Fronto and Alexander agreed with this opinion, and then Titus dismissed the counselors.

Titus ordered the commanders and the soldiers to rest, so that on the following day they might array themselves more firmly. On that same day fatigue and fear kept the Jews from battle.

On the following day the Jews gathered their whole army and boldly went out at the second hour of the day through the eastern gate and attacked the guards of the outer temple. The Romans stood firm and withstood the onslaught of the Jews, and arrayed themselves, and stood before the rebels like a wall, closed up tightly. But by fury and speed the Jews overcame the Romans. Titus saw the retreat of his soldiers, for he was watching from the Tower of Antonia, and came to their aid with chosen soldiers. The Jews could not withstand his attack, but fell before Titus, and the rest fled. When the Romans retreated, the Jews returned and attacked them. When the Romans turned back toward the Jews, they fled again. This continued until the fifth hour of the day, when the Jews at last fled and shut themselves in the inner temple.

Titus withdrew to the Tower of Antonia and prepared his soldiers to advance all together on the following day and surround the temple. But God had purposed otherwise and had already earlier condemned the temple to perish in fire. And the day appointed by fate for this destruction came—the tenth day of the month Loos, that is, August. On that very day the temple had earlier been burned by the king of Babylon. The flame began from the Jews themselves. When Titus withdrew, the Jews, having rested a little, again ran out against the Romans. These defeated the Jews and drove them as far as the temple, and themselves entered the temple.

In the temple one of the soldiers, not waiting for the emperor’s command and not fearing such an undertaking, with demonic boldness took fire, stood on a comrade’s shoulders, and threw it up to the golden window and to the doors through which one could get through to the temple buildings. When the flame blazed up, the Jews raised such a wail and cry as befitted that calamity, and rushed to hinder the fire and to avenge themselves on the Romans, sparing their lives, not guarding themselves, and not fearing.
