And near Ananus was Jesus, not such as Ananus, but the best among the others. But God condemned Jerusalem for the various sins of its inhabitants, and desired to cleanse this holy place with fire, and took away from the city its defenders, who were clothed in sacred garments and were leaders of the peaceful ministry. People came from the whole world to bow down to these men; now they were cast down, naked, and thrown to be devoured by dogs and wild beasts. Even righteousness groaned over them, because it had been overcome by malice.

Then the Idumeans and Zealots began to cut down the remaining people like a herd of worthless cattle. People of good families were bound and thrown into prison, expecting that someone among them would join the Zealots. And no one dared approach the prisoners—neither a relative to go to his relative, nor a son to his father—nor could anyone bury them. At night they took earth by handfuls and each sprinkled it on his dead. And twelve thousand of the nobles were killed.

And then Zechariah, son of Baruch, a very illustrious and very rich man, could not endure these murders and dared to denounce the evildoers and speak against them, if opportunity should arise. And the evildoers wanted to kill him and put him on trial. They gathered seventy of the elders and gave them authority to condemn the one who had railed against them. And the evildoers themselves stood before the judges and entered into debate with Zechariah, and said that he was betraying the city to the Romans. The judges said: "Who is the witness, or what evidence is there?" The Zealots answered: "We are eyewitnesses, we are witnesses; believe us." The judges said: "We cannot believe you, since you are the opposing party." Zechariah understood that no hope of salvation remained for him, and that he had been summoned not to a trial but to destruction; and first he laughed at the unjust speech of the accusers and in a few words turned aside the charge brought against him, and then he directed his speech against the slanderers. But they were rejoicing and wanted already to finish this mockery of a trial, and after the judges had asked their questions, they ordered them to judge according to truth. And the seventy judges found Zechariah innocent. They decided that if they too were to be killed, it was better for them to die with him than to live without him. The Zealots were angry with the judges, because they had accepted authority from them and had not understood that this was a mockery.

Two ungodly men attacked Zechariah and killed him in the middle of the temple, and, mocking him, said: "You are right in everything; here is your justification from us, and sure forgiveness." And they threw him into the nearest ravine by the temple. And against the seventy judges they drew swords and struck them on the neck with the flat of the sword, and drove them out of the court, so that they would scatter and tell everyone about the slavery that had come.
