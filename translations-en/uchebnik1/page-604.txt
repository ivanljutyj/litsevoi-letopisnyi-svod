It sanctifies all upon which it is laid—say, the water of baptism, and oil, and chrism, and the faces of believers, and the bread of the mystery, and the sacred cup, and all else; how then is the cross depicted from matter, as something vile and abominable, sanctified by baptism and matter and blood by you? But when Christ was nailed upon it in the flesh, He sanctified it by His blood and showed its image as sanctification to all believers: formerly it killed people, now demons, and to people it granted eternal life. Moreover, how dare you, making three wooden crosses and gathering them together, call them the Holy Trinity, and without trembling make an image of the blessed Trinity, one, uncreated, and uncountable?” The bishop's question. “For what reason do you fast with the Artsivurian fast?” The Armenians' answer. “In memory of a certain Sergius, an Armenian, who suffered in our country, we fast with such a fast.” The bishop's answer. “How can you, evil-faithed, prove that this Sergius was Orthodox and suffered for Christ?” The Armenians' answer. Being pressed, they said that it was the Ninevite fast, speaking falsely; then they said “Adam's”; then they said: “The Armenian, when they wished to be baptized”; then: “The fast of Constantine the Great.”

The bishop's answer. The saint, having exposed their lie from Scripture, said to them: “O foolish and slow of heart to believe truly; I will tell you what you yourselves know well: a certain Sergius, former teacher of the Armenian heresy, had a dog called Artsivurtsi; it was a messenger to the aforementioned Sergius, announcing his coming by demonic action, when he wished to come to a city or village where his disciples and partakers of his perdition lived; when they saw the aforementioned dog, they would go out a day in advance to meet the teacher. Once the dog was sent on such a vile service and was eaten by wolves; the next day Sergius came, and they did not meet him; and coming to them, he learned that his forerunner had not come to them, and he was greatly grieved, and sent to search for it, and they found only its bones. And because of that he commanded all Armenians to fast and grieve over its destruction on certain days of the year, and called the fast Artsivurtsev. Following this ordinance, you observe this fast.” They were seized with great shame, and many of them, repenting of their heresy, returned to the catholic church.

Hearing of this, the pious Emperor Manuel rejoiced greatly and wrote to him that he should also cleanse the Bogomil heresy from Christ's flock to the end, and send into exile those who did not wish to return to the catholic church. Likewise the Manichaeans and the disobedient Armenians, the saint sent all into exile and into various confinements; those who believed he numbered among his flock.
