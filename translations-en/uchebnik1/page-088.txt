Five hundred slaves carried precious incense in golden censers. Another five hundred slaves carried precious rose water in golden vessels. And five hundred horses in golden harness were led before the bier. And of every kind of golden weapon, one hundred items were carried. Herod’s body was carried five versts to the fortress Herodion, which he himself had built, and there they buried him. Such was the end of Herod’s life.

Book Two of Josephus. On the Conquest of Jerusalem

Archelaus mourned his father for seven days and arranged a lavish memorial banquet for the people, according to Jewish custom. Many are made poor by this custom, since many must be fed, even if one does not wish it. And if anyone does not fulfill this law, such a person is not considered virtuous.

Then Archelaus took off his mourning garments and put on royal ones. And he entered the assembly, and there they praised and glorified him in every way. And Archelaus greeted the people, seated on a golden throne on a raised place, and thanked them for the zeal they had shown at his father’s funeral, and also because they had gratified Archelaus and treated him as though he had already become king. And Archelaus said: "I will not dare to say that I am king until Caesar, who is lord over all, confirms my accession. For your loyalty and love I will give you whatever you ask. But when Caesar names me king, I will treat everyone better than my father did." The people rejoiced at these words and wanted to test how magnanimous Archelaus was, and they asked for even more, and shouted for him to lighten the taxes; others asked for an increase in pay; others for the release of the condemned. And Archelaus, wishing to please the people, said what they wanted, knowing that David and Solomon had obtained the kingdom of Israel by concessions, while Solomon’s son Rehoboam lost the kingdom because he would not make concessions. After this Archelaus offered sacrifice to God.

And Archelaus turned to merrymaking with his nobles. And then many of the Jews became drunk and began to lament and weep for the teachers of the Law and for those executed by Herod over the cutting down of the golden eagle.

This lamentation was open, and the wailing was loud, and the people were ordered to cry out, and deafening weeping was throughout the city, since the tortured were honored as those who had fallen for the ancestral law and for the Temple.
