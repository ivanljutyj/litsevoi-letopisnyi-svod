I say to you plainly that holy angels can separate us from our bodies in peace, and we can pass without sorrow and without hindrance through the principalities and powers of the air, if we have good guides, and if the demons do not find in us what they seek: they will see an undefiled body and be put to shame; they will see an incorrupt, pure, and radiant soul and be ashamed; they will see a tongue without blemish and guiltless and fall silent. And we shall pass by them and overcome them, saying: "Blessed is the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth. The snare is broken, and we are delivered." Then, as they meet us, angels would rejoice, the righteous would rejoice and kiss us, the venerable would be glad, saying: "Well have you come, friends of Christ and heirs of eternal blessings."

A Word of St Gregory the Theologian on the Departed. And again Gregory, surnamed the Theologian, at the burial of his brother Caesarius, said: "I submit to the wise words that every good and God-loving soul, when it is released from the body attached to it and exchanges this world for eternity, immediately receives perception and vision of the good that will be with it. And even while that which darkens it is being destroyed or laid aside—or I do not know how it is proper to speak of this—then already this good and God-loving soul enjoys a certain wondrous sweetness, rejoices, and with gladness awaits entry to its Master, for it has fled as from a cruel prison out of this life and has cast off the bonds that surround it, from which the wing of thought is drawn forth, so that a good soul, immediately upon its departure from the body, receives in imagination the sweetness of the blessedness that surrounds it."

A Word of St Athanasius on the Departed. Likewise Athanasius also says in the Life of St Anthony: "At one time the God-bearing Anthony stood for prayer at the ninth hour and realized that he was caught up in mind; and, without ceasing to stand, he saw himself as if he were outside himself, and certain beings led him through the air. Certain fierce and evil beings, standing in the air, also wanted to prevent Anthony from passing by them. But those who were leading Anthony resisted and fought for him. And those fierce and evil ones demanded an account of whether Anthony was guilty of anything, and they wanted to conduct the examination from Anthony's very birth. But those leading Anthony forbade them, saying: 'The Lord has blotted out what was from his birth. Only from the time Anthony became a monk and made his promise to God is it possible to make an examination.' And then they made this examination of Anthony's life and could not convict him, and the way became free and unhindered for him.
