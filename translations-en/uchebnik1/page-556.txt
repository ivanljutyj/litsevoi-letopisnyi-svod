Tsar Michael, together with Caesar Bardas and the parakoimomenos Basil the Macedonian, decided to go to war against Crete. But Leo the Philosopher begged Bardas not to go with them, saying: “If you go, you will not return.”

After the feast of Holy Easter the tsar set out with a large army and came into Thrace. When the army halted at a place called Vertograds, Basil gathered his intimates, and they began to deliberate how to destroy the Caesar. At that council were Marian, Subatius, and Bardas, Basil’s brothers, Asuleon his nephew, and also Peter the Bulgar, John the Chaldean, and Constantine the Archer.

But Comes John, having learned of this, came after sunset to the Caesar’s tent and said to Procopius, the Caesar’s servant, that when morning came his master, Caesar Bardas, would be cut to pieces.

Then Procopius went in to the Caesar to warn him. Bardas, having heard the servant, said: “Go and tell John: ‘Nonsense. You are young, and it is too early for you to bear the rank of patrician. You spread these rumors to get what you want.’” But all night the Caesar did not close his eyes.

As soon as dawn broke, Bardas summoned his intimates and told them everything, expecting to hear sensible advice from them. Then Philotheus, his friend, a protospatharius and genikos by rank, said: “In the morning, my lord, put on your gold-woven garment and appear before your enemies, and they will flee from your face.”

When the sun rose, Bardas, having put on his shining garment, mounted his horse and, surrounded by numerous intimates, headed for the tsar’s tent. With him was his chief saddler Eustathius, called Argus, that is, the Silversmith, a faithful and strong man.

But Constantine the Archer came out to meet Bardas with a bow (such was the agreement with Basil) and, returning, informed Basil of the Caesar’s arrival.

Basil came out, bowed to him, and taking him by the hand, entered the tsar’s tent together with him.

Caesar Bardas, sitting beside the tsar, said to him: “All the army, my lord, is assembled. Only give the order, and it will set out for Crete immediately.” At that time Basil, standing behind his back, gave a sign with his hand, pointing at the Caesar.
