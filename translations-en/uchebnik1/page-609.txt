Prince Yaroslavets rushed past them; suddenly they sprang out swiftly and pierced him with a spear.

His retinue galloped after him and took him, barely breathing and at death’s door; and they carried him into the tent, and there he soon died. All his countless warriors gathered—Hungarians, Czechs, Poles, the Pinyane, the Rostislavtsy, Volodar with Vasilko—and they wept much over him.

They also sent to the city of Vladimir to Prince Andrei Vladimirovich Monomakh, saying: “Prince Yaroslavets, your kinsman, sought his table, the city of Vladimir, and brought us to his aid; and now he lies dead, and there is no reason for us to fight with anyone.”

Prince Andrei Vladimirovich, hearing this, rejoiced and made peace with them, and gave them great honor and many gifts. And they buried Prince Yaroslavets in the city of Vladimir, which he had sought, desiring to live in it while alive, but suddenly he was laid in it dead.

For it is written: “Not to him who wills, nor to him who runs, but to God who shows mercy; for all things are possible with God, and nothing with men.” Prince Andrei Vladimirovich Monomakh established himself in the principality in the city of Vladimir, and all the army of Prince Yaroslavets dispersed to their homes.

In the same year the stone church of St. Michael fell in Pereyaslavl on the tenth day of the month of May, on Saturday, before vespers, which the most blessed Ephrem, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, had built and adorned in Pereyaslavl, in his own city.

In the same year Vsevolod Mstislavich went from Novgorod with the Novgorodians against the Yem and defeated them; but the way was difficult, and they bought bread for a nogata. In the same year Vsevolod Mstislavich married in Novgorod.

In the same year His Eminence Nikita, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus, confined the evil heretic Dmitr in prison in his city of Sinelitsa.

In the year 6632 from Adam (the year 1132 from Christ)

There was a great fire in Kiev, such that nearly all of it burned in two days; and along the Podol it burned, so that almost seven hundred churches alone burned; this happened on the 23rd and 24th days of June, on the Nativity of John the Forerunner.

In the same year there was a sign in the sun: on August 11, at the ninth hour of the day, before vespers, the sun began to wane and became like a small moon, and it grew dark in the afternoon, and all vanished, and darkness came and great fear, and there were stars, and the moon; and again the sun began to wax quickly, and became full, and all rejoiced.
