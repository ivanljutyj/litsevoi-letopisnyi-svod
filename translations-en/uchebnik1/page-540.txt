On the Slander against Patriarch Methodius. The heretics who secretly professed iconoclasm, seeing that Annius had been removed from the patriarchal throne and Methodius raised to it, bribed a certain harlot with gifts, sent her to Patriarch Methodius, and accused him of adultery.

Then, wishing to end the scandal, he exposed his private parts before everyone. And all saw them completely withered. He told them: “Once, when I was in Rome, a demon of fornication troubled me. And I began to pray to the holy apostle Peter that he might deliver me from such torment. Appearing to me at night, he touched my loins with his right hand and said: ‘You shall have no more of this.’ When I awoke, I could scarcely move; since then my flesh has remained deadened to this day, as you see.” And all glorified God.

While Michael was young, his virtuous mother, Empress Theodora, cared for him, wisely governing the heavily burdened ship of the kingdom like a skilled helmsman.

When Michael reached youthful age, he became excessively addicted to sensuality, drunkenness, and fornication, making himself like Nero. He squandered the entire treasury on the actors, buffoons, charioteers, and other impious men around him, greedy for refined food and drunk already in the morning; spending all his days with harlots, amusing himself and committing adultery, like Sardanapalus, who had reigned before, the last successor of Ninus, Michael also drank and sang obscene songs. And he ended his vile life like Actaeon, torn apart by his own dogs, which attacked him as though he were a beast.

Into what pits of passion did the actors surrounding Michael—men with shaved beards and whitened faces—not plunge him! It reached the point that he himself rode in a gold-forged chariot drawn by four horses.

Meanwhile the imperial treasury was being depleted; and every rabble surrounding the emperor grew rich. Thus unworthily and dissolutely Michael spent his life, often appearing alone, without bodyguards and armor-bearers, at the city hippodrome, shamelessly amusing himself with spectacles and rejoicing in horse races. He was not even ashamed to take into his arms the children of the charioteers participating in the races, like a loving father.
