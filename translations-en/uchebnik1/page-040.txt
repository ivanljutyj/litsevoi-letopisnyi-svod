And Herodias’s daughter Salome, having danced, asked that the head of John the Forerunner be cut off.

The second son was Herod Philip, whose wife Herodias had first been, and they had a daughter, Salome, who was the one who danced.

The third son was Herod Agrippa; he killed James, the son of Zebedee, about whom the people cried out: “Behold, the voice of God, and not of man.” Soon the wretched Herod Agrippa received condemnation, and for several days he rotted and was eaten by worms, and in cruel torments he ended his life, sending to the Lord the second martyr after Saint Stephen. This Agrippa, also called Herod, was clothed in a silver garment, and went out to the spectacle, and sat on a high throne, and addressed the people. The sun’s rays fell upon the garment, and a radiance like the dawn issued from the garment, and the faces of those standing by shone, and the wretched man became even more puffed up. And soon the angel of the Lord suddenly struck him, and Agrippa sprang from the throne, because his belly was cruelly tormented. And the divine apostle James, the son of Zebedee, was led to the path of death, and the one who had slandered him saw this, and repented, and fell down before the apostle with the words: “Forgive me, man of God, for I repent that I slandered you and thereby sinned.” But the blessed one kissed him and said to him: “Peace be with you, child; peace be with you concerning your transgression.” He then, with a great voice, called himself a Christian before all, and so joined the one whom he had slandered, and with him received the crown of martyrdom. Herod cut off James’s head, following the example of his impious father, for he too rose up against Christ and, in his madness, gathered and slaughtered His peers, the infants. And Herod was punished even in this life, and he shattered his own life; and it is just that judgment came upon the unrighteous man. After his departure from this light, the wretched man received torments, which was shown plainly. For immediately he received from God a sent deadly wound, which drove him into a merciless death. Therefore, as Josephus relates, his body was seized by disease and torn by various sufferings: there was a strong fever, and a terrible itching over his whole body and face, and continual deadly pains; his legs festered and began to stink; his belly burned; his private parts rotted, giving off worms; moreover his breathing became disordered and all his limbs began to twitch, and a foul stench always came from his mouth, so that his servants fled from him and loathed him and said: “This sickness is sent by God.” These sufferings afflicted him for a long time, and he could not endure so severe an illness, so that he even dared to end it himself. He asked for a fresh apple and a knife, as though he wished to cut and eat it himself; and he took the knife and directed it at his own throat, and wanted to stab himself.
