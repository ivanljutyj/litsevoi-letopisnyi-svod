They are received and placed among the stars, and afterward become good spirits and demigods, and show mercy to their kin with gentleness. But those who waste away in bodily illnesses, even if they are pure from defilement and sin, are nevertheless encompassed by earthly darkness, and there they remain without memory. If death is appointed to men, and no one can escape it, then it is better to die by iron than by disease. And he who does not use a convenient occasion for such a death is foolish. Moreover, I have said this to you not hoping for your salvation in this matter. Yet it also happens that brave men, though in great dangers, are saved. By these stones of the ruined wall you can climb up, while the new wall was recently built and will soon be destroyed. If you stand firm in this matter and help one another, then the enemies, seeing your determination, will weaken their pride, so that it may happen that without harm and without blood you will conquer as soon as you come to the wall. I know that the Jews will try to hinder you from climbing the walls, but if you endure the first onslaught, they will not hold out any longer. And if I do not bestow honor and gifts on him who first goes to the wall, to the envy of all, then let shame be mine. If this warrior remains alive, let him govern those with whom he is now equal. But if he falls and is killed, he will inherit respect and glory, and those born from him will have eternal honor and reward.” Thus spoke Titus, and all the soldiers were afraid because of the great danger.

One man, named Sabinus, a Syrian by birth, strong in both hands and soul, volunteered to go first. He did not look like a warrior: he was swarthy, lean, and awkward, but in him was a soul like that of a demigod, braver than his body. This Sabinus stepped forward first and said to Titus: “O king! Now I boldly give my head for you, and I am first to ascend the walls, and I pray that your good fortune may accompany my strength and will. If I fall before the deed is finished, know that I did not retreat, but resolved to die for you.”

Having said this, Sabinus took the shield in his left hand and raised it over his head, and in his right he took a drawn sword, and so went to the walls. It was at the sixth hour of the day. And there followed him eleven more warriors, inspired by his courage. Sabinus went before all with wondrous zeal. The defenders of the city, seeing him, hurled spears at him from the walls and shot countless arrows, and threw down great stones, and so struck eight of the advancing Romans and turned them back.

Sabinus withstood all that flew at him and, drowning in arrows, did not cease his advance until he climbed to the top. There he engaged the guards and defeated them. And all the Jews were terrified at his strength and might, and thinking that many Romans had climbed up, fled. But not without reason do some reproach fortune, saying that she is always envious of those who do good deeds and hinders them from completing most glorious exploits.
