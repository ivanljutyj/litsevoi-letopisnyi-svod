The pus was called “true fiery coals”; as they say, it spread over the whole body, causing dangerous calamities to the afflicted. And this pus appeared especially often in the eyes, so that it made tens of thousands of men, and women together with children, blind. At the same time, a revolt of the Armenians rose up against the tormentor. This happened at the same time as the illness of Maximian himself, because of his vileness, before God clearly exposing his pride and boasting: supposedly, because of his idolatry, neither captivity, nor famine, nor pestilence, nor wars occurred in his time. And while he was boasting, all this fell upon him, and he received his overthrow for instruction. For he himself was exhausted by the war with the Armenians together with his soldiers, while the rest of the inhabitants of cities and villages suffered from terrible famine and pestilence, and died in countless numbers in the cities, and even more in the villages and hamlets, and almost all perished swiftly, withering from lack of food and disease. And some chewed grass and, harming nature, perished; while others, weakened, like senseless idols swaying from side to side, exhausted and shaken, went about and fell at the crossroads, so that dead and naked bodies lay unburied for many days, seen in great numbers, becoming terrible food. And the pestilence did not cease in all the houses. Such was the punishment for boasting, and such was his end by an evil death. On the pillar he wrote that if all Christians were killed, then, as he thought, every prosperity and abundant rain would fill the Greek land. Because of this, pestilence, famine, darkness, and every evil that can be named came upon people, as has been said.

The 42nd Reign, of Constino, father of Constantine, who reigned in Rome

At that time Diocletian, having killed Carinus, the son of Carus, reigned in his place in Rome, making Maximian and Herculius co-rulers. Then these kings raised a terrible persecution against Christians.

And soon God brought all kinds of wrath upon the Roman land, because of which the Persians and Parthians, that is, the Sauromatians, with their king Uarkh and with a multitude of other barbarians, seized the Roman lands.

And as a result, the holders of the Roman scepters, namely Diocletian and Maximian Herculius, having consulted among themselves, sent to their priests, to Theon and Meneus, ordering them to collect from the Western limits to the East a tribute for the Parthian king Uaraakh, deciding among themselves to make peace, so that the Parthians would never attack the Romans, and to pay them peaceful tributes, and for the Romans to remain under tribute to the Persians, as it was.
