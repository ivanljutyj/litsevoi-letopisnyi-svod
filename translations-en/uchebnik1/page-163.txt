When Titus and Agrippa were going through Achaia by a long sea route in winter and had not yet reached Rome, Galba was killed. Galba had been emperor for 7 months and 7 days. After him Otho came to power.

Agrippa was not afraid and sailed to Rome. Titus, by God's providence, sailed from Hellas to Syria and quickly came to Caesarea to his father.

There were two opinions among the Romans about the war in Judea. Some advised fighting the Jews, as they had been fighting.

Others, learning that a revolt had arisen in Rome, feared for their fatherland and considered that it was not the time to fight in a foreign land. In those same days another disaster occurred in Jerusalem. There was a man there, Simon, son of Gioras, by birth from Gerasa. In deceit he was inferior to the robber John, that is Johanan, but in strength and courage he surpassed him. Therefore Ananus the high priest deprived Simon of his authority. Then Simon fled to the evildoers who had occupied Masada, a fortress near Jerusalem, and joined them. The robbers at first did not believe him and sent him to live in the outer fortress with wives and servants. The robbers themselves stayed in the inner fortress.

And when they went out to rob, Simon went with them. And they, seeing his abilities, grew accustomed to him and began to obey him. And he promised freedom to slaves and honor to free men.

Simon gathered around himself all kinds of wicked men and ravaged all the others. Many of the nobles saw his strength and success and joined Simon, and honored him as king, and obeyed his commands.

Simon found a fortified place called Nain, and there built himself something like a fortress, and dug many underground chambers and enlarged them, and brought there everything he plundered. And Nain was a refuge for him, and the underground chambers a storehouse for the plunder.

It was clear that Simon was preparing his soldiers for a campaign against Jerusalem. And the Zealots anticipated his arrival, armed themselves, and went against Simon. Simon raised his soldiers and fought with the Zealots, and killed many, and drove the rest as far as Jerusalem.

Then Simon with twenty thousand soldiers attacked the lands of Idumea.

The leaders of the Idumeans quickly gathered 15 thousand of the strongest men and resisted Simon, and fought from morning until evening.

Neither of them won, and they dispersed: Simon to Nain, and the Idumeans to their homes.
