In the thirty-ninth year of his reign and in the tenth month he established and gave orders to make a census of all the lands subject to him, which the Romans then held, during the second governorship of Agrippa. And all that was subject to Rome was described by Eumenes and Autalus, Roman nobles.

Great was the fear of Augustus, for he was very fierce and wrathful, and he was quick to pronounce condemnation, but afterward came to repentance and gentleness. Once he was sitting and administering justice, and he condemned many people to immediate and swift death.

A certain wise man could not approach Caesar and instruct him because of the crowd, so he wrote on a sheet: “Murderer, cease judging,” and sealed it and threw it to Caesar; he took it and read it.

And Caesar rose and left the throne and left all business unfinished; with him was a wise teacher from Alexandria named Athenodorus. He leaned to Caesar’s ear and said: “O Caesar Sebastus, one thing is lacking in you: do not pronounce sentence quickly, but first count silently to twenty-four, and when your wrath subsides within you, then judge each one according to justice without anger.”

This wise Athenodorus saw that Caesar was possessed by lust and wholly given to fornication, and that desire tormented him most of all for married women, and that by his authority he ordered husbands to bring their wives to him. Athenodorus wished to dissuade Caesar from such passion.

One day Athenodorus saw a certain counselor weeping, because he had been ordered to shut his wife in a litter and send her to Caesar. Athenodorus comforted him and ordered himself to be shut in the litter instead of the counselor’s wife and sent to Caesar. And Athenodorus entered the litter with a sword.

Caesar received the litter and broke the seals, and then Athenodorus came out, brandishing a sword. Caesar was amazed when he saw Athenodorus and grew numb with fear. Athenodorus said: “I have done this to teach you. If another had done this, one of those whose wives you have taken and defiled, he would certainly have killed you.” And Caesar bound himself with oaths that he would do such a thing no more.

When Athenodorus was departing for the land that had brought him forth, Caesar sighed from the depths and said: “I still need your good instruction; be with me, and Rome shall have you.”

This Caesar drove buffoons from him and honored only the wise, and honored them diligently. And for this reason they honored Athenodorus as no other noble had been honored in death. And Athenodorus said: “It is well for me that I die leaving Caesar alive.”
