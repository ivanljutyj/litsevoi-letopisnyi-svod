And Simon approached the walls of Jerusalem like a wounded beast, and since he could not reach those who had wounded him, he vented his wrath on any who came his way. Whoever came out—whether to gather vegetables or to fetch firewood, old men, women, or other weak folk—Simon cut off their hands and feet and sent them back into the city, so that the townspeople would be terrified. And Simon ordered the people he had mutilated to say in the city: “Simon swears by the living God, the Almighty, the All-Seeing, that if you do not soon return his wife to him, he will tear down the walls and enter, and will do to all the townspeople as he has done to us, and will spare no age, and will not inquire who is guilty and who is innocent.”

And they in Jerusalem were frightened by Simon’s threats and sent his wife out to him; then he was calmed and for a time ceased killing.

At that same time there was rebellion and discord not only in Judea but also in Italy. The emperor Galba was killed in the midst of Rome, and Otho was made emperor. Vitellius, with Roman soldiers from Germany, fought against Otho. On the first day Otho prevailed, and on the second Vitellius.

During the night Vitellius scattered special devices with three iron spikes, and when morning came, Vitellius pretended to flee, and Otho pursued him with soldiers and chased him to the place where those iron devices had been strewn, and the horses were lamed, and neither could the horses get out of there nor the soldiers themselves.

And Vitellius’s soldiers returned and killed all who lay there. Otho saw this and cut his own throat.

On the Lawlessness of the Inhabitants of Jerusalem

Vespasian, however, hastened against the remaining unconquered cities of Judea, conquered all the Gophnitic and Acrabatene regions and the cities of Bethila and Ephraim, and from there rode swiftly to Jerusalem.

Cerealis, Vespasian’s commander, was laying waste Upper Idumea and came to the city of Capharabin; he saw its walls strong and high and wanted to raise a siege mound. Suddenly the townspeople came out, begged for mercy, and surrendered. And no cities remained unsubdued except Herodium, Masada, and Machaerus. Jerusalem stood among them as the chief stronghold.

Simon, the leader of the brigands, after he had rescued his wife, stood by Jerusalem and again began to cut down all who came out of the city.
