The divine fathers cried out, saying: “We anathematize all those of whom it is spoken, together with all who have spoken and thought, or think, or will think such things, even to the end of times.” Likewise, concerning Theodore of Mopsuestia and the others, it was then said in the imperial writing: “It was the care of our Orthodox and pious forefathers who reigned before us that at all times heretical shoots be cut off by the councils of the venerable fathers, and that the right faith be preached purely in the world, and that the Holy Church of God be preserved.”

On the First Council. Thus, Constantine the Great opposed Arius, who blasphemed and said: “The Son is not consubstantial with God the Father, but is a creature, and was made out of nothing.” Constantine assembled 318 holy fathers in Nicaea, and he himself came then, and when Arius had been anathematized, he strove to establish the Orthodox faith. Therefore the divine fathers confessed: “The Son is consubstantial with God the Father.” Even to this day we thus sing and believe.

On the Second Council. Under Theodosius the Elder, Macedonius denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit, and Apollinaris and his disciple blasphemed against the dispensation of God the Word and said: “The Word of God did not receive a human mind, but was united to flesh having an irrational soul.” Theodosius assembled 150 holy fathers in Constantinople, and he himself took part in the council, and anathematized the aforementioned heretics, and destroyed their impious teaching and those who thought together with them, and established the preaching of the Orthodox faith.

On the Third Council. Under Theodosius the Younger, the impious Nestorius said that God the Word is one thing and Christ another; and he argued that God the Word is the Son of God the Father by nature, while Christ is the Son of God by grace; and this Nestorius rejected that the Holy Mary is the Theotokos. Theodosius assembled 200 holy fathers for the council in Ephesus, and sent for the nobles who wished to be present at the council, and ordered Nestorius also to come, and the holy fathers to make an investigation concerning him. And there was a rigorous investigation, and they condemned Nestorius, and anathematized those who thought with him.

On the Orthodox. After this event, those who were among the rejected Nestorians rose up against the venerable Cyril, and they tried, as far as was in their power, to have the former condemnation reviewed.
