Having received many gifts, this false Alexander sailed to the city of Mylae, where everyone also believed him, and all the people escorted him as far as Rome with honor and gifts, as if he were a king. And those who had seen the real Alexander alive swore that this was indeed he. And all the Jews of Rome gathered to see him, while the Jews of Mylae carried him, lifting him up together with his throne, sang joyful songs before him, and spent much to please him.

Caesar, however, knew well what Alexander looked like, since he had stood before him when he was at law with Herod. And Caesar understood the deception: his face was different, and his body was coarser than Alexander’s, which betrayed the slave in the impostor. And Caesar was greatly enraged, and seized the young man, and said: “I will grant you your life if you tell me who taught you to do this.” Then the seized man betrayed the Jew who had urged him to this deception, pointing out his resemblance to Alexander. And the young man said: “We have received as many gifts throughout the cities as Alexander himself did not receive while he was alive.” Caesar heard him out with a smile and assigned him to his rowers. And the Jews of Mylae, because they had carried him in their arms and shown him royal honor, were killed.

On John the Forerunner

At that same time a certain man among the Jews went about in strange clothing, having attached animal skins to his body at the places not covered by his hair, and in face he was like a wild man. And this man came to the Jews and called them to freedom, and said: “God has sent me to show you the lawful path, by which you shall be delivered from many rulers, and no mortal shall rule over you, save the Most High who sent me.” And people heard this and rejoiced. And people came to him from all Judea and from the regions around Jerusalem. And he did nothing else to them except immerse them in the waters of the river Jordan and send them away, instructing them to cease doing evil, and then a King would be given to them who would save them and subdue all the disobedient, while He Himself would submit to no one. At these words some laughed, while others believed them.

And they brought this man to the ruler Archelaus, and the teachers of the law gathered and asked him who he was and where he had been until that time. He answered them: “I am a man whom the Spirit of God led into the wilderness, and I fed on stalks and roots and shoots of trees.” They threatened him with torments if he did not renounce these words and deeds.
