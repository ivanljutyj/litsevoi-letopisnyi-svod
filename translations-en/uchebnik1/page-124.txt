And even before they saw the Roman army before them, the Jews fled with Josephus to Tiberias. And all the inhabitants of Tiberias were filled with fear, realizing that Josephus would not have fled had he not despaired of victory. Josephus foresaw what end awaited the Jews and realized that there would be no salvation for them unless they repented. He himself hoped for the mercy of the Romans, but he decided it was better to die with his people than to betray his fatherland and the authority given to him. And Josephus wrote to the rulers in Jerusalem the truth about everything, so that they might either agree to peace or send him help.

Vespasian took the Galilean city of Gabara and the surrounding villages, and spared neither old men nor infants, but put all to death.

Then Vespasian went to Jotapata. Learning of this, Josephus came there from Tiberias and supported the Jews. And someone informed Vespasian of Josephus’s arrival, and at once Vespasian moved toward Jotapata, thinking that with Josephus he would take all Judea.

And Vespasian sent Placidus to Jotapata with a thousand horsemen, so that they might surround the city and not let Josephus escape.

Vespasian himself, after one day, came with his whole army to Jotapata before noon and placed his foot soldiers around the city in two rows, and behind them another row of horsemen, so that all roads out of the city were blocked for the besieged.

The Jews came out and stood before the gates. Vespasian sent against them archers, stone-throwing engines, and everything that strikes from a distance, while he himself with foot soldiers began to climb toward the city where the walls could already be undermined.

Josephus was frightened by this, and he hastily came out of the city with all the Jews, and they engaged the Romans and drove them back, and fought all day.

At night the adversaries parted. That day the Romans had many wounded and thirteen killed, while the Jews had six hundred wounded and eight killed.

On the next day the Jews came out and attacked the Romans very fiercely, but the enemy proved even stronger than they. And so for five days they fought, and one could see spears breaking, swords grating, shields crackling, warriors rushing about, and the earth soaked with blood. Yet the Jews did not fear the Romans, just as the Romans did not despair when they saw how fortified the city was. This city stands entirely over a precipice whose bottom the human eye cannot discern. Only on the north was the approach narrow, since a mountain slope came up to the wall.
