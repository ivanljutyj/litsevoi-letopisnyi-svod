Even now evidence of this wrath of God can be seen in the land of Sodom: the shadows of the five overthrown cities, and the fruits that grow on the trees of Sodom, beautiful and seemingly edible, but if anyone touches them, they turn to dust and smoke.

After taking Jericho, Vespasian shut in the inhabitants of Jerusalem on every side, and entrenched himself in Jericho and in Adida, and from there sent Lucius with an army against Gerasa.

Lucius suddenly attacked the city and took Gerasa, and killed a thousand people, and sent soldiers through the villages, and cut down the inhabitants, and burned the villages. The women and children he took captive. Thus the whole hill country and the surroundings of Jerusalem were conquered.

All paths for the inhabitants of Jerusalem were closed. If anyone inside the city wished to flee to the Romans, the Zealots did not allow it, for they were always on guard and watched that no one went out. Even if the city’s inhabitants wished to go for bread or hay, or to bring in firewood, they could not get through.

Vespasian returned from Jericho to Caesarea and prepared the whole army to march against Jerusalem at dawn.

And when Vespasian was preparing to go, word was brought to him that Nero had been killed. Nero was emperor for thirteen years and eight days. When this Nero received power, he gave all affairs into the hands of wicked men, who then rose up against him and drove him out, and he fled to his village with four servants, for all the rest had left him, and there Nero killed himself.

After Nero, Galba was made emperor, and he came from Spain, and the soldiers were displeased with him and killed him, because he was humble.

And they made Otho emperor. Vitellius, a Roman commander, rose against him and replaced Otho. Then there was a rebellion against Vitellius himself, and a battle at the Capitol.

Antonius Primus and Mucianus, supporters of Vespasian, killed Vitellius, and defeated the Roman regiments that had fought in Germany, and halted the internal strife in Italy.

When Vespasian learned of Nero’s death, he decided not to go to Jerusalem until it was known who had become emperor. Having learned that Galba had become emperor, Vespasian did not dare continue the war until a command concerning this should come to him from Galba. And Vespasian sent Titus, his son, to Galba to greet him and to receive a written directive concerning the fate of the Jewish war. King Agrippa also sailed with Titus for the purpose of greeting Galba.
