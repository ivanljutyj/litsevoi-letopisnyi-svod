And they began to set up wall-breaking engines. Joseph and Chares, who were leaders in the city, saw that they could resist for only a few days without water and without engines, and they urged and encouraged their own men to leave the city.

When Joseph and Chares led the inhabitants of Gamala out of the city, they stood for a short time, but could not endure the stones and Roman spears flying at them, and retreated to the city.

The Romans placed “rams”—special wall-breaking engines—in three places, and no one hindered them, and they destroyed the walls.

And the Romans sounded trumpets, shouted, and clashed their weapons, and entered the city, and attacked the townspeople with noise. But they were surrounded on every side, and could not resist, and fled to the upper part of the city.

The Romans followed them, and the Jews turned and attacked them from the height, and killed them, since the place was narrow and awkward for the Romans. The Romans who were below could not help those above in the battle, and could not run out of the city because of the pressing mass of their own men, and the Romans harmed one another, and did not know what to do.

And the Romans began climbing onto the city buildings with their heavy weapons, and the roofs filled with Romans, and the buildings could not bear their weight, and collapsed, and fell upon one another, and crushed all the soldiers. Some were struck by stones, others choked in lime dust, and many of the Romans were buried alive under the rubble. Few of those who ran out from there were uninjured. Most of all, the Romans suffocated from the dust.

The inhabitants of Gamala thought this was divine aid, and, not caring that they harmed themselves by breaking down their own houses, pressed zealously upon the Romans, driving them onto the roofs. The streets were narrow, steep, and slippery. And the Romans with their heavy weapons kept falling, not knowing where to flee. The townspeople, being above, slaughtered the Romans like cattle; they threw stones from the ruined buildings, and pulled weapons from the hands of fallen Romans, and turned them against those still breathing.
