And this Ardabur sent the Goths to help, and brought the body of Simeon to Antioch the Great.

And they built for Simeon the Stylite a great house of prayer, and placed in it a reliquary with his relics, in the fourth year of the reign of Anastasius. Simeon was on the pillar forty years. Under this same Emperor Anastasius was John the Faster.

This same Emperor Anastasius was at first pious, but soon spewed forth evil poison, for he had hidden in his heart the evil seeds of the heresy of the accursed Eutyches. From this came a dark cloud, and a fierce storm, and heavy waves shook the ship of Orthodoxy.

Because of this, pious hierarchs were driven from their thrones like evil-doing robbers. Along with others, Anastasius condemned to exile Euphemius, Patriarch of Constantinople, and Flavian, Patriarch of Antioch, and Elias, Patriarch of Jerusalem, who did not obey Anastasius and refused to reject the Fourth Council; and these were exiled by Anastasius as Nestorians to the city of Petra, in so-called Third Palestine.

Emperor Anastasius, however, obeyed the impious heresy of Dioscorus and rejected the Fourth Council. And Anastasius sent to Jerusalem and compelled the pious people toward heresy, and appointed the presbyter Severus patriarch in Antioch the Great; this Severus was from among the monks.

Sabbas and Theodosius with ten thousand monks entered the Great Church and, stepping onto the ambo, cried out: “If anyone does not accept the four councils as the four evangelists, let him be cursed.” The emperor wrote a decree for their exile, but this was not permitted by God. The emperor oppressed the pious people everywhere. And from this there were frequent lamentations and wailings, cries and sighs everywhere, and beatings of the breast everywhere. Moreover, Emperor Anastasius wished to add the poison of impiety to the Trisagion hymn, namely: “Holy Immortal, crucified God, have mercy on us.”

And Anastasius sent the logothete, that is, the secretary, and the eparch, and when they had gone up onto the ambo in the Great Church to announce the emperor’s command to the people, a multitude of the pious, seeing this, rushed forward at once and drove the eparch and the logothete out of the church with stones, and set their houses on fire, and set many noble houses on fire, and cut down the nobles who were in their houses.

And the people came, and the emperor was mocked by them, and they rose up against the emperor. The emperor feared the people’s onslaught, and fled with the boyars, and hid himself. Thus the pious man is not ashamed before the emperor, but when piety is in danger, then he acts without partiality and does not fear the mighty of this world.
