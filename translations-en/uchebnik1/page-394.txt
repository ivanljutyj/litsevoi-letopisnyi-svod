And they sent Just’s head with the crown to Emperor Zeno. And then Emperor Zeno made the Samaritan synagogue that was on Mount Gerizim into a house of prayer of the Mother of God Mary. And Zeno restored the church of Saint Procopius that had been destroyed by the Samaritans. And Zeno issued a decree that no Samaritan should be placed in the army. And there was fear and peace.

In the reign of Zeno, Theodoric, deprived of the consulship, son of Theodemir, was a Byzantine commander, and he saw that Armatus had been received by Zeno, and he feared the emperor, and took his helpers, and went out from Constantinople to the river Salamvria, and captured those living there. And after this Theodoric crossed into Thrace and captured all of it. And he came against Zeno, and approached the suburb of Smokovnitsy, and he was outside Constantinople, and remained there many days, and could not harm the city.

And Theodoric departed from Constantinople and hastened to Rome, which King Odoacer, a barbarian, held in his power. And Theodoric fought with Odoacer. And all the Roman nobles conspired and betrayed Odoacer, and Theodoric received them without taking them captive; and he killed King Odoacer and took Rome.

And Theodoric became king in Rome in place of Odoacer and reigned 47 years. Theodoric then became a friend to Zeno and did everything according to his will, and recognized the consuls of Constantinople and the eparchs, and received from Emperor Zeno letters for his great princes, informing him who was coming for renewal in rank, and that one received the consular staff from this emperor. When this Theodoric was king in Rome, a certain noblewoman named Juvenalia came to him and told him: “For three years my dispute with the patrician Formus has lasted, but deliver me from it.” And judges were brought from both sides, and Theodoric said to them: “If you do not answer concerning their case and judge it by tomorrow, and do not resolve their dispute by the third day, I will cut off your heads.” And the judges went away for two days, and answered Theodoric, and resolved their dispute.

And Juvenalia lit a candle and came to Theodoric, giving thanks that she had been delivered from that dispute. And the king became angry with the judges and said: “How is it that they could not do in three years what they did in two days?” And Theodoric cut off the heads of both judges. And there was great fear. And Theodoric established order in all matters of law.

And Theodoric left the city of Rome and began to live in Ravenna, a coastal city, and lived there until his death.

And after his death his grandson Athalaric was king in Rome. He was an Arian by faith.
