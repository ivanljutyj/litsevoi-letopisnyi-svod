Yet do not the Thracians obey two thousand Romans? The Illyrians, the Dalmatians, and the Dacians, called Bulgars and living in Istria, are under a single Roman regiment. What need is there to speak of the Heniochi, the Colchians, the Tauri, the Bosporans, and all the peoples who live around the Maeotian Sea, who formerly had not even rulers of their own, and now are subdued by one thousand Romans? If any of the peoples could have made war against Rome, it was the Gauls, who are enclosed on the east by the Alps, on the north by the Rhine, on the south by the Pyrenees, and on the west by the ocean. But although they are so enclosed, and three hundred and five peoples submit to them, and their riches are like the sand of the sea, nevertheless they submit and pay tribute to the Romans, and gather riches not for themselves but for others. For eighty years they fought the Romans for their freedom, but Roman fortune subdued the Gauls to them even without weapons, and now 1,200 Roman soldiers keep them in slavery. The Gauls have more cities than the people who rule over them. And the Iberians, whose land bears gold and who live as far from the Romans as heaven is from earth, and the Lusitanians, and the blood-drinking Cantabrians, who have a breathing sea—even they did not escape the Romans. The Romans came as if under a cloud, and bowed these peoples beneath their weapons, and now one Roman regiment possesses them. The Parthians, rulers over a thousand peoples, are also obedient to Rome. The Carthaginians, famous for their great king Hannibal and their noble descent from the Phoenicians, fell under the hand of Scipio. Neither the inhabitants of Cyrenaica, tracing their lineage from Laconia, nor the Marmarides, nor the Dapsidians, nor the fearsome Syrtians, nor the Nasamones, nor the Moors, nor the steppe peoples could avoid Roman dominion. Even that land which occupies a third of the universe, inhabited by countless peoples, enclosed by the Atlantic Ocean, the Pillars of Hercules, and the Red Sea, including Ethiopia—even this land is under the Romans. And it feeds the Romans with the fruits of the earth for eight months a year, and gives tribute and everything needed by Roman governors, and does not consider it shameful, as you do, to submit to Rome. But why do I tell you of distant lands, when I can show Roman power by the example of Egypt, close to us? That Egypt extended its borders to Ethiopia, to Arabia, and to India. Seven million five hundred thousand people inhabit it, not counting the inhabitants of Alexandria, as the poll tax shows. But even Egypt does not refuse Roman rule. The city of Alexandria is so large, populous, and rich that it can withstand all Egypt. Its buildings stretch thirty versts in length and fifteen versts in width.
