And again the abomination of desolation came upon the Church of God, and mouths were opened speaking blasphemy against the glory of God and Divine Providence, theologizing falsely and deceitfully. Like a second Caiaphas, he appeared with that blasphemous council, which had long, vainly, and futilely tried to oppose the truth, threatening to destroy all that was sacred and ecclesiastical, hating Christ and His saints, because it was grievous for him to see Christ on icons. Thus, raving and mocking the veneration of icons, they rejected sacred images as utterly unknown. And who could say anything against this? For the reasoning of a madman, when he is mad, can neither be grasped nor refuted. He was greatly ignorant and impious, and did not understand that above the golden cherubim, which God commanded Moses the God-seer to set above the Ark of the Covenant in the Old Testament tabernacle, the glory of God appeared; and of this testify not only the Jews in Old Testament times, but also the preachers of grace in New Testament times. If cherubim adorned the temple built by Solomon, to whom wisdom was given from God, and if they were revealed to the holy prophet Ezekiel in the vision of the future temple, then you too, when you meet such mentions in the holy books, do not take them as testimonies of rendering honor to idols, and do not for this reason reject the God-inspired books; do not become like the faithless and foolish Jews and those like-minded with them, who distorted the Scripture and are worthy of their abominable teaching—or rather, of their unbelief and madness. For by this they openly blaspheme Christ, Who took a white cloth and wiped with it His beautiful and most radiant face, and sent the image imprinted on the cloth to Abgar, king of Edessa, who in faith asked for healing. From the time Christ suffered for us until today, the apostolic traditions and teachings preserve for us in the holy Gospels reminders of what Christ did for us and how He suffered for us, which we with reverence depict and venerate, although blasphemers rise up against these images. We also accept with obedience the ancient tradition that the holy apostle and evangelist Luke himself painted icons of the Savior and of the Mother of God, which are preserved in Rome even now. As for the numerous testimonies of the Jerusalemites who have been there, they confirm that the ancient tradition is preserved faithfully even to this day, and that the widely venerated sacred images are in agreement with it and are comely.
