They say that you have declared war on Rome, since you have not paid all the tribute to the emperor and have destroyed the covered passages from the Tower of Antonia. You can avert this accusation if you rebuild the passages and join them together, and also collect the remainder of the tribute. For the city does not belong to Florus, but to the emperor.” The people obeyed the king and divided: some began to build the covered passages, while others went among all the people to collect hastily the remainder of the tribute, forty talents.

Then Agrippa ordered the people to obey Florus until the emperor sent another governor in his place. But the people were angered by these words, and reviled the king, and threw stones at him, and drove him out of the city.

Some Jews united and went to the fortress of Masada, and entered it by night, and killed the Roman guard and stationed their own there.

Eleazar, son of the high priest Ananias, gathered the strongest young men and the temple servants, and became their commander. And he ordered the priests not to accept gifts from foreigners and not to offer sacrifices for the emperor. But the high priests and the leading men asked that the accepted custom be preserved, and said: “Our forefathers adorned the temple with offerings from foreigners, and accepted gifts from other peoples, and did not separate these sacrifices from those offered by the Jews. But you, acting thus now, will bring the Roman arms upon the Jews. Fine, reject the emperor’s sacrifices. But then know that, rejecting the Roman sacrifices, you will soon be unable to offer your own.”

None of the rebels heeded these words. Seeing that they could not stop their rebellion, the high priests and the leading men sent to Florus, urging him to come to the city with an army before the flame of war blazed up. However, Florus wanted precisely to kindle the war, and therefore gave no answer.

Agrippa, caring that his kingdom should not perish, sent to the aid of the high priests and leading men at Masada three thousand horsemen from Auranitis and Trachonitis with the commander Darius. With their support the high priests and the peacefully minded Jews entered the upper city, while the rebels seized the lower city and the temple.

And for seven days they fought, and much blood was shed on both sides, but no one could prevail.

And then came the Feast of the Carrying of Wood, on which it was the custom for all to bring wood to the altar, so that the fire might always have fuel and burn unceasingly.
