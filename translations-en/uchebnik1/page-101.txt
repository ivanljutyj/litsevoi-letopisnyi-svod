And He went to His usual place and did His usual works. And again a great crowd gathered around Him, and He was glorified for His deeds more than anyone. And the lawyers began to be tormented by envy, and they gave Pilate 30 talents of gold so that he would kill Him. But Pilate took the gold and allowed them themselves to fulfill their desire.

On the Crucifixion of Christ

And the lawyers took Him and crucified Him according to the ancestral law.

Later the people raised another revolt. Pilate took the sacred treasury called Korban and spent it on constructing an aqueduct through which water ran from the Jordan for 400 versts. And the people shouted at Pilate, and he sent soldiers to beat them with rods; the people ran, and 3 thousand people were trampled, and the rest calmed down.

On Gaius the Emperor

After Tiberius, Gaius became emperor. His own happiness was not enough for him. He destroyed the noble people in his own country and rushed, impious, against Judea. He sent Petronius with an army to Jerusalem, so that he would set up the abominable image of Gaius in the temple, and if the Jews opposed him, he would kill them and root out the whole people.

And Petronius set out, and the Jews learned of this: some believed and were afraid, but others did not believe. And Petronius came to Ptolemais, and the Jews met him in the field with their wives and children and begged him not to violate their ancestral law and to spare them. But Petronius heeded their entreaties and left the emperor’s image there. Ptolemais is a Galilean city, built on the seashore, on a large plain surrounded by mountains: on the eastern side are the Galilean mountains, 60 versts away, and to the south Mount Carmel, 100 versts away. Near the city is a river called Belus. There is the tomb of Memnon. There too is a round and deep sea, and the wind blows and brings sand, which becomes glass. And many ships come and take this glass, and it does not run out, because the wind constantly brings sand and replenishes what has been taken. But when they are filled and return the glass to its former place, it again becomes sand.

But Petronius allowed the people to withdraw to Galilee and summoned them all, and urged them to yield before the might of Rome and the threats of the emperor; he showed them how vain and useless their requests were, since all nations honor the emperor’s image equally with other gods, and only the Jews resist, which could be understood as a declaration of war on Rome. But the Jews wept and pointed out that the law and ancestral custom forbade them, both in the temple and in any other place, to set up anyone’s image.
