And Philip’s wife, Herodias, Herod Antipas, his brother, took as his wife. Because of this all the lawyers avoided Herod and did not dare to reprove him; and only this man, whom they called wild, John, came to Herod Antipas in anger and said: “Since you have taken your brother’s wife, lawless man, and your brother died a painful death, you too will be slain by the Heavenly Sickle, for God’s Providence will not be silent, but you will die in cruel sorrow in foreign lands, since you are not raising up seed for your brother, but serving fleshly lust and committing adultery, for four children were born to your brother.” Herod, hearing this, was enraged and ordered the man to be beaten and driven away; but he did not cease, wherever he met Herod, to reprove him before all, and he brought trouble to all.

And Herod ordered his head cut off. This man’s conduct was strange, and his manner of life was not like that of other people. As a bodiless spirit, so he lived. His mouth did not know bread; even at Passover he did not taste unleavened bread, saying: “for a remembrance to God, who delivered the people from bondage, it is given to eat, and for refreshment, for the path is sorrowful.” John did not allow wine or any other strong drink even near him. And he accepted no animal food. He reproved every injustice. And he fed on shoots of trees.

And soon Herod went to Tiberius and asked him to honor him with the royal name. And Tiberius was angered by his greed, and took the region from him, and joined it to Agrippa’s possessions, and exiled Herod, and with Herodias, and there both died in great sorrows.

And then Tiberius sent a governor to Judea. This governor secretly by night brought into Jerusalem an image of Tiberius, the so-called “simea,” and set it up in the city.

When morning came and the Jews saw the image, they fell into great agitation and were terrified by what they saw, since their law was violated, which commands that there be no images in the city. People from the surroundings of Jerusalem learned of what had happened and all came, zealous for the law, and rushed to Caesarea and begged Pilate to remove the simea from Jerusalem and allow them to observe the ancestral law.

Pilate refused their pleas. Then they fell to the ground and for 5 days and 5 nights did not move and waited patiently.
