Silva placed guards to watch the fortress diligently, so that no one might escape.

Eleazar, leader of the Sicarii of Masada, neither thought of fleeing himself nor ordered others to do so. He saw the burning walls and could not devise how to escape. And Eleazar reflected on the evil that the Romans would inflict on the Sicarii if they captured them. And he decided that all who were in the fortress must die together. And Eleazar gathered the most courageous warriors and said to them: “O blessed men! Long ago we unanimously resolved with you not to submit to the Romans or to anyone else, but only to God, for He alone is the ruler of men, righteous and true. And now the time has come which commands us to fulfill this resolve in deed. Let us not be ashamed! Previously we did not accept slavery when we lived here without want. Now we all understand that if they take us alive, we will have to accept both slavery and intolerable torment. For we were the first of all to begin war with the Romans, and now we remain the last who still fight them. I think this is a gift from God, which gives us the opportunity to die free—that He did not deliver us into the hands of foreigners at once, but covered us with the veil of night, and now we can gain advantage for ourselves—to obtain freedom by a courageous death with our beloved ones. The enemies wish to seize us alive. This may prevent us from dying. But if we continue trying to resist them, our resistance will be in vain, for it has now become obvious that we cannot overcome the Romans. We should have understood His Providence from the very beginning, when we first began to fight for freedom and saw with our own eyes signs from God, and realized that the Jewish race, once beloved of God and chosen by God, is now condemned to destruction. If God still looked upon us with gentle eyes, or if His wrath against us were small, He would not have left so many perishing people without help, would not have given such a holy city over to fire for destruction, would not have allowed foul hands and flame to touch His holy dwelling. Or do we alone, out of the whole Jewish people, hope to remain free? Are we so sinless before God and have no part in the lawlessness of our kinsmen? You see how God shows the vanity of our hopes. For neither the stronghold of the fortress helped us, nor necessary provisions, nor countless weapons, nor other preparations. But God Himself clearly takes from us every hope of salvation. For you yourselves saw how the flame directed at the enemies turned back against our walls, by God’s Providence.
