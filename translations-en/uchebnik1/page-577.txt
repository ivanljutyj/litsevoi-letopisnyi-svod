And Oleg approached Tsargrad on horses and in ships; with Oleg were two thousand ships, besides the cavalry.

The Greeks blocked the Sudy, the Golden Horn bay, and the harbor with iron chains and shut the city.

Oleg, having come ashore, ordered his warriors to make wheels and set the ships upon them.

And when a favorable wind blew, and the warriors raised the sails, and the ships moved across the field toward the city, and Oleg advanced upon it with a vast host. And seeing this, the Greeks were afraid.

And they sent envoys to Oleg and said: “Do not destroy the city; we will begin to pay you tribute, whatever you wish.”

Oleg withdrew a short distance from the city and began to prepare a peace treaty for all the land subject to him, and concluded the peace treaty, and established tribute from the Greeks for the Russian cities: first for Kiev, and for Chernigov, and for Polotsk, and for Rostov, and for Liubech, and for other cities.

And the Greeks brought him food and wine out of the city, but he did not accept them, because they were filled with poison. And the Greeks were afraid and said: “This is not Oleg, but Saint Demetrius, sent by God against us!”

And Oleg ordered tribute to be given for two thousand ships, twelve grivnas per man, and in each ship there were forty men. And the Greeks agreed to this, so that he would not make war on the Greek land, and for a long time they paid tribute to the Russian princes.

And Oleg ordered his host: “When you come, take from the tsar for the journey food for six months: bread, and wine, and meat, and fish, and fruit, as much as you need.” And the Greeks agreed to this.

And Oleg himself took golden precious silk fabrics and ordered the Greeks to sew sails for the Rus from precious heavy silk, and for the Slovenes from fine silk. And the Greeks did so.

And Oleg hung his shield on the gates of the city, signifying thereby victory over the Greeks, and Oleg decided to leave Tsargrad. And the Rus raised sails of precious heavy silk fabrics, and the Slovenes those of fine silk. And the wind tore the sails of fine silk. And the Slovenes said: “Let us return to our coarse cloths, because sails of precious thick silk were not given to the Slovenes.”

And Oleg returned to Kiev, bearing gold, and precious silk fabrics, and wine, and fruit, and all manner of precious things innumerable. And Oleg was called the Prophetic by the Greeks; the people were then pagans, not knowing Christ.
