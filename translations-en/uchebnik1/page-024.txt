And Augustus subdued the other governorships, except Cappadocia, which Archelaus held, and except the tetrarchy of Herod in the land of Judea, since both these rulers brought him great gifts. Herod, king of Judea, and Archelaus did honor to Augustus, and paved the roads near the city of Antioch the Great, for the roads were bad, and paved them with white stone at their own expense. Augustus commanded both those governors to name their capitals in his honor with the name Caesar. Herod the king-governor named his Caesarea Palestinian, and Archelaus named his Caesarea Cappadocian, which was also called Mazaca. Caesar was the uncle of that Augustus, and he gave them royal power until the end of his days.

And Augustus left Palestine and took Egypt, and subdued it, and entered Alexandria the Great, and there celebrated his victory, and subjected Egypt to Rome. And he placed in power in that Egypt and in Alexandria the first prince from among his men, by name Cornelius Gallus, whose rank he named Augustal, marking him with his own name.

And this Augustus crossed the sea and came to Rome, and became proud that he had subdued all the Roman regions. And he tormented the nobles, and reigned himself, remaining in great exaltation. And Augustus killed Brutus, the murderer of Caesar, his uncle. That Brutus had been left to guard Thessaly, and Augustus sent soldiers and cut off Brutus's head under the governors Calvinus and Pollio, as Lucan, the wise Roman writer, wrote.

In those years a certain Sovius, a nobleman from Antioch, came with Augustus to Rome, and died in Rome, and entrusted to his city in memory of himself to hold every 5 years for 30 years, in the days of the month Peritius, that is February, games with buffoon and puppet performances, and all sorts of contests with chariots and among foot soldiers. And Augustus came to Antioch a second time, and Augustus's son-in-law Agrippa then removed earth from the old building for spectacles, which had been buried because of ancient fear, and saw that it had been made with great skill, and Augustus marveled, and left Syria. There had previously been created a chariot-racing course, like on the Palatine Hill, at his own expense by Quintus Marcianus, the Roman governor, and he came to Syrian Antioch from Rome to Philip the Thick-Legged, a Macedonian who reigned in Antioch; he came to arrange that he should give tribute to Rome.

This glorious Augustus was the first sole ruler over the Romans, and he was called thus: Augustus Caesar Octavian; he was a conqueror, honored and sovereign, fortunate, an autocratic ruler. In the 14th year of his reign he killed Antony and Cleopatra.
