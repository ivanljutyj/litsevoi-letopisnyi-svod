In the same year Maximian Galerius reigned together with Constantine, with two Caesars appointed as emperors by the army; and they helped one another to strive zealously in the persecution of Christians.

On the War with Maxentius

Maxentius the tormentor, son of Maximian Herculius and brother of Theodora, wife of Constantius, father of Constantine the Great, who was paternal uncle to Dalmatius and Valerian, was the most crafty of all the Roman rulers before him. Having unlawfully made himself emperor in Rome, he raged and bore malice, robbed and extorted, and did the like. And many better than he, chosen by the council of elders, he put to death as Christians, while other men’s wives who had become free he subjected to unclean and vile adultery, giving them over to corruption and outrage, and he compelled others by tortures. This and much else did the blessed Emperor Constantine hear, and moreover, having made an agreement with the Romans not to leave unpunished the death of the emperors at the hands of the fierce tormentor and beast-like murderer, he was greatly angered and held a funeral feast for his good servants and God-loving men, strangers to injustice. And many times by letters he admonished Maxentius to cease doing evil, but he did not listen to him.

Constantine ordered arms to be taken up, so that in this way he might deliver the suffering Romans, who had no help, from these evils—in other words, save people. Hearing of this, the impious Maxentius, however, did not cease doing evil. In the East, Maximian Galerius did much evil even more bitter, having received East and West as an inheritance, and then raised up war and persecution against Christians. But in Rome the council of elders appointed Caesar Licinius as emperor.
