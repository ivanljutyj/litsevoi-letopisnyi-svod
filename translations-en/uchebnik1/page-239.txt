Scarcely had they dug a row when the prepared food began to run short, although they took it in small amounts.

And then Simon Niger saw a certain apparition, which came in white garments and said: “In the form in which you see me, so clothe yourself, and put on purple also, and go out there where I shall lead you. Then all who see you will be terrified, and no one will dare touch you, and you will go wherever you wish, unharmed.”

And then Simon Niger was seduced by this vision and dressed in white clothing and purple, and appeared from the earth at the place where the temple had formerly stood. The Roman guards saw him and were frightened by the unexpectedness, and did not dare touch him, thinking it was some miracle. Then they came near and began to ask him who he was. But Simon Niger did not reveal to them who he was, but told them to summon their commander.

And the guards quickly went for their commander. And Terentius Rufus came, for he had then been left as commander of this regiment, and he learned the whole truth from Simon Niger, and bound him, and placed him under guard in prison, and sent word to the emperor that Simon Niger had been taken captive. And so God brought Simon Niger and delivered him to his hateful enemies for vengeance, because of his cruelty and the great malice with which Simon Niger tormented the inhabitants of Jerusalem. He was not taken by force, but voluntarily surrendered himself to torment. He killed many without mercy, laying on them the false accusation that they were helping the Romans. But cunning cannot escape God’s judgment and wrath, and retribution from God will not weaken, but will find the lawless, sooner or later. God will bring an even more terrible torment upon the impious, and when they already think they have escaped God’s judgment, then sudden punishment will overtake them. Such punishment Simon Niger also received, having fallen into the hands of the Romans, who gnashed their teeth in rage against him. The appearance of Simon Niger from under the earth also exposed many other brigands hiding in underground chambers and passages.

The emperor returned to Caesarea Maritima, and Simon Niger was brought to him in chains, and he ordered him preserved for the triumphal procession which he intended to hold upon his arrival in Rome. While staying in Caesarea, Titus solemnly celebrated his brother’s birthday. And many of the captive Jews were tormented by beasts and fell in combats among themselves in honor of Titus’s brother. Other Jews were executed by fire at the spectacles. And 2,500 people were tormented in the imperial amusements. And so, punishing the Jews, the Romans did not consider this sufficient and wished to devise other punishments for them, still more terrible and unknown.
