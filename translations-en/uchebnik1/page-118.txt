It had split in two, and the one who got ahead of the other prevailed. Every day people spent in bloody clashes, and every night in fear. They joyfully plundered the property of murdered neighbors and, as if from war, brought booty into their homes. And he was famed who had plundered more than others. One could see cities full of unburied corpses. Both old men and infants lay dead, and the shameful parts of the slain were uncovered. Only the inhabitants of Antioch, Sidon, Apamea, and Gerasa spared the Jews living in their cities and let those who wished to leave them go in peace. In Agrippa’s kingdom, seventy of the noblest and most honored Jews assembled and came to Noarus, who was the royal administrator and military commander and at that time was acting for Agrippa. These leading men asked him to protect the Jews from the rebels. But Noarus seized them and killed them, and out of love of money he did many lawless things against the Jews.

The rebels came to the fortress called Cypros, which lies above Jericho; they killed its defenders and destroyed the fortress itself to its foundations.

And in the fortress of Machaerus the Jews ordered the Romans guarding the fortress to leave it. The Romans feared being subjected to violence by the Jews. When they managed to agree on peace, the Romans withdrew.

In Alexandria there was ceaseless enmity between the local inhabitants and the Jews. The reason was that the Jews had been zealous helpers to the founder of the city in his war against the Egyptians. For this support Alexander granted the Jews the honored right to live in Alexandria on equal footing with the Greeks. This privilege was preserved under Alexander’s successors, who also set aside separate places for the Jews, so that they might keep their law pure and not come into contact with foreigners. And when the Romans came to rule Alexandria, neither the first emperor nor those after him abolished this privilege that Alexander had given the Jews. Because of this the inhabitants of Alexandria were hostile to the Jews. At that time, when everything had fallen into confusion, the anger of the Greeks against the Jews blazed especially fiercely. And the Greeks planned to send Nero a report of what was happening among them.

While the Greeks were discussing this, they saw that ten Jews were among them. At once the Greeks shouted: “These are spies!” They seized three of them and led them away to be burned alive.

Then all the Jews rose up to take revenge. First they threw stones at the Greeks, and then with burning bundles of torches they rushed to set the whole city on fire, so that no one would escape the flames.
