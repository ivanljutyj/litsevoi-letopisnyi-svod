In breathing, for he marvelled, pondering those with whom we must do battle, and with what hardship we must pass through the air. And then Anthony remembered that the Apostle Paul says of this: “Against the prince and the powers of the air.” For the enemy has power to persecute, and to tempt, and to hinder those who wish to pass. Therefore, instructing us, the Apostle says: “Take up the whole armour, that you may be able to resist in the evil day,” and again: “that no enemy may have anything foul to say against us, and be put to shame.” For this reason let us devote ourselves to ceaseless fasting, knowing that if we grow slack for a single day, and time passes by, God will not forgive us, but on account of our slackness He will be all the more angry with us. Thus we hear in Ezekiel. Judas, having laboured many years, destroyed everything in one night. For our enemy does not grow weak, and always cares for our destruction, and if he finds us slack, he will attack us violently. And so that we may not be lazy, it is good always to study the apostolic saying: “For we die every day.” If we too live thus, we shall not sin. This saying means that every day, when we rise, we must think that we shall not reach evening, and likewise, when preparing to sleep, we must think that we shall not rise again.

After this, Anthony once made inquiry of certain people about how and in what place the soul abides when it is separated from this world. On another night someone from above called him, saying: “Rise and go, Anthony, and see.” And behold, Anthony went out and saw someone tall and terrible in appearance standing and touching the clouds; and he saw that certain ones were ascending, as it were winged; and that giant stretched out his hands and to some he forbade ascent, while others flew over him and then ascended without grief; and at these the tall one gnashed his teeth, but over those who fell away and did not ascend he rejoiced. And there was a Voice saying: “Understand what you see, Anthony!” And Anthony’s mind was opened, and he understood that this is the passage of souls, and that the standing giant is the devil, the enemy of our race; he detains the guilty and hinders their passage, and seizes them, but the innocent he cannot seize.

For these great matters the much-suffering Eustratius, when he was near his martyrdom, prayed thus: “Magnifying, I magnify Thee, O Lord, for Thou hast looked upon my lowliness and hast not shut me up in the hands of the enemy, and hast saved my soul from troubles. And now let Thy hand cover me and let Thy mercy come upon me, for my soul is troubled and is distressed within me when she goes out of this body. Lest at any time the malevolent counsel of the adversary meet it and turn it into darkness because of sins committed in this life that were unknown. Be merciful to me, O Master, that my soul may not see the darkened faces of the malevolent demons, but let Thy bright and most radiant angels receive it.
