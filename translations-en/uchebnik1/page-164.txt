And this Jacob came to Simon, and they swore that Simon would show Jacob honor with him, and Jacob would surrender all Idumea to Simon. Simon honored Jacob with a gift and sent him away with a promise of future honor.

Jacob came to his own and began to deceive them; he overstated the size of Simon's army fourfold, and then urged the commanders to surrender to Simon without a fight. Jacob also sent to Simon, telling him to march against the Idumeans, and he ordered a few soldiers to drive livestock behind them with tree branches tied to them, so that a great dust would rise and it would seem that all the soldiers were coming.

Jacob began to quarrel with the Idumeans and said, "Do you have no graves, that you are eager to die here?" When the Idumeans saw Simon's soldiers and the dust rising behind them, they thought those in the rear were more numerous than those in front, and they fled.

Simon first took the city of Hebron and captured much plunder in it.

The local inhabitants say that this city of Hebron was the dwelling of Abraham, ancestor of the Jewish people, when he moved from Mesopotamia; and his sons went from there to Egypt, and their tombs still stand there, skillfully made of beautiful marble. There too is a huge terebinth tree, which has stood since the Creation of the world until now. The city of Hebron is 2,300 years old; this city is older than all the cities of the Egyptians and the Jews.

From Hebron Simon began to burn cities and villages. He devastated the land so that he left no trace that anything had formerly been there. For besides the armed soldiers, another 40,000 people were going with him, and they did not have enough food. Simon treated the Idumean people with cruelty and hatred. Therefore he destroyed everything in succession; he trampled both fields and grass, leaving the land behind him as it is after locusts.

When locusts fly away from a place, that place remains bare. So too after Simon a desert remained.

Again the Zealots came against Simon and did not dare do battle with his army, but hid in various places at road crossings, and seized Simon's wife, who was passing by, along with her servants and belongings, and returned with joy, as if they had captured Simon himself. The Zealots thought that Simon would soon lay down his weapons and begin to plead for his wife. But Simon's heart was filled not with pity for his wife, but with anger against those who had seized her.
