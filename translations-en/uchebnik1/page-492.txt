When the emperor and his mother came to him and asked, “Why have you withdrawn?” he said with many tears, “It would have been better for me never to have sat on the patriarchal throne of this church, for it is cut off from the other holy patriarchal thrones and is accursed.” Hearing this, the emperor and empress departed in sorrow.

They sent a patrician to him to hear what he was saying. To them he said with boldness at the end of his instruction, “If there is no ecumenical council and the scandal of our faith is not corrected, you will have no salvation.” They said to him, “Why, then, did you write, when you were chosen to the patriarchate, that holy icons should not be venerated?”

He said to them, “For this reason I, weeping, have fled to repentance, and I pray that God may not torment me as a priest and shepherd who kept silent and did not preach the truth, for fear of your anger and of being killed.” Hearing this, they departed, having within themselves much perplexity and questioning among one another about all these words, which reveal the truth and expose the foul heresy. The patriarch died in peace and orthodoxy, and left great lamentation after him to the emperors and the Orthodox people of Constantinople, for he was a perfectly honorable man, respected, and adorned with every virtue. Therefore many had firm faith in him.

And in his place Tarasius was appointed, by rank an asekretis, that is, a secretary. At his instruction the emperor and empress gathered representatives from Rome and from the other patriarchal thrones, and from the whole universe, and having assembled in the reigning city, in the Church of the Holy Apostles, they began to read the Divine writings and to set them before the hearing of all rulers and people.

And the disciples of the teacher Constantine the Dung-named rose against them, drew their swords, and rejected the council.

The emperor at that time led them out unarmed, arranging a feast in the city of Malagina, and exiled them to confinement in their own lands.

And he sent the divine Tarasius with the previously named bishops to the city of Nicaea, to hold a council there. And there was a holy and ecumenical council, and the church received her old and first beauty.
