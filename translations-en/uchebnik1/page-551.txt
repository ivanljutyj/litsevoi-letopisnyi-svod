Then at last he understood that this was not a dream, and he led Vasily, dressed in rags and carrying a staff, into the church. In the morning he washed him, clothed him in worthy garments, seated him at table with himself, and began to treat him as a brother. Vasily was strong and brave, had a firm hand, a mighty chest, and powerful muscles.

At this time the commander from Caesarea brought to Tsar Michael a beautiful but unbroken horse. No one could hold it fast to look at its teeth and learn how old it was, and the tsar was greatly vexed by this. Then they told him about Vasily. The tsar summoned him and ordered him to tame that horse. Vasily subdued it with one hand, and it became as gentle as a lamb.

Then the tsar first entrusted him with the care of the imperial stables, making him a komes, and when Vasily had proved his courage to the tsar, he was named parakoimomenos, or chamberlain. This office is considered very honorable among the Greeks.

Tsar Michael rejoiced greatly over Vasily and brought him to his mother, Empress Theodora, saying: “See what a glorious man I have found!” Looking at Vasily, the empress answered Michael: “Do you see, O tsar and my child, this man, mighty and strong, so highly honored by you? Alas, he will swallow up and destroy our whole family, like a serpent, like a bird that pecks to death the weakest chick.” But the tsar did not heed his mother’s words.

On the Deprivation of Empress Theodora of the Empire

Empress Theodora had a brother named Bardas, a malicious and corrupt man. Penetrating Michael’s heart like a worm into sweet wood, he gnawed at it so much that he persuaded him to remove from the governance of the empire and from himself his mother, Empress Theodora. Bardas also advised the tsar to kill the logothete Theoctistus, who enjoyed the empress’s favor, since he envied him, understanding that as long as Theoctistus was close to the empress, he could not seize the empire.

One day, meeting Theoctistus as he walked, Bardas and his associates attacked him and, having killed him, cut him to pieces. Learning of this, Empress Theodora poured out her anger on Michael and on those who had killed Theoctistus. Bitterly weeping, with heavy forebodings, she withdrew to her bedchamber.

Michael, at Bardas’s instigation, inflicted various griefs on his mother. Thus he drove from the palace her daughters, his sisters Thecla, Anastasia, and Anna. With them he also sent to the Sardis monastery his mother’s favorite, the pious Pulcheria, forcing her to take the veil.
