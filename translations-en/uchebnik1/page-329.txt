Let him receive a worthy punishment; let his decision be recorded, what is written be read aloud, and let those who are judging end the trial and every dispute, and then let peace prevail.” Therefore it is unworthy to pronounce sentence or pardon without clarification and verification, and to condemn the slandered rashly, but rather with much examination and careful investigation, as the true word says in the Apostolic Constitutions: “Let the judge be without hypocrisy, neither ashamed before the rich, nor favoring beyond what is due, nor pitying the poor. Do not accept the person of the mighty, it says, nor show mercy to the poor in judgment, for the judgment is the Lord’s. And righteousness shall pursue the righteous, and unrighteousness shall not overtake the righteous.” Therefore one should not slander, nor believe slanderers without looking into the matter and without verification. For some out of envy bring slander against a brother, like the two elders against Susanna and the Egyptian woman against Joseph. So if you are a man of God, do not accept such a thing at once, lest you strike down the innocent. “You shall not kill the righteous and innocent,” it is said, “and you shall not kill the justified, nor accept a gift to slay a soul, for a gift blinds the eyes of the wise, and righteous words perish.” If you judge without hypocrisy, recognize the one who slanders his neighbor, and his false testimony, and declare that he is a slanderer and a liar, and “do to him as he wickedly devised against his neighbor; purge the evil from among you,” according to the divine Law of the Lord, since it is said of this: “He who kills a brother comes to the ears of the judge.” It is also written: “Who
