The 42nd Reign, of Michael Traulus, from Amorium, who reigned in Constantinople

After Leo, Michael the Amorian reigned. From a prisoner he became a lord, from a captive free. As tsar and autocrat, he reigned after Leo for 8 years and 9 months. Since he hated the holy icons, a painful dropsy was sent upon him.

But Tsar Michael ordered the confessors of Christ to be released from prisons and confinement. Coming to him, they spoke many times about the truth of icon veneration. To this he, foolish, replied: “In what form I found the Church, in that let it remain. Those who arranged it so know whether it is good or bad. As for you, do as you wish, but in my kingdom there shall be neither icons nor those who worship them.”

Michael was possessed by this harmful disease of impiety, which led him away from the path of salvation. For this reason he suffered many defeats in battles. The Hagarenes laughed at him and reviled him, and they often routed the Greek regiments. Their commanders, taking Greeks captive, called the ruler cowardly and weak-willed. And this caused pain and grief to his soul.

At that time misfortunes surrounded him on all sides like a turbulent sea, and the fiercest of them, like a violent storm against a frail boat, struck his heart.

For then Thomas [the Slav] appeared, of whom mention was made earlier. The wondrous recluse Kyknos prophesied to the commander Bardanius that he would not receive the kingdom, but those close to him would reign: first Leo the Armenian, then Michael Traulus, and the third Thomas, who, although he would labor much, would achieve nothing, but would lose his eyes and lose all that he had. This Thomas, forgetting the prophecy of the wondrous elder, being a man very cunning, hot-tempered, and insolent (and he came from a low and unknown family), appeared from Greece in the Syrian lands and, changing his name, began to be called Constantine, son of Empress Irene. Having seduced by this many of the barbarians and Christians, gathering from everywhere a numerous army, soldiers firm and brave, he swiftly attacked Michael, like a boar bursting into a meadow from a dense oak grove, or a young lion reared in the mountains by a fierce and bloodthirsty lioness. He was like Sennacherib, who besieged Jerusalem, exalting himself and boasting of the multitude of the army following him, and he did not know, as it seems, what was said: “A king is not saved by much strength, and a giant is not saved by the multitude of his might. A horse is a lie for salvation; in the multitude of his strength he shall not be saved.” And “The Lord will bring to nought the counsel of the nations, rejecting the thoughts of peoples, rejecting the counsels of princes. But the counsel of the Lord endures forever.”
