The Beginning of the Christian Kingdom, the First Kingdom of the Orthodox Tsar Constantine, in the Year 5843 from Adam (343 from Christ)

After Maxentius, the Great Constantine reigned in Rome, having received his scepter. And first of all he ordered the relics of the holy martyrs who had died for faith in Christ God to be gathered and buried as is fitting.

With zeal he sent orders everywhere to bring back the exiled Christians; those living in exile he returned, and those held in prisons he freed.

And he ordered that property be returned to those deprived of it, and that they be brought home with great honor; and that God’s churches and the houses of the saints be restored with all necessities and many vessels.

The Hellenic idol temples he ordered to be pulled down and destroyed by fire, and their treasures to be given to the holy churches. And indeed deeds soon accompanied words, and everywhere by law it was commanded that only Christians should hold and administer ecclesiastical authority, and that they should fast on Wednesday and Friday for the sake of Christ’s Passion, and rest and pray on Sunday for the sake of Christ’s Resurrection.

And that Jews should no longer buy slaves, nor offer sacrifices, and from then on not condemn anyone to crucifixion, since honor belongs to the Cross of Christ, and that the image of the Cross be stamped on the gold coin and put into circulation.

He had a wife named Maximilla, daughter of Diocletian. By her he begot three sons: Constantine, Constans, and Constantius. He had two brothers: Dalmatius and Valian Constantianus. Dalmatius had a son also named Dalmatius, and Valion Constantion had sons Gallus and Julian.

Hearing and seeing this, the Romans rejoiced and made merry, arranging a victory celebration for seven days; they ceaselessly glorified Almighty God and the Life-giving Holy Cross of the Lord, and without ceasing praised Tsar Constantine the Great.

In the Roman Capitol, which had a descent of 365 steps, a huge serpent appeared, which suddenly thrust out its head at sunset, terribly destroying those living in the city by its breath, especially the children. Concerning it, some of the Hellenes asked the divine Sylvester, saying: “Go, bishop, to the serpent and do so in the name of your God, that it cease destroying people for at least one year, and we will all believe and be baptized.”
