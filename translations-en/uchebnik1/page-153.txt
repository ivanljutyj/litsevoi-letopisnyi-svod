The Idumeans, however, pretended not to hear, but were angry that it was not opened to them, and were enraged that they were ordered to lay down their arms, considering that they would be like captives if they obeyed this command.

Simon, son of Cathlas, an Idumean nobleman, stood in a place where the high priests could hear him and said: “We do not wonder that the supporters of liberty have shut themselves off from you, but you have surrounded them and now before your own kinsmen have closed the people’s capital. When you receive the Romans, you will shout about this joy at the gates and hang up wreaths. With the Idumeans you speak from the walls and order us to lay down the arms that we have taken up for liberty. Who can endure such mockery from you? But since you have acted thus, we will stand here before the walls and seek how to avenge ourselves on you. And we will stand until the Romans withdraw without waiting for you, or until you are pleased to accept liberty and change your minds.” Jesus, hearing Simon’s answer and the Idumean outcry, left the tower. There was confusion in the minds of the Idumeans, and they were angry that they saw no help from those who had summoned them.

When the Idumeans stood by the walls of Jerusalem, there occurred at night an indescribable storm, with frequent and terrible thunder, and the earth shook and roared. This was a foreboding of disaster. The Idumeans thought that God was angry with them because they had raised arms against the capital. Ananus said: “God speaks for us.” But they did not guess what would happen. The Idumeans pressed closely together and stood thus, covering themselves with shields.

The Zealots took saws and sawed through the sacred doors; the guards, because of the storm and thunder, did not hear. The Zealots secretly went from the temple to the walls and with these same saws sawed through the gates. And the Idumeans entered and joined with the Zealots, and killed the guards, and set upon the people, and began to cut them down. And there was the cry of women and children, and there was no place to flee. And all crowded together and crushed one another, and the Zealots and Idumeans cut them down, and some threw themselves into the pits. And before dawn eighteen thousand were killed.

And when morning came, the Zealots and Idumeans dispersed through the city and plundered all the houses. And they sought the high priests, and having seized them, killed them, and stood over their corpses and laughed at them. And they said over Ananus: “Truly you are pleasing to Jerusalem and to the people, and worthy of the honor with which they honored you.” And over Jesus they said: “You are very eloquent and wise, and you labored much, speaking from the wall; now rest.” And they did not allow them to be buried, although the Jewish law commands even the condemned, the crucified, and the hanged to be buried before sunset. If I say that the death of Ananus was the beginning of the conquest of Jerusalem and the destruction of its walls, I will not lie.
