Was there any reason for me to rise against you? Was it from a desire to reign? But I was already reigning. Was it from your hatred of me? But you loved me more than all others. Was it from fear of someone? But, on the contrary, all the others feared me, for I was your protector. Or was I to rise against you because my wealth was diminished? But no one had such means of giving and taking as I did. And even if I had been the most lawless of all men, and even if I had within me the soul of a savage lion, how could your benefits and mercies, father, fail to overcome me and win me over to you? For you brought me near to yourself when I was rejected, as you yourself say, and set me apart among all my brothers, and made me king even during your lifetime; and because of the multitude of good things you did for me, everyone envied me. O wretched man that I am! O my accursed departure! I gave the envious much time. But know, father, that it was for your sake that I departed for Rome, so that Syllaeus might not do you wrong and overcome you in your old age. The witnesses of my piety are Rome and the lord of the whole universe, Caesar, who praised me many times for loving my father. Take this letter from him, father, for Caesar deserves greater trust than all these slanderers. Remember how unwillingly I sailed to Rome, for I knew the hostility toward me because of my kingship. You, father, destroyed me by giving the envious time when you sent me to Rome. I went to my accusers by land and by water, and no misfortune befell me—me, whom you call a parricide. Can it be that even this sign will not help me! Is it not evident that I am innocent before God and before you, father! But I beg you, do not believe what others testified under torture. Better order me to be cast into the fire, and let every instrument of torture be brought, and let them torment me without sparing my vileness. If I am a parricide, I should not die without torment! So Antipater cried out, weeping and in tears, and made everyone pity him. Only Herod did not become more merciful toward him, for he knew that the witnesses were not lying. Nicolaus, by the king's command, said much that exposed Antipater's deceit. All his crimes were revealed: that both his brothers had been killed because of his slander, and that he was plotting evil against his living brothers, for he who wished to poison his father would not spare even his brothers. Nicolaus proved that Antipater had also involved Pheroras in this affair, turning brotherly love into fratricide. Varus ordered Antipater to answer these accusations. But Antipater, lying at Herod's feet, said only: God is my witness, and the truth is on my side.

Herod ordered a man condemned to death to be brought, and gave him this poison to drink; the condemned man drank and died. Then the king had Antipater put in chains and sent Caesar a letter about everything. One can only marvel at Divine Providence, how evil is repaid with evil, and for good —
