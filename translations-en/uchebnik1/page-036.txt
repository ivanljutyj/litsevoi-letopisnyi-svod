Yet you, seeing their vile and God-abhorrent deeds, must not be frightened by the multitude of these people, nor by their sedition, nor by the height of the place. All this they have because of your sloth. If we go out against them, they will be thrown into confusion, and will be humbled, and will scatter. And if they do stand against us, God, being angry with them, will turn against them both their weapons and all that they launch at us. And their unrighteousness will come down upon their own heads. We need only resolve to go against them. As soon as we come, they will be destroyed at once. But if misfortune befalls us, let us accept a sweet death before the holy gates and lay down our souls not for wives and children, but for God and the Temple. I will be your leader both in counsel and in action, and I will take care to strengthen you, sparing neither my body nor anything that is needful.” The people listened to these words, and all gathered and went against the Zealots. “Zealots” was what the robbers called themselves, as though they were zealous for freedom. But it was not for freedom that they were zealous; they committed every kind of lawlessness. And then, having come together with them, the people began to throw stones at them between the city and the Temple. Those who fled they killed with weapons. When the Zealots attacked the people suddenly, they prevailed. But when the people gathered again into a crowd, they prevailed over the Zealots. And standing firm, the supporters of Ananus pressed the Zealots back into the Temple, driving them from the walls that surrounded it. And they ran to the Temple and closed the gates, and defended themselves from the covered passages and from the upper tiers of the Temple. Ananus could have taken the Temple then, but he feared bloodshed in the Temple; he did not want to defile the holy place. So Ananus stationed six thousand guards to watch the Zealots in the Temple and to wait to see whether they would surrender peacefully and come out, casting down their weapons. And the people guarded the Zealots in the Temple; each night there were six thousand guards, and they were relieved in turns. And the strife continued; the guards kept watch poorly, as though they did not remember those who were shut in. The rich and prosperous hired poor men as guards.

The high priest Ananus then appealed to the Zealots with a proposal for peace, so as not to defile the holy place with blood. And Ananus sent John to the Zealots, but before that he demanded that John swear that he would be loyal to Ananus and the people and would not disclose to the Zealots the plans, deeds, or words of Ananus and the people.
