Emperor Leo honored Zaoutzes with the title “father of the emperor”; he invented this title himself, for he fell in love with his daughter named Zoe, whose husband, Theodore by name and Guzuniatis by nickname, died after being poisoned.

War with the Bulgarians.
And news came that Samuel, prince of the Bulgarians, was preparing for war and had found this pretext for it. Zaoutzes, the father of the emperor, had a eunuch servant named Musik, who was friendly with merchants from Hellas, covetous and greedy for gold.

Angered, Samuel raised arms against the Greeks. The emperor sent against him the voivode Krinites with a large army and commanders.

There was a great battle in Macedonia, and the Greeks were defeated and cut down, as were Krinites himself and Kurtikios the Armenian, and many others.

Musik was friendly with the merchants Staurakios and Kosmas, who, wishing to trade with the Bulgarians through Musik, came to the city of Thessalonica, exacting huge taxes from the Bulgarians.

When the Bulgarians told this to Samuel, he sent word to Emperor Leo, but he considered it all a lie because of the love he had for Zaoutzes.

Samuel, seizing Khazars from Emperor Leo’s guard and cutting off their noses, sent them to Constantinople to shame the Greeks.

Seeing them, the emperor was filled with fury and sent Niketas, nicknamed Skair, along the Danube in boats called dromons to give gifts to the Hungarians, so that they would raise arms against Samuel.

He agreed with the Hungarians that they would raise arms against Samuel. Taking hostages from them, he returned to the emperor.

The emperor went to war with the Bulgarians by land and by sea.

By sea he sent Eustathios the drungary; by land, the patrician Nikephoros, called Phokas, who became domestikos of the Scholai after the death of Andrew.

When they reached the land of the Bulgarians, the emperor sent to Symeon a man named Constantine, of the rank of quaestor, since he loved peace and wished to be friendly with him. Symeon seized him and shut him in prison, thinking that he had come to him with deceit.

While Symeon was occupied in the war with Nikephoros, the Hungarians attacked and devastated the whole Bulgarian land.

Symeon, learning of this, turned against the Hungarians. They crossed the river, joined battle with the Bulgarians, and completely defeated them, so that Symeon barely managed to flee to Derestr.

And the Hungarians offered the emperor to ransom the Bulgarian captives from them; he did so, sending people of Constantinople for their ransom.
