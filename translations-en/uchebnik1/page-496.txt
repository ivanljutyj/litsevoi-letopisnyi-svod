Then burning ships were driven toward the city by the sea, while others, drawn into the deep, sank with their men.

Some burning ships sailed as far as the islands of Oxia, that is, Sharp, and Plati, that is, Flat.

Then those in Constantinople took courage, but the adversaries feared greatly, that is, exceedingly, and scattered.

When spring came, the bloody flux, a deadly disease, visited the Saracens together with famine, and a great multitude of them perished.

With the Saracens who remained, the Bulgars in Thrace made a campaign, that is, war, and killed twenty-two thousand Saracens.

On the fifteenth day of August the Saracens withdrew from Constantinople, and a great wind blew, and some near the island of Proconnesus sank on the shoals and perished; others put out from the city of Abydos, and suddenly, with wrath, a cloud “on three sides” came, full of death and with a great wind, and drowned them.

Out of eighteen hundred and seven hundred ships, only five hundred ships were wondrously saved. Those returned home and told of the Greek victory and of their own defeat.

About the man who gave alms but did not abandon fornication. In those years there was in Constantinople a man, famous and illustrious, very rich, and he loved and fed the poor exceedingly, but he also practiced fornication, the wicked sin of adultery.

He reached old age and did not cease giving very many alms, nor did he abandon his great lewdness; for he had grown old and his evil custom had taken root in him, and afterward he died suddenly.

An inquiry was held about him by Patriarch Germanus and the other bishops; some said at this inquiry that he was saved, having purchased the deliverance of his soul by wealth, for he gave alms from his own righteous labors and not from the labors of others; others, however, did not concern themselves with this and in reply said that a servant of God must be blameless and perfect, for it is written: “If he fulfills the whole law but sins in one point, he has become guilty of all”; “In whatever I find you, I will judge you,” said God.

The honorable patriarch proclaimed fasting and prayer and commanded all monasteries and hermits to pray to God, that it might be revealed to them by some revelation concerning that man.

After this a certain great God-bearing man appeared, who learned how and where that man was. Then the patriarch was summoned, and this man confessed to him with many tears before all the people, saying:
