Then Titus went to Berytus, a Roman city, and stayed there a long time, and he celebrated his brother’s birthday with even greater solemnity: with feasts, spectacles, and various other means. And the captive Jews perished at the shows, as before.

At that same time a deadly calamity befell the Jews living in the city of Antioch, for the townspeople, seizing a convenient opportunity, vented their old anger upon them. For a great multitude of the Jewish people was scattered throughout the whole world. They settled among foreigners and intermingled with them. Most of all the Jews intermingled with the Syrians, since these two lands lay close together. And the number of Jews in Antioch constantly increased, since it was a great city. And the Syrian kings who reigned after Antiochus gave the Jews the opportunity to live freely in Antioch.

Antiochus, who was called Epiphanes, having taken Jerusalem, also plundered the temple, and the one who reigned after him gave all the bronze vessels from the temple to the Jews of Antioch, so that they might use them in their assemblies. Also, part of the city was allotted to the Jews of Antioch with the right to live on equal terms with the Greeks.

And the kings who came afterward also honored the Jewish people, and attached themselves to its law, and took part in the furnishing of the temple, and adorned it with precious vessels. And the Jews attracted many Greeks to their faith and made them, as it were, part of themselves. But when the war began and Vespasian arrived in Syria, hatred of the Jews grew everywhere.

There was then a certain soldier of Jewish origin named Antiochus, who was held in honor because of his father, for his father was an elder of the Jews of Antioch. And this Antiochus came to the assembly of the inhabitants of Antioch and stood in the midst, and slandered his father and others, and said that they had conspired that night to burn down the whole city, and Antiochus delivered many Jews over to torture as participants in the conspiracy. And the people of Antioch, hearing this, could not restrain themselves and in a burst of fury threw the falsely accused into the fire.

And the people of Antioch rose up against the Jews to slaughter them and save their fatherland. But Antiochus wished to inflame the fury of the people of Antioch still more. To this end he first publicly departed from the Jewish law himself and began to offer sacrifices according to Greek custom, and then he began to compel all his kinsmen to do the same. And Antiochus said that whoever offered sacrifice as a Greek was not guilty, but whoever refused would be accused of complicity in the conspiracy and of seeking to burn the city. And the people of Antioch began thus to test the Jews, and only a few Jews offered sacrifice in the Greek manner; the rest did not submit and were killed.
