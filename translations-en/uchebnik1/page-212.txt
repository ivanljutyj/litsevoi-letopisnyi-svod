Titus was greatly grieved and sent word to John, that is, Johanan, and to his people: “Was it not you, vile men, who built this barrier before the sanctuary? Was it not you who set up the inscriptions and wrote the commandments, in Greek, in our own tongue, and in theirs, so that no one should cross this barrier? Was it not we who gave you the right to kill anyone who crossed this boundary, even if he were a Roman? Why then now, accursed ones, have you filled the holy place with abomination, trampling it and defiling the temple with your blood? I testify to you before my gods and before your God, who formerly was merciful to this place—though now I do not think so—and I testify before my soldiers and before you that it is not I who compel you to defile the sanctuary. If you indicate any other place for battle, I will not allow any man of mine to enter this holy place. I will keep the temple from defilement, even if you do not wish it.” So Josephus spoke from the emperor’s words. But the brigands with their leader were proud, thinking that they were entreated not out of goodwill toward them but out of fear of them.

Titus saw that the brigands neither spared themselves nor the temple, and he again prepared for battle, against his will. But it was impossible to bring the whole army to the temple, since the place was narrow and inconvenient. So Titus chose thirty of the best from each hundred and entrusted them to the commander Cerealis, and ordered him to attack the guards at the ninth hour of the night. Titus himself also wished to go with Cerealis.

However, the commanders dissuaded the emperor from going into battle, because it was important to them to preserve him, and the commanders said to Titus: “If you watch from the Antonia tower as the soldiers fight, they will all strive to be brave and valiant in your eyes.” And the emperor listened to his commanders and said to them: “For that reason I will remain on the tower, to see the soldiers better, so that the brave may not remain without honor nor the cowardly without reproach.”

So Titus took his place on the Antonia tower and began to watch on which side the advantage would fall. When the Roman soldiers sent by Titus approached, the Jewish guards, contrary to expectation, were not asleep; they leaped out with a shout and joined battle with the Romans. And the brigands who were inside the buildings heard the shouting of their comrades outside and ran to their aid. The Romans withstood the onslaught of those Jews who arrived first.
