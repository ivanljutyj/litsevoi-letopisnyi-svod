This Nero committed many mad deeds: he was cruel to his brothers, to his wife, and to his mother, and starved them to death; he was cruel to noble people, seduced women, committed adultery, and lost his mind from great wealth—I shall not describe all this now. I think no one among those to whom God has given reason even wishes to hear about it. Here I shall describe what happened under Nero in Judea.

Nero appointed this Felix as ruler over Judea. Felix captured three thousand robbers, and their leader, and many of his accomplices, and sent them to the emperor. The emperor crucified the most dangerous robbers, and took countless wealth from the elders and released them.

Then the robbers began to act differently. They killed people in the middle of the city, by day, most often on festivals. Mixing with the crowd and hiding sharp knives under their clothes, they stabbed their opponents, and then stayed near the slain and pretended to grieve for them and seek the murderers. The first to be killed by them was Jonathan the high priest, and after him many. And great fear seized everyone, for each constantly expected death, as in war.

At that time deceivers and cunning men, assuming a pious appearance, gathered the people for rebellion, urged people to revolt, taught them to fight, and led them into deserted places, as though God would there show a sign of freedom. Among these deceivers was one Egyptian, a sorcerer. He pretended to be a prophet and gathered 3,000 men.

This Egyptian led the people he had deceived through the desert to the Mount of Olives. From there he wanted to enter Jerusalem secretly, seize the Roman guards, and reign.

And news of this came to Felix, and he met the Egyptian with a thousand Romans, and there was a battle, and Felix won; the Egyptian and 50 of his companions fled. The land was cleansed of these too. But just as in a sick body, withdrawing from one member, the disease passes to others, so it was here. For sorcerers and magicians and all evildoers united and persuaded many Jews not to submit to Roman authority, and enticed people toward freedom, and threatened death to those who submitted.

And the robbers spread throughout the country and attacked unexpectedly the houses of the rich and lords, killing and plundering. Because of the speed of the rebels’ movement, the guard could not oppose them. In Caesarea, where Jews and Syrians lived together, a quarrel arose between them over whose the city was.
