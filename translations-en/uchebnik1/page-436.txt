May a sword cleave your neck! O envy, fierce beast, robber, persecutor, many-stinged scorpion, man-eating tiger, deadly herb! How long, O envy, wicked one, will you trouble life? For I too, worthless as I am, have fallen into your hands, and having tasted your arrows, I lay down and scarcely breathed. Suffering overcomes me, and weeping troubles me, and tears flow from my eyes. This we have said because inward bitterness compels speech.

On the Aphthartodocetic Heresy. Tsar Justinian was a zealot for true faith and raised a great struggle against the wrong-believers, and most of all against the pagans.

But afterward Justinian too was seduced by certain vile men and accepted the evil Aphthartodocetic faith, that is, the doctrine of incorruptibility, according to which Christ’s flesh, conceived of the All-Pure Virgin, was incorruptible until the Passion. When Patriarch Eutychius exposed this evil heresy, Tsar Justinian deposed him from the patriarchal throne and sent him into exile. And Justinian the Great reigned thirteen years after the Fifth Council.

The Seventeenth Reign, of Justin, Son of Justinian, Who Reigned in the City of Constantine

After Justinian the Great, his son Justin reigned for twenty years. He built royal palaces outside the city and a harbor near the city, and he named the harbor Sophinias, and the palaces he named Sophia, in the name of his wife Sophia.

In the time of this Justin the dome of Hagia Sophia collapsed. It was in the second year of Justin’s reign: on Thursday, at the sixth hour of the day, the glorious church dome, that is, the cupola, fell, and then the glorious and wondrous ambo was shattered, and all its ornaments were destroyed: sardonyxes, sapphires, pearls, pure glass, golden architraves, columns, and many other ornaments. The remaining parts of the dome remained unmoved.

Tsar Justin summoned the master architect who had built it before and asked him the cause of such a collapse of the dome. He said to him: “Your father built this church with great haste; therefore he completed it quickly, and hurried, and quickly removed the scaffolding, and quickly adorned it with mosaics. He made the church high, so that it might be seen from everywhere. And when the workmen cut away the scaffolding and threw it down, from that throwing the foundations of the church cracked, and the dome did not hold, and hence such a fall of the dome occurred.”

Tsar Justin sent to the Isle of Flowers, that is, Rhodes, as his father had, and made keramidia, that is, fired clay tiles, as before, and cut fifteen sazhens from the height of the church, fearing that it might fall as before.

Justin set wooden supports for one year, until he understood that the structure had become firm. But he could not make the ambo.
