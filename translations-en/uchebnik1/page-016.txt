Reign 11th in Syria. After Antiochus, Tryphon reigned.
Reign 12th in Syria. After Tryphon, Ptolemy reigned.
Reign 13th in Syria. After Ptolemy reigned Antiochus from the city of Side. Reign 14th in Syria. After Antiochus reigned Demetrius. These nine kings reigned sixty-six years.

Reign 15th in Syria. Then reigned Antiochus Grypus thirty-six years.
Reign 16th in Syria. Then reigned Seleucus, son of Grypus, also called Philopator, ten years.

Reign 17th in Syria. Then reigned Antiochus, surnamed Epiphanes Nicator, twelve years. In the reign of Antiochus there was a deadly disease, a flux, and many died in the city of Antioch; and a certain Chares ordered craftsmen on the mountain above the city to carve in stone a very great face, wearing a crown, looking upon the city and holding a trumpet in its mouth, and he wrote something on that face, and stopped the deadly eruptive disease. This face is to this day called Charonion. This same King Antiochus also built a house of assembly by the city of Antioch, so that all the nobles together with the townsmen and the governors might gather and consult about what should be brought before the king himself. King Antiochus also built other things and named them by his own name.

This King Antiochus roused his anger against Ptolemy, king of Egypt, because he levied tribute from the Jews related to Antiochus.

These Jews came from Palestine to Antioch and asked Antiochus to write a letter to Ptolemy, governor of Egypt, that the king should not levy tribute from them, since the Jews brought wheat from Egypt for their own food, for there was a great famine in Palestine, and the Jews bought wheat in Egypt.

Ptolemy, having received Antiochus’s letter, further ordered that tribute also be levied from the Jews. And immediately Antiochus came with an army against Ptolemy, because he had not obeyed his letter.

And the armies of Antiochus and Ptolemy met, and a great multitude of Antiochus’s soldiers fell, and he fled and came to Limyt.

The Jews who were in Jerusalem learned of this and held a festival, rejoicing at Ptolemy’s joy, thinking that Antiochus was dead. But what they thought did not happen, for Antiochus, gathering a multitude of soldiers, suddenly attacked Ptolemy and killed him, and cut down many of his soldiers. And when he learned what the Jews of Jerusalem had done, how they rejoiced over the victory over him, Antiochus took up arms against Jerusalem. Thus arose the sinful root—Antiochus the Proud, son of Seleucus Philopator; he took possession of all the cities then in Egypt, and took all captive.
