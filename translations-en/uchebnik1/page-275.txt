Reign 28th of Gordian, who was enthroned in Rome
in the year 5736 from Adam (the 236th year from Christ)

After Maximus and Balbinus, Gordian Caesar reigned. At the age of 13 he became autocrat and received Roman power. And although he entered into marriage with the daughter of Timisiclius, who was famed for her learning, he appointed him eparch of his court, and what he lacked because of his young age he made up for with good deeds. He also began war against the Parthians, accusing them of the death of his guardian. But Philip, the aforementioned eparch, treacherously killed him. And Gordian reigned 15 years.

Reign 29th of Philip, who was enthroned in Rome
in the year 5751 from Adam (the 251st year from Christ)

After Gordian, Philip reigned 5 years. He was a follower of the Christian faith, adorned with understanding and meekness. He was from Bostra, where he also founded a city and named it Philip's city.

This man, having concluded a peace treaty, was in alliance with Samor, king of Persia. That one was marvelous in the greatness of his body; never before him had there been such a man. And Philip was killed by Kedius together with his son, while laboring for the Christians. A heretic. Under him Sabellius, the founder of the heresy, became known.

Concerning Theodore the Jew

In his time a certain Jew, traveling with Christians in the deserts of the land of Syria, fell so ill that he could not move at all. But the Christians who were with him, with many tears, decided to leave him, fearing that they themselves might perish from the lack of water in that desert, since the drought was severe. The Jew, seeing them departing, began to say to them with weeping: “I adjure you by Him who created heaven and earth, who came down for the salvation of men, and who is to come to judge the living and the dead: do not leave me to die a Jew, but baptize me, and then you may depart from me.” They said: “It is impossible for us to do this, since we are not ordained; and besides, there is no water here either, as you see.” But he persisted in his demand, constraining and reproaching them with the same adjurations. Being in great difficulty, one of them said to the others: “Take him and undress him.” With great effort they stood him up as best they could and removed his clothes. He, taking sand in his hands, sprinkled the Jew’s head with it, saying three times: “Theodore is baptized in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,” and the others answered at each proclamation of the name: “Amen.” And soon he grew so strong that, with great eagerness and in good health, he went with them.
