On the Holy Sixth Ecumenical Council.
From the fifth council to the sixth, 130 years

The holy great sixth ecumenical council was held under Constantine the Bearded, in the thirteenth year of his reign and in the time of his son Justinian. There assembled 170 holy fathers, among whom the chief was Agatho, Pope of Rome, and the patriarchs were George of Constantinople; Theophanes of Antioch, appointed by the same council when Macarius was deposed; from Alexandria was Peter the monk, since there was no patriarch there at that time; in place of Theodore of Jerusalem, Andrew was sent by him, who later became archbishop of Crete, and at that time was a deacon and director of the orphanage. The total number of the holy fathers was 170; they assembled against those who had raised blasphemy against the holy, great, catholic, and apostolic Church, who wickedly babbled that our Lord Jesus Christ has one will and one operation. The leaders of the heretics were these: Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul, and Peter, patriarchs of Constantine's City; and Cyrus of Alexandria; and Theodore of Pharan, a feeble-minded bishop; and Macarius, called bishop of Antioch and afterwards deposed; and Stephen, his disciple; and Polychronius, a mad old man, who even attempted to raise a dead man, persisting in such delusion. All these the holy fathers gave over to anathema as fighters against God. But our Lord Jesus Christ they proclaimed in one person and in two natures — in divinity and humanity. They also found at that council that on some icons and sacred vessels our Lord Jesus Christ is depicted in the form of a lamb, at which the Forerunner pointed with his finger, according to the image of the ancient lamb proclaimed to us by the law and the prophets, prefiguring Christ our God as the Lamb.

And from that time the holy fathers decreed to depict our Lord Jesus Christ not in the form of a lamb, but in human form, coming to the Forerunner, who pointed to Him with his finger and said: "Behold the Lamb of God." And they commanded that He be worshipped, keeping in memory His life in the flesh, and His passion and death, which was for the redemption of the whole world.

In the days of this Constantine, the Saracens came with great armies against Constantine's City and landed by sea at Hebdomon, a suburb of Constantinople called the Seventh, and Constantine drew up his forces against them.
