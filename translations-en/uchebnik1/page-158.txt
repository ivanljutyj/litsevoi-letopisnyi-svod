Deserters from Jerusalem reported all this to Vespasian. Although the Zealots guarded all the roads so that no one might leave the city to go to Vespasian, many Jews secretly ran to the commander and begged him to come to the aid of Jerusalem and save the remaining city inhabitants. And the fugitives said to Vespasian: “Many in the city, among those who desired peace with you, have fallen for you at the hands of their own kin. The same fate awaits those still alive, if you do not hurry to save them.” Vespasian saw that the tears flowing from their eyes were genuine, and he pitied them.

And Vespasian set out as though he already intended to take Jerusalem. He indeed wanted to save the civilian population of Jerusalem, but he needed to destroy the other unsubdued cities so that the Romans would no longer have enemies anywhere.

And Vespasian came to Gadara, the chief city on the other side of the Jordan, on the fourth day of the month Dystrus, that is, March. And Vespasian entered Gadara, for the city elders sent to him with a petition in which they offered to secretly deliver the rebels into his hands. These elders desired peace in order to preserve their property, for they were very rich.

And the rebels saw their enemies in the middle of the city, quickly killed the city elders, and fled. But the people received Vespasian with honor.

And the townspeople destroyed the wall even before the Romans’ command, to show their loyalty to Vespasian. Placidus was sent, with 500 horsemen and three thousand foot soldiers, in pursuit of those who had fled from Gadara.

The fugitives, seeing the pursuit, entered the city of Bethennabris, armed themselves, went out against Placidus, and engaged his soldiers. But the Romans first retreated, luring the Jews so that they would move farther from the city walls.

Then the Romans went back against the Jews, surrounded them, and began stabbing them like wild beasts, both those who resisted and those who scattered; and no one escaped except a few of the strongest.

These strong men forced their way by strength through their own soldiers, for the guards would not let them into the city, and they cut them down and entered.

Placidus approached the city and fought hard until evening, and took the city. But those strong men fled, gathered others around themselves, and fled to Jericho.

Placidus pursued the fugitives all the way to the Jordan. At that same time the waters of the Jordan had risen from the rains, and the current was very swift.
