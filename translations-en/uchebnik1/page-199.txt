I think that then, in their fury, the Roman soldiers would have swallowed up the Jewish soldiers, had night not fallen and the troops not separated.

And afterward Titus consulted with the commanders and said: “The embankments by which we hoped to take the city have been destroyed, and the soldiers are grieved, since their great labor perished in a single hour, and I know that many have already despaired of taking this city.” The younger soldiers said to Titus: “Let us all advance together against the walls, and the Jews will not withstand our assault, for they will choke on our arrows.” Men of mature age said: “We should make other embankments in other places, and come with the whole army and block the Jews’ access to the embankments.” The old men said: “We must dig in around the city and not let the Jews go out, either to gather grass or for anything else. We should not engage in close combat, for despair is invincible. Why should we fight those who long to receive death from iron? If we keep the siege around the city, they will either die of hunger or surrender.” Titus thought and said: “We should neither delay so many soldiers nor fight those who are in despair. Nor can siege embankments be made, because neither wood nor earth remains, but it must be brought 120 versts. To guard the exits and surround the city is hard, since broad and deep ravines surround it. We must also fear night and sudden attack. If we guard even the gates, the Jews will make secret exits and come out secretly. They will obtain and bring in all they need by unknown paths, and will sit in Jerusalem, and drink, and eat, knowing no sorrow. And we will have to linger many days over a matter that does not require so much time. And our glory will be diminished because we spent much time taking it.”

Finally Titus said to the commanders: “Let us dig in around the city and make embankments. If anyone thinks this work is difficult, heavy, and great, let him consider that we are Romans, to do no small deeds, and people cannot accomplish anything glorious without sweat; only God can do that.” And Titus persuaded his commanders.

And Titus ordered the soldiers to begin the work, and a wondrous enthusiasm seized them, and they worked with zeal, competing with one another, regiment with regiment, centurion with centurion, commander with commander, as to who would appear more honored before the emperor and receive greater gifts.

Every day Titus went around and inspected how each was working.
