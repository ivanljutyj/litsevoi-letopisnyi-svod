I think that then, in their fury, the Roman soldiers would have swallowed up the Judaean soldiers, if night had not fallen and the troops had not separated.

Afterward Titus took counsel with his commanders and said: “The ramps by which we hoped to take the city have been destroyed, and the soldiers are grieved, because their great labor perished in a single hour; and I know that many have despaired of ever taking this city.” The younger soldiers said to Titus: “Let us all advance together against the walls; the Judaeans will not withstand our assault, for they will be overwhelmed by our arrows.” Men of mature age said: “We should make still other ramps in other places, and come up with the whole army and block the Judaeans’ access to the ramps.” The elders said: “We must entrench ourselves around the city and not allow the Judaeans to go out, either to gather grass or for anything else. We should not join in close combat, for despair is invincible. Why should we fight those who long to receive death by the sword? If we keep the siege around the city, they will either die of hunger or surrender.” Titus thought and said: “We should neither delay so many soldiers nor fight those who have despaired. Nor can siege ramps be made, because neither wood nor earth remains, but they must be brought 120 versts. To guard the exits and surround the city is difficult, since wide and deep ravines surround it. We must also fear night and sudden attack. If we guard even the gates, the Judaeans will make secret exits and come out secretly. They will obtain and bring in all they need by unknown routes, and they will sit in Jerusalem, and drink, and eat, knowing no hardship. And we will have to linger many days over a task that does not require so much time. And our glory will be diminished because we spent much time taking it.”

Finally Titus said to the commanders: “Let us entrench around the city and make ramps. If anyone thinks this task difficult, heavy, and great, let him consider that we are Romans precisely for doing no small deeds, and people cannot do anything glorious without sweat; only God can do this.” And Titus persuaded his commanders.

Titus ordered the soldiers to begin the work, and a wondrous zeal seized them, and they worked diligently, competing with one another—regiment with regiment, centurion with centurion, commander with commander—as to who would appear more honored before the emperor and receive more gifts.

Every day Titus went around and inspected how each was working.
