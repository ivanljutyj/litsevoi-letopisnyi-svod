The Miracle of Saint Sylvester: How He Raised a Bull. They spoke thus among themselves for many hours. Sylvester, by many examples from what is said of Christ in the Divine Scripture, completely defeated the Jews. Then one of them, named Zambri, said to the other Jews: “If you listen to Sylvester’s words, you will have to abandon the law of the fathers and follow a vain man, whom our fathers condemned. But let the king listen to me: let them bring an untamed bull, and I will show before them the power of our god, for I wish to reveal the truth not by words but by deeds.” Sylvester asked the king to have such a bull brought, and in passing asked Zambri why he requested the bull. He said: “Because not one of the living creatures that hears the name of our god will remain alive; I wish to show its power plainly by means of the bull.” Sylvester answered him: “And how did you learn that name? Did you hear it from another, or read it?” He said: “Neither writing, nor tree, nor stone can endure that name. But I, having fasted and poured water into a copper dish, read it with its help.” And while they were speaking of this, they brought the bull, held by many men, bound everywhere with chains, fierce and extraordinarily huge. And Zambri boldly said to Sylvester: “Now the end will come to your words and the foundations of your teaching. If you dare, in the name of your God, say this into the bull’s ear, and you will show faith in the Almighty. If not, I will say it, and all will come over to my faith.”

Some of the Jews urged Zambri to say that name into the bull’s ears. And he said it, and the bull at once stiffened, and, bellowing loudly and rolling its eyes, died. And the Jews boasted before Sylvester, while the Christians stood in complete silence.

And Sylvester asked the king to order the people to be silent. After this Sylvester stood in a high place and said in a loud voice: “Listen to me, nobles and all Christians, marveling at the death of the bull. I know that the Lord Christ, of whom I proclaimed to you, made the blind see, the deaf hear, the mute speak, and the dead rise in His name. Therefore it is clear to all that the name of the devil killed the ox but cannot revive it.” And Zambri, hearing this, tore his garments and said to the king: “I, lord, killed the bull in the name of Almighty God, but this man speaks shameful things and only boasts. Therefore it is better not to let him speak any longer.” Sylvester said: “Listen, Jew, to your Scriptures, and speak of them with your own lips. For it is written: ‘The Lord kills and makes alive. I will kill and make alive.’ And now again name that name into the ox’s ears—and if it lives, all will learn to believe in that name. But if not, then I will call upon the Lord Christ—”
