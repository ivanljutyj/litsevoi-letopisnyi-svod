And he said that this petition was merciless and cruel, and he did not listen to him.

The wise Amphilochius, however, remained silent then; soon he came to the emperor, who was sitting on the thrones with his son Arcadius, and entering, Amphilochius said: “Rejoice, emperor,” and greeted him, but did not add: “Joy to your son,” and he disregarded the son and did not greet him. Then the emperor was indignant and said: “Is he not also an emperor, as I am?” And Theodosius took the insult to his son as his own. “Remember this parable,” Amphilochius said to the emperor. “You see, emperor, how you did not tolerate the dishonor of your son. Believe me, that likewise those who speak blasphemy against His Son are abominable and intolerable to God.”

The emperor understood this and ordered all heretics to be expelled with threats and their assemblies dispersed; and he had Macedonius, Sabellius, and Apollinarius seated on unsaddled camels and sent them in disgrace into confinement. The people cried out: “Let us burn the lawless with fire,” but they were not allowed to do this; instead, the leaders of the heretics were sent into confinement in various regions.

About Bishop Ambrose and Emperor Theodosius

Satan, the hater of good, envied the virtue and piety of the great Emperor Theodosius and plunged him into cruel and inhuman bloodshed. Emperor Theodosius was journeying from Constantinople to Rome.

And Theodosius entered the city of Thessalonica, through which his path lay. The emperor’s soldiers, however, disturbed the city because of the lodgings in which they wished to rest.

The Thessalonians rose in revolt because of this and vexed the emperor, reviling him, and they stoned some of the nobles.

When the emperor learned of this, he was greatly enraged and ordered the governor of that city to be executed, since, invested with such authority, he had not been able to stop the lawlessness of the people.

The emperor, moved by fury, unjustly drew the sword and, without any investigation, killed seven thousand—the innocent together with the guilty. Having committed such an evil deed, Theodosius went to Italy, that is, to Rome.

And Theodosius came to the city of Mediolanum. Ambrose, bishop of Mediolanum, learned of this evil deed.

When the emperor wished to enter the church according to custom, Ambrose met him before the doors with boldness and did not let him enter, and said: “Do you not understand, emperor, that you have committed such a great murder!
