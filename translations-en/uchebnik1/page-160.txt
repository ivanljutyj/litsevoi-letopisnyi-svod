He joined with Trajan the commander, who had brought troops from Perea, since he had already conquered all the lands beyond the Jordan.

And many from Jericho, not waiting for the Romans, fled to the mountains opposite Jerusalem. Those who did not flee Vespasian put to the sword. The city of Jericho is built in a valley over which rise long mountains on which nothing grows; to the north they stretch to the land of Scythopolis, and to the south to the place where Sodom stood and to the Asphalt Lake. Nowhere are these mountains leveled or settled, because of the barrenness of the land upon them. Opposite these mountains are the Jordan mountains; they begin at Julias and end at Somorrha, where lies the mountain called Iron, which stretches to the Moabite region.

Between these mountains lies the valley called the Great. Its length is 1200 versts, and its width 120 versts.

And through the middle of the Great Valley flows the Jordan, and there are two lakes in the valley: the Asphalt and the Tiberian, different in nature. The Asphalt is salt and lifeless, while the Tiberian is fresh and full of life.

At harvest time this valley is scorched, and the air over it is harmful to health, since no rains come to that region and there is no water in it except the Jordan. Therefore the palm trees near the Jordan grow well and bear much fruit, while those farther away do not.

In Jericho there is an abundant well, in the ancient city. That city was the first of the cities of the land of Canaan to be conquered by Joshua son of Nun, the Hebrew commander. There is a legend about that well. Formerly it was destructive to all fruits: both those growing in the earth and those on trees, and to infants in their mothers’ wombs, and it brought every disease to those who drank from it. Later the prophet Elisha caused this well to bring life and health. The prophet Elisha was known to all and was a follower of the prophet Elijah. And the inhabitants of this land received him and honored him in Jericho, and Elisha wished to repay their hospitality with good, and bestowed this grace upon their land forever. Elisha saw that all was barren because of the water, and came to that place, and stood over the well, and lowered into it a clay vessel full of salt, and then raised his righteous hand over it, and directed a tender prayer to the well, and with tears implored the well to change its water and open beneficial springs. And Elisha prayed to God to command heaven to mingle with the water air favorable for fruits, and asked God to grant abundance to the inhabitants of Jericho, and the birth of children, and that this water, useful for fruits, should not fail among them as long as the inhabitants remained righteous. To these prayers Elisha joined the action of many herbs, using his wisdom.
