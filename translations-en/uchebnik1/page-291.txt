About him the all-knowing Eusebius says: “On them here the prophecy was clearly fulfilled: ‘The weapon of Pharaoh and all his force He cast into the sea; chosen horsemen, three hundred, that is, those holding three spears in their hand before the king, then the Red Sea and the deep covered them.’” So too Maxentius, and with him his armor-bearers and spearmen, drowned in the deep. And when he was pressed back to the river by the power given to Constantine from God, he was caught by his own cunning, and brought down upon himself this destructive cunning, which he himself had arranged. Concerning him the prophetic saying was spoken: ‘He dug a pit and fell into the hole he had made; his trouble returned upon his head, and his iniquity descended upon his crown.’ For Maxentius, the accursed tormentor, just such an end was prepared, and all who were with him also drowned in the water.

The divine Constantine, having sung the works of God, approached Rome with many victories and with his army without resistance. The Roman inhabitants—women and infants, the whole Roman people—with eyes shining with great and ineffable joy and having adorned their city with their souls, welcomed with rejoicing, as victor, deliverer, and savior, the precious Cross, and also the great and glorious holy Emperor Constantine.

He, like a garden of piety which he had acquired toward God, was not in the least shaken by the rejoicings, nor did he grow proud from the praises, well understanding that he had help from God. Soon, saved by his victorious hand, he ordered the image of the Cross to be set up as an icon. And from that time, whenever enemies happened to rebel, they held power but a short time, and soon received the torments of the condemnation appointed for them. But the pagans built another whole temple. About it the divine Eusebius relates, saying: ‘Sinners drew the sword, they bent the bow to shoot the poor and needy, and to slay the upright in heart. But their sword shall enter into their hearts, and their bows shall be broken.’ In the church of the pious he uttered the Holy Word, saying: ‘Henceforth do not fear, for you have been condemned; and do not be ashamed, for you have been brought low; for you shall forget eternal shame, and you shall not remember the reproach of your virginity. Arise, clothe yourself in strength for My glory, shake off fear and rise up.’”

On the Baptism of Emperor Constantine

This happened in the 7th year of Constantine’s reign. Since until that time the blessed Constantine did not know of the existence of divine baptism, a fearful sign was given to him. It happened that he fell ill, having fallen into elephantiasis, and being covered over his whole body with sores and scabs, he lay barely breathing.
