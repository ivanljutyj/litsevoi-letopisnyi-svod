And they saw a wondrous vision: a certain power approaching them, and a fearsome man with wings on his shoulders like an eagle’s, who promised them victory over Amuk. And they, resolving to fight Amuk, conquered and killed him. Then, out of gratitude to him who had appeared,

they built a church on the very place where they had seen the vision: they set up the holy image of the vision of the power, and named the place Holy Sosthenion, because those who had fled there were saved. Seeing this same image, the God-loving Tsar Constantine said to his nobles: “This is the image of an angel, honored by the Christian law.” Marvelling at the place and the image, the tsar prayed to God by night, asking Him to show him whose power this image was. And he fell asleep in that place, and heard, just as in the vision, a loud voice saying to him: “I am the Archistrategos of the Power of the Lord Sabaoth, the swift invisible protector of Christians from evil and impious tormentors, and from all unbelievers, and from barbarian tribes, the faithful servant of my Lord Jesus Christ.”

And at once the most holy Tsar Constantine arose, praising with exceedingly great joy the God of heaven and earth. And having adorned the place and built a beautiful church, and consecrated the altar, he named it the Church of Saint Michael, especially venerated by all the faithful at Holy Sosthenion.

The great tsar also built many other churches of the holy martyrs of Christ in various places and lands, building them with reverence and love; possessing vast riches from the royal treasuries, the holy Tsar Constantine distributed them by the grace of God to their churches.

On Arius the Heretic

Arius, whose name was akin to wrath, manifested his heresy in the churches of Alexandria—he made a pattern of enmity that ripened and could not endure the sight of the Church’s peace, for Arius was possessed by a hostile spirit. And speaking without understanding: “There was once when He was not, and afterward He came to be, Christ our God,” he taught that He was created as something other and perceptible, and the divine saying “and Immutable, and Timeless, and Unsearchable” he babbled as “and fallen, and temporal, and knowable.” The good Tsar Constantine learned of all this from the epistle of the holy true God-bearer, our father and hierarch Alexander, who expressed his indignation in the letter.
