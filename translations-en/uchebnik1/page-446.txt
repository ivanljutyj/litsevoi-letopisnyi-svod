Byzantium (586–805 from the Incarnation of Christ).
Book 2

The Eighteenth Reign, of Tiberius, who reigned
in Constantine’s City

After Justin, Tiberius, a Thracian by birth, reigned for 4 years. That emperor was perfect in body. When he was at the hippodrome, both parties of the people, the Greens and the Blues, cried out: “May we see the Roman Augusta, that is, the Byzantine Empress.” Tiberius declared: “Opposite the public bath of Dagisteus there is a church bearing the Augusta’s name.” Then the people glorified the empress, crying out: “Many years to the Empress Anastasia.”

But the heart of Sophia, Justin’s wife, was wounded with sorrow, for she did not know that Tiberius had a wife; for this reason she became friendly with him and by speaking well of him persuaded her husband to name Tiberius emperor, since this Tiberius was wise and faithful to the emperor. Sophia did this desiring to marry Tiberius, so as to be empress again. But Tiberius feared God and the Law more, and removed this Sophia to the palace built in her honor at the harbor of Julian, and ordered those in the rank of cubicularii, that is, bedchamber attendants, and many others to work and serve Sophia as she pleased, and to guard her firmly and strictly. And Tiberius honored Sophia as a mother, pleasing her in all things and fulfilling all her desires.

There was also horse racing, and people from both factions wore robes, special garments, extravagant, made of costly silk fabric called oxyea. So that people should not lose their property on foolish excesses, from then on Tiberius did not allow them to wear them thus, ordering the otoki, that is, the edges, to be made a finger’s breadth wider on their robes and so worn for beauty.

The same Tiberius adorned the so-called Golden Triclinium, that is, the dining hall, for the honored to recline in, and made it most radiant, as it had earlier been built from the foundation by Justin; and he built a public bath in Blachernae and adorned it, and he renovated many churches and hospices, and bestowed much wealth.
