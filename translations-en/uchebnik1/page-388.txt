The nobles and the eparch judge Isocasius in the city of Constantine, not the local prince, since Isocasius holds the rank of quaestor. And the emperor listened to Jacob, and ordered Isocasius brought from Chalcedon. And Isocasius was brought to the Baths of Zeuxippus, and he stood before the eparch Puzaeus. And Puzaeus began to say to Isocasius, standing before him with his hands bound behind his back: “Do you see yourself, O Isocasius, in what state you have come?” In answer Isocasius said: “I see, and I am not surprised; I am a man and have fallen into human misfortunes, but judge me now by the same judgment as you judged me before.” And the Byzantine people heard Isocasius’s speech, and stood and watched. And the people praised Emperor Leo greatly, who had rescued Isocasius, and they led Isocasius from Zeuxippus to the Great Church, where they named him by name, and he was made a catechumen, and was enlightened by Baptism, and was sent to his own place.

(This same Emperor Leo decreed: on Sunday there shall be no work. And he issued a divine ordinance concerning this: that not even pipes be played on Sunday, but all shall be idle. And the people were angry at this.

In the reign of Leo the Great, Aspar the patrician contemplated rebellion, and he was the senior of the nobles. And his head was cut off in the imperial palace. And the bodies of Ardabur and Patricius, sons of Aspar, who had also been nobles, were cut to pieces.

And there was a riot in the city of Constantine, for there were many Goths among the comites and among the other soldiers. And among them was one man, a noble Goth who had served Aspar, named Ostris, a comes. He entered the imperial palace shooting, together with other Goths, and there was a clash between the Excubitors—for so the emperor’s close guards were called—and that comes Ostris, and many were cut down. And Ostris saw that he was being overcome, and fled, taking Aspar’s concubine, a beautiful and wealthy young Gothic woman, who rode out with him on horseback to Thrace, where the Goths ravaged everything. The Byzantines sang of this Ostris, leaving a saying: “No one is a friend to a dead Goth except Ostris alone.”

This same Emperor Leo made a great persecution against those who held the Arian faith, that is, the Exakionite faith, because of the Arians Aspar and Ardabur. And Emperor Leo sent an order everywhere: the Arians were to have no assembly anywhere.

In this emperor’s reign a sign appeared in heaven: very dark clouds, then as if fiery, lasted 40 days. And all thought that the whole earth would be burned by fire. The emperor, Patriarch Gennadius, and all the people went every day with crosses, and with tears prayed to God for many days, that He would turn His wrath away from them.
