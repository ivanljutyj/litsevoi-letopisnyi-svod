The following speech of the emperor: “Against my will the soldiers put me forward, and it is not good for me to reject their efforts, nor will it be beneficial for my life. For the acquisition of royal power is a fearful thing: if someone is named ruler and then, without resistance, withdraws, he condemns himself to death through folly. I will rule over you as a good protector, not as a tormentor. To you all I grant the right to govern and judge. And I will be gentle, since I have before me the example of Gaius, whose murder teaches all rulers to rule wisely.” Agrippa conveyed this speech of the emperor to the Roman elders. They, relying on their army, on their virtue, and on the nobility of their lineage, answered: “We will not submit voluntarily to slavery.” Claudius, hearing this answer, sent Agrippa to them with these words: “I cannot abandon those who have sworn allegiance to me. I am compelled to fight you. Show me a place outside the city for battle. It is not good, through our folly, to defile our ancestral temples with the blood of our kinsmen.” Agrippa relayed all these words to the elders. Then one of the soldiers drew his sword and exclaimed: “Soldiers! What has come over us? We are rising up to fratricide; we direct our weapons against Claudius and our own kinsmen. Yet we have an autocrat who is blameless and just, of whom no accusation can be made.”

With these words this soldier left the assembly of the elders and led all the soldiers away after him. The townspeople, left without an army, were greatly frightened, and since there was no one to whom they could turn for salvation, they rushed to Claudius, outrunning the soldiers.

Claudius received the townspeople with joy and greeted them with honor. Then he went with them to offer a joyful sacrifice for the acceptance of power.
