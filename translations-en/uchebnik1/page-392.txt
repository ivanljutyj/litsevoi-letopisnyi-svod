In the reign of Basiliscus and Mark, his son, the first city of Syria, named Aleppo, received chastisement from God’s wrath, and for its restoration the king gave 50 litrae of gold.

And King Zeno came from Isauria with a very great multitude of soldiers to Constantinople, and sent to Antioch the Great a guard of Isaurians under the commander Trocundus. Zeno learned that Patriarch Peter, whom he had appointed, had taken the side of Basiliscus.

Basiliscus, learning of Zeno’s approach, sent the commander Armatus, and as many soldiers as he had in Thrace and in Constantinople he sent them all against Zeno, and Basiliscus adjured Armatus by the holy Baptism not to betray the soldiers.

And taking an innumerable multitude of soldiers, Armatus came; and King Zeno learned of this beforehand and sent to Armatus, promising to make him great commander for life and to appoint Armatus’s son Caesar.

Hearing this from Zeno, Armatus joined Zeno; and when he approached him, he did not then meet Zeno coming, but pretended that he was going by another way.

King Zeno with his soldiers rushed from Isauria, crossed the sea, entered Constantinople through the gate, and entered his royal palace with his soldiers. The soldiers and nobles received Zeno and were glad, and the Green faction loved him.

Basiliscus suddenly learned that Zeno was entering the royal palace and that all the citizens had received him, as had Empress Verina, Zeno’s mother-in-law; and Basiliscus took his wife and children and fled into the Great Church of Constantinople, into the Great Baptistery, and was betrayed by Armatus and Theodoric, the future consul.

Zeno opened the curtain of the hippodrome, and immediately entered and showed himself, and the citizens received him; and he sent them to the Great Church, and took from Basiliscus, from his wife, and from his son all the imperial regalia.

And Zeno brought Basiliscus out with his wife and children, having given them his word that he would not cut off their heads, and sent them to Limnae, a Cappadocian fortress, and they were thrown into one of the fortress towers and walled up there.

Isaurian soldiers guarded Basiliscus with his family, and the prisoners died of hunger and gave up their souls to God. And they buried them in that same fortress tower.
