On the Baptism of our Lord Jesus Christ and on John the Forerunner

In the year five thousand five hundred thirtieth, in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, the archangel Gabriel came to John, son of Zechariah, in the wilderness and said to him: Thus says the Lord, who created you and chose you from your mother’s womb: go into the inhabited places and baptize all who come to repentance, and behold, I will send My Only-begotten Son; He will come and be baptized by you, and will sanctify the waters and all who are baptized; over Him you will see the Spirit of God descending in the likeness of a dove and remaining on Him; He is My beloved Son, Judge of the living and the dead, delivering the faithful from all wrath. Hearing this, the Lord’s Forerunner John came to Salem, and Jews came and were baptized by him, confessing their sins.

They said then that a certain man was walking through Judea in strange clothes, having attached animal skins to his body in those places where his hair did not cover it, and his face was like a wild man’s. His manner was strange, and his life not human. Like an incorporeal spirit, so he lived. His mouth did not know bread; even at Pascha he did not taste unleavened bread, saying: This is given to be eaten in remembrance of God, who delivered the people from bondage. Wine and other strong drink he would not allow near him. He loathed every animal. Every injustice he exposed. He fed on honey of wild bees and splinters from trees, that is, oak tops.

And that man came to the Jews and taught them, and spoke of freedom: God sent me to show you the way by which you will escape from many rulers, and no mortal will rule over you, except the Most High who sent me. And the people heard this and were glad.

And people came to him from all Judea and from the surroundings of Jerusalem, and he did nothing else to them except immerse them in the streams of the Jordan and send them away, instructing them to cease doing evil, and then a King would be given to them who would save them and subdue all the disobedient, while He Himself would submit to no one. Some laughed at this man, others believed him.

And they brought him to the ruler Archelaus, and the learned in the law gathered and asked him who he was and where he had been until then.
