“Your enemies defeated you because of your greed. The military law cries out loudly against you. My father will also condemn you when this defeat is reported to him, for he grew old in battles but never once made such a mistake. The military law condemns to death those who violate order. Now I see, under my rule, a whole regiment has violated order. But know, shameless and disobedient, that even if one of the Romans defeats the enemy but does so without orders, he will receive no glory.” From these words it was clear that he wanted to hand all the guilty over to lawful judgment, so they lost heart, expecting just execution.

The commanders surrounded Titus and interceded for the soldiers, so that he would not destroy the small number of the disobedient for the sake of the many who had been obedient, and the disobedient would wipe out their guilt by future bravery. The emperor listened to this counsel, both for their request and for expediency. If only one had been guilty, punishment could have been carried out in practice. But if the guilty are many, then verbal punishment is sufficient. Titus forgave the soldiers this transgression and instructed them to be wiser in future and not to yield to deception like children. And Titus began to think how to avenge the Jews for the deceit. And within four days they leveled all the ground from the camp to the walls.

Then Titus stationed the strongest soldiers opposite the walls: in front three ranks of foot, then three ranks of horse, and the seventh rank — archers. Thus the Romans surrounded the city on all sides and without danger brought up pack animals, food, and property.

Titus entrenched himself opposite the Muzian Tower, while the rest of the soldiers stood opposite the Horse Tower, and the tenth legion remained on the Mount of Olives.

The city of Jerusalem was enclosed by three walls where there were no deep ravines. In those places where such ravines were, there was one wall. The city itself was built on two hills, and between the hills was a ravine in which some of the houses stood. Of these hills, one was higher and longer, and on it was situated the Upper City. King David, father of Solomon, who built the first temple, called the Upper City Zion, that is, Guardian, because it was fortified. The second hill was called Acra, and on it was built the Lower City on two knolls, between which was a deep ravine. Later, in the years of the reign of the Hasmoneans, this ravine was filled in order to join the city on one knoll with the temple on the other. After this they cut down the city’s elevation with iron tools and made it lower, so that the top of the temple was higher.
