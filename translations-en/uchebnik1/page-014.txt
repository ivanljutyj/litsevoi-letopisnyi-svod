This King Antiochus fled to the Persian land from that Tigranes. And Pompey Magnus came from Rome by the will of Caesar, and came to Cilicia, which had risen against Pompey, and he conquered Cilicia; and he fought with Tigranes, king of Armenia.

And Pompey overcame King Tigranes, and took Armenia, and Cilicia, and Syria, and destroyed the royal palace, and thereby avenged the Antiochians.

And Pompey entered the city of Antioch, and made it subject to Rome, and bestowed many gifts on its inhabitants, and built them a house for the assembly of the council, for it had collapsed. And they prayed to him and honored him as if he were of Athenian lineage. A certain man named Biblus, a commander of Pompey, was strong, and by the Phoenician Sea he found a village and surrounded it with walls, and called the city Biblus after his own name.

This Pompey asked of the Antiochians, in gratitude, the idol of Athena, which Seleucus had made—and it was fearsome—and the idol of Zeus the Thunderer, made by the same Seleucus—and this was terrible. And Pompey took these idols, and accepted them, and sent them to the Capitol as great gods, since the Antiochians were indebted to the Romans. And these idols exist to this day, and above the idols is this inscription: the people of the city of Antioch the Great, in gratitude to the Romans, showed honor by these idols.

King Antiochus, hearing of the destruction of Tigranes, king of Armenia, and of Pompey’s victory, came and fell before him, and asked him for his kingdom. Pompey received his petition and gave Antiochus the kingdom of Syria and Cilicia.

The Twenty-sixth Reign in Syria.

And this Antiochus ruled over all these for 19 years; in the month Daisios, that is, in June, Pompey departed from Antioch to Egypt, and this Antiochus, a Macedonian, son of Dionysus, reigned again. In those same years were Cicero and Sallust, wise Roman writers.

And King Antiochus, when dying, left all his property to the Romans and everything else. After the death of King Antiochus Dionysus, Antioch the Great came again under the Romans, and all Syria, and Cilicia, and all that the Macedonians had held. And the Macedonians reigned in Antioch the Great, that is, in Syria, and in Cilicia, and in other lands, from Seleucus Nicator to the coming of Roman power, for 200 and 60 and 3 years.
