Cleopatra received Antony into the city, and his soldiers, and married him.

Antony, taking her as his wife, returned with her against the Romans and overcame them, and renounced Augustus’s sister Octavia, his wife.

Antony gathered a very great multitude of soldiers, and persuaded others, and sent them many things, and promised to build many warships and war chariots, and sailed from Alexandria, and went against the Romans with Cleopatra, and intended to take Rome, and came from the land of Epirus to attack Rome.

Having learned of this return of Antony and Cleopatra, Octavian himself immediately armed against them in Rome, both for Rome’s sake and because of the insult to Octavian’s sister, whom Antony had wronged. This Augustus took a commander named Agrippa, Herod’s son, and gave his sister Octavia to him in marriage.

Octavian rushed from Rome with Agrippa the commander and his troops, and came to the land of Epirus, to a place called Leucada, and they met Antony and Cleopatra, and there was a great battle, as wise Virgil wrote in his account of the shield, in the eighth chapter. Multitudes of soldiers on both sides covered the sea, and so many of Antony’s and Cleopatra’s soldiers were cut down in the naval battle that the sea water mixed with blood, and the waves appeared bloody. Augustus prevailed, killed Antony, and took Cleopatra captive, and, having bound her, denounced her, and ordered her to be guarded, so that she might be brought to Rome as a captive and condemned. Cleopatra killed herself, was bitten by an asp, and died; she had carried asps with her in vessels on warships. Soldiers guarded her, but she died secretly from the asp, so that she would not be brought alive to Rome. After her death her body was brought to Rome, anointed with myrrh, as proof to Augustus’s sister, as Theophilus the Wise, the chronicler, wrote. Writers from Cleopatra’s homeland, from Alexandria the Great, report that Cleopatra was taken captive in Egypt, and say other things inconsistent with what we read in the Roman writers.
