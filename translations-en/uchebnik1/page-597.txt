He was there... And when Leon Phokas came to the village called Goliontovo, he was seized.

Romanos sent John Tuvakios and Leontios, his kinsman, to bring him to Constantinople.

Having seized him, they put out his pupils and the lights of his eyes, blinding him and darkening him. And nothing had been said to them about execution, but they did this of their own will, at which the emperor’s father was indignant.

In the month of August it became known that there was a conspiracy of Constantine Ktematines, David Kamoulianos, and Michael, the overseer of the Mangana Palace. They were beaten, publicly disgraced, and led through the whole city and sent into exile.

They brought Leontios, the magistros and domestikos, to Constantinople, and he paraded amid a crowd of people, riding on a female mule.

Empress Zoe, who had planned to prepare poisoned food for Romanos, was taken by Theoklitos the notary, as was part of his official duties. She was led out of the palace, brought into the convent of Saint Euphemia, and tonsured.

And Theodore, the overseer of Emperor Constantine, and his brother Simeon were invited to dinner by Theophylaktos, patrikios and komes.

When they were eating, John, the droungarios of the guard, called Kourkouas, entered with many people and, seizing them, sent them into exile in Opsikion, to their villages, as those plotting against Romanos.
