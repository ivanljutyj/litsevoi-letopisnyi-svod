On the Holy Second Ecumenical Council; from the First Council to the Second, 60 years passed

The Holy Second Ecumenical Council was in Constantinople under Emperor Theodosius; there were 150 holy fathers at it.

Gregory the Theologian was then bishop in Constantinople. He did not want this, but they seated him on the episcopal throne and compelled him. And the city was then freed from heretical deception. A few people in Egypt were displeased when they learned of this. The divine Gregory composed an oration, and after delivering it, withdrew from the episcopal ministry of his own will. When Gregory renounced the episcopate in Constantinople, the emperor and the council named Nectarius bishop. Nectarius was from Tarsus by birth, and was a high dignitary and held authority. Until that time he had not even been baptized; he was honorable and comely, and his life was wonderful.

The great Gregory went to Arianzus, a Cappadocian village that he had received as an inheritance from his father, and settled there, and for a time remained in silence and in higher wisdom, and passed into the other world as an old man full of days. Gregory was filled with every virtue.

The Second Council was in Constantinople, of 150 holy fathers, and this was in the reign of Theodosius the Great, under Damasus, Pope of Rome. At the council the elders spoke: Nectarius of Constantinople, Timothy of Alexandria, Meletius of Antioch, Cyril of Jerusalem, Gregory the Theologian. They spoke against Macedonius the Spirit-fighter, former bishop of Constantinople. This Macedonius, while he was still with Arius, blasphemed, and also reviled the Spirit, saying of Him: “He is not true God, but a creature.” Those assembled at that council heard this and appointed a bishop in Constantinople, and anathematized Macedonius, and with him Sabellius the Libyan, who glorified one Person in the Holy Trinity. And they also anathematized at the council Apollinaris of Laodicea, who said: “When the Word became incarnate, He was without a rational soul; in place of the mind, the Word was born in the soul.” The council decreed: “The Holy Spirit is God, the Life-giving, consubstantial with the Father and the Son.” And they added this at the council, and to what had been set forth by the holy fathers at Nicaea they appended concerning the Holy Spirit: “and the Holy, and Life-giving,” and the rest.
