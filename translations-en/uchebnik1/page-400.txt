This she did for many days, and the Holy Mother of God appeared to her and said: “Believe me, woman, that many times I wished to avenge you on Zeno, but his hand restrains me, for he is very merciful. No one can do anything against the merciful.” Under this same emperor Zeno were the venerable Theodora and Basil.

The Fourteenth Reign: Anastasius, Who Reigned in Constantine’s City

After Zeno, Anastasius Dicorus reigned, which means “of two eyes,” for one eye was black and the other white. He was from the city of Dyrrachium, or Dyrrachion, in the land of Epirus, from the officials called silentiaries. Anastasius came to the throne in the consulship of Olybrius, son of Areobindus. He reigned 27 years, 9 months, and 9 days. He was crowned emperor in the month of Xanthicus, that is April, on Holy Thursday of Great Week.

And Anastasius took for himself the empress Ariadne, Zeno’s wife. Anastasius was very tall. He heard that the Isaurians were gathering throughout the whole land, wishing to fall away from his rule.

And Anastasius sent princes and soldiers against the Isaurians, and crushed them. In the reign of this Anastasius there arose a certain man in Antioch the Great, from the city of Amida, named John, who knew how to work cunning tricks; he was proud and crafty, bought up many silver sellers, and was exiled to the city of Petra. Anastasius abolished the collection of the tax called chrysargyron, by divine inspiration; that chrysargyron was a great and terrible calamity for merchants and craftsmen.

In the reign of this Anastasius, the city of Amida, capital of Mesopotamia, and Theodosiopolis were taken by Kavadh, king of Persia. Kavadh came against the Greeks with a great force, took the Greeks who remained in those cities and the commander of the fortress of Constantina; and many other Greeks died in the Persian lands.

And Anastasius took the field against the Persians, and sent Areobindus, son of Dagalaiphus, commander of the East, the husband of the lady Juliana. Anastasius also sent the commander Patricius. And the Greeks and Persians joined battle, and many fell on both sides, and after this there was peace.

And the emperor Anastasius walled in all of Dara, a village in Mesopotamia. Dara was the name of the village from Alexander of Macedon. From that spear, called “dory” in Greek, it has its name, with which Alexander himself struck King Darius here. Now Dara is called the city of Anastasius.

In the reign of this Anastasius, the great Simeon the Stylite reposed, and also his mother Martha. At that time, crying out, the Antiochians asked Ardabur, son of Aspar, commander of all the East, for the body of the righteous man.
