Zoe, the emperor’s fourth wife, lived in the palace, wedded to the emperor. In his tenth year a new Basil appeared.

When the Bulgarian invasion of the Greeks was being prepared, the Agarenes, learning of it, set out with a naval force, appointing as their leader Leo the Tripolitan, from the city of Attalia, who had fallen away from Christian piety. Tripolis, which is in Phoenicia, is so called because it is divided among three peoples: the settled Aradians, the Tyrians, and the Sidonians.

When the emperor had gone away to the Vountinos (Voutovo) harbor to renovate the monastery of his protovestiarios Christopher, this news came that the Tripolitan was marching on Constantine’s City with a Saracen army.

So the emperor sent Eustathius, then drungarios of his naval force, with the whole fleet against the Tripolitan. But he, unable to oppose him, returned empty-handed.

The Tripolitan followed him as far as Abydos and Parium. On receiving this news, the emperor fell into utter despondency and helplessness.

And so, entrusting the fleet to Himerios, chief of the asikritoi, he sent him against the Tripolitan.

Passing by Abydos on the Hellespont, which is inhabited by settlers from Miletus—the Hellespont being named after Helle, the sister of Phrixus, who was cast into the deep—he then came into the deep, into the so-called Goat’s Deep, which took its name from the currents that have the appearance of a leaping goat. From there he hastened to Strobilos, which is by Kivyra—for Strobilos is named after the place, and Kivyra after Kivyros, the brother of Kidramos, while Lampsacus is named from the shining light: at night, when the Phocaean men who were building it prayed, the god shone forth—and the foundation was strengthened and firmly established. Then he passed the Ivrat, which is named after Ivran, the son of Anthas, whose parent was Staphylus, the beloved friend and grandson of Dionysus; and passing Samothrace and coming out to Thasos, which was formerly called Golden—Samothrace is an island in Thrace close at hand, formerly the Menagerie, because it was full of beasts, having been a sanctuary of the nymphs; after the stream had passed, it became an island, passed into the possession of the Samians who had moved there, and was called Samothrace.

Himerios reached the enemy. However, he did not even dare to approach their squadron; and the Tripolitan, driven back by God, returned of his own accord.

Entering Thessalonica, he ravaged it and captured Leo, their strategos, surnamed Chatzilak, and committed much slaughter and bloodshed.
