Hearing this, Theodotos did not reveal to Emperor Michael what she had said, intending to attach himself to the future emperor.

When Emperor Michael Rhangabe asked him, Theodotos answered: “She is possessed and shouts whatever comes to her; she knows no truth.” And Emperor Michael believed this.

After speaking with the emperor, Theodotos set out for the Acropolis and on the way met Leo riding a mule, as the woman had foretold. Stopping him, he said: “When you become emperor, if I find favor with you, keep me in mind.”

So Theodotos became a close friend of Emperor Leo; therefore Leo appointed him patriarch, having driven out Saint Nikephoros, and also because Theodotos was an iconoclast and an impious man, and, enjoying great trust with the emperor, he destroyed all the icons in the churches.

Guided by the patriarch, Emperor Leo, like a wild beast, tormented those who venerated the holy icons. Moreover, the palace protopsaltes, the regent of the singers, a great champion of lawlessness, always incited the emperor against the defenders of piety and urged the destruction of the holy icons. Once he stood with the emperor at a service in church and heard the reading from the book of the prophet Isaiah, where it said: “To whom have you likened the Lord? Can a carpenter make an image, or a goldsmith form a likeness?” Then the protopsaltes approached the emperor and said secretly: “Do you hear, emperor, what the prophet says? God should not be venerated in icons or in gold, and so it should be during your reign.” After this Leo became even more hardened (especially since he bore a beast’s name) and openly rose against piety, and subjected the Church of God to cruel persecutions until the righteous judgment of God overtook him. Openly denouncing his abominable impiety and cowardice, his sinful life, and foretelling his inglorious end, Saint Nikephoros says: “God sees that the righteous judgment of God will overtake us, for people have become fiercer than savage beasts! What can be worse, and what evil should we still expect? Behold, even imperial power, by God’s permission, has been taken by a half-breed, a barbarian by origin, fickle in character and weak-willed!” It is known that he came from the Armenian land, although some who know of his family, of himself, and of his father testify (though briefly and allegorically) that their family traces its beginning from other lands. From the evil Assyrian root this branch sprang. Leo’s father, who killed his own parent, was forced to flee to the Armenian land.
