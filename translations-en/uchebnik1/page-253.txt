The Parthians, though they were stronger than all the neighboring lands, nevertheless did not wish to renounce Roman dominion. Therefore Epiphanes and his brother, having learned how the autocrat had treated their father, hoped to obtain forgiveness from him themselves. And the emperor soon sent messengers to the sons of Antiochus and granted them forgiveness. They came to Rome with joy, and their father came from Lacedaemon, and all together they began to live in Rome, having received very great honor from the autocrat. He gave them for their maintenance more than 100 kapei of gold, and arranged a royal life for them, and they remained in Rome in wealth, joy, and gladness. The people of the Yasians were well known; they descended from the Pecheneg tribe and lived near the Tanais and the Maeotian Sea.

In those same times the Yasians planned to attack the Median land and ravage it. They asked the Hyrcanian king to let them pass, for he controlled the entrance that King Alexander had once closed with iron gates. And the Hyrcanian king let the Yasians through.

Suddenly, when the inhabitants of Media did not expect it, the Yasians attacked their land and captured countless people, livestock, and wealth; no one dared oppose them.

He who then reigned in Media, named Pacorus, fled in fear to a fortress. The Yasians also captured his wife and concubine, and Pacorus barely ransomed them by giving 100 talents of gold. The Yasians, without losses, ravaged those lands, and without fear reached even Armenia, destroying everything they met on their way.

Tiridates then reigned in Armenia, and he took up arms against the Yasians and fought with them. They almost took him alive; from afar they threw a net over him, and then they would have seized him by running up, if an Armenian warrior had not got ahead of them and cut the net, so that Tiridates was able to escape.

The Yasians were greatly enraged and completely devastated that land, and took countless booty in both lands—Media and Armenia—and returned again to their own lands.

On the City of Masada

To Judea, when Bassus died, Flavius Silva was sent in his place. He saw that the whole land of Judea was conquered, and only one fortress still continued to resist the Romans. Silva moved against it, gathering all his soldiers. The fortress was called Masada. Its commander was Eleazar, a strong man, grandson of Judas, about whom it was told earlier how he enticed the Jews not to submit to the emperor.
