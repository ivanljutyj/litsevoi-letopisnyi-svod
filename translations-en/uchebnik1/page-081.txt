First, a letter about the death of Pheroras was delivered to Antipater, and Antipater arranged a very great mourning for him. People thought he did this out of love for Pheroras, because he was his uncle. But it was not so. For Antipater did not grieve for Pheroras, nor did he lament his death. Antipater grieved that he had lost the one who was to poison Herod. And fear fell upon him lest Herod learn about the poison. And when Antipater was in Cilicia and received from Herod the letter of which we spoke, he rejoiced greatly.

And Antipater made a glorious feast for those who accompanied him and for the Romans. And the Romans took from him three hundred talents of gold, for they are very greedy and insatiable for wealth. If you give the Romans much today, tomorrow they will ask for still more. And just as the sea cannot be filled, nor hell be satiated, nor a woman's lust, so the Romans are insatiable for wealth. Truly they are the leeches of which Solomon speaks, who give their life and soul for reward, and can give away even the members of their body, and brothers, and children. Some of them in their madness consider courage folly. Others lust for wealth as crows for carrion. Many for wealth can betray even cities and their commanders. But we will write about the deeds of the Romans later, and now let us return to our story.

When Antipater arrived at Celenderis, first of all he learned of his mother's exile, and a great fear seized him, for his soul sensed the approach of misfortune. "For the Spirit is divine and will quickly perceive what is from God, as even the irrational creature does. And having from the spirit of the air, man understands beforehand the things that are brought about by the air." The most sensible of Antipater's close associates advised him not to fall into his father's hands until Antipater learned the reason for his mother's exile. Others, less sensible, desiring to see their homeland as soon as possible and giving no thought to Antipater's advantage, advised him to appear before his father quickly. And they said to Antipater: "If you delay, your father will think you are guilty, and slanderers will have time to blacken you. If they have now spoken against you, it is only because of your departure; but if they see your face, they will not dare to slander you. It is unreasonable to turn away from a manifest good because of an unclear cause. Therefore hasten to your father and receive the kingdom he promised you." And Antipater listened to these advisers.

But God's Providence was leading Antipater. And he sailed to Caesarea, and a most glorious emptiness met him: everyone avoided him, and no one dared approach him.
