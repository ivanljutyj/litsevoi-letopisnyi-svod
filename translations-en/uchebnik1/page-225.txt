The Romans also turned to the remaining porticoes of the outer temple, on which women and children and much other mixed multitude, numbering 6,000, had taken refuge. And before the emperor and the commanders gave the order, the soldiers, full of wrath, burned these porticoes, and all perished, and not one of them escaped: some threw themselves into the flames, while others were burned on the spot. One false prophet drew the people into this destruction; in those days he preached to the townspeople, saying: “God commands you to enter the temple, where a sign of your salvation will be revealed to you.” John, that is Johanan, and Simon Niger allowed these false prophets to go to the people, so that the people would wait for God’s help and not flee to the Romans. And as usually happens with those in trouble, if someone tells them that there is deliverance from misfortune, they believe it; and if this liar also invents something and promises a good outcome, everyone believes him and places all their hopes in him. So too this long-suffering people listened to deceivers lying in the name of God. But those who preached the truth about the coming desolation they did not listen to, and they did not believe clear signs. But, like madmen, soulless and eyeless, the Jews did not heed the divine preaching. They did not understand when a star stood over the city like a spear and remained for a whole year; its name was a comet, and its surname “Hairy.”

Another sign was this. Even before the war with Rome began, when the Jews were gathering for the Feast of Unleavened Bread, on the eighth day of the month Xanthicus, that is April, at the ninth hour of the night, such a light shone on the altar and on the temple that one might think bright day had come, and it lasted about half an hour. The foolish thought this was a good sign. But people well read in Holy Scripture foretold what actually came to pass, that is, disaster.

On that same feast someone brought a cow for sacrifice, and when it stood in the middle of the temple, it gave birth to a lamb.

The eastern gates of the inner temple, made of bronze and thick, which twenty men could barely move when closing, and the pillars of these gates were iron, and the bolt, the doorposts, and the lintel, and everything was set deep and made of long stones; and these gates opened by themselves at the sixth hour of the night.

The temple guards came and told the ruler, and he came with many people and could barely close them. And they considered this sign to be for good, and interpreted it to mean that God would open the gates of blessedness before the people. But the scribes understood this sign to mean that the stronghold of the temple would collapse of itself and open before the enemy without any effort on his part, and the sanctuary would be left desolate.
