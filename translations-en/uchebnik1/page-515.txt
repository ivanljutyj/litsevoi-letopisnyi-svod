And you, Theodotus, when you come to the Acropolis, two men will meet you. One of them will be riding a mule; he will be the king.” Hearing this, Theodotus did not reveal to King Michael what she had said, intending to attach himself to the future king.

When King Michael Rhangabe asked him, Theodotus answered: “She is possessed and cries out whatever comes to her; she knows no truth.” And King Michael believed this.

After speaking with the king, Theodotus set out for the Acropolis and on the way met Leo riding a mule, just as the woman had predicted to him. Stopping him, he said: “When you become king, if such be your grace, keep me in mind.”

Thus Theodotus became a close friend of King Leo; therefore Leo appointed him patriarch, having driven out Saint Nikephoros, and also because Theodotus was an iconoclast and an impious man, and, enjoying great trust with the king, he destroyed all the icons in the churches.

Guided by the patriarch, King Leo, like a wild beast, tormented those who venerated the holy icons. Moreover, the palace protopsaltes, the choirmaster of the singers, who was a great champion of lawlessness, always incited the king against the defenders of piety and urged the destruction of the holy icons. One day he was standing together with the king at a service in church and heard the reading from the book of the prophet Isaiah, where it said: “To whom have you likened the Lord? Can a woodworker make an image, or a goldsmith fashion a likeness?” Then the protopsaltes approached the king and said secretly: “Do you hear, king, what the prophet says? God must not be venerated either in icons or in gold, and so it should be during your reign.” After this Leo became even more hardened (all the more because he bore a beast’s name) and openly rose against piety, and subjected the Church of God to fierce persecutions until God’s righteous judgment overtook him. Openly denouncing his God-abhorrent impiety and cowardice, his sinful life, and foretelling his inglorious end, Saint Nikephoros says: “God sees that God’s righteous judgment will overtake us, for people have become fiercer than savage beasts! What could be worse, and what evil should we still expect? Already, by God’s permission, royal power has been taken up by a half-breed, a barbarian by origin, inconstant in character and weak-willed!” It is known that he came from the Armenian land, although some who know of his family, of him himself, and of his father testify (though briefly and allegorically) that their family traces its origin to other lands. This branch grew from the evil Assyrian root. Leo’s father, having killed his own parent, was forced to flee to the Armenian land.
