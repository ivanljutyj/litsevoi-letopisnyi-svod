ENGLISH:
...purchased. Before even an ass could have been heard speaking or crying out, lest there be vanity, so that my father's wife at least would leave the gods, and we might get a worthy price. And I thought in my heart, and they gave the price of the broken gods for the remaining gods, because in my heart I grieved how I should bring the merchandise to my father. And the broken pieces in the pouch [we threw] down into the river Tyras; thus it was with us. And they sank to the bottom of the river Tyras, and they were no more. While I was still on the way, my heart was troubled, and my mind was divided within me. And I said in my heart: What is this craftsmanship which my father does? Is he not rather the god of his gods, since by his carving and chiseling with his chisel they come to be? And again, is it fitting to bow down to my father, as if they were made by him? What are these works of my father? Behold, Marumath fell and could not rise in his temple, nor could I alone move him, until my father came and we raised him. And since we were not able, his head fell off, and he placed another god of stone upon it, which he also made without a head. And the other five gods were broken on the ass, they who could not even save themselves... to make clay of glory, because some called the broken ones 'brokenness'. And I thought in my heart: If this is so, how then...

NOTE:
A page of text from the Apocalypse of Abraham describing Abraham's doubts about idolatry and the futility of his father Terah's handmade gods.
